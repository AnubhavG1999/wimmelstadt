# A city of hand-built tiles

The world is made of tiles, one district to a tile. A tile is the look prototype's board: the same size, built by hand in the same way, and standing on a base of its own. This replaces [0008](0008-an-island-of-hand-planned-districts.md). The island of 0008 was built as far as its whole ground, twenty-three landmarks in their real shape and traffic on its roads, and set beside the look prototype it did not hold up: the collector found its density and its layout wrong. It was too large to build by hand. Its land covered about 1.36 million square units against the prototype's 47,000, so the ground between its landmarks had to be filled with ordinary buildings made by rule, which 0008 had itself given up, and a district such as Delhi came out as a few landmarks among too many plain houses: one landmark to about 14,000 square units, where the prototype has one to about 2,800. A tile has an edge, so it can be filled lot by lot until nothing in it is filler, it can carry roads as elaborate as the prototype's, and it can be designed, built and judged on its own. We gave up the coast, the rivers that crossed several districts and the seams where one district ran into the next. We also went back on what 0008 set out to do, which was to stop the city ending at the edge of a tile.

The size of a tile was settled by trying a larger one first. Two tiles of 280 units a side were blocked out next to each other with an avenue between them, and beside the prototype they lost as the island had: see the second option below. So the first tile is the prototype's own board. It became North America by keeping what on it was North American or invented, and replacing the rest.

## Considered Options

- **Keep the island and improve the fill.** The fault was the island's size, not the rules. No rule makes a building worth looking at, and there was more ground than could ever be filled by hand.
- **Tiles of 280 units, joined by avenues that belong to the city.** This was decided first, and two such tiles were blocked out side by side to judge it. Two thirds more ground than the prototype's board spread the landmarks out again. A boulevard and a freeway crossing on open ground read worse than the prototype's ring of raised expressway squeezed between towers, with streets passing under it. And tiles that share one large board lose the base that makes each of them look like a model on a table. The blockout is kept in `prototype/two-tiles`. Its stack, four ramps between a boulevard and the freeway above it, is a road pattern for a later tile.
- **A Downtown tile for everything invented.** The prototype's invented things, which are Halo Tower, the Gate Tower, the showroom and the car park, were to move to a tile of their own. But a tile of towers round a ring road already looks like a downtown, and other tiles will too. They stay where they are.
- **Nine tiles first, the rest later.** The collector chose to aim for every district, one after another.

## Consequences

- A tile is 208 units by 226, the size of the prototype's board, and stands on a base of its own. Inside its edge its ground is whatever its place needs: a harbour, a hill, a canyon, a wall of ice.
- Every tile holds at least the prototype's density of recognisable things, which are landmarks, named businesses and set-piece scenes. The prototype had 17. North America has 20. A place that cannot fill a tile shares one with a neighbour, as Monaco might with the Alps. It is not padded with ordinary buildings.
- No tile is another one in different clothes. The ring of expressway, the flyover and the spiral car park are North America's, and the next tile needs a road pattern of its own.
- North America is the first tile. From the prototype it keeps the Sears Tower, the Twin Towers, the Empire State Building, the Statue of Liberty, the Gateway Arch and the Golden Gate Bridge, and the four invented things. New on it are the Chrysler Building, the Space Needle, Coit Tower, the Painted Ladies, the Transamerica Pyramid, the Flatiron Building, Marina City, a diner, an arch for Route 66 and Wrigley Field.
- The prototype's European and Asian landmarks wait for tiles of their own: Big Ben, the Eiffel Tower, the Leaning Tower of Pisa, Kyoto Tower, the pagoda, the torii and the Taj Mahal. So do its Japanese signs and the brands with Japanese names. Their models stay in `prototype/look`.
- Every district of 0008 is still to be built, except that Downtown is no longer one of them.
- The city grows by whole tiles: a new place or a new idea is a new tile, and it arrives complete. This qualifies [0006](0006-the-whole-city-from-the-first-day.md). The city is whole at every stage, with no empty ground waiting to be filled, but it is not all there on the first day. Growth is still not a reward for collecting, and [0002](0002-the-world-only-grows.md) still holds: nothing already built changes.
- The island is kept as a record on the branch `island-prototype`. The look, the toy traffic, the hero pipeline, its landmark models and the research notes in `docs/districts/research` carry over.
- What 0008 said about names and care still stands. A business's name is checked before it goes on a sign, and stays without a logo. Places of worship, tombs and memorials are drawn with care and are never the setting for a joke. Nothing political is shown. Westeros is a homage in the project's own drawing. There is no toy track and there are no loops.
- Not decided: how the tiles sit together, and how a hero gets from one to the next. Avenues that belong to the city were the answer for the larger tiles, and the question is open again now that each tile has a base of its own. Also not decided: where a new hero arrives, since no tile has a harbour yet; whether the invented brands are the same on every tile; and how the app runs on the phone once there is more than one tile, which has not been measured.
