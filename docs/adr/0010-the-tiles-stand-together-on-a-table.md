# The tiles stand together on a table

Qualified by [0012](0012-the-board-holds-nine-tiles-at-a-time.md): the board holds nine tiles at a time, dealt onto it by chance at every load, and no longer every tile in the order they were built.

[0009](0009-a-city-of-hand-built-tiles.md) left open how the tiles sit together. They stand edge to edge on one table, each on its own base with its name on a brass plate, in rows, in the order they were built, and that table is the board. No road joins two tiles, and no vehicle leaves its tile. We chose this because the base and the edge are what make a tile look like a model, because a tile that knows nothing of its neighbours can be built by itself and arrive complete, and because it is the picture the collector had: "the game of thrones intro or a catan board with the mini ecosystems in each tile", where square tiles show plainly where one place ends and the next begins.

## Considered Options

- **Avenues that belong to the city, between the tiles.** This was tried with two larger tiles, and it lost the base that makes each one a model: see 0009.
- **One continuous map.** That was the island of [0008](0008-an-island-of-hand-planned-districts.md), which was set aside.
- **A view that settles by itself** on the whole board or on the nearest tile when it is let go. It was built, and the collector asked for it to be taken out: the view is free and stays where it is left. A plate at the top left names the tile under it.

## Consequences

- A hero cannot drive from one tile to the next. For now each hero is put on a tile at random whenever the page loads. A car transporter, on a road printed on the table, is the idea for carrying one across.
- A tall tile hides part of the tile behind it. The first tiles keep the back of the table, and each tile is laid out for the view the board opens with. Westeros's Wall, along its north edge, hides the south of Europe, which stands behind it.
- The whole board is drawn at once, so what a frame costs grows with the number of tiles. Four tiles took about 9 ms a frame on the desktop, and seven take about 12 ms. The sun's shadows are drawn only for what is in view. A tile that has water has the whole board drawn a second time, for the mirror image. The phone has not been measured.
- A tile's place on the table can change while the city is being built: a fifth tile started a wider row, and a seventh a third row, with two places empty beside it until more tiles come. When that stops is the founding's business, and is not decided.
- Not decided: whether the tiles stay in plain rows. A square grid shows as a diamond, and other arrangements would fill a wide screen better but need a table of another shape.
- Agreed for the board and not built yet: an opening sweep in which the tiles rise into place, weather of its own for each tile, things that cross the board such as a blimp and birds, and sound for each tile.
