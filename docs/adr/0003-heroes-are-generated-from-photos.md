# Heroes are generated from the collector's photos

Every hero is its own 3D model, generated by a cloud image-to-3D service from the capture of its diecast and then finished at the bench before it enters the world. The collector wanted each hero to be recognisable as that exact toy, which a library of hand-made shapes recoloured per diecast could not deliver, and wanted it to turn, drive and catch the city's night lighting like everything around it, which photo sprites could not. Generation runs in the cloud because the open models that can be run at home need about three times the graphics memory of the collector's desktop.

## Considered Options

- **A library of archetype shapes, recoloured per diecast.** Cheap and uniform, but two castings of the same kind of vehicle would look alike.
- **Turntable photo sprites.** Faithful from the photographed angles, but flat: the city cannot light them and a freely turning camera cannot go round them.
- **Running a generator on the collector's desktop.** No cost per hero, but current open models need 24 GB of graphics memory or more and the desktop has 8 GB.

## Consequences

- Every hero costs money and needs the network to make, though not to show.
- The capture is the master copy and is kept at a size good enough to generate from again. The generating service is a replaceable part, chosen at the prototype and open to change, so any hero can be regenerated without photographing it again.
- A generated model is never trusted as delivered. Finishing replaces its wheels, sets its lights and true size, restores its decals from the capture, and ends with the collector's approval.
- Heroes carry real makers' shapes and decals. That suits a private diorama and needs a second look before anyone else is let in.
