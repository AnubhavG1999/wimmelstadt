**Verification.** [u] = from memory, unchecked; unmarked = checked against the sources at the end (distances are calculated from Wikipedia coordinates). Search and fetch limits cut checking short twice, so shop frontages and street furniture are unchecked. T = turn. This runs to about 1,950 words: twenty landmarks plus the lap order would not fit in 1,700.

# A. CHINA

**1. Character.** Three skylines on one river. At the coast, grey stone 1920s fronts face glass supertalls across a hooked bend; upstream, a hill city piles onto a peninsula where a train runs through a tower block. In the north, yellow roofs line a dead-straight axis under a toothed wall on the ridge.

**2. Ground.**
- River: Chongqing's core is a peninsula pointing north-east, with the Jialing on its north flank and the Yangtze on its south. At the coast, as the 400 m-wide Huangpu, it flows north past the Bund (west bank), takes Suzhou Creek at the Bund's north end, then swings east round the Lujiazui nose (east bank).
- Streets: Shanghai, skewed grids under elevated expressways; Beijing, a compass grid on a 7.8 km axis; Chongqing, contour roads, bridges, tunnels, stairs.
- Placement: Beijing north, Wall beyond; Shanghai at the mouth; Chongqing far upstream, pandas (Chengdu) 270 km beyond it; Tianmen in the hills midway, south of the river; Guilin's limestone cones in the south toward Hong Kong.

**3. Landmarks.**
1. Great Wall, Mutianyu (60–70 km north-north-east of Beijing): grey granite, 7–8.5 m high, toothed parapets both sides, square watchtower every 100 m, forested ridge; gondola and toboggan chute. Road ends at the foot.
2. Lujiazui towers: Oriental Pearl (pink spheres on columns, spike [look u]) at the nose, 1 km due east of the Peace Hotel; 800 m south-east, Jin Mao (420 m stepped pagoda), the "bottle opener" 150 m east of it (492 m), Shanghai Tower 200 m south (632 m, twisted). Road loops under a ring footbridge [u].
3. Forbidden City: 961 × 753 m, long side north–south; walls (red [u]), 52 m moat, corner towers, yellow roofs on white marble; Jingshan hill directly north. Roads circle the moat.
4. The Bund: 1.6 km of stone fronts; south to north, domed [u] bank (No. 12), Customs House clock tower (No. 13), Peace Hotel's green pyramid at the Nanjing Road corner (No. 20), steel-truss Waibaidu Bridge. Boulevard in front.
5. Liziba station, 4 km west-south-west of Chongqing's centre: 19-storey slab; monorail in at floors 6–8, out the far side. Road and viewing deck below.
6. Temple of Heaven, 4 km south-south-east of the palace: round triple-roofed hall, dark-blue tiles, 36 m wide, 38 m tall, on three white marble rings. Park, no road.
7. Yu Garden teahouse, 1 km south of the domed bank, a block inland: timber pavilion on a pond, reached by a nine-turn zigzag bridge; reopened July 2025 after two years of repairs. Pedestrian.
8. Hongya Cave: 11-storey cascade of stilt houses on the peninsula's Jialing flank. Street at roof level, river road at the base [u].
9. Bird's Nest, 8.4 km due north of the palace: oval saddle of grey steel lattice round a red bowl. Plaza.
10. CCTV HQ, 5.7 km due east on the East Third Ring Road: 234 m glass loop of two leaning legs joined by angled arms. Ring road alongside.

**4. Roads and car culture.**
- Shanghai International Circuit, 28 km west-north-west of the Bund: 5.451 km, 16 turns, drawn like 上; "snail" T1–4, 1.2 km straight into the T14 hairpin, wing-like bridges over the pit straight. F1 since 2004 (not 2020–23), contracted to 2030.
- Dragon pillar: one fat pillar under the Yan'an/North–South elevated crossing, wrapped in silver relief with gold dragons [colours u]. Yan'an Elevated is the city end of the G50 Shanghai–Chongqing Expressway.
- Nanpu Bridge: cable-stayed; 180 m double spiral ramp on the west bank only.
- Maglev: elevated guideway from Longyang Road, 6.5 km south-east of the towers, to the coastal airport; 30 km in 8 minutes.
- Nanjing Road: west from the Peace Hotel; pedestrian, neon blade signs [u].
- Chongqing: Huangjuewan interchange (five levels, 37 m high, 14 or 15 ramps) 2.7 km north-east of the peninsula tip, across the Yangtze; cableway (1,166 m, two cabins) off the peninsula's south flank.
- Tianmen: 99 hairpins in 11 km, climbing 1,100 m to 999 steps under a 131 m rock arch; park buses on it, cable car overhead. VW ID.R record 7:38.585 (2019).

**5. Businesses** (all trading; frontages [u]).
- 全聚德 Quanjude roast duck: grey-brick gate, gold-on-black plaque.
- 同仁堂 Tong Ren Tang pharmacy: red-and-gold timber front.
- 南翔馒头店 Nanxiang dumplings, by the zigzag bridge: queue window.
- 和平饭店 Peace Hotel: revolving door under the pyramid.
- 海底捞 Haidilao hotpot: red sign, white "Hi" bubble.
- 瑞幸咖啡 Luckin Coffee: blue sign, white stag head.
- 中国石化 Sinopec fuel station: red-and-white canopy.
- Parodies: 全鸭德 ("All-Duck Virtue"), 慢幸咖啡 ("Slow-Luck Coffee"), 九十九弯刹车行 ("99-Bends Brake Shop").

**6. Street life.**
- Taxis: Shanghai by fleet (Dazhong mint-turquoise, Qiangsheng yellow, Jinjiang white, Bashi green, Haibo blue; older listing); Beijing two-tone with a yellow waistband; Chongqing all yellow.
- Plates: white on blue; trucks and buses black on yellow; electric cars black on a green gradient, one character longer. Prefixes 沪, 京, 渝 [渝 u].
- Shanghai trolleybuses (world's oldest network). [u] E-scooters, yellow and blue delivery riders, share-bike rows; blue street plates with pinyin and compass points; green expressway signs; stone lions, red lanterns, plane trees.

**7. Small scenes.**
- Phones go up as the monorail slides into the Liziba block.
- Dawn choir and fan dancers at the Temple of Heaven.
- Tourist on the toboggan chute below the Wall.
- Lost car circling Huangjuewan.
- Pandas chewing bamboo; cormorant fisherman under the karst cones [u].

**8. Watch out.**
- Maglev capped at 300 km/h since 2021; don't label it 431.
- Gone: Yan'an Elevated's ramp down to the Bund (2008); Chongqing's Jialing cableway [u].
- The peninsula tip now carries Raffles City, towers under a 300 m skybridge by Marina Bay Sands' architect; simplify it so the districts don't echo.
- Tianmen: a carmaker's stair-climb stunt in November 2025 damaged the steps; keep cars off them.
- IP: the Bird's Nest's owner won a 2011 ruling against fireworks shaped like the stadium; a bottle shaped like Beijing's CITIC Tower lost at first instance. Keep models unbranded and non-commercial; no real logos.
- Leave out (my judgement): Tiananmen Square and the gate portrait, flags, slogans, leaders, police and military vehicles, maps and borders, Taiwan, Tibet and Xinjiang.

# B. SINGAPORE

**1. Character.** One bay does the work: three towers carrying a boat, a grove of steel trees, two spiky shells, a white lion-fish spouting water and a giant wheel face each other across it. Behind come pastel shophouses under glass towers, then numbered slab blocks, all under rain trees. Once a year the bay roads become a floodlit race track.

**2. Ground.**
- Flat and largely reclaimed round a bay dammed into a reservoir [u]; traffic keeps left.
- From the Merlion (west shore, river mouth, facing east): Esplanade 330 m north; Flyer 1 km east-north-east with the NS Square site halfway; Marina Bay Sands 780 m south-east across the water, the Gardens 600 m east of it; Lau Pa Sat 830 m south-west; Chinatown 1.1 km west-south-west; Raffles Hotel 900 m north with the Padang halfway; Kampong Glam 1.8 km north-north-east; Little India 2.2 km north.
- Jewel is 17–20 km east-north-east; the Causeway is on the north shore; housing estates lie between [u].

**3. Landmarks.**
1. Marina Bay Sands: three 55-storey slabs with splayed legs under a 340 m boat-shaped deck that overhangs the north end by 66.5 m; white lotus museum at the water. A fourth tower is being built beside it (started July 2025, opening targeted 2031). Avenue at its foot [u].
2. Merlion: 8.6 m white [u] lion head on a fish body, spouting into the bay in front of the Fullerton Hotel; 2 m cub behind. Bridge road behind it.
3. Supertrees and domes: 18 steel trees 25–50 m with planted trunks and splayed purple [u] crowns; two glass shells, Flower Dome low and wide, Cloud Forest taller. Footpaths only.
4. Esplanade: two rounded shells under 7,000-plus pointed aluminium shades ("the Durian"). T14 in front.
5. Singapore Flyer: 165 m wheel, 28 capsules, three-storey terminal; final corners at its foot.
6. Raffles Hotel, 1 Beach Road: three-storey colonial block, verandahs, palm court; white walls, red tile roofs, doorman [u].
7. Shophouses [look u]: 2–3 storey terraces, arcaded "five-foot way", shutters, pastel fronts, clay roofs. Chinatown has Sri Mariamman Temple's Dravidian gate-tower; Little India the brightest colours and Tekka market; Kampong Glam a palm-lined street aimed at the gold-domed [u] Sultan Mosque.
8. Lau Pa Sat, 18 Raffles Quay: octagonal cast-iron market with a clock tower, dwarfed by towers. Boon Tat Street beside it becomes "Satay Street" from 7 pm (3 pm weekends) [car closure u].
9. Jewel Changi: glass-and-steel doughnut dome between Terminals 1–3; a 40 m waterfall drops through the hole and the airport Skytrain passes beside it.
10. Housing blocks: Pinnacle@Duxton, seven linked 50-storey slabs; ordinary estates [u] are pale slabs with colour bands, big block numbers, open ground floors and laundry poles.

**4. Roads and car culture.**
- Marina Bay Street Circuit (layout since 2023): 4.927 km, 19 turns, 62 laps, anticlockwise, at night; first Sprint in 2026; contract to 2028. Lap:
  1. Pits beside the Flyer; under the Benjamin Sheares Bridge viaduct into T1–3 "Sheares" (left-right-left).
  2. T5 right onto Raffles Boulevard, the fastest point (320 km/h).
  3. T7 "Memorial" at the Civilian War Memorial onto Nicoll Highway; T8 right onto Stamford Road; T9 onto St Andrew's Road along the Padang and old City Hall.
  4. T10 left, T11 right, T12 over Anderson Bridge, T13 hairpin at the Fullerton Hotel.
  5. Esplanade Bridge beside Merlion Park; T14 right in front of the Esplanade.
  6. 398 m straight on Raffles Avenue past the NS Square site; T16 right, T17 left, T18–19 flat past the Flyer.

  Marina Bay Sands is only seen across the water.
- Why cars cost so much: a buyer must first win a 10-year Certificate of Entitlement at auction; car numbers have been frozen since 2018. Small-car certificates hit a record S$139,001 in early November 2026. Registration tax adds 100% of the first S$20,000 of import value, rising to 320% above S$80,000, plus 20% excise.
- Road pricing: gantries over the lanes since 1998. Gantry charging ends 31 December 2026 and satellite-based ERP 2 starts the next day; all 95 gantries (62 already idle) are removed or modified between October 2026 and about June 2027, replaced by blue oval "ERP" lane markings and "ERP Charge Zone" signs. Model gantries for a 2026 scene, ovals after.
- Car vending machine: Autobahn Motors' glass tower, a supercar in every lit bay, 15 storeys, 60 cars, opened 2017 at Jalan Kilang; a second tower, Ten Square at 1 Short Street, wears a giant LED screen. A lift delivers the car in two minutes. An October 2026 article shows one running; I could not confirm which.
- Causeway: 1.056 km to Johor Bahru with a road, one rail track and a water pipeline; 300,000 crossings a day. A parallel RTS rail link is due February 2027.
- Also: the Thomson Road Grand Prix course (1961–73); [u] South Buona Vista Road's "99 bends".

**5. Businesses** (all trading; frontages [u]).
- Raffles Hotel's Long Bar, birthplace of the Singapore Sling.
- 亚坤 Ya Kun Kaya Toast: retro café, red-brown sign.
- 老曾记 Old Chang Kee curry puffs: yellow kiosk, red lettering.
- 美珍香 Bee Cheng Hiang dried meat: red front, gold characters.
- 天天海南鸡饭 Tian Tian chicken rice, Maxwell Food Centre: blue stall sign (stall itself unchecked).
- Mustafa Centre, Little India: long plain department store.
- Tiger Beer: blue-and-orange banners at coffee shops.
- Parodies: 老巴车 Lau Pa Chia ("Old Bus", after 老巴刹 Lau Pa Sat); Kedai Kopi Gantri (Malay, "Gantry Coffee Shop"); சிங்க டயர்ஸ் (Tamil, "Singa Tyres"; needs a native check).

**6. Street life.**
- Taxis: Comfort blue, CityCab yellow, Trans-Cab red, Strides Premier lime or silver, Prime copper, GrabCab green (new 2025); five yellow-top black cabs survive.
- Buses: all "lush green", many double-deck, more electric each year.
- Plates: white on black, or black on white (front) and yellow (rear); red plates mark off-peak cars. Singapore has no green EV plate.
- [u] Rain-tree canopy, planted medians, covered walkways, green street signs; English first, with Chinese, Malay and Tamil on notices and shop signs.

**7. Small scenes.**
- Race cars under floodlights past the Padang.
- Dusk at Lau Pa Sat: tables roll out, satay smoke.
- Lift lowering a red supercar from the vending tower.
- Tissue packet reserving a hawker table [u].
- Otter family crossing a bay road [u].

**8. Watch out.**
- The circuit's loop under the Bay Grandstand went in 2023 (grandstand demolished); NS Square is due 2027 and the 2027 layout is unconfirmed.
- Sentosa's 37 m Merlion closed in 2019 and was dismantled. Satay by the Bay was slated to close on 1 October 2026.
- ComfortDelGro's "Zig" rebrand (February 2026) reworks its blue; taxi colours may change.
- Trademarks: the Merlion is the Tourism Board's protected symbol and needs its approval; avoid F1, Singapore Grand Prix and Marina Bay Sands marks.
- Leave out (my judgement): worship scenes and deity figures, ethnic caricature, punishment jokes, politicians, cross-border disputes.
