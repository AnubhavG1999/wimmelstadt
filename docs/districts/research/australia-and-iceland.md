## A. Australia

**1. Character.** A red interior inside a thin green-and-sand rim, with one harbour city in the south-east corner: white shells, a grey steel arch, green-and-cream ferries. One road rings the coast, as the real Highway 1 does (about 14,500 km), and one dead-straight road crosses the treeless south. Two circuits of opposite character: a mountain road and a lakeside park. Giant roadside objects mark the highway stops.

**2. Ground.**
- South-east: Sydney on a branching harbour, centre on the south shore under Sydney Tower (309 m needle, gold turret [u]); Bondi's sand crescent, with the white-edged Icebergs pool at its south end [u], on the ocean side; Bathurst inland to the west [u].
- South coast: Melbourne's flat tram grid on the Yarra, Albert Park Lake just south [u]; the Great Ocean Road runs west along cliffs.
- Centre: flat red sand, Uluru alone. South edge: the treeless Nullarbor ("no tree"). North-east: the reef offshore.

**3. Landmarks.**
1. Opera House: white-and-cream tiled shell clusters on a pink-granite-clad podium, about 65 m high. Fills Bennelong Point, east side of Sydney Cove. Forecourt only, no road.
2. Harbour Bridge: grey [u] steel arch, 134 m high, four granite-faced pylons, just west of the Opera House. Eight lanes and two rail tracks go over; ferries under.
3. Uluru: one rust-red, flat-topped, ribbed monolith, 348 m high [u]. A road circles the base at a distance [u].
4. Mount Panorama: wooded hill with the track draped over it, pits at the foot. The road is the landmark.
5. Luna Park face: 9 m grin between two 26 m Art Deco towers at the bridge's northern foot (Milsons Point). People walk in through the mouth.
6. Flinders Street Station: long low building, copper corner dome, row of clocks over the door, backing onto the Yarra. Trams pass the entrance.
7. Twelve Apostles: seven tan limestone stacks up to 50 m in the surf. The road runs behind on the clifftop.
8. Big Banana (Coffs Harbour, 1964, yellow, walk-through) and Big Merino (Goulburn: concrete ram 15.2 m tall, shop inside, at a highway service station).

**4. Roads and car culture.**
- Mount Panorama: 6.213 km, 23 turns, 174 m of climb, anticlockwise [u]; between events a two-way public road at 60 km/h. Lap: Hell Corner, Mountain Straight (uphill), Griffins Bend, the Cutting (1-in-6 exit), Reid Park, Sulman Park (highest point), McPhillamy Park, Skyline (descent starts), the Esses, the Dipper, Forrest's Elbow, Conrod Straight (about 300 km/h), the Chase (added 1987), Murray's Corner.
- Bathurst 1000 today: the Repco Bathurst 1000, 161 laps each October, for Supercars. Makes: Ford Mustang, Chevrolet Camaro and, new in 2026, Toyota GR Supra. The 2026 grid had 11 Mustangs, 10 Camaros and 5 Supras; a Dick Johnson Racing Mustang won on 11 October. Holden is gone [u].
- Albert Park: 5.278 km, 14 turns, clockwise, public roads round the lake. Formula 1's Australian Grand Prix, the 2026 season opener.
- Great Ocean Road (B100, 240 km, built 1919 to 1932 by returned servicemen as a war memorial): Torquay, Bells Beach, Memorial Arch (log arch on stone bases; the road passes under), Lorne, Apollo Bay, Twelve Apostles, Loch Ard Gorge, London Bridge, Allansford.
- Nullarbor: the signposted "90 Mile Straight", 146.6 km without a bend between Balladonia and Caiguna; stretches of highway are marked as Flying Doctor airstrips.
- Road trains: three or four trailers, up to 53.5 m, yellow "ROAD TRAIN" boards.
- Utes: invented by Ford Australia in 1934; the Ford Ranger was the best-selling new vehicle 2023 to 2025; Deni Ute Muster at Deniliquin since 1999.

**5. Businesses.** Bunnings Warehouse (green shed, red hammer logo, sausage sizzle at the door) [u]; Repco (parts stores; the race's title sponsor); Ampol (red, white and blue servo) [u]; Harry's Café de Wheels (painted pie cart) [u]; Bondi Icebergs Club (white clubhouse over the pool) [u]; Big Banana Fun Park. Parody ideas: "Roo Bar Roadhouse", "Conrod Straight Mufflers", "The Big Hubcap".

**6. Street life.** Green-and-cream ferries [u]; Melbourne trams on 24 routes (white with green and yellow [u]; heritage W-class on the City Circle [u]; new G-class since July 2026); blue-and-white Sydney buses [u]; yellow Melbourne taxis [u]; white police cars with a blue checker band [u]; utes with bullbars, spotlights and UHF aerials; the red-and-white Spirit of Tasmania car ferry at Geelong [u]. Furniture: yellow diamond kangaroo, koala and wombat signs [u]; hook-turn signs; red-and-yellow beach flags [u].

**7. Small scenes.**
1. Campers, couches and flags on top of the Mountain as a Mustang, Camaro and Supra crest Skyline.
2. Sausage sizzle outside the hardware shed, utes lined up, dogs in the trays.
3. A road train overtaking a caravan on the 90 Mile Straight; a small plane on the airstrip markings.
4. Sunset car park at Uluru: campers facing the rock from afar, nobody on it.
5. A car waiting on the left for a hook turn as a tram passes.

**8. Watch out.**
- Uluru: the climb closed for good on 26 October 2019. Anangu ask that sacred rock detail is not reproduced; the north-east face is most sensitive ("wide shots from a distance"), and commercial image use needs a park permit. So: a plain, distant monolith with no path, climbers, surface detail or invented "Dreamtime" decoration. Use Uluru and Kata Tjuta, not Ayers Rock or the Olgas [u].
- Mount Panorama is also Wahluu (Wiradjuri, "to watch over").
- Twelve Apostles: seven left (falls in 2005 and 2009), on Eastern Maar country. Booked visits to the new Keerray Woorroong Visitor Centre precinct began 16 October 2026 (the official page still says the centre opens "late 2026"); free until April 2027, then a fee.
- Bondi: 15 people were killed in the December 2025 attack; a memorial is planned.
- Reef: mass bleaching in 2024 and 2025, none in 2026.
- Flinders Street was repainted in 2017 in stone and red: no longer mustard.
- Trademarks: Opera House [u], Supercars, Bathurst 1000, Repco, Bunnings, Formula 1.

## B. Iceland

**1. Character.** Black and moss-green ground, a white ice cap filling the south-east, white waterfalls dropping off a green escarpment along the south coast, and one thin road circling it all. A small capital of coloured tin-roofed houses [u] sits under one pale church spire. Steam instead of trees: a geyser, a milky-blue pool in black lava. Traffic is tall: big-tyred jeeps and campers, with sheep and horses at the roadside.

**2. Ground.**
- South-west: Reykjavík on a bay; beyond it the Reykjanes lava peninsula, with the Blue Lagoon 5 km from Grindavík.
- Inland: Strokkur, then Gullfoss up the same road [u].
- South coast, eastwards along the Ring Road [u order]: Seljalandsfoss, Skógafoss, Reynisfjara by Vík, black sand plains, Vatnajökull, Jökulsárlón.
- East: steep fjords; Seyðisfjörður at a fjord head, 27 km over a 600 m pass from the Ring Road.
- Interior: uninhabited highland desert, snowbound October to early June.
- Ring Road (Route 1): 1,309 km, fully paved, mostly one lane each way.

**3. Landmarks.**
1. Hallgrímskirkja: 74.5 m pale concrete [u] tower with stepped wings like basalt columns, on a hilltop in the centre; Leif Erikson statue in front. Streets end at its square.
2. Strokkur: a pool throwing a white column 15 to 20 m every 6 to 10 minutes; Geysir is 50 m north. Footpath only.
3. Jökulsárlón: 25 km² lagoon of white and blue icebergs, with seals, under the Vatnajökull ice cap (7,700 km², 8% of the country). The Ring Road crosses its outlet on a single-lane suspension bridge [u]; Diamond Beach is the black sand seaward.
4. Waterfalls: Skógafoss, a rectangular curtain 60 by 25 m [u]; Seljalandsfoss, a thin ribbon with a path behind it [u]; Gullfoss, two steps turning into a canyon [u]. Short spurs off the road.
5. Reynisfjara: black beach, a wall of grey hexagonal columns with a cave at the east end, spiky sea stacks offshore.
6. Harpa: 43 m faceted box of coloured glass on the harbour edge, lit at night; the coast road passes it.
7. Sun Voyager: skeletal stainless-steel boat, 18 m long, beside the coast road east of Harpa [u], pointing north.
8. Blue Lagoon: milky-blue pool in black lava, the Svartsengi power plant steaming behind, ringed by earth barriers.
9. Turf houses: timber gables with grass roofs merging into the hill [u].

**4. Roads and car culture.**
- Ring Road: 26 single-lane bridges remain (about 60 in 2010), mostly on the south-east glacial plains and in the Eastfjords, signed "Einbreið brú" [u]. The 255 m Hornafjarðarfljót bridge was bypassed in June 2026; all are due to be two-lane by 2038.
- F-roads: highland tracks, summer only, four-wheel drive needed on most, unbridged fords [u]. F35 Kjölur, F26 Sprengisandur, F208 to Landmannalaugar, F88 to Askja. Off-road driving is banned.
- Super jeeps: Arctic Trucks (Reykjavík, since 1990) rebuilds Toyota Hilux and Land Cruisers [u] on tyres up to 44 inches [u]. Formula Offroad (torfæra) hill-climbs run in gravel pits [u].
- Ferry: Smyril Line's Norröna, weekly, Hirtshals (Denmark) via Tórshavn to Seyðisfjörður; 800 cars; Iceland's only international car ferry.

**5. Businesses.** Bæjarins Beztu Pylsur (hot-dog kiosk since 1937, across from Harpa; red lettering, cartoon sausage); Bónus (yellow sign, pink pig) [u]; N1 (red fuel stations) [u]; 66°North (outdoor clothing) [u]; Arctic Trucks; Blue Lagoon. Parody ideas: "Pylsur & Púströr" (hot dogs and exhausts), "Jeppa-Jóns Risadekk" (Jeep-Jón's giant tyres), "Einbreið Brú Brugghús" (single-lane bridge brewery).

**6. Street life.** All [u] except the puffin figure. Yellow Strætó city buses; white police cars with blue and yellow blocks; rental Dacia Dusters and white campervans; Icelandic horses in roadside paddocks; sheep loose in summer, usually a ewe and two lambs. Furniture: yellow marker posts lining the road; signs "Blindhæð" (blind crest) and "Malbik endar" (tarmac ends); white-wrapped hay bales; red-roofed white farm churches. Puffins on grassy cliff tops in summer (60% of the world's breed in Iceland). Night: green aurora, autumn to spring.

**7. Small scenes.**
1. Stand-off at the single-lane bridge by the ice lagoon: a camper waits, a super jeep crosses, a seal on a floe below.
2. A ewe and two lambs holding up rental cars and one supercar.
3. A super jeep mid-ford; a small hire car stopped at the "4x4 only" sign.
4. Strokkur on a timer, a ring of figures with cameras up.
5. The Norröna's ramp down at Seyðisfjörður, caravans and expedition trucks rolling off.

**8. Watch out.**
- Reykjanes: nine eruptions on the Sundhnúkur crater row from December 2023 to August 2025 (the last ran 16 July to 5 August 2025). None since, as of early November 2026, but magma under Svartsengi is at a record and the Met Office expects another intrusion, possibly an eruption.
- Grindavík (about 3,800 people) was evacuated in November 2023; three houses burned in January 2024 and a worker was lost in a fissure; some 400 live there now. Lava buried the Blue Lagoon car park in November 2024; it reopened behind barriers and closed briefly in April and July 2025. Whether it is open this week was not confirmed.
- Showing it: a low crater row, cooled black lava and grey earth barriers are the true picture. No lava near houses, burning buildings, fleeing figures or joke signs.
- Reynisfjara: six sneaker-wave deaths since 2007, the latest in August 2025. February 2026 storms stripped the sand and brought down part of the cliff slope (reports differ on whether columns fell). Keep figures back from the water.
- Seyðisfjörður: mudslides in December 2020 destroyed 12 houses.
- Maps older than June 2026 show the old Ring Road line at Hornafjörður.
- Never show tyre tracks off-road.
- Trademarks: Blue Lagoon, Bónus, 66°North, N1, Arctic Trucks, Smyril Line.
