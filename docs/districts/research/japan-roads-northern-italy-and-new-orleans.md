## 1. Japan: car culture and mountains

**From above (u).** A lit expressway on stilts rings a bay of container piers. Inland, a lone snow cone with a circuit on its skirt; behind, a white mountain wall over thatched valleys.

**Ground (u).** Daikoku is a pier island off Yokohama between two cable-stayed bridges. Fuji stands inland south-west, Speedway on its east foot, Hakone south-east. Haruna and Irohazaka lie north of the plain. Alps furthest inland: Matsumoto in the eastern basin, Tateyama crossing to the Japan Sea, Shirakawa-go west, Jigokudani north-east. Suzuka and Liberty Walk belong far west by Nagoya.

**Landmarks**
1. Daikoku PA: car park and low rest house inside the coils of a stacked spiral junction (u); reached only from the expressway, never on foot.
2. Umihotaru: mid-bay island with rooftop viewing deck; the Aqua-Line's tunnel from the west becomes bridge here; long white ship-shaped block (u).
3. Suzuka (u): figure-eight; Ferris wheel and amusement park behind the main grandstand. Lap: downhill start, Turns 1–2, S Curves, Reverse Bank, Dunlop, Degner 1–2, under the bridge, hairpin, 200R, Spoon, back straight over the bridge, 130R, chicane.
4. Fuji Speedway: 1.5 km straight, cone filling the west (u); Fuji Motorsports Museum on site since 2022.
5. Haruna (u): five stacked hairpins on a wooded slope below a crater lake.
6. Irohazaka (u): two one-way zigzags, 28 bends down, 20 up, a kana signboard at each.
7. Snow corridor (u): bus-wide slot between vertical snow walls at 2,450 m; opens about 15 April, walls to about 20 m, walkable until late June.
8. Alps trio (u): Matsumoto's black five-tier keep in a moat; Shirakawa-go's steep thatched A-frames; Jigokudani's steaming macaque pool.

**Roads and car culture.** Daikoku has no timetable; weekend nights are busiest: GT-Rs, Supras, RX-7s, widebody supercars. Nominally open 24 hours (u). When full, the entrance shuts without notice; press reports (2018–2021) describe exit requests from 20:30, two to three hours to clear, toilets usable, EVs admitted to charge. Works closures are announced (2 August 2026, 21:00–06:00, everything shut). Ask before photographing; police check noise and illegal parts. No official 2026 rulebook found. Hakone Turnpike (u): private toll hill road, coast to about 1,000 m. Touge kit (u): concrete gutters inside bends, yellow chevrons, numbered-bend boards, mirrors.

**Businesses.** Liberty Walk: headquarters near Nagoya with café and finished builds, Tokyo showroom for walk-ins; demo cars outside (u). Top Secret (Chiba) and RWB (Kashiwa): working garages, contact first; gold demo cars, sticker-covered tin shed (u). Spoon Type One, Suginami: white unit, blue-yellow trim (u). Mooneyes Area-1, Yokohama: yellow shop and café, public (u). Parodies: Kado no Tōfu-ten ("Corner Tofu"), Habahiro Works ("Wide Works").

**Vehicles (u).** Black-and-white police cars, yellow expressway patrols, white kei trucks, chrome dekotora, indigo taxis, Alpine Route highland buses.

**Scenes.** Daikoku at 20:30, hazards on, patrol car at the ramp. Dawn tofu delivery below the hairpins. Suzuka crossover, one car over, one under.

**Watch out.** Honda Welcome Plaza Aoyama closed 31 March 2025. Daikoku is a working rest stop: parked meet, never racing. Rest (u): Initial D (shop lettering, two-tone hatchback with shop text) and Wangan Midnight are protected; tuner logos are trademarks; the Turnpike's name follows its sponsor, latterly Anest Iwata; Fuji is sacred; Shirakawa-go is lived in.

## 2. Northern Italy

**From above (u).** Flat hazy plain of poplars and red-tiled towns between snow peaks and soft hills. A long lake pokes into the mountains; eastward an island city sits in a lagoon on one causeway.

**Ground (u).** The Po plain runs west to east: Turin at the west end inside the Alps' curve; Monza just north-east of Milan; Como about 40 km north where foothills begin, Villa d'Este on the lake's south-west arm; Sant'Agata on dead-flat farmland near Modena, Maranello about 25 km south-west on the first Apennine slope; Venice at the Adriatic end. Alps are the back wall, Apennines the front.

**Landmarks**
1. Lamborghini, Sant'Agata (u): low white sawtooth sheds behind a two-storey glass museum box and black-glass office cube.
2. Ferrari gate, Maranello (u, colour unsure): long two-storey terracotta block, central arch, yellow script on the roofline.
3. Fiorano (u): private figure-eight test track, about 3 km, white farmhouse infield, beside the factory.
4. Museo Ferrari (u): glass front under a red curved fascia, streets from the gate.
5. Monza: slim circuit in a walled wood; old 4.25 km oval, two steep concrete bankings, bridging the modern track (u). Region budgeted €4 m to restyle the bankings; no start confirmed.
6. Lingotto, Turin: 500 m five-storey white slab, rooftop loop with banked ends, blue glass bubble, steel gallery box (u). Roof is now La Pista 500, Pinacoteca Agnelli's planted open-air art space; 2026 commission: Nathalie Du Pasquier's flags.
7. Villa d'Este (u): pale lakeside palace, terraced garden; concours each May, BMW-backed.
8. Venice (u): Ponte della Libertà, straight road-and-rail causeway, nearly 4 km; Piazzale Roma, where roads end, with 1930s multi-storey Autorimessa Comunale; Rialto, one white stone arch carrying two shop rows.

**Roads and car culture (u).** A4 along the Alps' foot; Via Emilia "Motor Valley"; 1920s Milan–Como Autostrada dei Laghi; Mille Miglia from Brescia; trade-plated test cars near both factories. Checked: Venice day-tripper fee on 60 dates, 3 April to 26 July 2026, 08:30–16:00, €5 or €10.

**Businesses.** Pinacoteca Agnelli (operating 2026). Rest (u): Museo Automobili Lamborghini; Museo Ferrari; Ristorante Cavallino, low farmhouse opposite the gate; Autorimessa Comunale; Hotel Villa d'Este, seasonal. Parodies: Scuderia Tortellino, Autorimessa Acqua Alta.

**Vehicles (u).** Red Ferraris, lime and orange Lamborghinis, pastel Fiat 500s, Ape three-wheelers, dark-blue Carabinieri, light-blue-and-white Polizia; black gondolas, varnished water taxis, vaporetti, yellow ambulance boats.

**Scenes.** Test car leaving the arch past fans. Joggers and planters on the roof track. Cars spiralling into the garage as passengers change to boats.

**Watch out.** Monza: works since 2024, no racing planned November 2026 to July 2027. Rest (u): Parabolica is now Curva Alboreto; Lamborghini's museum was briefly MUDETEC; Ferrari polices horse, script and name hard; bull, 500 and Mille Miglia arrow are marks too; "The Italian Job" roof chase is film property; Venice: overtourism and flooding are sore points.

## 3. New Orleans

**From above (u).** Low, flat, tree-heavy city inside a tight brown river bend, the river riding higher than the streets behind levees. Small balconied grid at the bend, a pale dome behind, a vast lake north crossed by two straight bridges.

**Ground (u).** The river turns hard at Algiers Point; the French Quarter holds the high natural levee facing it; ground then falls into a bowl. Canal Street runs from river to cemeteries, Dome upriver of it; St. Charles Avenue follows the bend through the Garden District. Lake Pontchartrain bounds the north, Causeway leaving from the western suburbs; Crescent City Connection just upriver of downtown. Open sea about 100 miles downriver.

**Landmarks (u)**
1. Jackson Square: white cathedral with three slate spires, twin arcaded buildings, fenced garden with equestrian statue, red-brick gallery rows either side.
2. Quarter block: pastel stucco townhouses, cast-iron lace galleries on thin posts, courtyards; neon on Bourbon Street.
3. Natchez: white three-deck sternwheeler, red paddlewheel, twin black stacks, at the Quarter wharf.
4. Crescent City Connection: twin grey steel cantilever trusses.
5. Superdome: squat bronze drum, concave sides, shallow white dome.
6. Causeway: two parallel low trestles, about 24 miles, one drawbridge hump.
7. Cemetery: walled block of whitewashed house-tombs in tight rows; one at the Quarter's edge, more at Canal Street's end.
8. Levee: grass bank, floodwall gates, promenade, rail tracks.

**Roads and car culture.** Streetcars run in the grassed median, the "neutral ground"; residents park there when flooding rain is forecast (u). Floats are towed by farm tractors (u). Bourbon Street: bollards from Canal to St. Ann; permanent swing gates proposed December 2025, outcome not found. NOLA Motorsports Park, Avondale: road course and kart track, active in 2026.

**Businesses (u).** Café du Monde: open pavilion, green-and-white striped awning. Preservation Hall: weathered bare-wood shuttered front. Commander's Palace: turquoise-and-white Victorian, striped awnings. Mardi Gras World: riverside float warehouse. Plus NOLA Motorsports Park (checked). Parodies: Laissez les Bonnes Roues Rouler Garage, Lagniappe Lube.

**Vehicles.** St. Charles line (route 12): 1920s olive-green cars, Canal Street to Carrollton (u). Canal (47, 48) and Riverfront (49): red cars (u). St. Charles had summer 2026 track works, through service restored 4 August; Riverfront briefly suspended March 2026. Also (u): mule carriages, towboats pushing barge rafts, freighters above street level.

**Scenes.** Green streetcar under oaks, beads in the branches, cars perched on the median before a storm. Tractor-drawn float past ladder seats. Sunday second line: brass band, sashed club members, parasols. Natchez passing a freighter.

**Watch out.** Bourbon Street: vehicle attack on 1 January 2025; keep vehicles out of crowds. Rest (u): Katrina (2005), so no flooded streets, rooftop rescues or Dome-as-shelter. Cemeteries are active burial grounds, St. Louis No. 1 by guided tour only: generic tombs, no real names, no voodoo or ghost gags. Mardi Gras Indian suits are sacred, authored works. Lee Circle became Harmony Circle; the Dome's name is a sponsorship, Caesars since 2021. Marks: Saints fleur-de-lis, krewe names, "A Streetcar Named Desire".
