## 1. Character
Westeros reads as grey stone, dark timber and snow. A massive ice wall sits in the north, and castles with square keeps and round towers stand along a medieval road network. Colour comes mostly from house banners (red lion, grey wolf, black dragon on red, black stag) and from the red of King's Landing against the grey north. Silhouettes to lean on: the ice wall, a cluster of towers on a hill, a domed pit, and winged dragons. The look is feudal and wintry, not fairytale.

## 2. Ground
- **The Wall:** books and show agree on about 700 ft tall (up to 900 ft at foundation blocks near Greyguard) and 300 miles (100 leagues) long. It is made of ice, with some stone and rubble and a wooden stair. North of it are the Haunted Forest and free-folk land. South of it are the Gift (farmland), then the Kingsroad.
- **The Kingsroad:** runs south from Castle Black through Winterfell and the neck to the Trident and the Crossroads, then on to King's Landing. It has tolls, inns, hamlets and mud.
- **The North:** pine forest, snowfields, low hills.
- **King's Landing:** three hills, Aegon's High (Red Keep), Visenya's (Great Sept) and Rhaenys's (Dragonpit). It sits on the Blackwater Rush and Blackwater Bay, with seven gates and the Flea Bottom slum in the lowest ground.
- **Winterfell:** flat ground within walls over 100 ft high. It has a godswood and hot springs, with Winter Town outside.

## 3. Landmarks (most iconic first)
1. **The Wall:** a long grey-white ice cliff with a jagged crown, bluish near the top. It sits at the north edge of the map, with white ground below. The Kingsroad passes through its tunnel.
2. **Castle Black:** a low cluster of dark timber halls, a keep and towers on the south face. The iron-barred gate tunnel runs through the ice. A winch cage (iron cage on a rope, hauled by a winch) and a wooden switchback stair climb the face. Book detail: Jon has the tunnel blocked with rubble. Show detail: a big lift. The Kingsroad runs through.
3. **King's Landing and the Red Keep:** a red stone fortress with many towers (Maegor's Holdfast, Tower of the Hand, White Sword Tower), crowning Aegon's High Hill above the Blackwater Bay harbour. The Kingsroad enters the city at gates below it.
4. **Great Sept of Baelor:** white marble with seven crystal towers around a central dome, on Visenya's Hill, with a plaza before it. In the show it is destroyed by wildfire.
5. **The Dragonpit:** a huge domed circular ruin on Rhaenys's Hill. Books: collapsed dome. Show: open bowl. A street runs beside it.
6. **Winterfell:** grey stone Great Keep, Broken Tower, high walls with square towers, a godswood and a glass garden. Winter Town clusters outside. The Kingsroad meets its gate.
7. **The Iron Throne:** a jagged seat of melted swords in the Red Keep's throne room, a black spiky-metal silhouette. In the books it is much larger than in the show. It is indoors, so it needs an open roof or window to be seen from above.
8. **Harrenhal:** a ruined, fire-scarred castle of five huge melted towers by the Gods Eye lake, near the Kingsroad.
9. **Dragonstone:** a black volcanic island castle with dragon-shaped carvings (strongest in the show), at the sea edge.
10. **The Crossroads and the Inn at the Crossroads:** a timber inn, a stable and a signpost where the Kingsroad meets other roads.

## 4. The three dragons
- **Drogon:** black with red markings. Largest and wildest in the books; Dany escapes Daznak's Pit on his back. Show: the one Dany rides, who burns the Red Keep and later carries her away.
- **Rhaegal:** green with bronze. Medium-sized and the most placid. In the books chained in Meereen's pyramid. Show: killed by Euron's ballistas.
- **Viserion:** cream and gold. The smallest, also chained in the books. Show: killed by the Night King beyond the Wall, then flies as a blue-eyed ice dragon that breaches the Wall at Eastwatch with blue fire.
- **Flight:** bat-winged, long necks and tails, fire from the mouth. A toy-model shape is a wide wing silhouette with a spiked tail. Dragons are wilder and less tame in the books than in the show.

## 5. In-world businesses
- **Inn at the Crossroads:** a busy timber inn, where Hot Pie bakes in the show.
- **Tobho Mott's smithy:** Street of Steel, King's Landing, a forge and armourer.
- **Street of Flour bakeries and the Flea Bottom bowl of brown:** cheap stew sold from stalls.
- **Chataya's House:** Street of Silk, a pleasure house.
- **Mole's Town:** a hamlet of taverns near Castle Black.
- **Castle Black armoury and kitchens:** a plain smithy and mess hall.
- **Winter Town inns and smithies:** the market below Winterfell.
- **The Twins:** the Freys' tolled bridge across the Green Fork.
- **Pycelle's chambers:** a maester's apothecary and scribe.
- **Blackwater Bay port stalls:** fish and cargo.

Invented names in the in-world style: the Frozen Flagon, the Wall-Watcher's Rest, the Dragon's Dropping Inn, Brannock's Barrel, the Salted Hound.

## 6. Street life
Horses, ox-carts, merchant ships and war galleys. House banners: Stark (grey wolf on white), Lannister (gold lion on crimson), Targaryen (red three-headed dragon on black), Baratheon (black stag on gold), Night's Watch (black cloaks). The people are knights, smallfolk, septons, maesters, Watch brothers, wildlings and merchants.

## 8. Rights and care
George R. R. Martin owns the books and names (Bantam/Penguin Random House); HBO/Warner Bros. owns the show's visual designs, logos and title treatments. A private homage should avoid copying the logo, the show's title typography and title-sequence design, specific costume and set designs, and actors' likenesses. Prefer the books' own descriptions for the dragons and the Wall. Keep it non-commercial and private.
