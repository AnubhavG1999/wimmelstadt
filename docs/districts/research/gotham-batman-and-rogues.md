# Batman and his rogues for the tile

This note is for the figures of a Gotham tile: Batman, his allies and his rogues gallery, with their looks, their haunts and their small stories. Gotham as a place, its districts, streets, bridges and buildings, is in [gotham-the-city.md](gotham-the-city.md), written beside this note. A place appears here only where it belongs to a character, such as the Batcave, the police roof, Ace Chemicals or the Iceberg Lounge. This note says what each haunt needs, and the city note says where it stands. Read on 2026-11-20.

**What the collector asked for, on 2026-11-20:**

- Batman and his rogues, each recognisable, each with a haunt on the tile;
- small stories for them, 20 to 50 seconds each, several at a time, as the dragons have on Blackwater Bay;
- the characters as the DC comics draw them, in their best-known and lasting looks.

So the note is built round that:

1. Batman: his look, his vehicles, the Bat-Signal, how he moves, and his places;
2. the allies worth a cameo;
3. the rogues, in three tiers;
4. their stories;
5. how several play at once;
6. what to leave out or soften;
7. questions for the collector.

**Named characters.** [building-a-tile.md](../../building-a-tile.md) says that a place from a book "portrays no character", and the Westeros and Blackwater Bay tiles keep to that. Like the Red Rising tile, this one portrays named characters because the collector asked for them. That belongs in the tile's `NOTES.md` as its first liberty.

**The source rule.** The figures are drawn from the comics alone, in their lasting looks. The tile takes nothing from:

- the films: the serials of 1943 and 1949, the 1966 film, and the films from 1989 to 2022;
- the 1966 television series;
- Batman: The Animated Series (1992) and the animated series after it;
- the Arkham games and other games;
- the television series Gotham.

Some iconic elements began in one of these and were then taken into the comics: the Batcave, Alfred's moustache, the grapple gun, Harley Quinn, Mr. Freeze's domed helmet. The note says so wherever it happens and judges each case on its own. Section 6 lists them.

## How sure each statement is

- **[wp Article]**: from that article of the English Wikipedia, read in full today. Wikipedia names the comic where shown, and the comics themselves were not opened. **Unless a second source is named, the statement rests on that one article.**
- **[s]**: a web search's summary of pages I could not read. Weak. Where those pages are fan wikis, the tag says so.
- **[mem]**: my own memory of the comics, not confirmed today. Check it before building.
- **[tile]**: this note's proposal. It is not in the comics.
- **[film]**, **[TV]**, **[anim]**, **[game]**: from a film, the 1966 television series, the animated series, or a game. Not to be used.

**Caveat on sources, read first.**

- Every Wikipedia article below was read as its full plain text through Wikipedia's own interface (`api.php`). They are secondary sources: they retell the comics and cite issues, but no comic was opened today.
- No fan wiki page was read. Three facts rest only on search summaries of fan wikis and collectors' pages: the first issue of the Ace Chemical plant, and the first issues of the Batboat and of the Batcycle.
- Wikipedia describes costumes thinly. Many details of the looks below are from my memory and are marked [mem]: Two-Face's split suit, Poison Ivy's red hair, Nightwing's costume, Gordon's glasses and moustache, and Bane's, Man-Bat's, the Mad Hatter's and Ra's al Ghul's looks.

Comics abbreviations: **Det.** Detective Comics, **B** Batman, **WF** World's Finest Comics, **B&B** The Brave and the Bold. "Det. 27 (1939)" means Detective Comics #27, of 1939.

## 1. Batman

### His look: the grey suit with the yellow oval

**What every version shares** [wp Batman; wp Batsuit]:

- a cape with a scalloped hem, and a cowl with two pointed ears. Wikipedia calls them "the two most instantly recognizable parts of Batman's silhouette" [wp Batsuit];
- a cowl over most of the face, whose eyes are drawn white, without pupils, as Bill Finger asked from the start [wp Batsuit];
- a bat emblem on the chest;
- a utility belt, "almost always yellow". Until 1989 most artists drew it as a plain belt with a buckle and capsules [wp Batsuit; wp Batman's utility belt];
- gloves with three fins on long cuffs. The first gloves were short, plain and purple, and the fins came in 1940 [wp Batsuit].

**What changes by era** [wp Batsuit]:

| Years | Body | Cape, cowl, gloves, trunks, boots | Chest |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1939 to 1964 | Grey. Finger made the suit grey and gave Batman a cape instead of wings. | Black, printed with blue highlights, so they came to look dark blue | A plain black bat |
| 1964 to 2000 | Grey | Blue-black | A black bat on a yellow oval, from the "New Look" of 1964: thirty-six years |
| The 1990s, after "Troika" | Matte black, "very similar to the suits worn in the film Batman (1989)" | Black | |
| 2000 to 2021 | Grey [mem] | Black or blue-black | A larger black bat, after the Golden Age's, with no oval |
| 2011, the New 52 | Armour plates over a bodysuit. No trunks [mem]. | A "para-cape" for gliding | |
| 2016, Rebirth | | The cape lined with purple | The bat outlined in gold |
| 2021 on | | | The yellow oval again |

**Recommendation: the suit of 1964 to 2000.** A grey body, with cape, cowl, gloves, trunks and boots in a deep blue-black, the black bat on a yellow oval, and the yellow belt with capsules. Why:

1. **It is the comics' own.** Every film suit since 1989 is black armour [mem], and the comics' black suit of the 1990s looked very like the 1989 film's [wp Batsuit]. Grey and blue reads as the comics.
2. **It lasted.** It ran for thirty-six years without a break, and has been back since 2021 [wp Batsuit].
3. **It reads at person scale.** It is four colour blocks (grey, blue-black, the yellow oval and the yellow belt), where the black and grey of 2000 to 2021 is two.
4. **Blue-black is faithful.** The comics' black was printed with blue highlights [wp Batsuit]. It also keeps the cape readable against a night sky [tile].

The details are the tile's [tile]:

- ears of middling length, since their length varies with the artist [wp Batsuit];
- a cape to mid-calf, with five scallops;
- white eyes, which at night can glow faintly as two points;
- trunks.

### His vehicles

**The Batmobile** [wp Batmobile unless named]:

- **1939.** In his first story, Det. 27 (1939), Batman drives a red car that has no name.
- **1941.** The name first appears in Det. 48 (February 1941).
- **B 5 (March 1941)** brings "a long, powerful, streamlined Batmobile with a tall scalloped fin and an intimidating bat head on the front". Three pages later the Joker forces it off a cliff.
- **The settled form.** The designs "settled on a large, dark-colored body and bat-like accessories such as large tailfins scalloped to resemble a bat's wings". Wikipedia's article on Batman gives "an imposing black car, often with tailfins that suggest a bat's wings" [wp Batman].
- **1966 to 1968.** The television car, a Lincoln Futura rebuilt by George Barris, was carried into the comics [TV]. Left out.
- **After 1968.** A plainer car, whose only decoration of note is a bat's head painted on the bonnet.
- **1985.** The Super Powers toy line's Batmobile was drawn into the comics, with front and rear canopies, "Coke-bottle" sides and fins built into the body.
- **The Dark Knight Returns** turns it into a tank on treads. That book stands outside the main comics.
- **Since the 1990s.** Many designs; a flying Batmobile in 2009; a redesign in 2011; a rectangular armoured car after 2016.

**Recommendation: the Batmobile of B 5 (1941).** A long, low car in dark blue-black, with a bat's head on its nose and one tall scalloped fin rising from its tail. Why:

- It is the comics' own, unlike the cars of the films and of the 1966 series.
- It keeps coming back in the comics. The Dark Knight Strikes Again draws it again, and Red Rain has an open car with a single bat-wing at its rear [wp Batmobile].
- At toy size the fin and the bat's head say Batmobile from every side [tile].

A bubble canopy over the cockpit is from my memory of the Batmobile of 1950 [mem]. Toy size: about 2 units long, the longest car on the tile [tile].

**The Batmobile is the tile's own show.** Like the dragons, it is neither a hero nor traffic. A hero stands for a diecast the collector owns ([CONTEXT.md](../../../CONTEXT.md)). If the collector ever buys a diecast Batmobile, that is a hero and a separate question.

**The Batplane, which the films call the Batwing** [wp Batplane]:

- **The Batgyro**, in Det. 31 (1939): an autogyro with a bat motif at the front. Batman could "park" it in the air, where it hovered and waited for him.
- **The Batplane**, from B 1 (1940): a fixed-wing plane with its propeller at the front and a bat motif on its nose cone. At first it carried a machine gun, which the tile leaves out.
- **A jet** from B 61.
- **The name.** "Batwing" came from the 1989 film and was carried into the comics. That film's plane, shaped like the bat emblem [mem], was designed by Anton Furst and Julian Caldow [wp Batplane; film]. Left out.

**Recommendation: the Golden Age Batplane.** A dark single-engined propeller plane with a bat's head on its nose [wp Batplane] and wings scalloped like a bat's [mem]. Rarely, the Batgyro can appear instead, hovering "parked" over a roof while Batman climbs down a line [tile, after wp Batplane].

**The Batboat.**

- It first appeared under that name in Det. 110 (April 1946), as a gift from Scotland Yard. An unnamed boat appeared earlier, in B 5 (1941) [s, from fan-wiki and collectors' pages].
- Look [tile]: a dark speedboat with a scalloped fin at its stern like the Batmobile's, so that the family reads as one.

**The Batcycle.**

- One weak source gives Det. 233 (July 1956) [s, one page of Everybody Wiki]. From memory, that issue is also Batwoman's first, and she rode a motorcycle, so the source may be counting hers [mem].
- Wikipedia names the Batcycle only among the bat-vehicles that "soon followed" the Batmobile [wp Batmobile].
- Look [tile]: a black motorcycle with a bat's-head fairing and two small fins. Batgirl can ride the same machine in her own colours.

All of them live in the Batcave, which holds "the Batmobile in all its incarnations", motorcycles, the Batplane and watercraft, with "separate exits for each type" [wp Batcave].

### The Bat-Signal

- **What it is.** A searchlight with a bat emblem fixed over its lens, which throws a bat onto the clouds at night. It first appeared in Det. 60 (February 1942) [wp Bat-Signal; wp Batman].
- **Where.** On the roof of police headquarters [wp Commissioner Gordon; wp Bat-Signal].
- **Who.** The police use it to call Batman, and to frighten criminals [wp Bat-Signal].
- **Its misuses**, each a story [wp Bat-Signal]:
  - the Scarecrow turned it into a jack-o'-lantern with an orange bulb and painted eyes (Batman: Haunted Knight, 1996);
  - crime bosses shone it upside down to ask for Batman's help (Legends of the Dark Knight 6, 1989);
  - the Riddler used it to call Batman (Batman: Dark Victory, 1999);
  - the villain Signalman trapped Batman inside it (Det. 466, 1976).
- **The Joker's own.** In "The Joker Follows Suit" (1942), the Joker built a Joker-signal to call his gang, a Jokergyro, and a Jokermobile with a large Joker face on its bonnet [wp Joker].

For the tile [tile]:

- the searchlight on the police roof, with its beam a pale cone;
- a low cloud that drifts over the roof at dusk to catch the bat, which sits in a pale disc on it;
- the cloud is a stage of its own, used by one signal at a time (section 5).

### How he moves

- **Over rooftops.** The comics have him "easily running across rooftops in a Parkour-esque fashion" [wp Batman].
- **On a line.** A handheld grapple shoots a claw on a cord that pulls him up [wp Batman's utility belt]:
  - until 1991 the comics' Batman threw his line, a rope with a batarang or hook, by hand;
  - a rifle-like grapple gun first appears in The Dark Knight Returns 1;
  - the handheld grapple gun first appeared in the 1989 film;
  - it came into the comics with Norm Breyfogle in B 458 (January 1991) and slowly replaced the rope.

  **On the tile:** a line flies up to a cornice, and Batman swings in an arc beneath it. Whether it is thrown or fired hardly shows at person scale. What shows is the arc and the thin, taut line [tile].
- **The cape.**
  - "Originally, Batman did not use the cape to glide." A gliding "para-cape" came only when Dick Grayson and Damian Wayne made one for their own costumes, after Grayson became Batman, and again with the New 52 suit of 2011 [wp Batsuit].
  - The cape that turns rigid and flies is Batman Begins' "memory cloth" [film; wp Batsuit].
  - Long glides that start with a boost from the grapple are the Arkham games' [game; wp Batman's utility belt].

  **On the tile:** the cape spreads like wings in every drop, as the comics draw it [mem], and a drop may end in a short glide to the next roof. No long glides across the tile.
- **Gargoyles.** Batman crouched on a gargoyle over the city is one of the comics' commonest pictures [mem: no source read today names it]. Gotham's gargoyles belong to the city note. One of them, high up and facing the police roof, is Batman's home on the tile [tile].
- **Batarangs.** They have been in the comics since Det. 31 (1939) [wp Batman's utility belt]. On the tile a batarang only ever knocks a prop away: an umbrella, a coin, a canvas or a bulb [tile].
- **Bats.** In Batman: Year One (1987), a sonic device in his boot heel calls live bats to cover him [wp Batsuit]. A cloud of bats makes a good exit [tile].

### At person scale

At 3.2 m to a unit a person is about 0.55 tall, which is small in the opening view [tile, by arithmetic]. Batman, at about 0.6, reads by [tile]:

- his silhouette: the two ears and the cape;
- his colours: the yellow oval and the yellow belt on grey and blue-black;
- his eyes: two faint white points at night;
- his habit: crouched on his gargoyle when he is at home.

**The comics already love giant props.** The Batcave keeps a giant penny, a full-size animatronic Tyrannosaurus rex and an oversized Joker playing card as trophies of stories from 1946 to 1950 [wp Batcave]. So the tile can keep the figures at person scale and draw their props and effects large:

- Two-Face's coin a hand across;
- the Penguin's umbrella half a unit wide;
- the Riddler's question marks a third of a unit tall;
- the Joker's cards as big as a door.

### His places

**Wayne Manor and the Batcave.** The cave lies under the manor [wp Batman; wp Batcave].

- **Its origin.** It was created for the 1943 film serial, and appeared in the comics from Det. 83 (January 1944) [wp Batcave]. It has been the comics' own ever since.
- **Its doors.** The way in from the house is a secret door, "often behind a grandfather clock". "Another secret entrance, covered by a waterfall, pond, hologram, or a camouflaged door, allows access to a service road for the Batmobile" [wp Batcave]. Which comics drew the waterfall was not found, and Batman Begins has one [mem]. Use the camouflaged door in a hillside, which needs no check [tile].
- **The trophies** [wp Batcave]:
  - the T. rex, from B 35 (1946);
  - the penny, from WF 30 (1947), later said to be from a case of Two-Face's;
  - the giant Joker card;
  - the rest come from "The Thousand and One Trophies of Batman!", Det. 158 (1950).
- **The rest of it** [wp Batcave]: a large bat colony, the computer, and a hangar, garage and dock.
- **For the tile** [tile]: a cutaway in the hillside under the manor, open to the view, as the glossary means the word ([CONTEXT.md](../../../CONTEXT.md)). In it:
  - the Batmobile in its bay, the Batplane at a hangar door and the Batboat at a water gate onto the river;
  - the T. rex, the penny and the card, placed where they read from afar;
  - bats under the roof.
- **Leave out** the glass case that holds the costume of Jason Todd, a Robin killed by the Joker [wp Batcave; wp Robin] (section 6).

**The police roof**, with the Bat-Signal (above). **The gargoyle** (above).

## 3. The rogues gallery

**How the tiers were judged:**

- whether a stranger would know the figure at person scale;
- whether the look was born in the comics;
- whether the gimmick can be shown cheerfully;
- whether the figure has a haunt in Gotham;
- whether there is a comics story for them that small figures can play.

### Must have

**The Joker.** First in B 1 (1940) [wp Joker].

- **Look.**
  - "A purple suit with a long-tailed, padded-shoulder jacket, a string tie, gloves, striped pants and spats on pointed-toe shoes (sometimes with a wide-brimmed hat)" [wp Joker].
  - Chalk-white skin, green hair, red lips and a fixed grin [wp].
  - Since Neal Adams (1973), taller and leaner, with a long jaw [wp].
  - A green or orange shirt [mem].
  - Props: a flower in his lapel, playing cards, a joy buzzer, jack-in-the-boxes [wp].
- **Haunt: Ace Chemicals.**
  - A chemical plant in Gotham "where the Joker supposedly originated" [wp List of DC Universe locations].
  - In Det. 168 (1951) the Red Hood leaps into a vat of chemicals to escape Batman [wp Joker]. The plant there is the Ace Chemical Processing Plant, next door to the Monarch Playing Card company that he robbed [s, from fan-wiki and review pages].
  - The 1989 film renamed it Axis Chemicals [film; wp List of DC Universe locations]. Use Ace.
  - **For the tile:** tanks, pipes and catwalks over a vat that glows green, behind a gate, with the playing card company's sign next door [tile].
- **His car: the Jokermobile** [wp Jokermobile]:
  - first in B 37 (1946), white "to resemble the Joker's skin";
  - in B 52 (1949), a race car with the Joker's face on its front;
  - it could lay "a plank path to go through chasms";
  - in B&B 191 (1982), a giant boxing glove springs from its boot.

  Its machine guns are left out.
- **Gimmick:** crimes as jokes, with playing cards, gag props, and his own face put on everything, fish included [wp Joker].
- **At person scale:** purple with green, which no other figure wears together; the hat; the white face; the white Jokermobile with its face; and a playing card as big as a door left behind him [tile].

**The Penguin.** First in Det. 58 (December 1941) [wp Penguin].

- **Look.** "A short, fat man with a long nose who wears a monocle, top hat, and morning suit while carrying his signature umbrella", often in white tie and tails [wp Penguin]. Spats [mem]. His cigarette holder [mem] is left out.
- **Haunt: the Iceberg Lounge.**
  - His nightclub, restaurant and casino, a front for his crimes, where Batman sometimes goes for news of the underworld [wp Penguin].
  - It appears in the comics' stories after 1986. The year it first appeared was not found.
  - He grew up in his parents' bird shop [wp].
  - **For the tile:** a club with an iceberg over its door and a roof terrace he can land on by umbrella [tile].
- **Gimmick:** birds and umbrellas [wp Penguin]:
  - an umbrella whose canopy turns into spinning blades, "used as a miniature helicopter" when he needs to escape;
  - birds that he calls to "attack and confuse" his enemies;
  - cars and other vehicles with "an avian theme".
- **At person scale:** the shortest and roundest figure, in black and white, under a top hat, with an umbrella twice as wide as he is, spinning over his head when he flies [tile].
- **From the films, left out:** the Penguin's flippers, his duck-shaped boat and his army of penguins, all from Batman Returns [film; mem].

**Catwoman.** First in B 1 (1940) as "the Cat", a jewel thief [wp Catwoman].

- **Her looks, as the comics have drawn them** [wp Catwoman]:
  - in her first story, no costume;
  - a cat mask (B 3);
  - a dress with an eared hood, then a catsuit;
  - green in the 1960s and mostly purple in the 1990s, then black.

  Since Ed Brubaker and Darwyn Cooke's revamp of 2001 she wears a black bodysuit after Emma Peel's in The Avengers, a cowl with ears, "domino-shaped infrared goggles" and claws. Her weapon is a bullwhip.

  **Use Cooke's look.** The comics have kept a black bodysuit since 2001 [wp]. Her black stitched suit is Batman Returns' [film], and the comics' purple suit of the 1990s was the most revealing [mem].
- **Haunt: the East End**, whose people she protects in the 2001 series while she is still a cat burglar [wp]. On the tile, its rooftops [tile].
- **Gimmick:** a cat burglar: jewels, rooftops and an escape [wp].
- **At person scale:** black like Batman, but with no cape. She has two ears, round goggles that catch the light, a whip-line between the roofs, and a cat at her heels [tile].

**The Riddler.** Det. 140 (October 1948), by Bill Finger and Dick Sprang [wp Riddler].

- **Look.** A domino mask, and "either a green unitard decorated with question mark prints or a green suit and bowler hat". The question mark is his sign, in black, green or purple [wp]. A cane with a question-mark handle [mem].

  **Use the suit and bowler,** with one large question mark on the back and the cane [tile]. The small question marks of the unitard blur at person scale. The bowler gives him a silhouette.
- **Haunt: the pier of his first story,** where he built a glass maze [wp]. He had worked at a carnival, cheating customers with puzzles [wp]. **For the tile:** a puzzle booth on the pier under a big question-mark sign [tile].
- **Gimmick:** he cannot help leaving riddles as clues to his own crimes [wp].
- **At person scale:** bright green, the bowler and the cane, and the trail of question marks he leaves behind him [tile].

**Two-Face.** Det. 66 (August 1942) [wp Two-Face].

- **Look.**
  - Harvey Dent, once Gotham's district attorney. Half his face was scarred when a mob boss threw acid at him in court. In The Long Halloween it is the left half [wp].
  - His suit is split down the middle, one half sober and the other loud [mem].
  - His coin is the mob boss's "lucky two-headed coin", which Dent scarred on one side [wp].
- **Haunt: the courthouse** where he was scarred [wp]. His hideouts and henchmen go in twos [wp]. **For the tile:** the courthouse steps, and a bank [tile].
- **Gimmick:** the coin decides. If it lands on the scarred side, he commits a crime; on the clean side, a good deed. In his first story he robbed a bank and then gave the loot to charity [wp].
- **At person scale:** the only figure whose colours split down the middle, left from right, with a coin a hand across that glints when he flips it [tile]. The scarred half is a flat pale green, with no wound drawn [tile].

**Poison Ivy.** B 181 (June 1966) [wp Poison Ivy].

- **Look.** "Barefoot with long flowing hair, plant vines extending over her limbs, and a green one-piece suit adorned with leaves", sometimes with green skin [wp]. Red hair [mem].
- **Haunt: Robinson Park.** In No Man's Land (1999) she turned it into "a tropical paradise", about thirty blocks of it [wp]. Later stories put her at the Gotham Botanical Gardens [wp].
- **Gimmick:** she commands plants, and takes nature's side against people [wp].
- **At person scale:** green leaves and red hair. Her real signature is the vines and flowers that follow her, which can be any size [tile].

**Mr. Freeze.** First in B 121 (February 1959), as Mr. Zero [wp Mr. Freeze].

- **Look.** His experimental ice gun backfired and spilled freezing chemicals on him, so he must live below freezing in a sealed cold suit. He carries a freezing gun [wp].
  - The domed glass helmet and goggles seem to come from the 1966 television series. The comics took them over, and the name Mr. Freeze came with them in Det. 373 (1968) [s; the sources differ].
  - The look of Mike Mignola's redesign for the animated series (1992), with red goggles [mem], is [anim]. The comics adopted it later [wp].

  **Use:** a pale, bald man under a clear dome, on a bulky suit of white and ice blue, with a freezing gun. That is the comics' look since the late 1960s, without Mignola's particular design [tile].
- **Haunt.** In his first story he kept "his secret cold hideout, near the mountains" [wp]. **For the tile:** a cold store or ice works on the docks, frosted over, with refrigeration plant on its roof [tile].
- **Gimmick:** everything ice: an ice gun, a large ice cannon, and thefts of gems and from banks [wp].
- **At person scale:** the glass dome catching the light, a white trail of ice wherever he fires, and frost round his feet [tile].
- **Left out:** the story of his dying wife Nora was written for the animated series (1992), and the comics took it in afterwards [wp; anim]. It is sad, and it was not the comics' own.

### Should have

**Harley Quinn.**

- **Origin.** She was made for the animated series (1992) by Paul Dini and Bruce Timm. She came into comics in The Batman Adventures 12 (September 1993), a tie-in to the series, and into the main DC comics with Batman: Harley Quinn (October 1999) [wp Harley Quinn].
- **Her looks** [wp Harley Quinn]:
  - Timm's design: a jester's hat, ruffled collar, diamond pattern and domino mask on a red-and-black bodysuit. The comics drew it from 1999 to 2011.
  - The New 52 look of 2011 (red and blue, a corset, and hair half red and half blue) was taken from the Arkham games [game].
  - The jacket and dyed hair of 2016 came from that year's film Suicide Squad [film].
  - Amanda Conner's roller-derby costume of 2013 brought the red and black back.

  White face [mem]. **Her best-known look is the animated series' design.** That is why she is not a must-have: the comics wore it for twelve years, but did not make it. It is the collector's call (section 7).
- **Haunt.** No Gotham haunt of her own was found. Her own series put her on Coney Island [wp]. Since the animated series, and in the comics, she has been a close friend of Poison Ivy's [wp]. So her haunt is Ivy's park, or a funfair booth [tile].
- **Gimmick:** an acrobat with clown's gags: an oversized mallet, popguns, a gun that shoots a boxing glove, and two pet hyenas, Bud and Lou [wp]. That the hyenas began in the animated series is from memory [mem].
- **At person scale:** the two-pointed jester's hat in red and black, cartwheels, and a mallet as big as she is [tile].
- **Care:** the comics show her love for the Joker as an abusive relationship [wp Joker; wp Harley Quinn]. She is never shown with him. She plays with Ivy instead.

**The Scarecrow.** WF 3 (September 1941). His fear toxin came in B 189 (1967), drawn as a bag of dust [wp Scarecrow].

- **Look.** A lanky man, modelled on Ichabod Crane, in a scarecrow's clothes with a stitched burlap mask [wp]. A floppy hat, with straw at his cuffs [mem]. Some versions add a noose round his neck [mem], and he has carried a scythe [wp]. Leave both out.

- **Gimmick:** fear. His dust makes people see what they fear [wp]. On the tile it is a harmless puff of orange dust, and a flock of crows [tile; the crows are mem].

**Bane.** Batman: Vengeance of Bane 1 (January 1993) [wp Bane].

- **Look.** A giant in a luchador's mask. Graham Nolan designed it after Mexican wrestlers [wp]. Tubes run into his head to feed him a drug, Venom [wp]. A black mask, bare arms and dark tights [mem]. The mask in the film The Dark Knight Rises is [film].
- **Haunt.** He grew up in Peña Duro prison on the island of Santa Prisca, and later served time in Gotham's Blackgate prison [wp]. **For the tile:** the docks, beside a freighter [tile]. Blackgate is the city note's.
- **Gimmick:** strength with a plan. In Knightfall he destroyed the walls of Arkham, let the inmates out, and wore Batman down while he caught them again [wp].

**Killer Croc.** Det. 523 (February 1983) [wp Killer Croc].

- **Look.** At first "a powerfully built man covered entirely in green scales", human in the face. More reptilian in later comics [wp]. Ragged trousers [mem].
- **Haunt: the sewers** [wp]. **For the tile:** a manhole, and the mouth of a storm drain on the river [tile].
- **Gimmick:** a former sideshow wrestler, strong and fast in the water [wp].
- **At person scale:** green bulk rising out of a manhole, and a splash [tile].

**Man-Bat.** Det. 400 (June 1970), by Frank Robbins and Neal Adams [wp Man-Bat].

- **Look.** A creature half man, half bat [wp]. Brown fur, leathery wings and bat's ears [mem].
- **Haunt.** None given. Kirk Langstrom is a zoologist who studies bats [wp]. **For the tile:** a belfry or clock tower, with bats [tile]. That he worked at a Gotham museum of natural history is from memory [mem].
- **Gimmick:** a scientist turned into a bat by his own serum, whom Batman turns back. Some stories put him on the good side [wp].
- **At person scale:** the only large winged figure in the sky, brown, with wings three times a person's height [tile].

## 4. Stories

Each story plays for 20 to 50 seconds, from a known comics scene or a classic set-up, in moves that small figures and vehicles can make. Nobody is hurt. A chase ends in an escape or a net. A netted rogue is lifted into a police van and driven to Arkham's gate, and story 14 lets them all out again. Where the comics' scene had harm in it, the "In the comics" line says so. These are the tile's version of the dragons' moments [tile].

1. **The Signal** (Det. 60, 1942; the police roof) [wp Bat-Signal; wp Commissioner Gordon].
   - **Who:** Gordon and Batman.
   - **Where:** the police roof, the cloud above it, and Batman's gargoyle.
   - **What:**
     - At dusk Gordon steps out of the roof door, crosses to the searchlight and throws its switch.
     - The beam climbs, and the bat appears on the cloud.
     - On his gargoyle, Batman stands and his cape opens.
     - He drops a storey with the cape spread. His line catches a cornice, and he swings in an arc onto the police roof, landing crouched behind Gordon.
     - Gordon turns, startled. They talk: Gordon points across the city.
     - Gordon looks where he pointed. When he looks back, the roof is empty, and the cape is just whisking over the parapet.
   - **Ends:** Gordon shrugs and switches the light off. About 30 seconds.
   - **In the comics:** Batman gone before Gordon finishes his sentence is a running joke [mem]. This story is the overture: the bat on the cloud tells the viewer that Batman's next story is starting.

2. **The Jokermobile** (B 37, 1946; B&B 191, 1982; the Joker-signal of 1942) [wp Jokermobile; wp Joker].
   - **Who:** the Joker, two henchmen in clown masks [tile] and the Jokermobile; Batman and Robin in the Batmobile.
   - **Where:** from Ace Chemicals along the main avenue to a bridge that lifts or swings.
   - **What:**
     - Over Ace Chemicals the Joker's grinning face lights up on the cloud, and the henchmen hurry in at the gate.
     - The Jokermobile rolls out: white, with the Joker's huge grin on its nose, playing cards fluttering from its tail.
     - The Batmobile comes out of its hidden door in the hillside and gives chase.
     - The Jokermobile's boot springs open, and a giant boxing glove on a spring punches at the Batmobile's nose. The Batmobile swerves and keeps on.
     - The bridge is opening for a boat. The Jokermobile shoots a plank across the gap, drives over, and draws the plank in behind it.
     - The Batmobile stops at the edge.
   - **Ends:** the bridge comes down, and a giant Joker card spins down onto the Batmobile's windscreen. Batman holds it up. About 45 seconds.
   - **In the comics:** the first Jokermobile had machine guns, and in B 5 (1941) the Joker forced the Batmobile off a cliff [wp Batmobile]. Neither is shown.

3. **The Laughing Fish** (Steve Englehart's story of 1978, in Det. 475 and 476 [mem for the issues]) [wp Joker].
   - **Who:** the Joker, fishermen, and Batman in the Batboat.
   - **Where:** the harbour wall and the fish market.
   - **What:**
     - The Joker skips along the harbour wall with a watering can of green.
     - He tips it into the water, and green rings spread.
     - Fish leap one after another, each with a white face and a red grin. They are drawn three times life size, so that they read.
     - The fishermen's nets come up full of grinning fish, and a fishwife drops her basket.
     - The Batboat swings round the end of the pier. The Joker bows, hops into a little rowing boat painted with his face, and rows away laughing.
   - **Ends:** the Batboat circles the rowing boat, which is empty but for a jack-in-the-box flying a flag with the Joker's face. The fish dive back. About 40 seconds.
   - **In the comics:** the Joker tries to copyright the grinning fish, and kills the officials who stand in his way [wp Joker]. Nothing of that is shown. A crate marked "PATENT PENDING" is enough [tile].

4. **The Penguin's umbrella** (Det. 58, 1941) [wp Penguin].
   - **Who:** the Penguin, Batman, Robin, and a flock of birds.
   - **Where:** from the art museum's portico to the Iceberg Lounge's terrace.
   - **What:**
     - The museum's bell rings. The Penguin waddles out of the portico with his umbrella under his arm, rolled-up canvases peeping out of its handle.
     - Batman drops from the pediment, and Robin runs round the corner.
     - The Penguin snaps open his umbrella. The canopy becomes spinning blades and lifts him, his legs paddling and his tails flapping.
     - A flock of birds bursts up round him and flusters the two below.
     - A batarang spins up and knocks the canvases out of the handle, and Robin catches them.
     - The Penguin drifts away over the rooftops shaking his fist, and lands on the Iceberg Lounge's terrace.
   - **Ends:** he bows to his customers, and the birds settle on the iceberg sign. About 40 seconds.
   - **In the comics:** in his first story he stole two paintings by hiding them in his umbrella's handle [wp]. His umbrellas also hide guns [wp]. None is shown.

5. **Catwoman on the rooftops** (B 1 to 3, 1940; her whip) [wp Catwoman].
   - **Who:** Catwoman, a black cat and Batman.
   - **Where:** a jeweller's skylight in the East End, and three roofs.
   - **What:**
     - A skylight lifts. Catwoman climbs out with a glittering necklace, and a cat follows her.
     - Batman lands on the same roof.
     - She cracks her whip round a chimney and swings to the next roof, and he swings after her on his line.
     - On the third roof she stops at the edge, turns, and tosses the necklace back to him.
     - He catches it. She blows a kiss and drops out of sight.
   - **Ends:** Batman looks over the edge, where only the cat sits. It flicks its tail and trots away. About 35 seconds.
   - **In the comics:** she escaped him like this in her early stories [wp]. Nothing needs softening.

6. **Riddle me this** (Det. 140, 1948) [wp Riddler].
   - **Who:** the Riddler, Batman and Robin.
   - **Where:** a street of signs, then the pier.
   - **What:**
     - Green question marks light up one after another, on a wall, a shop sign, a billboard and a lamp-post, in a trail to the pier.
     - The Batmobile follows the trail, and each mark goes dark behind it.
     - On the pier stands a maze of glass panes. Batman and Robin go in, while the Riddler capers on its roof.
     - The panes slide. They turn left, then right, and come out at the far end.
     - The Riddler backs away and slips off the pier with a splash.
   - **Ends:** a big green question mark bobs on the water. The Riddler's head comes up beside it, and he swims away pushing it. About 45 seconds.
   - **In the comics:** the maze was built to explode, and after the blast only his "?" was left floating in the water [wp]. Here there is no blast.

7. **Two-Face's coin** (Det. 66, 1942) [wp Two-Face].
   - **Who:** Two-Face, Batman, Robin, a charity collector, and a police van.
   - **Where:** a bank and the pavement outside it.
   - **What:**
     - Two-Face strides out of the bank with two sacks.
     - He flips his coin, large and glinting. It lands clean side up, and he drops both sacks into the collector's charity box. The collector stares.
     - Batman lands between them. Two-Face flips again.
     - The coin lands on its edge and rolls away down the pavement, with Two-Face running after it.
     - It rolls against Batman's boot, and Two-Face stops dead. Robin drops a net over him.
   - **Ends:** the police van takes him away, and Batman hands the sacks back to the bank. About 40 seconds.
   - **In the comics:** he robbed a bank, then gave the loot to charity at the coin's word. His first story ends with the coin landing on its edge [wp].

8. **Ivy's vines** (Robinson Park, in No Man's Land, 1999) [wp Poison Ivy].
   - **Who:** Poison Ivy, Batgirl, and a window cleaner.
   - **Where:** the edge of the park, and a tall building facing it.
   - **What:**
     - Ivy steps out of the trees, barefoot, and raises her arms.
     - Vines race up the building's face, storey by storey, and flowers open at every window. The window cleaner's cradle is wreathed in them.
     - Batgirl arrives on her motorcycle, her red hair flying.
     - Ivy laughs. A huge flower opens beneath her and lifts her back over the trees.
   - **Ends:** the vines slide back down the wall and leave a window box in flower at every window. Ivy sits in her blossom in the park. About 35 seconds.
   - **In the comics:** she once used giant vines to destroy buildings, and her kiss poisons [wp]. Here the vines only climb, and she kisses no one.

9. **Freeze on the avenue** (B 121, 1959) [wp Mr. Freeze].
   - **Who:** Mr. Freeze, Batman, Robin, and the traffic.
   - **Where:** an avenue with a steam vent, outside a jeweller's.
   - **What:**
     - Freeze steps out of the jeweller's with a case of gems and points his gun down the avenue.
     - A white sheet of ice runs along the road. Cars slide gently and turn to a stop, and a hydrant freezes into an ice fountain.
     - The Batmobile slides in sideways, and Batman and Robin jump out.
     - Freeze fires twice. Batman and Robin stand frozen in two blocks of ice.
     - Batman rocks his block until it topples against a steam valve, and steam bursts from the vent in the street.
     - The blocks melt, and the two step out. The steam fogs Freeze's dome, and he backs away blind.
   - **Ends:** Robin flips a net over Freeze. The ice on the avenue melts in the steam, and the traffic moves on. About 50 seconds.
   - **In the comics:** Batman and Robin were frozen in blocks of ice in Mr. Zero's hideout, and Batman broke a steam pipe to melt them free [wp]. The tile moves the scene to the street.

10. **The pumpkin in the sky** (Batman: Haunted Knight, 1996) [wp Bat-Signal; wp Scarecrow].
    - **Who:** the Scarecrow, his crows, Gordon and Batman.
    - **Where:** the police roof and its cloud.
    - **What:**
      - At night the searchlight comes on by itself. On the cloud is not a bat but a grinning orange jack-o'-lantern.
      - Gordon bursts out of the roof door.
      - The Scarecrow capers on the parapet, with crows rising round him.
      - Batman arrives on his line. The Scarecrow flings a pouch, and a puff of orange dust bursts over him.
      - Batman's cape sweeps the dust away, and the crows scatter.
    - **Ends:** Batman unscrews the orange bulb and peels the painted eyes off the lens, and the bat comes back to the cloud. The Scarecrow flaps away into the dark among his crows. About 35 seconds.
    - **In the comics:** the Scarecrow altered the signal to tell Batman he had kidnapped Gordon [wp Bat-Signal]. There is no kidnapping here, and no visions of fear.

11. **Out of the Batcave** (Det. 83, 1944, and the trophies of 1946 to 1950) [wp Batcave; wp Alfred Pennyworth].
    - **Who:** Alfred, Batman, Robin, and the Batmobile.
    - **Where:** Wayne Manor and the cutaway cave.
    - **What:**
      - Alfred comes out onto the manor's terrace, looks up at the bat on the cloud, and goes back in.
      - Below, the cave's lights come on bay by bay: the T. rex, the giant penny, the giant Joker card, the Batplane, and the Batboat at its water gate.
      - Batman and Robin run down a stair to the Batmobile. Its canopy closes, and its engine glows.
      - A rock face in the hillside slides aside, and the Batmobile shoots out onto its road.
    - **Ends:** the door closes, and the lights go out bay by bay. Alfred lights the porch lamp. About 30 seconds. A dawn variant brings the Batmobile home the same way.

12. **Man-Bat at the belfry** (Det. 400, 1970; the bats of Batman: Year One, 1987) [wp Man-Bat; wp Batsuit].
    - **Who:** Man-Bat, a cloud of bats, and Batman.
    - **Where:** a belfry, and the sky round it.
    - **What:**
      - At dusk a cloud of bats pours out of the belfry, and among them is a much larger winged shape.
      - Man-Bat swoops in loops round the tower, and a flock of pigeons scatters.
      - Batman follows from cornice to cornice and lands on the belfry's ledge, with Man-Bat across from him.
      - Batman holds up a small vial. Man-Bat hesitates, takes it and drinks.
      - He folds his wings and shrinks into a man in a white coat, who sits down, dizzy.
    - **Ends:** Batman helps Kirk Langstrom up, and the bats stream back into the belfry. About 35 seconds.
    - **In the comics:** Batman finds a way to reverse the change, and Langstrom becomes Man-Bat again and again [wp]. A gentle story.

13. **Croc under the street** [wp Killer Croc].
    - **Who:** Killer Croc, Nightwing, and a fish van.
    - **Where:** a manhole by the market, and the storm drain's mouth on the river.
    - **What:**
      - A manhole cover lifts an inch, and two yellow eyes look out.
      - Croc heaves himself out beside the fish van and grabs a crate.
      - Nightwing flips down from a fire escape, his sticks spinning.
      - Croc roars, tucks the crate under his arm, and drops back down the manhole.
      - A moment later he bursts out of the storm drain into the river, swims a few strokes and dives.
    - **Ends:** the manhole cover spins down into place. One fish flops back out of it, and Nightwing hands it to the driver. About 30 seconds.

14. **The breakout** (Knightfall, 1993) [wp Bane]. Rare.
    - **Who:** Bane, every rogue netted so far, a guard, and the Bat-Signal.
    - **Where:** Arkham's gate.
    - **What:**
      - Bane walks up to Arkham's gate, takes hold of its bars, and lifts it out of its frame.
      - Out run the rogues netted earlier, each in their colours, and each heads off to their own haunt.
      - The Bat-Signal lights over the city.
      - Bane leans the gate against the wall, dusts off his hands, and walks away to the docks.
    - **Ends:** the gate leans, and a guard scratches his head. About 30 seconds.
    - **In the comics:** Bane blew up Arkham's walls, then broke Batman's back once Batman had worn himself out catching everyone [wp]. Neither is shown. On the tile this story is the reset: it sends everyone home.

15. **Harley's mallet** (Harley and Ivy as friends, from Gotham City Sirens, 2009 to 2011) [wp Harley Quinn]. Only if Harley is kept.
    - **Who:** Harley, her two hyenas, Poison Ivy and Batgirl.
    - **Where:** the lawn of Ivy's park.
    - **What:**
      - Harley cartwheels across the lawn, the hyenas bounding after her.
      - She bops a "WANTED" poster of herself with a mallet as big as she is. It squeaks [tile].
      - Batgirl rides up.
      - Harley whistles. A vine curls down from a tree, wraps round her waist and lifts her into the branches beside Ivy.
    - **Ends:** the two wave from the treetop, and the hyenas chase their tails at the foot of the tree. About 30 seconds.
    - **In the comics:** her mallet is a weapon, and she has guns [wp]. Here the mallet squeaks, and she has no guns.

Two more, if wanted:

- **A. Clayface at the pictures** (Det. 40, 1940; Matt Hagen) [wp Clayface]. A statue of a film monster stands on the cinema steps. It steps down, slumps into a brown blob and slides into a drain, while a doorman rubs his eyes.
- **B. The Batgyro** (Det. 31, 1939) [wp Batplane]. The autogyro hovers "parked" over a roof while Batman climbs down a line, then climbs back up as it drifts away. A quiet moment between stories.

## 5. Several at once

The Blackwater Bay director is the model: it chooses what plays next, keeps at most three dragons in the air, and lets no two moments share a piece of sky ([NOTES.md](../../../app/src/tiles/blackwater-bay/NOTES.md)). Here it is the same, with stages instead of sky [tile throughout].

**The stages.** Each story claims a stage: a piece of the tile at one level. The levels are underground, street, rooftop and sky. The cloud is a stage of its own.

**The rules:**

1. **At most three stories at once,** as the bay has at most three dragons in the air.
2. **Each figure can be in one place only.** Batman, Robin, Batgirl, Nightwing and each rogue are in at most one story at a time, and the Batmobile is in at most one. A figure in a story is gone from its home: the gargoyle is bare, and the Lounge's terrace is empty. So the viewer learns who lives where.
3. **No two stories share a stage.** In particular:
   - one signal on the cloud at a time (stories 1, 2 and 10);
   - one story in the sky at a time (4, 12, and the Batgyro of extra B);
   - stories 2, 6 and 9 all want a long street, so they run together only if the city note gives them different streets.
4. **No two green figures at once.** The Riddler, Ivy and Croc are all green, so no two of them play together. The Joker's purple holds him apart.
5. **Start apart, so the climaxes alternate.** A new story starts only when the youngest one has passed its climax, about 15 to 20 seconds in.
6. **The Signal points.** Every story Batman leads starts with the bat on the cloud, or with Batman leaving his gargoyle. The eye goes up to the cloud, then down to where he lands. The Drift camera, which leans towards whatever is happening ([CONTEXT.md](../../../CONTEXT.md)), can follow him.
7. **Allies widen the cast.** Batman leads one story, and Robin, Batgirl or Nightwing lead the others. Stories 3 and 13 start without any hero, so they fill gaps.
8. **Day and night.** The signal stories (1, 10, and the Joker-signal in 2) need dark. In daylight the lighter capers play: 3, 7, 8 and 15.
9. **The revolving door.** A netted rogue rides in the police van to Arkham's gate. Story 14 (or, between its runs, a quiet slip back after a few minutes) sends every rogue home. In the comics the Joker is always sent to Arkham rather than to prison [wp Joker], and the rogues get out again, as in Knightfall [wp Bane].
10. **The opening.** The first view should have the bat on the cloud, as Blackwater Bay opens with Balerion's laps: story 1, then 11, with story 3 or 13 beside it.

### Harm, which the comics are full of

- **Deaths.**
  - The Joker kills by the dozen, and his "venom" leaves a grin on its victims [wp Joker].
  - He killed the second Robin, Jason Todd, in 1988 [wp Robin], and shot Barbara Gordon in The Killing Joke (1988) [wp Barbara Gordon].
  - Hush tried to kill his parents [wp Hush]. Croc once ate a doctor [wp Killer Croc]. Ivy has killed with her kiss [wp Poison Ivy].

  None is shown or hinted at. Nobody falls, bleeds or stays down.
- **Batman's parents,** shot in Crime Alley [wp List of DC Universe locations]. Nothing of Crime Alley is drawn. The grandfather clock at the cave's door is set, in the comics, to the hour of their murder [wp Batcave], so the tile's clock shows some other time, or there is no clock.
- **The Robins' griefs:**
  - Dick Grayson's parents killed when their trapeze was sabotaged [wp Robin], so there is no circus or trapeze for Robin;
  - Jason Todd's memorial case in the cave [wp Batcave].
- **Bane** broke Batman's back [wp Bane], and his tubes feed him a drug [wp]. The tubes stay as part of his costume, but the tile has no drug and no fight with Batman. He is a strongman who lifts things.
- **Guns,** none anywhere [tile]. The comics have plenty:
  - an early Batman's holstered pistol [wp Batsuit];
  - the first Batplane's and the Jokermobile's machine guns [wp Batplane; wp Jokermobile];
  - the Penguin's umbrella guns [wp Penguin];
  - the Joker's trick guns, including the one that shoots a "BANG" flag [wp Joker];
  - Harley's popguns and firearms [wp Harley Quinn];
  - Two-Face's pistols [mem].
- **What stays, softened** [tile]:
  - the Joker's lapel flower squirts water, where the comics' squirts acid [wp Joker];
  - Harley's mallet squeaks;
  - Catwoman's whip only ever wraps round chimneys and lamp-posts;
  - batarangs only knock props away;
  - the freezing gun freezes streets and things, and Batman and Robin only once, in story 9, as in 1959, before steam frees them;
  - the Scarecrow's dust is a puff that Batman's cape sweeps away. His noose and scythe are left out.
  - the Joker's laughing gas touches only the fish.
- **Smoking.** Gordon chain-smokes in the comics [wp Commissioner Gordon], the Penguin has a cigarette holder [mem], and the Joker has exploding cigars [wp Joker]. None is shown.

### Care

- **Arkham Asylum** is a psychiatric hospital in the comics, introduced as Arkham Hospital in 1974 [wp Joker]. The rogues' "madness" runs through the comics as a joke, and it is a joke at the expense of real illness. On the tile Arkham is only a gothic gate in a wall, where the police van stops. There are no cells, no straitjackets and no words like "loony". If the collector prefers, Blackgate prison's gate does the same job [wp List of DC Universe locations].
- **Disfigurement as villainy.**
  - Two-Face's scarred half [wp Two-Face];
  - Croc's skin condition, for which he was called names as a child [wp Killer Croc];
  - the Joker's bleached skin [wp Joker];
  - the Penguin, bullied for his shape [wp Penguin].

  All are drawn as colours and shapes, not as wounds. Croc is a green giant, never a "freak".
- **Revealing costumes.** Catwoman's 1990s suits, Harley's New 52 corset, and some drawings of Ivy are the most revealing. The tile uses the full suits given above.
- **Harley and the Joker** are never shown together [wp Harley Quinn].
- **The Mad Hatter, Ra's al Ghul and Hush** are left out, for the reasons given in section 3.
- **Names and logos.** Like the series name on Blackwater Bay, the comics' logos and title lettering are left off the boards and the screen. The bat on the Signal and on Batman's chest is the tile's own drawing of it [tile].

## 7. Questions for the collector

1. **Harley Quinn.** Her best-known look is the animated series' jester suit, which the comics drew from 1999 to 2011. Should she wear it, and move up to must-have? Or Amanda Conner's comics look of 2013? Or should she wait?
2. **Batman's colours.** Now: the grey suit with the yellow oval and blue-black, as in 1964 to 2000. The alternative is the plain black bat on grey and black, as in 1939 to 1964 and 2000 to 2021.
3. **The Batmobile.** Now: the car of B 5 (1941), with its single fin. The alternative is a later comics car, such as the round car of 1985 with its two canopies.
4. **Figure size.** Now: true person scale, with props and effects drawn large, as the comics' giant props are. The alternative is the named figures at one and a half times a person, as small landmarks are drawn larger.
5. **Night.** The Signal needs dark and a cloud. Does the tile keep the board's clock, or have its own nights?
6. **Arkham.** Now: a gothic gate that the rogues are driven to and come out of. The alternative is Blackgate prison's gate.
7. **The three left out:** the Mad Hatter, Ra's al Ghul and Hush. Leave them out, as recommended?
