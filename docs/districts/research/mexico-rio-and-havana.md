# Wimmelstadt: Mexico, Rio de Janeiro, Havana (checked to 18 Nov 2026)

**[u]** = general knowledge, not checked this session. The web-search and page-fetch budgets ran out partway, so many look, colour and position details carry it; unmarked time-sensitive facts were checked. ## A. Mexico

**1. Character.** A high, flat basin city ringed by volcanoes: a vast bare square with a giant flag, a boulevard with a golden angel on a column, stepped pyramids outside town. Pink-and-white taxis, marigold orange and painted canal boats give the colour. Its car identity: an F1 lap through a stadium, the Vocho, Baja desert racers.

**2. Ground** (positions [u], figures checked). Mexico City: about 2,240 m on a drained lakebed, tight central grid. Zócalo centre-east; Bellas Artes and the Torre Latinoamericana a few blocks west; Paseo de la Reforma runs on south-west past the Angel to Chapultepec hill. Arena México south-west of the centre, Autódromo east-south-east, stadium far south, Xochimilco's canals and chinampa garden islands beyond. Teotihuacan 40 km north-east. Popocatépetl 70 km south-east: symmetrical 5,393 m cone, steaming crater, a saddle road to its twin Iztaccíhuatl. Baja far north-west: desert peninsula, the arch at its tip where Pacific meets Gulf of California.

**3. Landmarks.**
1. *Zócalo*: 240 m paved square, cathedral north, National Palace east, giant flag on a central pole. Roads ring it; pedestrianised 2024 [u].
2. *Angel of Independence*: 36 m column, gilded 6.7 m Victory; traffic circles its Reforma roundabout.
3. *Teotihuacan*: Pyramid of the Sun, 65 m; Moon pyramid closing the north end of the 2 km Avenue of the Dead. Pedestrian.
4. *Autódromo Hermanos Rodríguez*: 4.304 km, 17 turns; the lap runs between the grandstands of the old Foro Sol ballpark (now Estadio GNP Seguros): a road through a stadium.
5. *Estadio Banorte (ex-Azteca)*: giant concrete oval, ring roof [u], 87,500 seats.
6. *Bellas Artes and Torre Latinoamericana*: white-marble palace, orange-yellow dome [u]; opposite, the slim 1956 tower, 44 floors, 166 m.
7. *Chapultepec Castle*: pale palace with watchtower on a wooded hill; Reforma was laid out to reach it.
8. *Chichén Itzá and Tulum*: El Castillo, 55 m square base, 30 m high, four stairways, temple on top; Tulum's small castle on a 12 m sea cliff, walled on the land side.
9. *El Arco, Cabo San Lucas*: granite sea arch at Land's End, sea lions; seen from boats.

**4. Roads and car culture.**
- Mexico City GP: contracted to 2028; 2026 race 1 November.
- Day of the Dead parade: inspired by *Spectre* (2015); tenth edition 31 October 2026, from the Diana Cazadora roundabout along Reforma, Avenida Juárez and 5 de Mayo to the Zócalo, with floats and catrinas.
- Vocho: standard taxi from 1971, built in Puebla until 2003; none after 2012, though "vochotaxis" still work Cuautepec's steep streets.
- Taxi colours (nicknames vary by source): 1950s "cocodrilos", big sedans, green with white triangle teeth; 1970s–80s yellow-and-white Vochos; 1991–2008 green-and-white Vochos; 2008–2014 wine-red and gold; since 2014 pink and white with "CDMX", now on small sedans, hybrids and EVs. A new livery was announced in December 2025; design unconfirmed.
- Baja 1000 (SCORE, since 1967): normally starts in Ensenada, as a loop or point-to-point to La Paz. The 59th (13–15 November 2026) instead started and finished in San José del Cabo, won by Bryce Menzies' Trophy Truck; 2027 returns to Ensenada. Field: Trophy Trucks, buggies, Baja Bugs, side-by-sides, motorcycles, quads.

**5. Businesses** (operation not re-checked [u], except Arena México). Sanborns in the Casa de los Azulejos: palace front in blue-and-white tiles. Churrería El Moro: blue-and-white tiled shopfront. Arena México: lucha libre, 16,500 seats, block-letter marquee, shows Tuesdays and Fridays. Café de Tacuba. Pastelería Ideal. Oxxo corner shops, red and yellow. Parodies: "Vulcanizadora El Popo", "Churrería El Motor", "Tacos El Pistón".

**6. Street life.** Brightly painted trajineras poled along the canals. [u]: red Metrobús, orange metro trains, taco stands, organ grinders, shoeshine chairs, papel-picado bunting.

**7. Scenes.** Giant skeleton figures parading down Reforma. Race cars inside the stadium section. Trajinera jam with a mariachi boat. Taxi rank by era: yellow Vocho, green Vocho, pink sedan.

**8. Watch out.**
- Sponsor names: the Azteca became Estadio Banorte on 14 March 2025; FIFA called it Mexico City Stadium. It reopened 28 March 2026, hosted the World Cup opener (Mexico 2–0 South Africa, 11 June) and four more matches, and is the only stadium used in three World Cups. Use a generic name.
- Teotihuacan: a gunman killed a tourist at the Pyramid of the Moon on 20 April 2026; the site reopened two days later with that pyramid closed. No climbing there or at Chichén Itzá: figures stay at the base.
- Marks: F1, FIFA, SCORE, Volkswagen, real wrestlers' masks. Leave out cartel and border imagery, protests, sombrero clichés; Day of the Dead is remembrance, not horror.

**Isthmus.** Panama Canal, Atlantic to Pacific: Gatún (3 chambers, 26 m lift), Pedro Miguel (1), Miraflores (2); electric "mule" locomotives steady the ships. The 2016 locks, Agua Clara and Cocolí: 3 chambers each, water-saving basins, no mules. Bridge of the Americas (1962), Pacific entrance: steel through-arch, 1,654 m long, 61 m clearance, four lanes, road over the canal.

## B. Rio de Janeiro

**1. Character.** Granite domes rising straight out of sea and city, a pale statue with open arms on the highest spike, scalloped beaches edged in black-and-white wave paving. Forest and hillside communities fill the gaps.

**2. Ground** (layout [u], figures checked). No grid: thin strips of blocks between sea and rock, joined by tunnels and coast roads. Guanabara Bay lies east, its mouth opening south; the centre is on its west shore, with Lapa at the southern edge and Santa Teresa on the hill above. Sugarloaf guards the bay mouth. On the open Atlantic beyond it: the 4 km crescent of Leme and Copacabana, then Ipanema and Leblon running west to the Two Brothers. Behind Ipanema, the Rodrigo de Freitas lagoon (2.4 km²), linked to the sea by a canal. North of it Corcovado (about 700 m) rises from forest, the statue facing east. Beyond that massif: the Maracanã, with the Sambadrome between it and the centre. Niterói is across the bay.

**3. Landmarks.**
1. *Christ the Redeemer*: 30 m figure, 8 m plinth, 28 m arm span, pale soapstone; summit only, no through road.
2. *Sugarloaf*: bare 396 m dome behind the 220 m Morro da Urca; two cable spans from Praia Vermelha, glass cabins.
3. *Copacabana*: black-and-white wave mosaic (Burle Marx rebuild, 1970) beside six-lane Avenida Atlântica; the six-floor 1923 Copacabana Palace, white [u]; a fort at each end.
4. *Maracanã*: wide oval under a membrane roof ring (white [u]), 73,139 seats.
5. *Lapa Arches*: 42 arches in two tiers, 270 m long, 17.6 m high, white [u]; the open-sided tram (yellow [u]) runs along the top, streets pass beneath [u].
6. *Sambadrome*: about 700 m parade strip between concrete stands, ending at a 90 m three-legged arch.
7. *Selarón Steps*: 215 tiled steps, 125 m, green, yellow, blue and red [red u]; Lapa up to Santa Teresa; pedestrian.
8. *Two Brothers*: twin unequal peaks at Ipanema's west end [u], Vidigal on the slope.
9. *Niterói*: saucer museum, 16 m high, 50 m wide, on a stem above a pool (white, red ramp [u]), on a cliff facing Sugarloaf; the 13.29 km, eight-lane bridge, centre span 72 m high, takes the road over the bay.

**4. Roads and car culture.** The Avenida Niemeyer cliff road [u]; the 1930s–50s Gávea street circuit, "Trampolim do Diabo" [u]. Jacarepaguá held the Brazilian GP in 1978 and 1981–89, closed in 2012 and was demolished for the Olympics; its replacement at Guaratiba was still only a project in August 2026. Carnival's top parades fill three nights, 7–9 February 2027. Brazil built the Kombi until December 2013.

**5. Businesses.** Confeitaria Colombo (1894): Belle Époque front, mirrored Art Nouveau salon. Copacabana Palace (Belmond). [u]: Garota de Ipanema bar, Bob's burgers (red sign), Granado pharmacy (vintage apothecary front), beach kiosks. Bar Luiz is bankrupt and shut. Parodies: "Calota de Ipanema", "Borracharia Dois Irmãos", "Oficina Pão de Asfalto".

**6. Street life.** Taxis: yellow, dark-blue side stripe, roof "TÁXI" light. Buses: moving since December 2025 to a livery with one colour per region (orange in Campo Grande). Lifeguard posts numbered 1 (Leme) to 6. [u]: Fuscas, Kombi vendor vans, moto-taxis, parasols, footvolley nets.

*Hillside communities.* About a quarter of Rio lives in them (Rocinha is the largest; Vidigal faces Ipanema). Show an ordinary lived-in neighbourhood: small brick and painted houses stepping uphill, stairways, roof terraces, water tanks, shops, a football pitch, kites [u]. Invented "Morro" name; no guns, raids, squalor or sightseeing jeeps.

**7. Scenes.** Samba float and drummers entering the Sambadrome. Tram crossing the arches above traffic. Footvolley and a coconut kiosk on the wave paving, taxis outside the Palace. Kites over roof terraces.

**8. Watch out.**
- Christ: escalators shut since 3 August 2026 for about ten months, capacity halved. Religious monument; the Archdiocese asserts image rights [u].
- Sugarloaf zip-line: licences annulled in 2026, works suspended; leave it out.
- Tram: Silvestre branch reopened 11 June 2026.
- Maracanã: officially Estádio Jornalista Mário Filho; run by Flamengo and Fluminense to 2044.
- Marks: club crests, samba-school names. Leave out police, militia and campaign material; keep Carnival figures unsexualised.

## C. Havana

**1. Character.** A low pastel city behind a long curving seawall, a fortress and lighthouse across a narrow harbour mouth, one big dome inland. Arcaded streets and 1950s American cars in candy colours do the rest. Flat and compact: Rio's opposite.

**2. Ground.** Flat shore; the harbour is a pocket bay entered by a narrow channel opening north [u]. East head: El Morro on its rock, La Cabaña fortress (1774) on the hill behind. West head: La Punta fort, where Malecón and Prado both begin. Old Havana fills the channel's west bank: tight colonial streets, small squares. The Prado runs south as its boundary with Centro Habana, to the Capitolio. The Malecón runs 8 km west along the open coast to Vedado, past the Hotel Nacional on its bluff, ending at the Almendares river. A 1958 road tunnel under the harbour mouth links the Prado end to the Morro side [u].

**3. Landmarks.**
1. *Malecón*: low concrete seawall, broad pavement, wide road, facing row of pastel arcaded houses [u]. Winter cold fronts throw 4–6 m waves over the wall and the avenue is closed.
2. *El Morro*: angular stone bastions and dry moat on the east headland, lighthouse (1840s) at the seaward tip; reached by the tunnel.
3. *El Capitolio* (1929): neoclassical block 207 × 91 m, columned portico, 55-step stair, dome about 92 m (restored 2019), pale stone [u].
4. *Paseo del Prado*: raised tree-shaded terrazzo walk, marble benches, bronze lions (1928), a traffic lane each side [u]; Malecón to the Fuente de la India.
5. *Hotel Nacional* (1930): eight-floor block with two towers [u] on Taganana hill.
6. *Old Havana squares*: Plaza de la Catedral (baroque church), Plaza Vieja, Plaza de Armas closed by the Real Fuerza fort, Plaza de San Francisco by the port; arcaded, mostly pedestrian [u].

**4. Roads and car culture.** The classics are *almendrones* ("big almonds") or *máquinas*: about 60,000 pre-revolution American cars, mostly 1950s Chevrolet, Ford, Dodge and Plymouth, also Buick, Pontiac and Oldsmobile, many re-engined with diesels. Two uses: polished convertibles (pink, turquoise, red, two-tone [u]) waiting in rows at Parque Central for tourist tours; worn sedans as *colectivos*, shared taxis on fixed routes. Coco taxi: yellow fibreglass half-coconut on three wheels, two-stroke, two or three passengers behind the driver; since the late 1990s. The Malecón hosted the Cuban Grand Prix street races of 1957–58 [u].

**5. Businesses** (status fragile in 2026). El Floridita, daiquiri bar on Obispo, pink corner building with neon script [u]: open in May, nearly empty. Hotel Nacional: listed operating, July–August. Hotel Inglaterra, Parque Central: open, few guests (September). La Bodeguita del Medio: reported closed in September; verify. Coppelia ice-cream park: intermittent. Tropicana cabaret: unverified. Parodies: "El Floridiésel", "La Bodeguita del Pistón", "Taller El Almendrón".

**6. Street life.** Ladas and Moskvitches, electric tricycles; [u]: bicycle taxis, horse carriages, sidecar motorbikes. Lions and benches on the Prado; [u] balconies with washing, fishermen on the wall. Real billboards are political: use blank or invented signs.

**7. Scenes.** Wave bursting over the seawall onto a passing convertible. Candy-coloured convertibles lined up below the dome. Bonnet-up roadside repair with neighbours helping. Coco taxi passing a horse carriage by the Prado lions.

**8. Watch out.**
- 2026 crisis: US oil blockade since January, six island-wide blackouts by November, visitors down about 64% to August. Meliá ended all Cuban operations on 24 July and Iberostar withdrew; the Parque Central, Manzana and Raquel hotels were shut in September; many almendrones are parked for lack of fuel. Model the timeless postcard city, undated.
- Hotel Saratoga: wrecked by the 2022 explosion, still being rebuilt in May 2026; leave it out.
- Leave out portraits, slogans, Plaza de la Revolución, the US embassy and its protest stage, queues and blackouts. The Capitolio seats the National Assembly: show the building only.
- Marks: Havana Club and Cohiba (disputed), Bacardi, Hemingway's likeness, car-maker badges.
