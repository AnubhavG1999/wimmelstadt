# Gotham: the wider cast

This note adds figures to the Gotham tile that the first two notes lack. Batman, his first allies, his rogues and their stories are in [gotham-batman-and-rogues.md](gotham-batman-and-rogues.md), here called **the rogues note**. The city is in [gotham-the-city.md](gotham-the-city.md), here called **the city note**. Nothing in either is repeated here except where this note changes it. Read on 2026-11-20.

**What the collector asked for, after those notes were written:**

> "the Joker in there for sure, maybe a Justice League gimmick where they show up, the obvious rogues, penguin, bane, 2 face etc, ra's league of shadows, the robins too, and more"

- **The Joker, the Penguin, Bane and Two-Face** are already in the rogues note, the first two as must-haves and Bane as a should-have, each with a haunt and a story. Nothing is added here. Bane could move up to must-have.
- **Ra's al Ghul** was left out in the rogues note (section 3, "Nice to have"). The collector has now asked for him, so section 2 below replaces that decision.
- **New here:** the four Robins (section 1), Ra's al Ghul and his League (section 2), the Justice League as a rare visit (section 3), and a short bench (section 4). The table at the end lists every figure recommended here.

**The scale.** The rogues note sized figures at true person scale, a person about 0.55 units tall. The collector's brief for this note gives an ordinary person 2.3 units and Batman about 2.9, on a board 208 wide, with named figures 2.6 to 3.5. This note uses 2.3 and 2.9. The rogues note's sizes (Bane 0.8, and so on) need scaling by about 4.2 to match [tile].

## How sure each statement is

The tags are the rogues note's, plus one:

- **[wp Article]**: from that English Wikipedia article, read today as full plain text through `api.php` and searched by me. **Unless a second source is named, the statement rests on that one article.**
- **[wp~ Article]**: from that Wikipedia article, read today through a fetching tool that hands back a summary written to my questions, not the full text. Weaker than [wp]: the summary may have dropped or blurred something. Seven articles were read this way: Jason Todd, Tim Drake, Damian Wayne, League of Assassins, Ra's al Ghul, Talia al Ghul and Justice League.
- **[s]**: a web search's summary of pages I did not open. Weak.
- **[mem]**: my own memory of the comics, not confirmed today. Check it before building.
- **[tile]**: this note's proposal. It is not in the comics.
- **[film]**, **[TV]**, **[anim]**, **[game]**: from a film, a live-action series, an animated series or film, or a game. Not to be used.

**Caveat, read first.**

- No comic was opened. Wikipedia retells the comics and names issues.
- Wikipedia describes costumes thinly, as the rogues note found. Most colour details below for the Justice League, Ra's, Talia, the Red Hood and the bench are [mem]. Where Wikipedia does give a costume, it is marked.
- Fan wikis were not read.

Abbreviations as in the rogues note: **Det.** Detective Comics, **B** Batman, **B&B** The Brave and the Bold, **JLA** Justice League of America.

## 1. The Robins

All four stand on the tile at once, as the tile already mixes years [tile]. Dick Grayson is Nightwing, Jason Todd is the Red Hood, Tim Drake is Robin, and Damian Wayne is Robin.

**A clash with the rogues note.** That note's "Robin" is Dick Grayson in his suit of 1940, and it has Nightwing too, so Dick would stand on the tile twice. **Recommendation:** drop the 1940 Robin, and give his parts in stories 4, 6, 7, 9 and 11 to Tim Drake [tile]. The tile then has one Dick (Nightwing) and two boys in Robin suits who can be told apart (below). If the collector wants the 1940 suit, Damian can borrow it as a dressing-up joke instead, since in the comics he once wore Jason's old tunic from its memorial case [wp~ Damian Wayne]. That case is a grief, so the joke would need a different source [tile].

### Dick Grayson, Nightwing

- **First appearances.** As Robin, Det. 38 (April 1940), with the costume designed by Jerry Robinson after N. C. Wyeth's Robin Hood [wp Dick Grayson]. As Nightwing, Tales of the Teen Titans 44 (1984) [wp Nightwing; wp Dick Grayson]. The name itself is older: Superman used it in Kandor in Superman 158 (1963) [wp Nightwing].
- **His looks as Nightwing, by era:**
  - **1984:** a blue suit with a wide gold collar flaring up behind the head, gold gloves and boots, and gold "wings" from the shoulders [mem]. Often called the "disco" suit [mem].
  - **Late 1980s:** a dark blue and grey suit with a high collar [mem].
  - **1995 on, "the third costume":** a black bodysuit with a stylised blue "wing" across the shoulders and down the arms to the hands, colouring his two middle fingers. First in Nightwing: Ties That Bind 2 (October 1995), designed by the cover artist Brian Stelfreeze [wp Dick Grayson]. Wikipedia calls it "iconic".
  - **The New 52, 2011:** the same suit with the wing in red instead of blue [wp Dick Grayson].
  - **Rebirth, 2016:** black and blue again, with a thinner V-shaped bird from chest to shoulders, a blue domino mask, a blue "swish" on the shins, and straps on the forearms. Meant to "harken back" to the suit of 1995 [wp Dick Grayson].
- **Recommended look: the suit of 1995.** A black body, one blue bird spread from shoulder to shoulder and down both arms to the two middle fingers, a black domino mask, black hair [mem: worn longish in the 1990s]. Why:
  - it is the comics' own, by a comics artist, and the one later suits return to [wp Dick Grayson];
  - at toy size it is two blocks, black and blue, and the blue chevron across the chest reads from far off;
  - the red of 2011 would set him beside the Red Hood and Red Robin, and muddy the group [tile].
- **Not the comics':** Chris O'Donnell wears a Nightwing-like suit in Batman & Robin (1997) [film; wp Nightwing]. The Titans series (2018) has its own [TV; mem]. Neither is used.
- **Build.** Shorter than Bruce Wayne [wp Dick Grayson]. Lean, an acrobat [wp Dick Grayson]. About 2.75 [tile].
- **Prop: twin escrima sticks**, which in some versions join into a staff [wp Nightwing]. Savate and escrima are his favourite styles [wp Dick Grayson]. On the tile: two short sticks that spin and twirl, blue at the tips [tile].
- **His city.** Blüdhaven, "an economically troubled neighbor of Gotham City" [wp Dick Grayson]. It is off the tile.
- **Haunt in Gotham.**
  - He has worked beside Barbara Gordon as Oracle, and they were long a couple [wp Dick Grayson]. Oracle's home is the Gotham Clock Tower [city note, section 2].
  - He mended a broken street lamp on his return to the city [city note, section 2, item 16].
  - **For the tile:** the roofs round the Clock Tower, where he runs a loop of flips, and a ladder at a street lamp below [tile]. Not Park Row: the rogues note keeps Crime Alley off the tile.
- **Griefs to leave out.** His parents, the Flying Graysons, were killed when their trapeze was sabotaged [rogues note, section 6; wp Dick Grayson]. Since the New 52, Haly's Circus has stood in Gotham's Amusement Mile [city note]. The tile can have the funfair, but no trapeze act tied to Nightwing [tile]. Leave out also the Joker's killings in Haly's Circus in "Death of the Family" [wp Dick Grayson], and his shooting by the KGBeast [wp Dick Grayson].

### Jason Todd, the Red Hood

- **First appearances.** B 357 (March 1983), first full appearance in Det. 525 (April 1983); first as Robin in B 366 (December 1983) [wp~ Jason Todd].
- **As Robin.** Before 1986 a blond boy who dyed his hair black, in Dick's old suit. After Crisis, a street orphan who tried to steal the Batmobile's tyres in Crime Alley [wp~ Jason Todd].
- **The name "Red Hood" is the Joker's first.** The Red Hood who leapt into the vat at Ace Chemicals in Det. 168 (1951) was the Joker before his fall [rogues note, section 3]. Jason took the name on his return [wp~ Jason Todd].
- **His return:** "Under the Hood" (2005), his identity shown in B 638 [wp~ Jason Todd; the year of #638 not given there].
- **His looks as the Red Hood:**
  - **The helmet.** A red, smooth, faceless hood like a motorcycle helmet, given him by Talia al Ghul in the comics' account [wp~ Jason Todd]. It is the one constant.
  - **2005 on:** the helmet over a brown leather jacket, dark grey or black body armour and trousers, and boots [mem].
  - **2011 on (Red Hood and the Outlaws):** the helmet, the jacket, and a red bat emblem on a dark grey armoured chest [mem]. Wikipedia confirms that the comics drew an armoured suit with a red bat symbol "used by Jason Todd" [wp Dick Grayson].
  - **Not the comics':** the Arkham Knight's armour is the game Batman: Arkham Knight (2015) [game; wp~ Jason Todd]. The animated film Batman: Under the Red Hood (2010) tells his return [anim; wp~ Jason Todd]. Neither is used. I could not confirm whether the jacket and the red bat came from either. My memory is that the jacket is the comics' own from 2005 and the red bat from 2011, before the game [mem].
- **Recommended look: the comics' Red Hood of 2011.** The red helmet, a brown leather jacket open over a dark grey chest with a red bat, dark trousers and boots [mem, checked against wp Dick Grayson for the red bat]. At toy size: one red ball for a head on a dark body, with a red bat in the middle. No other figure on the tile has a red head without a face [tile].
- **Build.** Broad, built like Batman [mem]. About 2.9, as tall as Batman [tile].
- **What he carries, with no guns.**
  - In the comics he carries two pistols, a dagger, shuriken and batarangs [wp~ Jason Todd]. **The guns are left out**, as the rogues note leaves out every gun.
  - **For the tile:** his motorcycle, red with a dark tank [tile; that he rides one is mem]. In his hands, a pair of batarangs, which only knock props away as Batman's do [rogues note], or nothing. No dagger: a blade in a fight stops at a clash, and the Red Hood's stories have no fight [tile].
- **Haunt.** During Battle for the Cowl he worked from an abandoned subway [wp~ Jason Todd]. **For the tile:** the mouth of a disused subway entrance, boarded up, with a ramp his motorcycle rides out of [tile]. Not Crime Alley, for the reason above.
- **Griefs to leave out.**
  - **His death:** in "A Death in the Family" (B 426 to 429, 1988), the Joker beat him with a crowbar and left him in an explosion, and readers voted by telephone for his death [wp~ Jason Todd; wp Robin]. Nothing of it: no crowbar anywhere on the tile, no explosion, and no Joker beside him.
  - **His memorial case** in the Batcave [rogues note, section 6].
  - **His killings** as the Red Hood, and his guns [wp~ Jason Todd].
  - **The Lazarus Pit**, which brought him back in the comics' account [mem]. The Pit is in section 2, without Jason.

### Tim Drake, Robin (and Red Robin)

- **First appearances.** Batman: Year Three (1989), and B 436 (August 1989). His origin is "A Lonely Place of Dying", ending in B 442 [wp~ Tim Drake]. Robin at fourteen, after long training [wp~ Tim Drake].
- **His looks, by era** [wp~ Tim Drake]:
  - **1989 on, the first suit:** red torso with yellow stitching and belt, green short sleeves, green gloves and trousers, black boots, and a cape black outside and yellow inside. Neal Adams redesigned the whole suit, and Norm Breyfogle drew its "R" and the staff. First worn in B 457 (1990) [mem].
  - **After 2005:** mostly red and black, in tribute to Superboy.
  - **Red Robin, 2009:** a long-sleeved red tunic, black boots, tights, gloves, cape and cowl.
  - **The New 52 Red Robin, 2011:** red and black in one piece, with wings.
  - **Rebirth, 2016:** red torso, yellow belt, black trousers, green short sleeves, and a black-and-yellow cape.
- **Recommended look: the first suit of 1989 to 1990, as Robin.** Red tunic, green short sleeves and gloves, green trousers, black boots, yellow belt, a black cape with its yellow lining showing when it swings, a black domino mask, black hair [mem for the mask and hair]. Why:
  - it is the comics' own, by Neal Adams, and it ran for most of his fifteen years in Robin [wp~ Tim Drake];
  - it keeps the green that says "Robin" beside Batman, as the 1940 suit did;
  - the black outside of the cape is what sets him apart from Damian (below);
  - Red Robin is red and black, like the Red Hood [tile].
- **Build.** A teenager, slim [tile]. About 2.4, a little over an ordinary person and well under Batman [tile].
- **Prop: a collapsible bo staff**, his main weapon from his first suit on [wp~ Tim Drake]. Its first issue was not found. On the tile the staff extends with a snap, and he pole-vaults on it [tile].
- **What he is.** The detective of the Robins, a genius with computers [wp~ Tim Drake].
- **Haunt.** In Tim Drake: Robin (2022 to 2023) he lived on a houseboat at the Gotham marina [wp~ Tim Drake]. **For the tile:** a small houseboat at a pontoon on the waterfront, with a laptop's glow in its window at night [tile]. That his family's house stood next to Wayne Manor is [mem].
- **Griefs to leave out.** His parents' deaths [mem]; his time "dead" and back [mem]. None needed.

### Damian Wayne, Robin

- **First appearances.** As an unnamed baby, Batman: Son of the Demon (1987), not counted as canon then. As Damian, B 655 (2006), "Batman and Son", by Grant Morrison [wp~ Damian Wayne]. Robin at ten, the fifth Robin, after Battle for the Cowl, with Dick Grayson as his Batman [wp~ Damian Wayne]. The series is Batman and Robin by Morrison and Frank Quitely (2009) [wp Dick Grayson].
- **Whose son.** Bruce Wayne's only child, by Talia al Ghul, and trained by the League of Assassins [wp~ Damian Wayne]. So he is the bridge to section 2.
- **His looks** [wp~ Damian Wayne]:
  - **Before he was given the name:** Jason's old tunic over a black and white League bodysuit, with a grey hood and cape.
  - **The official suit, 2009:** the Robin tunic, a black suit beneath, a yellow para-cape for gliding, a green mask, green gloves, flexible boots and a black hood. Dick made the para-capes for both their suits [wp Dick Grayson].
  - **Rebirth, 2016:** armoured gauntlets and boots, and a black outer cape trimmed yellow.
  - **His own series:** black and grey with red trim.
- **Recommended look: the suit of 2009.** Red tunic over black sleeves and trousers, a black hood, a green mask, green gloves, and a cape that is **yellow outside** [wp~ Damian Wayne; the colours of the tunic and the hood's lining are mem]. Short dark hair [mem].
- **Build.** Small. He is "short for his age", often taken for younger than ten-year-old Jon Kent [wp~ Damian Wayne]. About 1.95, the smallest of the heroes and shorter than an ordinary person [tile].
- **Prop: a sword.** He "would also carry a sword" [wp~ Damian Wayne]. A katana, in my memory [mem]. On the tile it stays sheathed on his back and comes out only for a salute or a clash [tile]. Leave out his brass knuckles [wp~ Damian Wayne].
- **His pets**, a gift to a toy [all mem; not in the article as summarised]:
  - **Titus**, a black Great Dane;
  - **Alfred the cat**;
  - **the Bat-Cow**, a cow he saved;
  - **Goliath**, a winged Man-Bat-like creature.
  Section 4 judges each.
- **Haunt.** His base with Dick was "beneath Wayne Tower" [wp~ Damian Wayne]. **For the tile:** a door at Wayne Tower's foot, with a garage ramp going down, and Titus lying across it [tile]. Wayne Tower is the city note's centrepiece.
- **Griefs to leave out.** His death in Leviathan, in Wayne Tower's lobby, and his return from Apokolips [wp~ Damian Wayne]; his killings and his training to kill [wp~ Damian Wayne]. None needed.

### Their stories

Each 20 to 50 seconds, no harm, as in the rogues note's section 4 [tile throughout].

- **R1. Race over the roofs.** (The four Robins as brothers: Dick "an older-brother figure" to all three [wp Dick Grayson].)
  - **Who:** Nightwing, Tim, Damian and Titus; the Red Hood at the end.
  - **Where:** the Clock Tower roofs and three roofs beyond.
  - **What:** Nightwing taps a chimney: a race. He flips from roof to roof. Tim vaults the gaps on his staff. Damian glides on his yellow para-cape and lands first, arms folded. Titus runs along the street below and barks at each landing.
  - **Ends:** a motorcycle roars along the street. The Red Hood, on it, holds up a finger: he got there first by road. Damian stamps. About 35 seconds.
- **R2. The Batmobile's tyres.** (Jason's first meeting with Batman, when he tried to steal its tyres [wp~ Jason Todd].)
  - **Who:** Batman, the Red Hood, the Batmobile.
  - **Where:** a side street where the Batmobile stands while Batman is on a roof.
  - **What:** Batman climbs down his line and finds the Batmobile on bricks, one wheel gone. He looks up and down the street. A tyre rolls slowly out of an alley towards him, and stops at his boot.
  - **Ends:** the Red Hood leans on the alley's corner, waves two fingers, and rides off on his motorcycle. Batman fits the wheel. About 30 seconds.
  - **In the comics:** the theft was in Crime Alley. The tile moves it to any side street.
- **R3. Two Robins and a question mark.** (Tim and Damian as rivals: Damian left the Titans when Tim came back, as the team "does not need two Robins" [wp~ Damian Wayne].)
  - **Who:** Tim, Damian, the Riddler.
  - **Where:** the Riddler's pier [rogues note].
  - **What:** a big green question mark lights up on the pier. Both Robins land, from opposite sides, and see each other. Both run. Damian is faster. Tim pole-vaults over him and lands first.
  - **Ends:** they each grab an end of the question mark, which comes apart in two. The Riddler laughs from his booth and drops the shutter. About 30 seconds.
- **R4. The street lamp.** (Nightwing mending a lamp [city note].)
  - **Who:** Nightwing, and passers-by.
  - **What:** at dusk one lamp on the avenue stays dark. Nightwing drops from a fire escape, climbs the post, twists the bulb, and slides down as it lights.
  - **Ends:** a passer-by looks up at the lit lamp; the roof above is empty. About 20 seconds. A filler between larger stories.
- **R5. Titus walks Damian.** At the manor's lawn Damian tries to walk Titus on a lead. Titus walks him, across the lawn and round a tree, until Alfred whistles and Titus sits. About 25 seconds [tile].

Her death as Robin at Black Mask's hands, though undone, sits at the centre of her Robin story [wp Stephanie Brown]. **Recommendation: leave out for now.** If a fifth bird is wanted, the purple Spoiler can join R1, as the one figure in purple among the Robins.

The collector's phrase "ra's league of shadows" is the film's name. The figures are the same either way: the comics' League of Assassins are Ra's's own, and what the tile draws is them [tile].

The League's name also carries a problem for a cheerful toy: "Assassins" is the one word. On a board or screen the tile need show no name at all, as it shows no logos [rogues note, section 6]. In the note and the code, "the League" will do [tile].

### Ra's al Ghul

- **First and makers.** B 232, "Daughter of the Demon" (June 1971), by Julius Schwartz, Dennis O'Neil and Neal Adams [wp~ Ra's al Ghul]. His daughter Talia came a month before, in Det. 411 (May 1971), by O'Neil and Bob Brown [wp~ Talia al Ghul].
- **The name.** Arabic for "Head of the Ghoul", rendered "the Demon's Head", after Algol, the Demon Star, which Schwartz found in an astronomy book [wp~ Ra's al Ghul].
- **His look, as Adams drew him.**
  - O'Neil did not want a costumed or superpowered villain. Adams drew on striking faces, and named the actor Jack Palance [wp~ Ra's al Ghul].
  - Tall and spare, with a high widow's peak of black hair, grey or white at the temples, sharp cheekbones, heavy brows, and a thin moustache and short beard [mem].
  - A long green robe or tunic with a high collar, and a cape, often green lined with darker green, and trousers tucked into boots [mem]. Some artists add gold trim [mem].
  - At home in his lairs he wears the robe; in the field he has worn a suit [mem].
- **Since.** The same face and the green, kept by nearly every artist [mem]. His age has varied, from about 450 to over 700 years [wp~ Ra's al Ghul].
- **His manner.** He calls Batman "Detective", out of respect, and keeps that word for him [wp~ Ra's al Ghul].
- **Recommended look:** Adams's. A tall figure, about 3.0, in a long green robe with a high collar and a darker green cape, black hair with white temples, a short beard. Gold at the collar and cuffs, a hand's width, so the green reads as rich and not plain [tile]. **Prop:** a straight sword at his hip, for the duel only [tile].

### Talia al Ghul

- **First.** Det. 411 (May 1971) [wp~ Talia al Ghul].
- **Who.** Ra's's daughter, a spymaster, Batman's lasting love and adversary, Damian's mother, and later the founder of Leviathan to oppose Batman Incorporated [wp~ Talia al Ghul].
- **Look.** Not described in the article as summarised. Long dark-brown hair, and either a dark green or black dress or a black catsuit [mem].
- **Recommendation:** long dark hair, a dark green long-sleeved bodysuit or gown, a gold belt, about 2.7 [tile; mem for the base]. The green ties her to her father's colour.
- **Film:** Marion Cotillard in The Dark Knight Rises [film; wp~ Talia al Ghul]. Not used.

### Ubu

- The League's second-in-command and Ra's's bodyguard. "Ubu" is the title of a tribe, and when one Ubu dies another takes the name [wp~ League of Assassins].
- Drawn as a huge bald man, bare-chested, in a loincloth or sash [mem]. In the early comics he was close to a crude caricature: a mute, barely clothed brute in the service of an Eastern master [mem].
- **Recommendation: leave out**, or draw him only as a tall bodyguard in the League's own grey and green, with no bare chest and nothing that marks him as the "savage servant" [tile]. Leaving him out costs nothing: no story below needs him.

### What a League assassin looks like

- Recruits are called Ghuls. They carry "distinct black emblems" [wp~ League of Assassins].
- In the comics they are usually drawn in ninja-like wraps, black or dark grey, with a hood and a cloth over the face, and swords [mem].
- **Recommendation:** dark grey wraps with a dark green sash, a hood with a face cloth, about 2.6, lean, carrying nothing in the hands; a sheathed sword on the back at most [tile]. Three at a time, never a horde [tile].

### The Lazarus Pit

- A reservoir of rejuvenating chemicals that brings the dying and dead back; it can drive the returned mad for a while [wp~ Ra's al Ghul]. The name is the biblical Lazarus's. The first was called the Well of Sins [wp~ Ra's al Ghul].
- Its colour is not given in the article as summarised. The comics draw a stone pool of glowing green liquid in a cavern, steaming [mem].
- **For the tile:** a green-lit pool in a stone basin, which only glows and steams. No one goes in it [tile]. Its stories in the comics are deaths, so the tile uses its light and never its purpose.

### Ra's in Gotham

- After his return in The Resurrection of Ra's al Ghul (2007 to 2008), he set up his base in a Gotham City penthouse, where Batman later beat his ninjas and him [wp~ Ra's al Ghul].
- In "Contagion" (1996) he loosed a virus on Gotham; in B 400 (1986) he let Batman's foes out to show the city's folly [wp~ Ra's al Ghul]. Neither is a scene for a toy.
- No Lazarus Pit in or under Gotham was found [wp~ Ra's al Ghul].
- **Recommendation: a penthouse with a roof garden** on one of the tall towers, with dark green awnings, potted palms, and a pool that glows green at night [tile]. The pool is the tile's liberty, since no Pit in Gotham was found. Note it in the tile's `NOTES.md`.

### The duel, and other scenes

- **Batman 244 (September 1972), "The Demon Lives Again!"**, by O'Neil, Adams and Dick Giordano [s, several listings]. Ra's, risen from a Lazarus Pit, challenges Batman to a duel with swords in the desert [s]. Adams's cover shows Batman lying at Ra's's feet [s]. Both men fight bare-chested in my memory [mem; no search result mentions it].
- Other scenes [mem unless marked]: Batman and Talia in each other's company; Ra's at a long table telling Batman he would make him his heir; the "Detective" greeting [wp~ Ra's al Ghul].

### Drawing them with care

- **What the creator said.** Adams said Ra's was not meant to be tied to one people: "He's not necessarily Arabic. He's not necessarily Eastern" [wp~ Ra's al Ghul]. His ancestry has changed from story to story, and his mother was introduced as an Arab woman in 2021 [wp~ Ra's al Ghul].
- **What to avoid** [tile]:
  - turbans, curved scimitars, desert robes worn as costume, or a long drooping moustache: the signs of the "Eastern villain" of old adventure stories;
  - hordes of faceless masked killers from the East, attacking a Western city;
  - Ubu as a savage servant;
  - any lettering in Arabic or a mock script.
- **What to draw:** a gentleman villain in green, courteous and patient, who bows to his foe and calls him "Detective". His colour, green and gold, and his manners are what mark him, not a people [tile].

### Recommendation for the tile

- **Who:** Ra's al Ghul and Talia, and three League assassins. Damian links them to the Robins. Ubu left out [tile].
- **Where:** the penthouse and its roof garden, with its green-lit pool [tile, after wp~ Ra's al Ghul].
- **At home:** Ra's stands at the parapet with his hands behind his back, looking down on the city. Talia sits by the pool. The three assassins kneel in a row on the roof's edge, then vanish and reappear on another roof [tile].
- **Stories:**
  - **L1. The duel at the clash.** (B 244, 1972 [s].)
    - **Who:** Ra's, Batman, Talia.
    - **Where:** the roof garden.
    - **What:** Batman lands on the roof. Ra's bows and holds out a second sword, hilt first. Batman takes it. They circle once. The blades meet, high, and hold. A shower of sparks.

  - **L2. Grandfather's visit.** (Damian, Ra's's grandson and trained by the League [wp~ Damian Wayne].)

  - **L3. Shadows on the roofs.** Three assassins run across the roofs at night, leaping in a line. Nightwing and Tim follow. The assassins vanish in a puff of dark smoke at a chimney. The Robins arrive and find a single dark green feather [tile] on the chimney pot. About 30 seconds.

## 3. The Justice League shows up

### The founding seven, and where they met

- **First:** B&B 28 (March 1960), by Gardner Fox under Julius Schwartz, a revival of the Justice Society under a new name [wp~ Justice League]. Its own series followed in 1960 [wp~ Justice League].
- **The seven:** Superman, Batman, Aquaman, the Flash, Green Lantern, the Martian Manhunter and Wonder Woman, with a teenage honorary member, Snapper Carr [wp~ Justice League].
- **Which Flash:** Barry Allen, whose first appearance in the League is B&B 28 [wp Barry Allen]. **Which Green Lantern:** Hal Jordan, "a founding member" [wp Hal Jordan].
- **Where they first met as a team:** in JLA 9 (1962), the founders met when they each fought one of seven alien invaders, the Appellaxians [mem]. B&B 28 has them fight Starro, a giant starfish from space [mem].
- **Their first base:** a hollowed-out mountain outside Happy Harbor, Rhode Island [wp~ Justice League], called the Secret Sanctuary [mem].
- **The New 52 retelling (Justice League 1, 2011)** opens in Gotham, with Batman and Green Lantern chasing a creature over the rooftops and the police in pursuit [mem]. That is the comics' best "the League comes to Gotham" scene. In it Cyborg replaces the Martian Manhunter as a founder [wp~ Justice League says Cyborg became a founder in the New 52].
- **After Crisis**, Black Canary was told as a founder in place of Wonder Woman for some years [mem].

### Each one's lasting comics look

Colours in big blocks; what is a film's or a show's; how each arrives; one thing that reads at toy size [tile for the arrivals and gimmicks, unless marked].

**Superman.** Action Comics 1 (1938), by Jerry Siegel and Joe Shuster [wp Superman].

- **Look.** Shuster designed "tights with an 'S' on the chest, over-shorts, and a cape", after strongmen and wrestlers, and his boots are red [wp Superman]. So: a blue suit, a red cape, red trunks over the blue, red boots, a yellow belt, and the red and yellow "S" shield; black hair with a curl on the forehead [mem for the belt, shield colours and curl].
- **The trunks.** Removed in the New 52 (2011) and restored in Action Comics 1000 (2018) [mem]. Keep them: they are the comics' look for seventy years, and the films' suits without trunks and with dark, textured cloth are [film; mem].
- **Height and build.** About 3.0, broad and square-jawed [tile].
- **Arrives:** a red and blue streak down from the sky, landing on one knee, cape settling [tile].
- **Reads at toy size:** he lifts the biggest thing on the tile, a bus or the Batmobile, over his head with one hand [tile]. The red cape in flight.

**Wonder Woman.** All Star Comics 8 (1941); first feature in Sensation Comics 1 (1942) [wp Wonder Woman].

- **Look.** At her debut, a red top with a golden eagle, a white belt, blue star-spangled bottoms, and red and gold boots [wp Wonder Woman]. In 1982 a stylised "WW" replaced the eagle, and some form of it has been on every costume since [wp Wonder Woman]. Her magic items: the Lasso of Truth, indestructible bracelets, and a tiara she can throw [wp Wonder Woman]. Black hair [mem]; the tiara gold with a red star [mem].
- **Recommended:** red top with the gold "WW" or eagle across the chest, blue shorts with white stars, red boots with a white stripe, silver bracelets, gold tiara, gold belt, the golden lasso coiled at her hip [wp Wonder Woman; mem for the boots' stripe and the belt].
- **Not the comics':** the darker armour with a skirt of leather strips is the films' from 2016 on [film; mem]. The comics' Rebirth look (2016), with a red cape, pteruges and shin guards, came close to it [wp Wonder Woman; that it followed the film is mem]. Use the classic suit.
- **Height and build.** About 2.95, tall and athletic [tile].
- **Arrives:** in her **invisible plane**, which the comics drew "semi-transparent for the reader's convenience" [wp Wonder Woman]. On the tile: a pale, glassy outline of a jet, with her sitting in it, gliding down onto a roof [tile]. Unmistakable at any size.
- **Reads at toy size:** the golden lasso, a bright loop that flies out and catches a prop [tile].

**The Flash: Barry Allen.** Showcase 4 (October 1956), by Robert Kanigher and Carmine Infantino [wp Barry Allen].

- **Look.** "A distinct red and gold costume", stored compressed in a ring [wp Barry Allen]. A red suit and cowl, a yellow lightning bolt on a white disc on the chest, yellow boots, a yellow belt, and small yellow wings at the ears of the cowl [mem for the details].
- **Not the comics':** the television series' dark red leather suit (2014) and the films' armoured suits [TV; film; mem].
- **Height and build.** About 2.8, slim, a runner [tile].
- **Arrives:** a red streak along the avenue that stops dead, and there he is [tile].
- **Reads at toy size:** a red blur with yellow sparks that does a dozen small things in a second: sweeps the Riddler's trail clean, sets every stalled car straight, fills a fishmonger's baskets [tile]. His costume springing out of his ring is a good small trick [wp Barry Allen].

**Green Lantern: Hal Jordan.** Showcase 22 (September to October 1959), by John Broome and Gil Kane; a test pilot [wp Hal Jordan].

- **Look.** A green body and black legs and shoulders, or green with black sides; white gloves; a green domino mask; the lantern emblem on a white disc; brown hair greying at the temples [mem]. His ring "channels willpower into energy constructs", and makes "shields, weapons, and even his uniform" [wp Hal Jordan].
- **Which Lantern:** Hal is the founder [wp Hal Jordan]. John Stewart is the best-known Lantern to many, because the Justice League animated series (2001) cast him [anim; mem]. Use Hal.
- **Not the comics':** the CGI suit of the film Green Lantern (2011) [film; mem].
- **Height and build.** About 2.85 [tile].
- **Arrives:** flying, wrapped in a thin green glow [mem: the comics draw it so].
- **Reads at toy size:** a **green construct**, as big as a building: a bridge, a giant catcher's mitt, a net, a ramp [tile, after wp Hal Jordan]. The ring could not touch anything yellow in the Silver Age [wp Aquaman, which names "Green Lantern's vulnerability to the color yellow"]; a gag waits in the Batmobile's yellow and the Bat-Signal [tile].

**Aquaman.** More Fun Comics 73 (November 1941), by Paul Norris and Mort Weisinger [wp Aquaman].

- **Look.** "His trademark orange and green costume" [wp Aquaman]: an orange shirt of scales, green gloves and trousers, a yellow-gold belt with an "A" buckle, blond hair, clean-shaven [mem for all but the colours]. Blond hair is in the comics' origin [wp Aquaman]. He carried the Trident of Neptune before the New 52 [wp Aquaman].
- **Not the comics' lasting look:** long hair and a beard were the comics' for a few years in the 1990s [wp Aquaman], and since 2018 the film's look, with tattoos, has shaped the image most people know [film; mem]. Use the orange and green, clean-shaven.
- **Height and build.** About 2.9 [tile].
- **Arrives:** up out of the river or the harbour, on the back of a dolphin or a great seahorse [tile; that he rode a giant seahorse in the Silver Age is mem].
- **Reads at toy size:** he talks to sea creatures, by telepathy in later stories [wp Aquaman]. On the tile, fish leap in arcs round him, and a whale's back rises in the bay [tile]. He is the answer to the Joker's grinning fish (J2 below).

**The Martian Manhunter.** Det. 225 (November 1955), by Joseph Samachson and Joe Certa: born in Batman's own comic [wp Martian Manhunter].

- **Look.** A green-skinned Martian [wp Martian Manhunter]. Bald, with a heavy ridged brow and red eyes; a blue cape with a high collar; red straps crossing in an X over a bare green chest; blue trunks and boots [mem]. DC later redesigned his costume to cover his chest [wp Martian Manhunter]. Use the X straps, which read best at toy size, with the chest drawn as a smooth green block [tile].
- **Height and build.** The tallest of the seven, about 3.2, broad [tile].
- **Powers.** Shapeshifting, intangibility and invisibility, among many [wp Martian Manhunter].
- **Arrives:** he fades into sight, or steps out of a wall as though through a doorway [tile, after wp Martian Manhunter].
- **Reads at toy size:** walking through a wall; and turning from a passer-by in a grey coat into himself, the green spreading from his hands [tile]. A love of a brand of chocolate biscuit is a comics joke from Justice League International [mem]; a biscuit tin in his hand is a small extra [tile].

**Batman** is the seventh, and is the tile's already.

### Their stories

A rare gimmick, so one visit at a time, perhaps once in ten minutes, and each guest leaves when the story ends [tile].

- **J1. Not who Gordon expected.** (The rogues note's story 1, the Signal.)
  - **Who:** Gordon, Superman, Batman.
  - **Where:** the police roof.
  - **What:** Gordon lights the Signal. A streak of red and blue comes down out of the cloud, and Superman lands behind him. Gordon turns, and blinks.
  - **Ends:** Batman drops onto the roof on his line. Superman shrugs and smiles, and they both go off over the parapet, one flying and one swinging. About 30 seconds.
- **J2. The fish go home.** (After the rogues note's story 3, The Laughing Fish.)
  - **Who:** Aquaman, the fishermen, the grinning fish.
  - **Where:** the harbour.
  - **What:** the grinning fish are still leaping. Aquaman rises from the water on a great seahorse and raises a hand. The fish line up behind him in a long arc.
  - **Ends:** he rides out of the bay with the fish behind him, leaping in turn. A fisherman waves his cap. About 35 seconds.
- **J3. The green bridge.** (After the rogues note's story 2, the Jokermobile.)
  - **Who:** Green Lantern, Batman in the Batmobile, the Joker.
  - **Where:** the opening bridge.
  - **What:** the Jokermobile has crossed on its plank and drawn it in. The Batmobile is stopped at the gap. A green glow comes over the river: Green Lantern makes a green bridge across, and the Batmobile drives over.
  - **Ends:** the green bridge fades behind it. The Joker, ahead, throws his hat on the ground. About 30 seconds.
- **J4. The lasso and the umbrella.** (After the rogues note's story 4, the Penguin's umbrella.) The Penguin rises on his umbrella. An invisible plane's outline glides past, and Wonder Woman's golden lasso loops the umbrella's handle and lowers him gently to the street, where the police van waits. About 30 seconds.
- **J5. A dozen things at once.** The Flash sweeps through the city in a red blur: every one of the Riddler's question marks vanishes, the ice from Mr. Freeze is shovelled into a heap, a cat is lifted down from a tree. He stops by Batman's gargoyle for half a second, salutes, and is gone. About 20 seconds.
- **J6. The Martian at the window.** A man in a grey coat stands on the police roof beside Gordon. He turns green and tall: the Martian Manhunter. He nods to Gordon and walks through the roof door without opening it. About 20 seconds.

**The rule** [tile]: one League member on the tile at a time; never a League member and Batman fighting; a League story only follows the rogue's own story, so the rogue's gag plays first.

## 4. And more: a short bench

Each: first appearance, look in blocks, a haunt, keep or leave.

**Batwoman, Kate Kane.** 52 week 7 (2006), by Greg Rucka, Geoff Johns, Grant Morrison, Mark Waid and Alex Ross [wp Kate Kane]. Ross designed the suit from the Batgirl suit he had drawn for Barbara Gordon, "with red accents in place of the traditional yellow" [wp Kate Kane]. So: a black suit, a red bat on the chest, red gloves, boots and belt, a black cape lined red, and long red hair, which is a wig over her own short hair [wp Kate Kane for the red accents and the wig; mem for the rest]. Haunt: the rooftops of Old Gotham, perhaps the cathedral's [tile]. A Silver Age Batwoman, Kathy Kane, was a different character [wp Kate Kane]. Her live-action series (2019) is [TV; mem]. **Keep**, as a should-have: black and red with a red bat is easy to read, and she is a Gotham hero in her own right. She and the Red Hood both carry a red bat: give hers a bright red cape lining so the two differ [tile].

**Ace the Bat-Hound.** B 92 (June 1955), by Bill Finger and Sheldon Moldoff; a German Shepherd whom Batman gave a hood-like mask with the bat-emblem as a dog tag [wp Ace the Bat-Hound]. Retired in 1964 with the "New Look" [wp Ace the Bat-Hound]. A tan and black shepherd in a small black hood mask [mem for the colours]. Haunt: the Batcave and the manor's lawn; in a later version Alfred adopts him from the Gotham Pound [wp Ace the Bat-Hound]. **Keep.** A dog in a mask is a toy's delight, and he is the comics' own.

**Bat-Mite.** Det. 267 (May 1959), by Finger and Moldoff: an imp from the fifth dimension who adores Batman and sets up silly events to watch him, "more of a nuisance than a supervillain" [wp Bat-Mite]. "A small, childlike man in an ill-fitting copy of Batman's costume" [wp Bat-Mite]: a baggy grey and purple suit, a cape too long, big ears on his cowl [mem for the colours]. Haunt: none; he pops up anywhere [tile]. **Keep, as a rare gag.** He is the most cheerful thing in Batman's comics. He appears in a puff, makes one prop silly (the Batmobile's fins turn pink, the Signal's bat grows a smile), and vanishes when Batman frowns [tile, after wp Bat-Mite]. About 1.0 tall.

**Titus.** Damian's dog, a black Great Dane, from Batman Incorporated (2012) [mem]. Black, long-legged, with a bat on his collar [mem]. Haunt: at Damian's heel, and Wayne Tower's door [tile]. **Keep**, as Damian's mark (section 1).

**The Bat-Cow.** A calf Damian saved and kept, from Batman Incorporated vol. 2 1 (2012) [mem]. A black and white cow, sometimes with a bat-like patch [mem]. Haunt: a paddock at Wayne Manor [tile]. **Keep, as a gag**, grazing at the manor. Leave out where she came from (a slaughterhouse) [mem].

**Alfred the cat.** Damian's cat, from Batman Incorporated (2012) [mem], named for the butler. In one story he wears a little mask [mem]. **Keep, tiny**, on the manor's terrace beside Alfred the butler: the joke of two Alfreds [tile].

**Clayface.** The rogues note keeps him as a gag: a statue on the cinema steps that walks off and melts into a drain [rogues note, section 3]. Nothing new. **Keep as it stands.**

**The Court of Owls and its Talons.** First mentioned in B vol. 2 2 (2011), seen in vol. 2 3, by Scott Snyder and Greg Capullo [wp Court of Owls]. Gotham's oldest and richest families, in white owl masks, who use "murder and money" and send trained assassins, the Talons [wp Court of Owls]. A labyrinth under the city, and hidden rooms in every building of the Alan Wayne Trust [wp Court of Owls]. Talons wear dark armour, goggles with gold lenses, and knives [mem]. The Gotham series (2015) and the game Gotham Knights (2022) took them up [TV; game; wp Court of Owls]. **Leave out.** Their whole idea is murder, and Dick Grayson's tie to them is as a boy chosen to be a Talon [wp Dick Grayson]. A small stone owl carved over one old door is a nod for those who know [tile].

**Killer Moth.** B 63 (February 1951), by Bill Finger, Dick Sprang and Lew Sayre Schwartz: a man with no powers and a "Mothmobile, zipline cables, a cocoon gun, and an air pistol" [wp List of DC Comics characters: K]. He was an "anti-Batman" who answered a moth-signal from crooks [mem]. A purple suit with orange or green panels, a moth's wings on his back, antennae on his hood, round goggles [mem]. Haunt: a warehouse roof with a moth-shaped searchlight [tile]. He fights Batgirl more than Batman [wp List of DC Comics characters: K]. **Keep, as a should-have.** His cocoon gun is a net by another name, which is how the tile ends a chase; his moth-signal is a funny echo of the Bat-Signal on the cloud. Leave out the air pistol.

**Firefly.** Det. 184 (1952), by France Herron and Dick Sprang: Garfield Lynns, at first a special-effects man who used lighting effects in his crimes; after 1986 a pyromaniac in a fireproof suit with wings [wp Firefly (DC Comics)]. **Leave out.** His lasting version burns things, and a fire in a toy city reads as a disaster. The early light-show version is little known.

**The Ventriloquist and Scarface.** Det. 583 (February 1988), by John Wagner, Alan Grant and Norm Breyfogle: Arnold Wesker, a meek man ruled by a dummy dressed as a 1920s gangster, "complete with pinstripe suit, cigar, and Tommy gun" [wp Ventriloquist (character)]. Scarface says "Gatman" for "Batman" [wp]. **Leave out.** The dummy's prop is a gun, and the joke is a man dominated by his own split mind, which falls on the wrong side of the rogues note's care for illness.

**Solomon Grundy.** All-American Comics 61 (October 1944), by Alfred Bester: a murdered merchant risen from Slaughter Swamp, near Gotham, as a zombie [wp Solomon Grundy (character)]. A huge figure with chalk-white skin, lank hair and a ragged suit [mem]. He began as the first Green Lantern's foe [wp]. **Leave out.** A murdered man returned as a zombie does not suit the tile. His nursery-rhyme name ("born on a Monday") is charming, but he would need a swamp.

**Deathstroke.** The New Teen Titans 2 (1980), by Marv Wolfman and George Pérez: a mercenary, "the greatest and deadliest assassin", with a black and orange mask over a missing eye [wp Deathstroke]. His enemy is Dick Grayson and the Titans [wp]. **Leave out.** Guns and swords are his look, and he is the Titans' foe more than Gotham's.

**Black Mask.** B 386 (1985), by Doug Moench and Tom Mandrake: Roman Sionis, a crime lord with "a fixation on masks and torture", who murdered his parents and carved his mask from his father's coffin [wp Black Mask (character)]. **Leave out.** There is nothing in him for a toy.

**Azrael.** Batman: Sword of Azrael 1 (October 1992), by Denny O'Neil and Joe Quesada: Jean-Paul Valley, raised as an assassin by a religious order, who wore the cowl as a harsher Batman in Knightfall [wp Azrael (DC Comics)]. His suit is red and gold, with gauntlets whose blades burst into flame, and he has carried a flaming sword [wp]. **Leave out.** An assassin with a burning sword, and a story about a Batman gone too far.

**Huntress, Helena Bertinelli.** A Gotham mafia boss's daughter whose family was "gunned down" before her; trained in archery, her weapon a crossbow [wp Huntress (Helena Bertinelli)]. Her own series began in 1989 [mem]. A purple and black suit with a cape, a white cross on the chest, long black hair and a mask [mem]. **Leave out.** Her origin is a massacre and her prop is a weapon.

**Krypto the Superdog.** Adventure Comics 210 (March 1955), the model for Ace [wp Ace the Bat-Hound]. A white dog in a red cape [mem]. **Keep, only with Superman's visit**, if wanted: he and Ace chase each other once round the manor's lawn [tile]. Rare.

**Others judged and not added.** Oracle is Barbara Gordon, who is already on the tile as Batgirl [rogues note]; her Clock Tower is Nightwing's haunt here. Lucius Fox and Harvey Bullock are not known to a stranger at toy size [tile].
