# House of the Dragon for the tile

This note is for a tile of the age of the Targaryen dragons, as Fire & Blood tells it: the Conquest, the dragonlords' height under Jaehaerys, and the Dance of the Dragons. It builds on [westeros.md](westeros.md), [westeros-dragons-and-more.md](westeros-dragons-and-more.md) and the built Westeros tile's [NOTES.md](../../../app/src/tiles/westeros/NOTES.md), and does not repeat them. The eleven dragons themselves, their looks and their sizes, are in [the-great-dragons.md](the-great-dragons.md), which is being written beside this note. Here they appear only by where they were and what they did. Read on 2026-11-19.

"House of the Dragon" is the name of the television series as well as a name for House Targaryen. The tile takes nothing from the series: not its castles, its dragons, its lettering or its map, and it portrays no character. The name is not lettered on the tile.

## How sure each statement is

- **[v]**: confirmed by a source listed at the end. The book and the section are the ones the source gives.
- **[bk]**: from my own reading of the books, not confirmed today. Check it before building.
- **[tile]**: this note's proposal for the tile. It is not in the books.
- **[series]**: from the television series. Not to be used.

Caveat on sources, read first. A Wiki of Ice and Fire (awoiaf.westeros.org) refused fetches again (HTTP 403), as it did for both Westeros notes. Two kinds of source could be read. The first is web searches over its pages, whose results summarise the pages they name. The second is the Portuguese-language Gelo e Fogo wiki, which follows A Wiki of Ice and Fire closely and cites the books by section; its pages were read in full. Two of the searches mixed in the wiki's own pages on the series' episodes. Anything found only there is left out or marked. Fire & Blood is cited by its section titles, as the sources cite it.

Book abbreviations: **F&B** Fire & Blood, **TWOIAF** The World of Ice and Fire, **AGOT**, **ACOK**, **ASOS** as in the Westeros notes.

## 1. What the tile is

The Westeros tile already holds the whole continent, squeezed onto one board. Its King's Landing is about 35 across, with the Red Keep, the Great Sept of Baelor and the Dragonpit in ruin. Its Dragonstone is a castle whose towers are dragons, with a harbour, and its Dragonmont is a steep cone with a glowing crater on whose rim Drogon lands. It has Harrenhal's melted towers and the Gods Eye, riders, carts and wains on a tree of roads, and three dragons flying a display. A new tile has to be a smaller piece of that world, drawn larger, in another age.

### A. The Dance, from the Gods Eye to the Gullet

**What it is.** The strip of the crownlands and the riverlands where the Dance was fought in 129 and 130 AC. Harrenhal and the Gods Eye are in the west, King's Landing in the middle, Rook's Rest on the coast, and Driftmark, Dragonstone and the Gullet in the east. Tumbleton lies off it to the south-west [bk].

**Its dragons.** Most of the deaths of the Dance happened here: Meleys at Rook's Rest, Vhagar and Caraxes over the Gods Eye, and Dreamfyre in the Dragonpit [v]. Vermithor died at Tumbleton, off the strip [v].

**What the Westeros tile already shows.** Nearly all of it. Harrenhal, the Gods Eye and the Isle of Faces, the crossroads, King's Landing, Dragonstone and the Dragonmont are its riverlands and its King's Landing.

**How it would be its own.** As a war map of one year, with castles and camps as they stood in 130 AC. But it would read as the middle of the Westeros tile drawn larger, and its subject would be a run of deaths. Not recommended.

### B. Blackwater Bay in the age of the dragons (recommended)

**What it is.** One bay and its shores:

- King's Landing on the west shore, with the Dragonpit whole on the Hill of Rhaenys;
- the Rosby road along the north shore, to Rosby, Stokeworth, Duskendale and Rook's Rest;
- Crackclaw Point and Claw Isle at the north-east;
- Dragonstone and the lairs of the Dragonmont at the mouth of the bay;
- Driftmark, with Hull, High Tide and Spicetown, across the Gullet from Massey's Hook;
- the kingswood and the Wendwater on the south shore.

Its moment is 129 AC, the last year in which all of it stood at once. The Dragonpit's dome fell in 130, and High Tide and Spicetown burned that year [v].

**Its dragons.** Ten of the eleven have a moment on its water or its shores (section 6). Aegon's landing with Balerion, Vhagar and Meraxes. Balerion's year away and his return. Vermithor and Silverwing at the Golden Wedding and on their flight to Dragonstone. Meleys claimed in the Dragonpit, out of it at a coronation, and fallen at Rook's Rest. Vhagar claimed on Driftmark. Vermithor, Silverwing and Sheepstealer won in the Sowing and flown at the Gullet. The Cannibal in his lair. Caraxes over the Red Keep. Dreamfyre in the Dragonpit [v]. Only Terrax has no place in Westeros at all.

**What the Westeros tile already shows.** King's Landing and Dragonstone, at a third of the size and a century and a half later. It has none of Driftmark, High Tide, Hull, Spicetown, the Gullet, Rook's Rest, Duskendale, Rosby, Stokeworth, Crackclaw Point, Claw Isle or Massey's Hook. Nor has it the Dragonpit whole, its vaults, the Dragonkeepers, or the lairs in the Dragonmont.

**How it would be its own:**

- Its ground is a bay with islands in it, and nothing on the ground joins its shores. Hong Kong's harbour has a tunnel under it, and Westeros is one continent.
- Its age: the Dragonpit whole with its forty vaults; no Great Sept of Baelor, which was begun under Baelor I, who reigned from 161 to 171 AC [v]; the Red Keep new; High Tide with its silver roofs; and Spicetown, which was never rebuilt [v].
- Its scale: King's Landing takes a third of the board, not a corner of one region. The Dragonmont is a mountain of lairs, not a cone for one dragon to stand on.
- Its dragons are at home: they go into and out of their lairs and vaults, and a sheep waits for one at dawn.
- Its roads and its traffic (section 3): five small loops on five pieces of land, a causeway that the tide covers, and drovers with flocks for the dragons.

### C. From Old Valyria to the Dragonmont

**What it is.** The dragons' homes in order: the Valyrian Freehold before the Doom, the voyage west, Dragonstone the outpost, and the Dragonpit at the end.

**Its dragons.** This is the only framing in which Terrax is at home. The books name him once, in TWOIAF, as the dragon on which Jaenara Belaerys flew deep into Sothoryos, coming back to the Freehold after three years, before the Doom [v]. Balerion was hatched in Valyria, the last of the five dragons brought west [v]. That Aerea's year away on Balerion took her to Valyria is a guess of Septon Barth's, not a fact [v].

**What the Westeros tile already shows.** Only Dragonstone and the Dragonmont.

**How it would be its own.** Wholly. But the books give Valyria as legend and ruin, with few buildings described, so most of the tile would be invented, and none of the Dance is there. It is a tile for another day, if the dragons' origins are wanted. Not recommended now.

### The recommendation

**B, Blackwater Bay in 129 AC.** Ten of the eleven dragons have a moment on it, all three ages are there, and its centrepiece, the Dragonpit whole, is the one thing the Westeros tile cannot have: its own Dragonpit is a ruin a century and a half later.

Its risk is that it shares two places with the Westeros tile, King's Landing and Dragonstone. The answer is to build them from what the Westeros tile left out: the Dragonpit's dome, doors and vaults; Dragonstone's lairs, its hall and kitchens in the shapes of dragons, its garden and its town, and not its towers alone.

A variant, if a tile of one city is wanted: King's Landing alone in 129 AC, as dense as Delhi. It loses Dragonstone, Driftmark, Rook's Rest and the Gullet, and with them the lairs and four of the dragons' moments.

## 2. The things to find

Bearings are the books'. Distances are not, since the books give almost none: the board squeezes the bay as the Westeros tile squeezes the continent. Positions are on the board, x east and z south, as a first proposal [tile], and section 3 has the map. Everything that stands, stands as it was in 129 AC. Scenes from other years are marked with their year.

### King's Landing, on the west shore

1. **The Dragonpit, whole. The centrepiece.** A vast vaulted building under a great dome on the crown of the Hill of Rhaenys, one of the largest in the city [v; F&B, "Birth, Death, and Betrayal Under King Jaehaerys I"]. Benches round its sand hold about 80,000 [v; F&B, "The Year of the Three Brides—49 AC"]. Maegor began it after 42 AC on the site of the Sept of Remembrance, as a great stable of stone for dragons, and Jaehaerys finished it in 55 AC [v; F&B, "The Sons of the Dragon"; TWOIAF, "Maegor I"]. Dragons kept in it grew less large than those that lived free [v]. Where: in the city's north-east, north of the Red Keep [bk, for the hill's place in the city]; board (-62, -50). Toy: a drum about 44 across, its dome to 40 with a hill of 8, the tallest thing built [tile]. Period: 55 to 130 AC.
2. **The forty vaults.** In a ring beneath the dome, each closed by thick iron doors on both sides, with long brick-lined tunnels dug into the hill [v; F&B, "Jaehaerys and Alysanne—Their Triumphs and Tragedies"; "Rhaenyra Triumphant"]. A lair had a back door: the mob of 130 AC broke in through one [v]. So, a ring of dark iron doors in the hill's flank round the drum's foot, one or two of them open and warm inside at night [tile].
3. **The bronze doors.** The main gate, of bronze with some iron, wide enough for thirty knights to ride through abreast [v; ASOS ch. 8; F&B, "The Blacks and the Greens"]. On the drum's south-east side, facing the opening view, with a paved forecourt before them [tile: the books do not say which way they face].
4. **The Dragonkeepers.** An order of 77 warriors that Jaehaerys founded in 56 AC, after Aerea flew off on Balerion, to guard the dragons day and night in the Dragonpit and on Dragonstone. They wore black armour, and helms crested with a row of dragon scales that runs on down the back, smaller as it goes [v; F&B, "Birth, Death, and Betrayal"]. Figures at the doors, on the forecourt and at the vaults [tile]. Period: 56 to 130 AC.
5. **The Hill of Rhaenys**, named for Aegon's sister-queen [v]. The Sept of Remembrance stood on it until Maegor destroyed it in 42 AC [v; F&B, "The Sons of the Dragon"]. Only the hill and its name are shown, nothing of the sept or its end (section 8).
6. **The Street of the Sisters**, which runs between the Hill of Rhaenys and Visenya's Hill [v]. The drovers' way up to the Dragonpit [tile].
7. **The Red Keep on Aegon's High Hill**, in the city's south-east corner [v]. It was begun in 35 AC where Aegon's timber fort, the Aegonfort, was torn down [v; awoiaf "Red Keep"]. Pale red stone, drum towers, and Maegor's Holdfast within [bk, as in the Westeros notes]. New in 129 AC. Where: over the mouth of the Rush, about 55 south of the Dragonpit; board (-52, 6). Toy 30 with its hill of 8, lower than the Dragonpit [tile]. Whether its barbican and the White Sword Tower, which the Westeros tile left out, stood in 129 AC was not found.
8. **The Red Keep's outer yard**, where Balerion came down out of the east in 56 AC, after a year away [v; awoiaf "Balerion"]. Open ground inside the walls, large enough for a dragon [tile].
9. **Visenya's Hill**, west of the Street of the Sisters; board (-90, -12). There is no Great Sept in 129 AC. A grand sept of Aegon I's reign may have stood there, and what became of it is unknown [v]. So its crown is houses, or one plain sept drawn with care [tile].
10. **The walls and gates**, built from 20 to 26 AC [v]. The Rosby road leaves by the Iron Gate [v]. The Dragon Gate, the Lion Gate and the River Gate are the later books' names, confirmed for the Westeros tile [v]; the King's Gate, the Old Gate and the Gate of the Gods are from memory [bk]. Which names the gates had in 129 AC was not found.
11. **The mouth of the Blackwater Rush, and Aegon's strand.** Aegon landed at the river's mouth beside three hills in 2 BC, with fewer than 1,600 men, and raised the Aegonfort on the highest [v; F&B, "Aegon's Conquest"]. Quays and galleys along the river front [bk]. A small scene: three ships of the Conquest drawn up on a strand below the High Hill, their sails furled [tile]. Board (-36, 20).
12. **A drovers' pen inside the Iron Gate**, with sheep and cattle waiting for the Dragonpit [tile]. Dragons eat meat and fish and take food from keepers [v]. How the Dragonpit's dragons were fed was not found. Board (-44, -68), east of the Dragonpit's shadow.
13. **The Golden Wedding's procession.** On the seventh day of the seventh moon of 49 AC, after rites in the Dragonpit, the wedding party went in procession to the Red Keep [v; F&B, "The Year of the Three Brides"]. A small scene of banners and a crowd between the two hills. No one is portrayed [tile].

22. **The Dragonmont.** The volcano the island is made of, still active: pale grey steam rises from hot vents, and smoke from its peak [v; awoiaf "Dragonmont"]. Dragonglass lies deep inside it, black, green, purple and red [v; ASOS ch. 78], and is not seen. Where: the north of the island, about 150 east of the Dragonpit across the bay; summit at board (88, -30). A toy mountain 50 high with broad flanks for its lairs, not the Westeros tile's steep cone [tile].

24. **Vermithor's and Silverwing's caverns.** Vermithor stayed on Dragonstone after Jaehaerys died, and Silverwing went back there after Alysanne died. Each made its lair in one of the smoking caverns [v]. Two large mouths side by side on the south-west flank, facing the view [tile].
25. **Sheepstealer's cavern, and the sheep below it.** He laired in the caverns of the Dragonmont. He hatched when Jaehaerys was young, went unclaimed for decades, and was won in 129 AC by a girl of sixteen who left a freshly killed sheep for him every morning [v; F&B, "The Red Dragon and the Gold"]. ## 3. The ground and the roads

### The ground

- **Water.** The bay fills the middle of the board, brackish at best and salty at worst [v]. It opens east into the Narrow Sea between Dragonstone and Massey's Hook, and that is the water that runs to the board's edge, with the Rush from the west edge and the Wendwater from the south.
- **Islands.** Dragonstone at the mouth [v]. Driftmark inside it, west and south of Dragonstone [v]. High Tide's tidal islet [v]. Claw Isle off Crackclaw Point [v]. Rocks near the mouth of the Rush (item 50).
- **Relief.** King's Landing's three hills [v], at 8, 8 and 6 [tile]. The Dragonmont, a toy mountain of 50 [tile]. Crackclaw Point's hills [bk]. Driftmark low [v], and Massey's Hook low [bk].
- **A tide** [tile]. The flats round High Tide's causeway are covered and uncovered in a slow round of a few minutes. The engine's water has one level, so the tide is the tile's own, as the Westeros tile's kraken was: a second flat of water over the sands that rises and falls.

### The roads: five loops on five pieces of land

The pattern, which no other tile has: **every road keeps to its own land, and nothing on the ground joins one land to another.** Ships, a ferry and the dragons are all that cross the water [tile]. The Westeros tile is one tree of roads, and Hong Kong joins its two shores by a tunnel.

1. **King's Landing**: one loop inside the walls, up the Street of the Sisters to a ring on the Dragonpit's forecourt, down past the Red Keep's gate, and along the river front. The road a hero is seen best on in the city is the climb to the forecourt.
2. **The Rosby road**: from the Iron Gate along the north shore past Rosby, Stokeworth and Duskendale to Rook's Rest, where it turns in a ring before the castle's gate, and back. It shares a lane with the city's loop at the Iron Gate, the one place where two loops meet. A road along water, seen across the bay.
3. **Dragonstone**: from the harbour up through the castle's gate and out under the Dragon's Tail arch, round the south and west foot of the Dragonmont past the mouths of the lairs, and down to the harbour. The Westeros tile has no road on Dragonstone.
4. **Driftmark**: a ring round the island past Hull, Castle Driftmark, the shipyards and Spicetown, with the causeway to High Tide as a branch that ends in a ring in its court. Its south side runs along the Gullet at the front of the opening view: **this is the road worth watching.**
5. **The south shore**: a track through the kingswood from the ferry's landing, past the sheepfold, over the Wendwater by a ford, and back.

Traffic keeps left, as on the Westeros tile, since this is the same world, unless it is chosen otherwise [tile]. The roads are packed earth and setts, and on Dragonstone black stone [bk]. Every lane is 4 wide, as on the Westeros tile, since nothing wide drives them.

### What travels the roads

None of the Westeros tile's seven kinds of traveller (a rider at the gallop, a knight under a banner, two brothers of the Night's Watch, a cart, a haywain, a covered wain, a wheelhouse). This tile's own, each a `TrafficKind` [tile]:

- **A drover and his flock**: a man with a crook, a dog, and a dozen sheep packed round them, as one traveller. Going up to the Dragonpit's pen full, and back empty. Sheep are what the books' dragons are known to take [v].
- **A drover and three or four cattle**, the same way.
- **A litter**, curtained, on the shoulders of four bearers [bk: litters are in the books].
- **A string of pack mules** with bales of spice from Spicetown.
- **A fishwife's barrow**, from Hull's quay.

As on the Westeros tile, a hero would be the only car, if that choice holds here.

What else moves:

- The ferry over the Rush, on a line of its own.
- Ships on lines of their own: the Velaryon fleet in and out of the Gullet, fishing boats in the bay, and the Triarchy's galleys at anchor off the east edge. The Westeros tile sails a Lyseni galley, so these lie still in a line, many and small.
- The tide at High Tide.
- Steam from the Dragonmont's vents, and smoke from its peak [v].
- The dragons, whose looks and flight are the other note's.

Fire harms nothing, as on the Westeros tile, and goes only straight up, over open sea, and into the smoke over the Dragonmont's peak. It never goes over the city, a town, a ship or a sept [tile].

## 4. Places for heroes

Each opens to the south-east, has the whole car on ground of its own, and has nothing taller than a person between it and the view [tile].

1. **An open vault of the Dragonpit.** One of the forty, its iron door swung back in the hill's south-east flank, with a hero parked inside where a dragon would lie. The Dragonpit is forty garages for dragons. The best of these places. Board about (-44, -36).
2. **The forecourt before the bronze doors**, where thirty knights could ride abreast [v, the doors]. A hero on show before the doors, on the drum's south-east side so that the drum does not hide it.
3. **A lair on the Dragonmont**: a cave mouth on the south-west flank, lit inside, with a hero in it as a car stands in a garage cut into rock.
4. **The sands by High Tide's causeway**, at low tide: a hero on the wet sand, as on a beach, at the front of the view. If the tide is built, the place is the causeway's landward end and not the sand.
5. **The slip at the shipyards**: a hero on a slipway at Driftmark's east end, facing east like a ship about to be launched.
6. **Dragonstone's stone pier**, by the old inn with the gargoyle, facing south.
7. **Spicetown's quay**, among the bales from the east, facing south onto the Gullet.

None stands where the Sept of Remembrance stood, at Rook's Rest, or on the strand of the funeral.

## 5. The screen's picture

One screen, where the opening view sees it. Three ideas, each a picture of the place and none of them house words over a picture, which is the Westeros board's way:

1. **"GIVE WAY TO DRAGONS."** A road sign as a sign-painter of King's Landing would paint it: a red-bordered triangle with a black dragon coming in to land, the words in plain capitals beneath, and "by order of the Dragonkeepers" small at the foot. On a timber board at the edge of the Dragonpit's forecourt, facing the opening view, where the Street of the Sisters comes up. The wink: the toy cars have a sign to obey, and the dragons have the right of way [tile].
2. **"LOST: ONE SHEEP."** A shepherd's notice: a big white sheep, "Last seen leaving with a dragon", and "Reward. Ask at the fold." On the gable of a barn by the sheepfold on the Wendwater, or on Spicetown's market board, both at the front of the view [tile, after Sheepstealer's hunting, v].
3. **The Painted Table.** Aegon's table, a map of Westeros carved and painted, more than fifty feet long, in the top room of the Stone Drum [v; ASOS ch. 54]. Drawn as if seen from above, with three small dragons over Blackwater Bay, on a board at Dragonstone's harbour [tile]. The quietest of the three.

## 7. Names on boards

In the books, and safe to letter [v]: King's Landing, the Dragonpit, the Hill of Rhaenys, Visenya's Hill, Aegon's High Hill, the Red Keep, the Street of the Sisters, the Iron Gate, the Blackwater Rush, Blackwater Bay, Rosby, Stokeworth, Duskendale, Rook's Rest, Crackclaw Point, Claw Isle, Dragonstone, the Dragonmont, Driftmark, Hull, High Tide, Spicetown, the Gullet, Massey's Hook and the kingswood.

Where the books or the sources leave a name unsure:

- **The Hill of Rhaenys** is also written Rhaenys's Hill [v: both]. That it stands in the city's north-east, north of the Red Keep, is from memory of the books' city map [bk].
- **The gates.** The walls stood by 26 AC [v], but which names the gates had in 129 AC was not found. The Dragon Gate, the Lion Gate and the River Gate are confirmed for the later books [v]. The King's Gate, the Old Gate and the Gate of the Gods are from memory [bk].
- **Castle Driftmark** is the wiki's name for the old castle. The books call both the castle and the island Driftmark [bk], and a board can say Driftmark.
- **Hull** is on the island's north side, beneath the old castle, by the English wiki [v]. The Portuguese wiki also puts it on the south side once [v]. North is the better reading.
- **Spicetown** was never rebuilt after 130 AC [v], so it is on no map of the later books. Its board belongs to this tile's year.
- **Sharp Point**: both wikis put it at the eastern tip of Massey's Hook [v]. From memory I had it on Crackclaw Point. Check a map of the books before it is lettered.
- **Stonedance**, House Massey's seat on Massey's Hook, is from memory [bk].
- **The Wendwater**: the English wiki names it as Sheepstealer's hunting ground [v]. The Portuguese wiki has a river it calls Guaquevai running into the bay from the south [v]. That the two are one is my inference.
- **The Dragonmont**: the books say "the Dragonmont", and the Westeros tile's board says Dragonmont. Either will do.
- **The spears of the merling king** (item 50) are from memory of ACOK [bk]. No board until checked.
- **No board** for the Sept of Remembrance or the Aegonfort, which were gone by 129 AC [v], and none with the series' name.

Signs are in the Common Tongue, in plain lettering, as on the Westeros tile. Two arms were checked: the Targaryen red three-headed dragon on black [v, as in the Westeros note] and the Velaryon silver seahorse on sea green [v]. The arms of Staunton, Celtigar, Massey, Rosby, Stokeworth and Bar Emmon were not checked: plain colours only, or none.

**The tile's pin on the map.** Blackwater Bay is on the land the Westeros tile already draws on the map, on its east coast about two thirds of the way down [bk]. So this tile needs no land of its own: its `place` can be a point on that coast, worked out from `COAST` in the Westeros tile's `index.ts` [tile].

## 8. Care, and what is the series' and not the books'

### Care, as on the Westeros tile

- **Deaths and riots are not shows**, and nothing of them is drawn: the storming of the Dragonpit and Dreamfyre's death; Meleys's fall at Rook's Rest; the dead of the Sowing and of the Gullet; Rhaenyra's death on Dragonstone [v].
- **The Sept of Remembrance** and its end, burned in 42 AC while the Warrior's Sons held it [v], are not shown. If a sept is drawn on Visenya's Hill, it is drawn whole and quiet, with no fire near it.
- **No rider** is shown on a dragon, as on the Westeros tile: a rider would be a portrait of a character.

### What may be expected from the series and is not the books'

- **Dragonstone's castle.** The series' castle on a rock, reached by a long stair and a stone bridge, is its own, and so is the real place where it was filmed [series]. The books' castle is made of dragon shapes, over a town and a harbour [v].
- **The Dragonpit, the Red Keep, High Tide, the Driftwood Throne and the Painted Table.** The series' designs of all five are its own [series]. This tile takes only the books' words in section 2.
- **Laena's death.** In the series she dies in Vhagar's fire on Driftmark's shore [series]. In the books she bears a son who lives an hour, takes a fever, and dies trying to reach Vhagar, collapsing on her way [v; awoiaf "Differences between Fire & Blood and House of the Dragon"]. So there is no fire on the strand.
- **The Sowing.** The series stages it in a cave of the Dragonmont, with characters of its own [series, from the wiki's page on the episode]. In the books it was Jacaerys's call, four dragons were won, and Sheepstealer was won with a sheep every morning [v].
- **Meleys at the coronation.** Both tell it in the Dragonpit. How she came out in the books, from the vaults beneath, could not be read apart from the wiki's summary of the series' episode. Check F&B, "The Dying of the Dragons—The Blacks and the Greens", before a show is built on it.
- **Terrax** is a dragon neither of Fire & Blood nor of Westeros. The books' one mention of him is in TWOIAF [v].

### Liberties this note proposes [tile]

- The bay squeezed onto one board, its bearings kept and its distances not.
- One year, 129 AC, for everything that stands, with small scenes from 2 BC and 49 AC.
- The eleven dragons together, and Terrax as a visitor.
- The tide.
- The Dragonpit's doors facing south-east, and a vault and a lair as places for a hero.
- The Dragonmont 50 high with broad flanks, not a cone.

### Verified

- The Dragonpit: its dome, its bronze doors for thirty knights abreast, its 80,000 seats, its forty vaults with iron doors and its tunnels; begun by Maegor after 42 AC on the site of the Sept of Remembrance and finished in 55 AC; Balerion the first dragon in it, in 56 AC; Meleys claimed in it; the Golden Wedding's rites in it in 49 AC; Dreamfyre's death and the dome's fall in 130 AC.
- The Dragonkeepers: 77, founded in 56 AC, in black armour with scale-crested helms.
- King's Landing: the three hills and the Street of the Sisters; the Red Keep in the south-east corner on the Aegonfort's site, from 35 AC; the walls, from 20 to 26 AC; the Iron Gate and the Rosby road; the Great Sept begun under Baelor I; an earlier sept on Visenya's Hill whose fate is unknown.
- The Conquest: the landing at the Rush's mouth in 2 BC, Rosby and Stokeworth, Harrenhal and the Field of Fire.
- The bay: Dragonstone at its mouth; Driftmark west of it, low and fertile; the Gullet between Driftmark and Massey's Hook; Rook's Rest on the north shore; Crackclaw Point the north arm and Claw Isle off it; Massey's Hook the south arm with Sharp Point at its tip; the kingswood on the south shore; Duskendale the second port; the water brackish.
- Driftmark: Castle Driftmark and Hull on the north side; High Tide of pale stone with silver roofs on its tidal islet; Spicetown; the shipyards; the Driftwood Throne's legend; the Velaryon arms; the burning of 130 AC.
- Dragonstone: the Stone Drum, the Windwyrm, the Sea Dragon Tower, the hall and the kitchens as dragons, Aegon's Garden, the town and the pier; the Dragonmont active, with vents; its six lairs at the start of the Dance, with the Cannibal's and Grey Ghost's on its east side.
- The dragons' moments in section 6 marked [v], and Terrax's one mention, in TWOIAF.
- Laena's death as the books tell it and as the series does.
