# A. GERMANY

**1. Character.** A grey racetrack loops 20.8 km through dark forested hills, a castle ruin beside it. A blue-signed Autobahn runs dead straight across. Each city reads by one shape: twin Gothic spires at a river bridge, a ball-on-needle tower, glass car silos, a white turreted castle under the Alps. Old towns are red roofs and half-timber; the port is brick warehouses on canals.

**2. Ground.** Real map: Hamburg and the Elbe north; Wolfsburg (canal) and Berlin north-east; Cologne west on the Rhine; the Nürburgring in the Eifel hills south of it (u); Stuttgart south-west; Rothenburg mid-south; Munich south-east; Neuschwanstein on the southern edge at the Alps' foot. The Fairy Tale Route (600 km, Hanau to Bremen; Sababurg "Sleeping Beauty" castle in the Reinhardswald forest) crosses the wooded centre-north: a natural Grimm seam. Black Forest: south-west, towards France (u). Berlin has wide axial boulevards, Rothenburg forked lanes inside walls, Hamburg quays.

**3. Landmarks.**
1. *Nordschleife*: narrow grey track, red-white kerbs, clockwise; banked concrete Karussell; Nürburg castle (round keep on a cone) inside the south end (u). B258 runs beside the Döttinger Höhe straight, where the public entry barriers are; a road passes under at Breidscheid (u).
2. *Brandenburg Gate*: 12 columns, five passages, chariot on top, 26 m; pedestrians only, boulevards east and west. Reichstag (grey block, glass dome (u)) one block north.
3. *TV Tower*: 368 m concrete needle with steel ball; Alexanderplatz, about 2 km east of the Gate (u).
4. *Cologne Cathedral, Hohenzollern Bridge*: dark twin spires; three steel arches on the cathedral's axis, station between; trains and walkers only, no cars; love-lock fence.
5. *Neuschwanstein*: white walls, blue-grey cone turrets, on a crag; reached by carriage or shuttle, not car (u).
6. *Elbphilharmonie*: brick warehouse base, wave-topped glass crown, 108 m, on the Elbe; Speicherstadt warehouse rows beside it hold Miniatur Wunderland.
7. *Autostadt towers*: two 48 m glass cylinders, 400 cars each, fed by tunnel from the VW plant; four power-station chimneys beside, on the canal.
8. *BMW four-cylinder*: 101 m, four silver cylinders; bowl-shaped museum at its foot (u); BMW Welt across the road; Olympic park behind.
9. *Stuttgart*: Mercedes-Benz Museum (clover-leaf drum at the factory gate); Porsche Museum (angular body on three legs; three 911s on stalks on the roundabout (u)).
10. *Plönlein*: thin yellow half-timbered house at a street fork (u); Siebers tower left, Kobolzell gate right; lanes pass through both.

**4. Roads and car culture.**
- Lap order: Sabine-Schmitz-Kurve, Hatzenbach, Hocheichen, Quiddelbacher Höhe, Flugplatz, Schwedenkreuz, Aremberg, Fuchsröhre, Adenauer Forst, Metzgesfeld, Kallenhard, Wehrseifen, Breidscheid (lowest), Ex-Mühle, Bergwerk, Kesselchen, Klostertal, Karussell, Hohe Acht (highest), Wippermann, Eschbach, Brünnchen, Pflanzgarten (jumps), Stefan-Bellof-S, Schwalbenschwanz, Kleines Karussell, Galgenkopf, Döttinger Höhe, Antoniusbuche, Tiergarten, Hohenrain.
- Public sessions, 2026: no booking; €30 a lap Mon–Thu, €35 Fri–Sun and holidays; road law applies (keep right, pass left only); no timing, drifting or racing; cars must manage 130 km/h; no vans, pick-ups, motorhomes; no motorcycles since 2025. A Koblenz court (June 2026, not final) held the 130 km/h advisory speed applies.
- Events: 24h race (May), Rock am Ring, Truck Grand Prix, Oldtimer Grand Prix, DTM; centenary fan weekend 19–20 June 2027; Essen Motor Show 28 Nov–6 Dec 2026.
- Autobahn: white-on-blue signs; no general limit, advisory 130. End-of-limits sign: white disc, five thin black diagonal stripes. Brown landmark boards; rescue lane in jams (u).

**5. Businesses.** Aral (blue-and-white diamond sign (u); about 2,400 stations); ADAC (yellow patrol vans) (u); Hofbräuhaus Munich (gabled inn, crowned "HB") (u); Käthe Wohlfahrt, Rothenburg (year-round Christmas shop, parcel-laden vintage van outside; current trading (u)); Sparkasse (red S with dot) (u); Deutsche Post (yellow, black horn) (u); Miniatur Wunderland. Parody: "Bäckerei Brezel & Gretel", "Autohaus Vollgas & Söhne", "Gasthaus Zum Goldenen Kolben".

**6. Street life.** Taxis ivory (RAL 1015), though six states, Baden-Württemberg and Rhineland-Palatinate among them, allow any colour; police blue-and-silver with neon yellow (u); yellow post vans and boxes (u); white ICE trains with a red stripe; yellow Berlin buses and trams (u); stop signs: white U on blue, white S on green, green H on yellow (u); yellow town-name boards, advertising columns, wrought-iron inn signs in Fraktur (u); the hatted East German crossing-light man in Berlin.

**7. Small scenes.** Queue at the Nordschleife barrier; a hatchback tipping into the Karussell; a glass lift raising one car in an Autostadt tower; a couple clipping a lock to the Hohenzollern Bridge as a train passes; Christmas-market huts round a church, or a beer garden under chestnuts (u); in the forest, donkey, dog, cat and cockerel stacked, a gingerbread house, a tower with a braid.

**8. Watch out.** Cologne Cathedral has charged tourists €12 since 1 July 2026. Techno Classica left Essen for Dortmund (Sept 2026); Essen's spring show is now Retro Classics. The Nürburgring's ring°werk is being repurposed. Galeria department stores filed for insolvency again (Oct 2026). Miniatur Wunderland opened a rainforest section (Nov 2026) in a second block, reached by a bridge over the canal. Neuschwanstein: UNESCO since 12 July 2025; the name is a Bavarian state trademark (upheld 2018). Car badges, the Nürburgring name and outline (u) and Ampelmann goods are protected: use look-alikes. Show present-day Germany: no Nazi-era symbols (illegal there) (u), nothing making light of the Wall.

# B. SCOTLAND

**1. Character.** A grey castle on a black crag, an old town running down the ridge behind it. Three bridges abreast over a firth, one of them red. Beyond, emptiness: brown-green treeless mountains, long narrow lochs, one thin road with passing bays, a curved viaduct carrying a steam train. Grey stone, slate roofs, white distillery sheds with pagoda vents.

**2. Ground.** Edinburgh, south-east: Castle Rock at the west; the Royal Mile drops one mile east along a ridge (109 m to 42 m) to Holyrood; Arthur's Seat (251 m) a mile east of the castle; railway in the valley north, bridged to Princes Street. Forth bridges 9 miles west; Falkirk midway to Glasgow on the Clyde (u). The A82 runs north: Loch Lomond, Glencoe, Fort William, Loch Ness, Inverness (u). Glenfinnan lies west of Fort William, Eilean Donan north-west towards Skye, John o' Groats at the north-east tip (u). Hadrian's Wall (73 miles, coast to coast) is wholly in England, on the Liverpool side, and was never the border.

**3. Landmarks.**
1. *Edinburgh Castle*: grey walls on a cliff-sided rock; one approach, from the east, up the Royal Mile to the Esplanade.
2. *Forth Bridge*: red steel, three diamond cantilever towers, 2.5 km long, 110 m high; trains only. West of it: Forth Road Bridge (suspension; buses, taxis, cycles and walkers, no cars), then Queensferry Crossing (cable-stayed; M90) (order and types u).
3. *Glenfinnan Viaduct*: 21 pale concrete arches on a curve, 380 m long, 30 m high; train over, river under; monument and Loch Shiel below, where the A830 passes (u).
4. *Eilean Donan*: grey keep on a tidal islet where three lochs meet; arched stone footbridge; A87 on the shore (u).
5. *Urquhart Castle*: ruin on a headland on Loch Ness's north-west shore, five-storey tower stump; A82 directly above.
6. *Glencoe, Ben Nevis*: east–west glen, Three Sisters buttresses on the south wall, pyramid of Buachaille Etive Mòr at the east mouth; A82 through it. Ben Nevis (1,345 m (u)) humps over Fort William.
7. *Kelpies*: two 30 m stainless-steel horse heads, one bowed, one tossed up (u), beside the M9 and a canal. *Falkirk Wheel*, a few miles west (u): 35 m two-armed rotor lifting boats 24 m to an aqueduct.
8. *Finnieston Crane*: 53 m hammerhead crane, 46 m jib, on the Clyde's north bank in Glasgow.
9. *Hadrian's Wall*: low stone wall along crags; milecastles (small gated squares) every Roman mile (u), two turrets between; B6318 Military Road alongside. Sycamore Gap, by Milecastle 39: tree felled 28 Sept 2023; the fenced stump has a ring of shoots 2–3 ft tall (Sept 2026).
10. *John o' Groats*: white multi-armed signpost (free public one since 2013) where the road ends (u).

**4. Roads and car culture.**
- North Coast 500: 516 miles, launched 2015, starts and ends at Inverness Castle (reopened as a visitor attraction 9 Feb 2026). Clockwise: Muir of Ord, Bealach na Bà, Applecross, Gairloch, Ullapool, Scourie, Durness, Thurso, John o' Groats, Wick, Dunrobin Castle, Dingwall.
- Bealach na Bà: single-track, sea level to about 626 m, gradients near 20%, Alpine-style hairpins; often impassable in winter; unsuitable for learners and very large vehicles.
- Single-track rule: pull into a passing place on your left, or wait opposite one on your right; give way uphill; never park in one.
- Glen Etive side road (Skyfall); Rest and Be Thankful (A83; landslide closures; old military road, once a hillclimb (u)).
- Knockhill, Fife: 2.04 km, raced in both directions, Duffus Dip to Taylor's hairpin; touring cars, superbikes. Bo'ness hill climb (1932, Scotland's first purpose-built venue; yearly revival). Riverside Museum, Glasgow (Zaha Hadid; Scottish-built Argyll, Arrol-Johnston, Hillman Imp). Jim Clark museum, Duns (u).

**5. Businesses.** Tunnock's (Uddingston; red-and-gold wafer stripes); Irn-Bru (A.G. Barr; orange drink; 125 years in 2026); Walker's Shortbread (Aberlour; tartan packs); Barrowland Ballroom (Glasgow; big animated neon sign); Green Welly Stop (Tyndrum; road-trip halt since 1965); Deacon Brodie's Tavern, Royal Mile (u); Applecross Inn (u); Strathisla distillery, Keith (twin pagodas) (u). Parody: "The Drookit Coo" (pub), "Glen Dreich Distillery", "Bonnie Banger Motors".

**6. Street life.** Black cabs (u); Lothian buses madder red and white; Edinburgh trams white and red (u); ScotRail dark blue with a white saltire (u); small Glasgow Subway trains, traditionally orange (new fleet 2024); Police Scotland half-Battenburg checks, some lettered in Gaelic too; red pillar and phone boxes with the Scottish crown, no royal cypher; black-white-red CalMac ferries (u); bilingual Gaelic road signs, white "Passing Place" signs, snow poles, cattle grids (u); setts and narrow closes off the Royal Mile.

**7. Small scenes.** Steam train on the viaduct, photographers on the hillside (u); shaggy ginger Highland cows holding up two cars at a passing place; a statue wearing a traffic cone, Glasgow (u); a piper on the Esplanade (u); a camper van meeting a sports car on a Bealach hairpin; three humps in Loch Ness beside a tour boat.

**8. Watch out.** Model Sycamore Gap as a stump. Jenners department store shut in 2021; still being converted to a hotel. North Bridge reopened 31 July 2026. Arthur's Seat: wildfire July 2026; Radical Road path shut since 2018. Jacobite: 2026 season ran 1 June–23 Oct after a door-locking dispute (press reports); 2027 dates unpublished. Glencoe's white roadside cottage Allt-na-Reigh is reported demolished. The Finnieston Crane carried the "Glasgow Rose" artwork for the 2026 Commonwealth Games (still up? (u)). Forth Road Bridge no longer takes cars. Rights: Kelpies copyright stays with sculptor Andy Scott's company; "NC500" is a company brand; Harry Potter and Game of Thrones belong to Warner (u), so use a plain steam train and ice wall. The Glencoe massacre (1692), Culloden and the Clearances are not joke material (u).
