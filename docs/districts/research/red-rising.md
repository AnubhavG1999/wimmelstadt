# Red Rising for the tile

This note is for a tile from Pierce Brown's Red Rising saga, drawn from its six novels: Red Rising, Golden Son, Morning Star, Iron Gold, Dark Age and Light Bringer. Read on 2026-11-20.

**What the collector asked for, on 2026-11-20:**

- an Iron Rain, falling on part of a planet;
- in it, the saga's iconic figures: Darrow the Reaper, the Sovereign, Aja au Grimmus, Lorn au Arcos, Alexandar au Arcos, Sevro, Ragnar, and as many more as are truly recognisable;
- small stories for them, as the dragons have on Blackwater Bay;
- a look that is spectacular.

So the note is built round that:

1. the Iron Rains of the novels, and which one to show;
2. the scene: part of Mars, the fleet, and the city it falls on;
3. the cast, in three tiers;
4. their stories;
5. the screen;
6. what to leave out or soften;
7. the tile's place on the map;
8. questions for the collector;
9. the first research, kept with less weight in two appendices: Hyperion on Luna, the place this note first recommended, and the other places weighed.

**Named characters.** [building-a-tile.md](../../building-a-tile.md) says a place from a book "portrays no character", and the Westeros and Blackwater Bay tiles keep to that. This tile portrays named characters because the collector asked for them. It is his decision, and it belongs in the tile's `NOTES.md` as its first liberty. Everything else here keeps the rule that a place from a book is drawn from the books alone.

**What the tile takes nothing from:**

- the Sons of Ares comics or their audio dramas;
- The Book of Lorn, a serial of prose and comic pages published since September 2025 through the Patreon of the author's merchandise company [w The Book of Lorn]. No other novella was found;
- the board game;
- the covers or any official art;
- the fan wiki's pictures.

The seventh novel, Red God, had no publication date as of August 2026 [s booksofbrilliance.com, 7 August 2026].

## How sure each statement is

- **[w Page; book ch.]**: from that page of the Red Rising fan wiki (red-rising.fandom.com), which gives the book and chapter shown. The books themselves were not opened. **Unless a second source is named, the statement rests on that single fan-wiki page.**
- **[w Page, uncited]**: the wiki says it without naming a book. Treat it as unverified. Many of the characters' looks are of this kind; the cast tables say which.
- **[x]**: the wiki's source is not one of the six novels: the Sons of Ares comics or their audio dramas, The Book of Lorn, or the author's posts and interviews. Not to be used. (Most of the characters' exact heights in feet and inches come from one of his Reddit answers, so they are [x]. Ragnar's and the Ash Lord's are in the books.)
- **[s]**: a web search's summary of a page I could not open. Weak.
- **[bk]**: my own memory of the books, not confirmed today. Check it before building.
- **[real]**: a fact about the real Moon, Mars, Earth or old maps, from memory.
- **[tile]**: this note's proposal. It is not in the books.
- **Silent**: the books give no look for it, as far as the wiki reports them. It has to be invented.

**Caveat on sources, read first.**

- Fandom refused the plain fetcher (HTTP 402), as it did for the Westeros notes.
- The wiki's public interface (`api.php`) opened in the desktop app's browser pane. It returned about ninety pages, each with its footnotes citing novel and chapter. Among them:
  - the wiki's own long account of the Lion's Rain, chapter by chapter through Golden Son 35 to 43;
  - the pages of every figure below.
- No second source could be read for the details:
  - Google Books refused its search (HTTP 429, no quota);
  - the publisher's excerpt pages did not load;
  - web searches for the Iron Rain's chapters, the Occular Sphere and the Circada Maxima found only the wiki or general plot summaries.
- So nearly every fact rests on one fan wiki. Where the wiki's source is the comics or The Book of Lorn, the fact is marked [x] and left out.

Book abbreviations: **RR** Red Rising, **GS** Golden Son, **MS** Morning Star, **IG** Iron Gold, **DA** Dark Age, **LB** Light Bringer. **PCE** is the books' count of years after the Conquering.

## 1. The Iron Rain

An Iron Rain is the books' word for landing an army from orbit (RR 29) [w Iron Rain]:

- warships fire Golds and Obsidians in starShell armour from spitTubes, with hivepods of twelve Grays among them;
- the first wave takes landing sites, the next waves take the cities;
- the fleet bombards chosen targets from orbit (GS 38, 41; DA 10–13).

The great Rains are given names afterwards (MS 2; DA 40; LB 55) [w Iron Rain]. Lorn's saying, "When falls the Iron Rain, be brave", stands at the head of Golden Son's third part [w Iron Rain; GS Part III].

### How an Iron Rain looks, as the books give it

Everything in this section comes from the wiki's account of the chapters named. The books' own pictures of the fire and the trails could not be read today. Where this section says how they would look, it is marked [tile].

**The fleet overhead.**

- Over Apollonia, the Augustan and Bellona warships fire missiles at each other (GS 38) [w Lion's Rain]:
  - ripWings and wasps chase each other between them;
  - carriers and destroyers send out leechCraft and troop carriers in waves;
  - railguns and missiles tear holes in both lines;
  - both sides aim at engines, to cripple ships rather than destroy them.
- The Pax, Darrow's flagship, is a dreadnought five kilometres long (GS 22), with at least eighty railguns and eight engines (MS 45) [w Pax (Ship)]. Its forward hangar is 600 m long, with hundreds of spitTubes in rows along the port wall and causeways for starShells between them (GS 37) [w Pax (Ship)]. Its hull's colour: **silent**.
- The Warchild, the Bellona flagship, is painted blue and silver like the rest of the Bellona fleet (GS 38) [w Warchild].
- Victra's destroyer, hollowed out to carry ten times its leechCraft, runs at the Warchild and lets forty of them go. They latch onto its hull, and the Telemanus giants board (GS 38) [w Lion's Rain; w Warchild].

**The launch.**

- A spitTube is a railgun for people. Soldiers in starShells are fed in by hydraulic teeth, wait behind its magnetic stream, and are fired at six to ten times the speed of sound (GS 20; IG prologue) [w SpitTube].
- **The shell itself.** A starShell is armour four metres tall and about three tonnes (DA 11): like a shelled golem with long ape-like arms (IG 53). It flies on built-in gravBoots and shoulder thrusters (DA 10) [w StarShell].
- **Dressed for the fall.** To drop through air it wears two extra layers: panels that cut friction and burn away on the way down, and a clumsy outer armour against the heat, shed after landing (GS 38) [w StarShell].
- Hivepods of twelve elite Grays fall with the shells (GS 38) [w Iron Rain].

**The fall.**

- Once through the upper air, gunfire from the ground carves holes in the swarm (GS 38) [w Lion's Rain]. The falling soldiers fire their gravBoots and break into a thousand squadrons, each to its own landing point.
- Bellona ripWings chase them down into the air, where the fighters are slower and clumsier than in space, and are shot down.
- The leading wave dives through the clouds and streaks low over the ocean. Two waterships fire up at it, and missiles from a starShell answer.
- A second wave of starShells pierces the atmosphere while the first is still knocking out the ground's cannons (GS 38).
- How the falling shells look from below: **silent**. [tile: each a bright point with a trail, flaring as its friction panels burn and flake away. In clear air the trails fan out where the boots fire.]

**From above.** Darrow flies up through the clouds to call his fleet over the noise of a thunderstorm coming from the east (GS 38) [w Lion's Rain]. Above the cloud lie the ships; below it, the storm and the guns.

**The landing.**

- Darrow's group comes down on a snow-covered mountain east of Thessalonica, 300 km from its target (GS 38) [w Lion's Rain]. They fight north past missile turrets, gunships and drones, calling down strikes from orbit.
- The next wave lands in the valley to the north, at the Institute, which has been seized as a staging post (GS 38, 39).
- A landing is hard. In the Rain on Mercury a fallen starShell pins Lysander to the ground, and another lands beside him with its boots blazing (DA 16) [w Lysander au Lune].
- What a landing does to the ground: **silent** beyond that. [tile: a ring of dust and a scorched mark, out of which the shell stands up, splits its outer armour like a husk and steps out lighter.]

**On the ground.** Behind the starShell knights come (GS 39) [w Lion's Rain]:

- Grays on speederbikes;
- spider tanks;
- dropships carrying men closer to the walls;
- columns of tanks and of infantry in hover skiffs;
- Augustan ripWings clearing the way overhead.

A cloud of drones rises from a depot in the canyon wall (GS 39). Over Agea a defensive shield covers the city. Its generators stand in a park, and when they are blown it shatters as its nexuses overload one by one (GS 41).

### Which Iron Rain the tile shows: the Lion's Rain over Agea

**The Lion's Rain, at the hour it reaches Agea.** It is the only Rain the novels follow from the fleet all the way down into the streets of a city, and every reason points to it:

- **Recognised.** It is the saga's own Iron Rain: the one that gave the words their fame, called by the Reaper and named for the Augustan lion.
- **It falls on a city.** Agea has the roads a tile needs. The books give its walls, its river under the walls, its towers, its Citadel and landing pads, its shield, and its canyon (section 2).
- **The cast is there.** Darrow, Sevro and the Howlers, Ragnar, Virginia and Lorn come down in it or attack with it, and the Sovereign, Aja, Karnus, Fitchner and Cassius defend the city (GS 38–42). Of the collector's named figures only Alexandar is missing, a boy in 742 (section 3).
- **It is Mars**, the planet the saga is named for.
- **It is spectacular in the books' own terms**: a fleet battle overhead, a rain of armoured knights, a ground column, a river ambush, a gate opened, a shield shattered, and a shuttle chase from the Citadel.

The other Rains are the wrong ground or are told rather than shown:

- Mercury's two are on a continent of mountains and waste;
- the Wolf Rain is told, not shown;
- the Rain on Phobos lands on a small moon.

## 2. The scene: part of Mars, the fleet, and Agea

### The planet

**Its colour.** Seen from orbit, the terraformed Mars of the books is green and white, with plants and clouds (GS 36) [w Mars]. Its old red has mostly gone (GS 36), so the planet the Rain falls on is green, white and blue, with red only where the ground is still bare. That is a surprise worth keeping.

**Its surface where the Rain falls.**

- The Rain launches over the continent of Apollonia (GS 38) [w Lion's Rain].
- Apollonia lies across the Thermic Sea from Cimmeria (DA 43) [w Mars].
- The fall passes over open ocean (GS 38) and lands on snowy mountains east of Thessalonica (GS 38). Thessalonica is a city on the eastern shore of the Thermic Sea, south and west of Agea [w Mars].
- Agea itself lies in the middle of the Valles Marineris, the solar system's greatest canyon (GS 39) [w Agea].
- The Institute's valley is in an upper arm of the same canyon, 80 km east (GS 39) [w The Institute].
- The southern continents' highlands carry a great conifer forest, the Martian taiga (GS 47) [w Mars].
- On the day, a thunderstorm comes in from the east (GS 38) [w Lion's Rain].
- In the real Mars the Valles Marineris runs east and west just south of the equator [real]. The books keep the name and the place.

**How to show part of a planet** [tile]. Three ways, best first:

1. **A dome of planet.** The tile's ground rises gently to a crown and falls away at the edges, a slice off the top of a globe, so that the city stands on a curve.
   - The canyon of the Valles Marineris cuts across the crown, west to east.
   - The edge of the dome carries a band of cloud and the thin, pale blue glow of the atmosphere, with green land, a strip of the sea and white snow on the far mountains showing through.
   - The roads still lie on the slopes. The engine already has sloping ground.
   - The curve is a toy's, much stronger than any real horizon, as the tiles' mountains are toys.
2. **A globe in the sky.** A small globe of Mars on a brass arm above the north-east corner, with the fleet round it and the Rain's trails running from the fleet down onto the tile. It is spectacular, but it puts the planet in two places.
3. **A curved horizon behind the north edge.** A slice of the globe seen edge on, standing up like a backdrop. It would hide the tile that stands behind it on the board, so it is not recommended.

### The fleet overhead [tile, after GS 38]

The fleet hangs on slim brass arms at about 120 to 160 above the tile, in two lines with the Rain between them:

- **The Augustan line, east:** the Pax, long and dark, with its rows of spitTube mouths lit, and the next wave going out of them.
- **The Bellona line, west:** the Warchild and her consorts in blue and silver.
- **Between them:** ripWings and wasps on looping lanes; Victra's destroyer on its run, with leechCraft flying from it to the Warchild; and flashes of railgun and missile fire.

The real books give the scale: a dreadnought four to six kilometres long [w Dreadnought; partly comics], a torchShip 500 to 800 m [w TorchShip, uncited]. As toys, the Pax is about 70 long and the others smaller [tile]. Every ship's colour except the Bellona blue and silver is **silent**.

### Agea and the canyon: the things to find

What the novels say, with the chapters the wiki gives [w Agea unless named]. Much of the wiki's Agea comes from the comics and The Book of Lorn: its Heritage District, Hadrian Park, the opera, the duelling clubs and its Forum. Those are marked [x] and left out. Positions are a first proposal [tile], with x east and z south.

**The canyon**

1. **The Valles Marineris.** Agea sits in its middle (GS 39). Its walls hold factories, hangars and barracks for the city's reserve garrisons (GS 41), and a depot from which drones rise (GS 39) [w Lion's Rain]. On the tile:
   - the north wall is the board's north edge, cut with hangar mouths and lit windows;
   - the south side is a low rim, so the view is not blocked;
   - the canyon's floor carries the highway.
   The walls' colour: **silent** [tile: banded rust and ochre rock under green growth, as a terraformed planet's would be].
2. **The Institute's gate building**, cut into the canyon wall just outside the city, with an open marble square before its gates, pillars on either side, looking out over Agea and the canyon (RR 16, 18) [w The Institute]. North-west, high in the wall: (-70, -100).
3. **The Institute's valley and Olympus**, up an arm of the canyon 80 km east (GS 39) [w The Institute].
   - Olympus is an artificial mountain floating two kilometres up, with a bone-pale marble citadel on top (RR 42) [w The Institute].
   - In the Lion's Rain the Institute was seized as a staging post. Ragnar and Proctor Jupiter took it, and Darrow's people met at Olympus (GS 38, 39) [w Lion's Rain].
   On the tile it is the canyon's north-east branch, with Olympus floating over it at about 60 up, small and far, as a toy mountain [tile].
4. **The thunderstorm from the east** (GS 38). A grey cloud bank on the east edge, with lightning, through which the second wave falls [tile].

**The city**

5. **Agea**, the capital of Mars and the seat of House Augustus, a city of twenty million in 742 (GS 39). Life there is fast and cheap (RR 16).
6. **The towers.** Kilometre-high skyscrapers of steel and glass, which by night look like a garden of jewelled blades (RR 16; GS 41). Board: the north half of the city, 90 to 130 high [tile].
7. **The north wall and the south wall.** Perimeter walls 200 m high close the city's north and south borders, with lakes and rivers running under them through thick durosteel grates (GS 39). Board: the north wall at z -70, about 25 high; the south wall low, at the south edge [tile].
8. **The north gates.** Ragnar's Obsidians open them from inside for the Augustan infantry (GS 41) [w Lion's Rain]. Board (-20, -70) [tile].
9. **The river and its grates.** The river flows into the city under the north wall. The Sons of Ares spent the night before the Rain drilling through the grates (GS 37, 39) [w Lion's Rain]. Inside the wall it runs between mud banks (GS 39, 40). Board: from the north wall at x 40, south through the city to the south wall [tile].
10. **The forest inside the north wall**, which separates the wall from the commercial district (GS 41). A belt of trees at z -60 [tile].
11. **The commercial district**, south of the north wall (GS 41). The towers.
12. **The lowColor districts**, farther south (GS 41). Low, dense blocks [tile].
13. **Noise bubbles** keep each block's sound in (RR 16). **Silent** as to look [tile: faint domes of shimmer over a few blocks].
14. **Rooftop nightclubs**, such as Tryst and Temptation (RR 16). Tryst has gravity floors that let its dancers float (RR 15) [w Technology]. A club on a tower's roof with floating dancers [tile].
15. **The shield.** A defensive forcefield over the city, with its generators in a park on the far side of the city from Darrow's way in. Virginia blows them, and the shield shatters as its nexuses overload one by one (GS 41) [w Lion's Rain]. Its look: **silent** [tile: a faint dome over the city, about 100 high, with four generator pylons in a park at the east]. The centrepiece of story 5.
16. **The spaceport**, on the south edge of the city (LB 14). Optional.

**The Citadel**

17. **The Citadel**, the government's seat, a cluster of white and red spires 15 km south of the north wall, with lion statues for House Augustus (GS 41). It is the centrepiece. Board (-60, 10), its spires 60 to 90 high, the tallest the city's own [tile]. The spires' shapes: **silent**.
18. **Its outer wall** (GS 41) [w Lion's Rain], with a decorative wall and parks where Golds and Silvers take afternoon tea (GS 41).
19. **Its landing pads**, large enough for a hundred craft (GS 41). Octavia's shuttle and three decoys stand here in the Rain (GS 41) [w Lion's Rain]. Board (-30, 30), open to the south-east [tile].
20. **The bunker** under the Citadel, where the Sovereign waits out the battle (IG 40) [w Lion's Rain]. A shut door [tile].
21. **The Field of Mars**, a plaza of white stone at the entrance to the Citadel's grounds, where Triumph parades end (GS 51). An obelisk was raised there to Darrow's Triumph and destroyed later (MS 8). Board (-50, 50) [tile].
22. **Eo's memorial** on the Citadel's grounds, raised after the Republic freed the city (DA 18). It is a memorial of a later year: leave it out of the Rain's hour, or draw it with care.

**Under the city** (optional, a cut-away)

23. **A mine.** Each of Mars's thousand cities is tied to a hundred helium-3 mines underground (RR 8, 9) [w Mars], which justifies a small cut-away at the south edge [tile]:
   - a tiered round pit like Lykos's Common, with homes in its walls (RR 2, 3) [w Lykos];
   - a gravLift shaft;
   - a clawDrill, the saga's best-known machine. It is about 90 m long and shaped like a hand, with the cockpit at the elbow and tentacle-like drills for fingers (RR 1; MS 46) [w ClawDrill].

### Roads, traffic and places for heroes [tile]

**Roads.**

- **The canyon highway** runs along the canyon floor from the west edge, past the hangar mouths, into the city by the north gate, and on up the north-east branch towards the Institute. **This is the road worth watching:** the Augustan ground column drives it in the books (GS 39).
- Agea's avenues form a grid inside the walls.
- A loop climbs to the Citadel's landing pads and runs round the Field of Mars.
- A road runs along the river's east bank.

**Traffic.** The tile's own kinds, after GS 39:

- spider tanks;
- speederbikes;
- hover skiffs carrying infantry;
- dropships, flying low over the column.

All their looks are **silent**. The wiki's gravBike, which floats half a metre up on thrusters [w Ships and Vehicles, uncited], can serve for the speederbikes. They drive in convoy towards the north gate, and the heroes drive among them.

**Places for heroes:**

1. a landing pad at the Citadel, beside the Sovereign's shuttle;
2. the Field of Mars;
3. the marble square before the Institute's gate, looking out over the city;
4. a hangar mouth in the canyon wall;
5. a rooftop club's deck;
6. a place in the column on the canyon highway, in convoy.

Each opens to the south-east.

## 3. The cast

### Times and sides

The figures lived at different times and fought on different sides:

- Lorn dies at the Triumph after the Lion's Rain (GS 51) [w Lorn au Arcos].
- Aja dies in the bunker under the Citadel of Light on Luna (MS 62) [w Aja au Grimmus].
- Ragnar dies on the ice of Mars's south (MS 29–30) [w Ragnar Volarus].
- Alexandar is a boy in 742. He watched Lorn teach Darrow in secret (IG 45), and is grown only in Iron Gold and Dark Age (IG 16, 27; DA 59–60, 80) [w Alexandar au Arcos].
- Sefi, Holiday, Thraxa, Lysander's generation and the Ash Lord's family belong to the later books.

Gathering them in one scene is the tile's liberty, as the eleven dragons together are Blackwater Bay's. The tile is the Iron Rain itself, not one day in 742.

**How the scene holds them without a muddle** [tile]:

- **Two levels, two sides.** Everyone who attacks comes from above: the fleet, the falling shells, the Rain. Everyone who defends holds the ground, the walls and the Citadel.
  - The attackers wear the Reaper's red and the Howlers' wolf-grey, under the Augustan lion.
  - The defenders wear the Bellona blue and silver (GS 38) and the Praetorians' purple and black (GS 11).
- **Later figures stand where their own Rains put them.**
  - Republic figures fall with the attackers: Holiday, Thraxa, Alexandar, Orion on a bridge, and Sefi on her griffin.
  - Society figures of the later books hold the walls with the defenders: Atalantia, the Ash Lord, Atlas, Kalindora, Lysander and Apollonius.
  - Nobody stands with the side they never fought on.
- **One stage at a time.** Each story plays on its own small stage and lights up in turn, as the dragons' moments do (section 4). The rest of the cast stands in place, so the eye follows one fight at a time.
- **One board name.** The plate names the tile for the Rain, not for a year.

### Must have

**Darrow, the Reaper.**

- **Body:** large and tall even for a Gold, broad in the chest, golden hair and eyes after his carving [w Darrow O'Lykos, uncited]. Virginia saw him as all sharp angles, as if carved from marble in anger [w Darrow O'Lykos, uncited]. His exact height comes only from the author's answer on Reddit [x].
- **Marks:** a skull tattoo on his right hand, from Ragnar, for their blood brotherhood [w Darrow O'Lykos, uncited; it is in MS by the wiki's Sevro and Ragnar pages].
- **Armour:** in battle, pulseArmor painted blood red [w Darrow O'Lykos, uncited]. In the Lion's Rain he falls in a starShell (GS 38). An EMP kills his armour at the river, and he runs the last two kilometres to the landing pads without it (GS 40, 41) [w Lion's Rain].
- **Weapons:**
  - a bone-white razor with a salt-stained calf-leather grip, a parting gift from Virginia (GS 1; MS 3). He wears it wrapped round his forearm (GS 18, 28) and sets its blade to the curve of a slingBlade [w Darrow O'Lykos]. Sevro later engraves it with scenes of his family (MS 3).
  - His sign is the slingBlade: the curved, half-moon blade of a Helldiver of the mines (RR 1, 2), which he carved into the doors of Castle Mars (RR 35).
  - He took the name Reaper from a grain scythe at the Institute (RR 21) [w Darrow O'Lykos].
- **In the scene:** first out of the Pax's spitTubes, then at the river, in the streets and on the landing pads.

**The Sovereign, Octavia au Lune.**

- **Look:** an ageless face, kept young by cellular rejuvenation, with the Peerless scar on her right cheek its only flaw (RR 2) [w Octavia au Lune]. The Peerless scar runs along the right cheekbone (RR 5) [w Peerless Scarred].
- **Her clothes:** **silent**.
- **Her sceptre:** the Dawn Scepter, a staff a metre long of gold and iron, tipped with the Society's pyramid (DA 30, 31) [w Dawn Scepter].
- **Her guard:** the Praetorians, in purple and black (GS 11) [w Praetorian Guard].
- **Her house:** **silent** as to sign and colour.
- **In the Rain:** she waits in a bunker under Agea's Citadel with Aja, Fitchner and Karnus (IG 40). When the shield falls, she is taken to her shuttle on the landing pads, with three decoy shuttles starting their engines with hers (GS 41). Darrow gets aboard, and she has the pilot roll the ship (GS 42) [w Lion's Rain].
- **In the scene:** walking to her shuttle among her guards, then aboard.

**Aja au Grimmus, the Protean Knight.**

- **Look:** tall and powerful, with large hands, dark skin and very white teeth [w Aja au Grimmus, uncited].
- **Armour:** the Protean Knight's, in gold and midnight blue, emblazoned with blue sea serpents [w Olympic Knights, uncited for this item]. Every Olympic Knight also wears a cloak bearing the Society's pyramid, and a signet ring (RR 17) [w Olympic Knights].
- **Teacher:** the only student Lorn ever took, in 711 (GS 12) [w Lorn au Arcos].
- **Weapon:** a razor (MS 2) [w Aja au Grimmus].
- **In the Rain:** she escorts the Sovereign to the shuttle. When Darrow primes a grenade aboard, she kicks it out of the hatch, then knocks him to his knees (GS 41, 42) [w Aja au Grimmus; w Lion's Rain].

## 4. Stories

Each story plays for 20 to 50 seconds, from a scene in the books, in moves a small figure can make. Nobody dies on the tile. Where the book's scene ends in death, the line says so, and the toy stops at the clash. They are the tile's version of the dragons' moments [tile].

1. **The Rain lands** (GS 38).
   - **Who:** the Pax and the fleet; a wave of starShells; Bellona ripWings; the canyon's guns.
   - **Where:** from the eastern ships down to the canyon floor and the Institute's branch.
   - **What:** the Pax's rows of spitTubes flash. A column of glowing shells streaks down, their outer panels flaring and peeling away. Ground cannons on the canyon wall fire upward. At cloud height each shell fires its gravBoots, and the column breaks into small squadrons that fan out. Two ripWings dive after them and fall behind. The shells hit the canyon floor in rings of dust, stand up, split their bulky outer shells and step out.
   - **Ends:** a second wave begins, high above.
   - **In the book:** many die to the guns, among them the Howler Harpy (Daria). On the tile nobody falls.
2. **Into the river** (GS 39–40).
   - **Who:** Darrow, Virginia, Ragnar, the Howlers, a few Obsidians; Cassius and a squad on the wall.
   - **Where:** the river where it runs under the north wall.
   - **What:** the group wades into the river short of the wall and goes under. They come up inside the wall among the mud banks and rise on gravBoots. A pulse of light ripples out from the bank, their boots sputter, and they drop into the shallows with a splash. Darrow and Ragnar haul the others up the bank one by one, while Cassius watches from the wall.
   - **Ends:** the group shakes off the mud and runs into the streets.
   - **In the book:** the EMP is set off by a small Brown girl. Most of the group drown or are killed in the mud, among them Weed and Rotback (GS 40). On the tile the pulse comes from a device on the bank, there is no child, and nobody drowns.
3. **Ragnar opens the north gate** (GS 41).
   - **Who:** Ragnar and his Obsidians; the Wind Knight; Cassius.
   - **Where:** the north gate.
   - **What:** Ragnar climbs the gate's tower with a razor in each hand. He clashes with the Wind Knight, who is knocked off the parapet and floats away on gravBoots. Cassius, in white with his golden sun, crosses blades with Ragnar three times and leaps back. Ragnar sets his shoulder to the gates, and they swing open. The ground column pours through: spider tanks, speederbikes, hover skiffs.
   - **Ends:** Ragnar raises both razors on the tower.
   - **In the book:** the Wind Knight dies, and Cassius barely escapes.
4. **Sevro and the last boots** (GS 41).
   - **Who:** Sevro, the Howlers, Darrow.
   - **Where:** the streets from the north gate to the Citadel's outer wall.
   - **What:** Sevro hops from roof to roof on the only working boots, waving the Howlers left and right past Bellona patrols. At the Citadel's wall he lifts Darrow to the top and goes back for the next. His boots die in mid-hop; he drops lightly and shrugs, and the Howlers throw back their heads and howl.
   - **Ends:** Darrow runs on alone across the grounds. The same as the book, which has no death here.
5. **Mustang drops the shield** (GS 41).
   - **Who:** Virginia and her Obsidians; the shield; the next wave.
   - **Where:** the generator park, and the dome over the whole city.
   - **What:** Virginia sets charges on the four pylons and runs. One after another the pylons flare. Ripples race across the dome, and it breaks into falling flakes of light, section by section. At once a new wave of starShells streaks down through the open sky.
   - **Ends:** the sky over Agea is clear, and the fleet is seen sharp. The most spectacular of the twelve. The same as the book, with no death in it.
6. **The Sovereign's shuttle** (GS 41–42).
   - **Who:** Octavia, Aja, Karnus, Fitchner, the Praetorians, Darrow.
   - **Where:** the Citadel's landing pads.
   - **What:** Praetorians in purple and black march the Sovereign to her shuttle, and three decoys start their engines with hers. Darrow sprints across the pads, weaves through the guards and leaps onto her ramp as it lifts. The shuttle rolls. A glowing grenade tumbles out of the hatch, kicked by Aja, and bursts harmlessly in a puff of light. Karnus swings and Darrow vaults over him. Fitchner turns with his pulseFist flashing, seizes Darrow and drops from the ramp on blazing gravBoots. The four shuttles climb, swap places, and one shimmers out of sight.
   - **Ends:** Fitchner sets Darrow down on a pad, salutes, and is gone.
   - **In the book:** Darrow kills Karnus, Fitchner wounds the Sovereign and carries Darrow away (GS 42, 43), and Fitchner is killed before the Triumph two weeks later [w Agea].
7. **Lorn at the south wall** (GS 39, 41).
   - **Who:** Lorn and his force; the Bellona defenders.
   - **Where:** the south wall.
   - **What:** Lorn walks to the wall at the head of his men without hurrying, his razor snapping between whip and blade as he turns the bolts aside. His force charges, falls back and charges again. Then the defenders rush away north towards the opened gate (as in GS 41). Lorn lifts his razor, and his force goes over the empty wall.
   - **Ends:** Lorn stands on the parapet and looks up at the Rain.
   - **In the book:** he lives through the battle and is killed at the Triumph two weeks later (GS 51).

## 6. Problems: what a cheerful toy should leave out or soften

### What the Rain itself must lose

- **Death.** It is everywhere in the Lion's Rain. The toy stops each fight at the clash, and nobody falls, drowns or bleeds (section 4):
  - Howlers killed by the guns and in the river (GS 38, 40);
  - Proctor Jupiter and most of Darrow's Obsidians, drowned or cut down in the mud (GS 40);
  - the Wind Knight killed (GS 41);
  - Karnus beheaded aboard the shuttle (GS 42).
- **The slaughter of the Bellona family's children at their estate in Agea**, on the Jackal's orders during the battle (GS 37, 43; MS 62) [w Lion's Rain]. Left out entirely.
- **The child with the EMP.** A small Brown girl is used to set off the EMP at the river (GS 39). The tile has no child in the battle.
- **The Triumph that follows** (GS 51): its massacre, Fitchner's head, and Lorn's death. Left out. The Field of Mars stands empty of it.
- **Nuclear weapons** (MS 63–64; DA 33) and orbital strikes on cities. None on the tile.
- **The dead as decoration.** No bodies, no wreckage with crew, and no ruins of the battle.

### What the figures must lose

- **Their deaths.** Nobody portrays the deaths of Aja, Ragnar, Lorn, Fitchner, Quinn, Alexandar or Pax. Each figure is shown alive and in their best moment.
- **Cruelty.**
  - The Jackal's acts, among them the cannibalism at the Institute (GS 7), have no place. He only watches from a tower.
  - Aja's killing of Quinn and Trigg (GS; MS 2) [w Aja au Grimmus] is not shown.
  - The Sovereign's past (the Burning of Rhea) stays out.
- **The Fear Knight's armour**, etched with screaming children (DA 89). Shown plain bone-white, or Atlas is left out.
- **Eo**, whose story is her hanging, is never in the battle (section 3).
- **Mickey the Carver** is left out of the battle. His twelve fingers come from a tweet [x].
- **Weapons.** Razors, pulseFists and bows are drawn as the books have them, since the collector asked for the fights. No guns are aimed at anyone outside a story's clash.

### Care

- **Memorials.** Eo's memorial on the Citadel's grounds (DA 18) is drawn with care or left out. It is never the setting for a joke or a fight.
- **Named characters.** The tile portrays them at the collector's wish. The rule against it in [building-a-tile.md](../../building-a-tile.md) is set aside here only, and `NOTES.md` should say so.
- **The caste system.** The crowds of Agea, if shown, are of every Color, by hair and eye colour:
  - Golds tall;
  - Obsidians the largest;
  - Reds small;
  - Grays in uniform;
  - Blues bald and tattooed (RR 12; DA 1) [w Color; w Blue].
  No segregated paths, and no "lowColor" signs. Pinks appear only as pink hair in a crowd [w Pink].

## 7. Where it goes on the map

The map on the desk is of Earth, and Mars is not on it. Two ways:

1. **Within today's `place`** [tile]. Give Mars its own land: a round disc about 7 degrees across, in open sea in the North Pacific, centred near 166° west and 35° north, with `words: 'Mars'`.
   - Put the pin where Agea is: in the Valles Marineris, a little south of the planet's equator [real]. So the pin sits a little below the middle of the disc.
   - The spot is clear of Hawaii, about 155° to 160° west and 19° to 22° north [real]. It is also clear of the label MARE PACIFICUM (150° west, 28° south), the sea serpent (118° west, 47° south) and Westeros (139° west, 30° south), in `app/src/engine/room/map.ts` and the Westeros tile's `index.ts`.
   - A rounded disc reads as a planet that has rolled onto the map, which suits a toy.
2. **Better, but it needs the engine**: a small roundel of Mars in a clear margin of the sheet, beside the round pictures of the poles that the sheet already has.
   - Some seventeenth-century world maps carried the planets in their borders, Mars and the Moon among them. Willem Blaeu's wall map of 1606 is the example I remember [real, from memory, unverified].
   - The roundel could show the books' terraformed Mars: green and white.
   - `place` would need a way to name the disc it stands on. That is engine work, done in its own commit.

If a Luna tile is ever built (Appendix A), the same choice would put the Moon in the other margin.

## 8. Questions for the collector

1. **The Rain.** The Lion's Rain over Agea stands now. The alternative is Atalantia's Rain on Mercury, falling on mountains and waste.
2. **The planet.** Now: the tile's ground as a dome of planet. The alternative is a small globe hung over the tile.
3. **The fleet.** Now: on brass arms above the tile. The alternative is ships on lanes of their own that come and go.
4. **The figures' scale.** Figures the size of the tile's people are too small to read. Now: the cast twice the people's height, as landmarks are drawn larger than scale. The alternative is people's scale.
5. **How the stories play.** Now: one at a time, in turn. The alternative is several at once.
6. **The traffic.** Now: the Augustan ground column of spider tanks, speederbikes and hover skiffs, with the heroes among them. The alternative is plain toy cars.
7. **Which side of the road**, since the books do not say.

## Appendix A: Hyperion on Luna, the place first recommended

Before the collector's wishes were known, this note recommended Hyperion, the capital on Luna, in the autumn of 753 PCE. It has the most novel-sourced detail of any place in the saga, a layered city, flying traffic, ripWing races, and Earth in its sky. It is kept here for a second tile, or as a backdrop. Every item is [w Hyperion] with the chapter shown unless another page is named. "Silent" marks a look the books do not give.

**The Citadel of Light**, on the city's western edge, ringed by forest (IG 29, 41):

1. The Sovereign's spire, the highest point on the grounds (GS 14), about 2 km tall (GS 11) [w Summit], with a landing pad (MS 60) and a roof garden (GS 11). Its shape: silent.
2. The Occular Sphere, a glass room hanging above the spire's tip like an upside-down teardrop (DA 30). A search found no other source.
3. The roof garden as a winter fairyland for the Summit's gala of December 741 (GS 11) [w Summit]. The gala ended in a massacre (GS 12).
4. The Dragon Maw, a 2-km gravLift to a secret bunker (MS 60).
5. The Palatine Hill, with landing pads, officials' homes and the Politico School (GS 6; IG 11, 41, 59). Earth hangs beyond its spires (GS 6) [w Golden Son, Chapter 6].
6. The Esqueline Hills, with gardens and villas (IG 6, 23, 29).

## Appendix B: the other places weighed

- **The Institute's valley under Olympus** (RR; GS 39):
  - the castles of thirteen Houses among mountains, rivers and the Greatwoods, whose trees are as tall as buildings (RR 30);
  - Olympus floating 2 km up (RR 42);
  - Red Rising prints a map of the valley [w The Institute].
  Its story is children killing children (the Passage, slavery by standard, cannibalism, rape), and it would be the Westeros tile's castles in other clothes. On this tile Olympus appears far up the canyon, as the Lion's Rain's staging post.
- **Lykos**, the mine under Mars (RR 1–5; GS 47–49): the Can, the tiered Common, twenty-three townships carved round it, the horizonTram, the gravLift, the Webbery and the bubbleGarden [w Lykos]. Underground, dark and grim, so it is better as the cut-away under Agea (item 23).
- **Phobos**, Mars's hollowed moon (MS 14–37; LB 20–33) [w Phobos]: a city of starscrapers under two crossed rings of docks, Mars filling its sky. Thinly described, and a moon 22 km across does not lie flat.
