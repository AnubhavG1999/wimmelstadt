Builds on [westeros-dragons-and-more.md](westeros-dragons-and-more.md) and does not repeat it. This note is for eleven great dragons of the Targaryen era, to be built as toys for the Westeros tile so that each is known at a glance. For each it gathers what the books say of its size, its shape and its colours, who rode it and how it ended, the scene it is known for and where that happened, and where the television series drew it differently. The eleven are those of a fan artist's chart of dragon heads, which ranks them from largest to smallest; section 13 ranks them again from the books, and section 14 says how big each should be on the tile. As for the rest of the tile, nothing here is taken from the television series: it appears only so that it can be avoided.

Caveat on sources, read first. A Wiki of Ice and Fire (awoiaf.westeros.org) refused the plain web fetcher again (HTTP 403), but its pages opened normally in an ordinary browser, and every dragon's page was read there today, with the book and chapter or section that each fact cites. Gelo e Fogo (wiki.geloefogo.com), the Portuguese wiki the earlier note used, was read beside it and agreed wherever both spoke. The books themselves were not read today: what this note gives as the books is the wikis' close paraphrase, with their citations. Fire & Blood's chapters have titles, not numbers, and are given by title. The author's words outside the books come from So Spake Martin (the archive on westeros.org), his blog and press reports of interviews, and each says which. Marked **(judgement)**: my own reading where the books are silent or disagree. Marked **(choice)**: a suggestion for the model, not a fact. Marked **(unverified)**: from my own reading, or seen only in a source I could not check. Section 15 lists the sources and what was and was not verified.

Book abbreviations: **AGOT** A Game of Thrones, **ACOK** A Clash of Kings, **ASOS** A Storm of Swords, **ADWD** A Dance with Dragons, **F&B** Fire & Blood, **TWOIAF** The World of Ice and Fire, **TPATQ** The Princess and the Queen, **TRotD** The Rise of the Dragon. **HOTD** is the television series House of the Dragon. Years are counted from Aegon's Conquest: AC after it, BC before it.

## 1. What holds for all of them

- **Two legs, and the wings are the forelimbs.** The author has said so in a forum answer (So Spake Martin, 16 April 2008) and on his blog ("Here There Be Dragons", 11 July 2024). On the blog he adds that dragons like lairs on mountain tops, volcanic ones best, and that the wild dragons of F&B lair on Dragonstone.
- **They grow all their lives**, as long as they have food and freedom (ASOS ch. 8, Daenerys I). Dragons kept in the Dragonpit grew less than free ones (ASOS ch. 8; ADWD ch. 68, as the wiki gives them). Age is not a sure measure: the Cannibal, perhaps the oldest, was smaller than Vhagar and Vermithor, and Vermithor outgrew Dreamfyre, who was older (F&B, as the wiki reads it).
- **Black teeth and black bones.** The skulls in the Red Keep's cellar shine like onyx, their teeth like curved knives of black diamond (AGOT ch. 13, Tyrion II).
- **Their fire grows hotter with age.** At their height Balerion and Vhagar could melt steel and stone (TPATQ; F&B).
- **The books measure only one dragon**: Drogon's wings span twenty feet in ADWD (ch. 52, Daenerys IX). Everything about the great dragons is a comparison. One other number: in 56 AC Balerion came back with a gash almost nine feet long down his left side (F&B, "Jaehaerys and Alysanne: Their Triumphs and Tragedies").
- **Young dragons as yardsticks.** In 130 AC Moondancer, a young dragon, was no larger than a warhorse and weighed less (F&B, "Rhaenyra Triumphant"). In ADWD Rhaegal's head is larger than a horse's (ch. 68, The Dragontamer). Drogon then is barely big enough to carry Daenerys (the author, below).
- **What the author has said outside the books.** He has given no figure for any of the eleven. At the 92nd Street Y in October 2014 he said Drogon was still a very young dragon, barely able to lift Daenerys, while Balerion was much closer to Smaug in size and ferocity (as reported by TheOneRing.net). In April 2014 he is reported to have told Entertainment Weekly that Jordi González Escamilla's painting of Aegon on Balerion, made for TWOIAF, shows how big a grown dragon gets (reported by ScreenCrush and the Daily Dot; the EW page is gone, **unverified**). At a London event in August 2019 he said that if he could ride a dragon it would be Balerion, the biggest, who remembered Valyria (a fan's report on So Spake Martin). Through his team he gave Vhagar's colours (section 3).
- **Fan and press figures.** For Balerion they run from about 60 m to over 150 m long. The Daily Dot (2014) measured the painting above and got about 76 m at the Conquest, and over 150 m at his death by stretching that forward. Press figures for the series' dragons (Vhagar about 150 m, Caraxes about 46 m, Meleys 34 to 37 m) are guesses about the series, seen only in search results. None of these figures is the author's, and none is used here.

## 2. Balerion, the Black Dread

**Size.**
- The largest of all the Targaryen dragons (TWOIAF; F&B). In Daenerys's telling his wings were so vast that whole towns lay in their shadow when he passed over (ACOK ch. 12, Daenerys I).
- His is the largest of the nineteen skulls in the Red Keep. Its jaws could have swallowed an aurochs whole, or one of the hairy mammoths of the wastes beyond Ibben (AGOT ch. 13). His teeth were as long as swords (AGOT: the wiki cites ch. 13, while a fan archive puts the phrase in ch. 32, Arya III; which is **unverified**). Arya runs out of the cellar through his skull (AGOT ch. 32), and Shae stands inside the jaws of Balerion or Vhagar (ASOS ch. 58, Tyrion VII).
- **At each age.** He came from Valyria with Aenar Targaryen in 114 BC, already hatched, and was over a century old at the Conquest. In 54 to 56 AC he was the largest living dragon in the world (F&B, "Birth, Death, and Betrayal Under King Jaehaerys I"). In 75 AC the dragonkeepers called him old and slow (F&B, "The Long Reign: Policy, Progeny, and Pain"). By 93 AC he had stopped growing; he was sluggish, heavy and hard to rouse, and could barely take to the air (same). He died in 94 AC, about two hundred years old (ASOS ch. 8; at least 207 by the wiki's reckoning), of old age (the author on his blog, 12 March 2014, as the wiki cites it).
- **Against the others.** By 129 AC Vhagar had grown nearly as big as Balerion had been at the Conquest (ADWD ch. 8, Tyrion III), and he went on growing for ninety years after it. At his largest he was the biggest of the eleven, and by a clear margin.
- **Against Drogon.** In ACOK Aggo calls Drogon Balerion come again: a name, not a size. By the estimate in section 14, Balerion at his largest was some 7 to 13 times Drogon's length in ADWD **(judgement)**.

**Shape.** The books give vast wings, a huge jaw, teeth as long as swords, and in old age a heavy, slow body. They say nothing of his horns, crest, head, neck or tail. For the model **(choice)**: the broadest wings of the eleven, a heavy head with a deep jaw and long black teeth that show, and a body built as in his prime, at Harrenhal.

**Colours.** Black scales and black wings (ACOK ch. 12; F&B, "Aegon's Conquest"; F&B, "The Sons of the Dragon"). His fire was black, sometimes swirled with red (F&B, "Aegon's Conquest"). His eyes are not described. Unlike Drogon he has no red on him: red only in his fire.

**Riders and fate.** Aegon I, who claimed him before the Conquest; Maegor I, from Aegon's death in 37 AC; Princess Aerea, who flew off on him in 54 AC and was brought back dying two years later, perhaps from Valyria, with him wounded; Viserys, in 93 AC. He died of old age in 94 AC, and his skull hung in the throne room of the Red Keep, later in a cellar.

**Its moment.**
- **Harrenhal, 2 BC.** When King Harren had not yielded by sunset, Aegon took Balerion up high and came down inside the walls. The fire was so hot that the towers melted and twisted like candles, into the shapes they keep today (TWOIAF, "The Conquest"). On the tile: Harrenhal, whose five melted towers are already the aftermath. For the tile: Balerion climbing at dusk, then sweeping low over the walls with his shadow over the whole castle, his fire only straight up.
- **The Field of Fire, 1 BC.** With Vhagar and Meraxes, the only battle of the Conquest in which all three flew (TWOIAF). In the Reach, and a battle. Afterwards the three waited at the Trident until the King in the North knelt (TWOIAF): "Along the Trident" is on the tile, and the three Conquest dragons at rest on its bank would be a quiet scene.
- **A gentler one: his last flight, 93 AC.** Viserys woke the old dragon in the Dragonpit and flew him three times round King's Landing, not daring to try for Dragonstone (F&B, "Policy, Progeny, and Pain"). On the tile: King's Landing and the Dragonpit. Three slow, heavy laps.

**The series.** Game of Thrones (seasons 7 and 8) and HOTD (season 1) show only his skull, which is the series' own design. A prop history book in HOTD has Daenys the Dreamer claim him in Valyria when he was no bigger than a horse; that is not in the books (the wiki). Nothing of the series' skull, horns or head belongs on the model.

## 3. Vhagar

**Size.**
- In the Red Keep's cellar one could ride a horse down her skull's gullet, though not out again; Meraxes's skull was bigger, and Balerion's the biggest (AGOT ch. 13).
- Her breath, it was said, could melt a knight's armour and cook the man inside (ACOK ch. 12).
- **At each age.** She hatched on Dragonstone in the Century of Blood (52 BC, by the wiki's reckoning), so she was about fifty at the Conquest. In 75 AC the young Meleys easily outflew her and Caraxes (F&B, "Policy, Progeny, and Pain"). By 129 AC she had grown nearly as big as Balerion had been at the Conquest, and was the largest living dragon (ADWD ch. 8). She was then the survivor of a hundred battles, matched by no living dragon in size, and her roar shook the foundations of Storm's End (TPATQ). She died in 130 AC at 181, the greatest of the Targaryen dragons since Balerion's death (F&B, "Rhaenyra Triumphant").
- **Against the others.** Five times the size of Arrax (F&B, "A Son for a Son"). Caraxes was about half her size (F&B, "The Blacks and the Greens"). None of the blacks' dragons could match her; Jacaerys hoped that three together might (F&B, "The Red Dragon and the Gold"). Meleys might have stood a chance against her alone (same).
- **The contradiction.** AGOT makes Meraxes's skull bigger than hers. That was written long before F&B, and Meraxes died 120 years before Vhagar; the wiki takes it as a slip or a misremembering. This note follows the later books **(judgement)**.
- **Against Drogon.** Some 5 to 10 times his length in ADWD **(judgement)**, section 14.

**Shape.** The books make her huge, old and battle-hardened, and slower than younger dragons: Meleys outflew her in 75 AC, and Arrax was younger and swifter in 129. Her head, horns, neck, wings and tail are not described, nor any scars. For the model **(choice)**: the heaviest build of the eleven, with a deep chest and a thick neck, slow and few wingbeats, and the old scars that a hundred battles would leave.

**Colours.** The books never give them. The author's team told the artist Sam Hogg, who asked when painting her for the 2021 calendar: bronze with greenish-blue highlights, and bright green eyes (So Spake Martin, "Vhagar's Color", 16 January 2021, from Hogg's own posts). Her fire's colour is not given.

**Riders and fate.** Visenya, from before the Conquest to 44 AC; then no rider for 29 years; Prince Baelon, 73 to 101 AC; Laena Velaryon, from before 115 to 120 AC; Prince Aemond, 120 to 130 AC. She was killed with Aemond above the Gods Eye in 130 AC and sank in the lake. Years later her bones were raised with Aemond's still chained in the saddle, and her skull went up on the throne room's wall.

**Its moment.**
- **The duel above the Gods Eye, 130 AC**, with Caraxes (section 8). On the tile: Harrenhal and the Gods Eye.
- **Storm's End, 129 AC.** Aemond came on her to win Lord Borros; her roar shook the castle's foundations, and she hunted Arrax down over Shipbreaker Bay in a storm (TPATQ). On the tile: Storm's End. For the tile, the roar and a flight out over the bay into cloud, without a kill.
- **A gentler one: the Eyrie, 1 BC.** Visenya brought Vhagar down in the Eyrie's yard, and the boy king Ronnel Arryn begged a ride: he flew three times round the summit of the Giant's Lance, and his mother knelt (TWOIAF, "The Conquest"). On the tile: the Eyrie and the Giant's Lance. Three slow laps round the peak.

**The series.** HOTD draws her as a sagging, ancient giant with features of a tortoise and a monitor lizard (TV Tropes), and its effects supervisor called her infirm (Television Academy, 2023). The series' head and body are its own; keep only the colours the author's team gave.

## 4. Meraxes

**Size.**
- Her skull was bigger than Vhagar's and smaller than Balerion's: one of the three largest of the nineteen (AGOT ch. 13).
- She was said to swallow horses whole (ACOK ch. 12).
- **At each age.** She hatched on Dragonstone in the Century of Blood (between 114 and 88 BC, by the wiki's reckoning) and died in 10 AC, at the very most about 120 years old.
- **Against the others.** The wiki doubts both claims: only Vhagar is said by the histories to have grown almost as big as Balerion, and Meraxes died at least 58 years younger than Vhagar did. If AGOT is right she is second of the eleven; if the later books are, she is smaller than Vhagar **(judgement: third)**. Against Vermithor nothing is said; they were of about the same age at their deaths, and this note puts her above him because of the skull **(judgement)**.
- **Against Drogon.** Some 4 to 9 times **(judgement)**.

**Shape.** The books say nothing of it. Rhaenys spent more time on dragonback than her brother and sister together (TWOIAF), so for the model **(choice)**: the Conquest build, but lighter and longer in the wing than Vhagar.

**Colours.** Silver scales and golden eyes (TWOIAF, "The Conquest"). Her fire is not described.

**Riders and fate.** Rhaenys alone, from before the Conquest to 10 AC. At the Hellholt in Dorne a scorpion bolt went through her eye; falling, she destroyed the castle's highest tower and part of its wall, and Rhaenys died with her (TWOIAF, "Dorne Against the Dragons"). A peace mission later returned her skull; decades on, the rest of her bones still lay in a field at the Hellholt (F&B, "Policy, Progeny, and Pain").

**Its moment.**
- **Storm's End, 1 BC.** At the Last Storm the rain kept her on the ground, yet she burned the Storm King's vanguard; afterwards Rhaenys flew her into Storm's End itself to parley with Argella (TWOIAF). On the tile: Storm's End. For the tile: a silver dragon coming down through rain into the castle's bailey.
- **Sunspear, 1 AC.** Rhaenys found Dorne's castles empty, and at Sunspear the old Princess Meria told her that Dorne would neither fight nor kneel (TWOIAF). On the tile: Sunspear. A silver dragon circling the Tower of the Sun and leaving.
- Rhaenys once said she meant to fly Meraxes across the Sunset Sea to see what lay beyond (TWOIAF): a flight west off the edge of the tile.

**The series.** The wiki lists no appearance in it.

## 5. Vermithor, the Bronze Fury

**Size.**
- **At each age.** He hatched in 34 AC, by legend from an egg that Rhaena laid in Jaehaerys's cradle (F&B, "The Sons of the Dragon"). He was already a large dragon by 48 AC (same), and in Jaehaerys's reign only Balerion and Vhagar were larger (TWOIAF, "Jaehaerys I"). In 54 AC he carried the king and a guard together (F&B). By 130 AC he was almost a hundred (F&B, "Rhaenyra Overthrown").
- **Against the others.** After Vhagar's death in 130 AC, Hugh Hammer claimed a crown as the rider of the oldest and largest living dragon (TPATQ). He was bigger and heavier than Seasmoke, three times the size of Tessarion, and as big as the two of them together (F&B, "Rhaenyra Overthrown", partly in Lord Blackwood's words). He outgrew Dreamfyre, though she was older (F&B, as the wiki reads it). The largest dragon hatched after the Conquest; below Balerion and Vhagar, and perhaps level with Meraxes **(judgement)**.
- **Against Drogon.** Some 3 to 6 times **(judgement)**.

**Shape.** Older, bigger and heavier than Seasmoke, and old and hoary by 129 AC. His head, horns, neck and tail are not described. For the model **(choice)**: a heavy, broad-chested build, second only to Vhagar's.

**Colours.** A bronze dragon with great tan wings (TPATQ). His fire is not described. At the Battle of the Gullet five dragons' fire was blue and orange, red and gold, without saying whose (F&B, "The Red Dragon and the Gold").

**Riders and fate.** King Jaehaerys I, by 48 AC until 103 AC; then no rider for 26 years, in a cave of the Dragonmont; Hugh Hammer, a blacksmith's bastard, from the Sowing in 129 AC. At the First Battle of Tumbleton Hugh turned against Rhaenyra. At the Second, in 130 AC, Hugh was killed before he could mount; Vermithor rose alone, tore off Seasmoke's head, and died with his wings in rags. His bones and Seasmoke's became a sight for travellers at Tumbleton.

**Its moment.**
- **The Second Battle of Tumbleton, 130 AC.** Tumbleton is not on the tile, and it is a battle.
- **Oldtown, 54 AC.** Jaehaerys brought Vermithor down on the marble plaza before the Starry Sept, while Alysanne set Silverwing on the top of the Hightower (F&B, "Birth, Death, and Betrayal"). On the tile: Oldtown, the Starry Sept and the Hightower. A caution: the tile keeps dragons' routes away from septs (section 6 of the earlier note), and a landing at the sept's door is against that rule, so it needs a decision.
- **The Fourth Dornish War, 83 AC.** In one day, Jaehaerys on Vermithor, Aemon on Caraxes and Baelon on Vhagar came down on a Dornish fleet crossing the Sea of Dorne for Cape Wrath and burned it; no bolt pierced their scales (F&B, "Policy, Progeny, and Pain"). The sea south of Storm's End is on the tile, and fire over open sea is within the tile's rules: three great dragons together in one pass over the southern sea.

**The series.** HOTD makes him huge, heavy and bronze, with a scar across his upper lip and a sagging throat (TV Tropes). Its head and horns are the series' own.

## 6. The Cannibal

**Size.**
- The largest and oldest of the three wild dragons on Dragonstone, the others being Sheepstealer and Grey Ghost (F&B, "The Red Dragon and the Gold"). He attacked smaller dragons, and at the Sowing he drove Sheepstealer off (same).
- **At each age.** Unknown. Some on Dragonstone said he lived there before the Targaryens came in 114 BC; Munkun and Septon Eustace doubted it (same; TWOIAF, "Aegon II").
- **Against the others.** Not bigger than Vhagar and Vermithor (the wiki's reading: after Vhagar died, Vermithor was called the oldest and largest living dragon, while the Cannibal still lived). Larger than Sheepstealer. Against Silverwing nothing is said **(judgement: about her size, or a little more)**.
- **Against Drogon.** Some 3 to 5½ times **(judgement)**.

**Shape.** The books give nothing but his colour and that he was big and wild. For the model **(choice)**: a wild dragon, never saddled, lean and scarred from a life of fighting.

**Colours.** Black as coal, with green eyes of a menacing look (F&B, "The Red Dragon and the Gold"). His fire is not described. To tell him from Balerion, who is also black **(choice)**: smaller, green-eyed, and with a fire that is not black.

**Riders and fate.** Never ridden. Before the Dance a dozen would-be riders tried him, and his lair was strewn with their bones. He was one of the four dragons alive at the end of the Dance, and vanished some time after (TWOIAF, "Aegon II").

**Its moment.**
- **His lair, 129 AC.** He laired at the back of the Dragonmont, on its eastern side (F&B, "The Short, Sad Reign of Aegon II"), and at the Sowing none of the dragonseeds was fool enough to disturb him (F&B, "The Red Dragon and the Gold"). On the tile: Dragonstone and the Dragonmont. A black shape stirring at a cave mouth on the mountain's far side, seen only now and then.
- **The salute, 132 AC.** At Lord Corlys Velaryon's burial at sea, it was said, the Cannibal took wing and flew over as if in salute; Gyldayn thinks he was likelier to eat the body (F&B, "Under the Regents: The Hooded Hand"). At sea; where is not said. For the tile: the black dragon rising from the Dragonmont and passing once over a ship in the sea by Dragonstone.

**The series.** The wiki lists no appearance in it.

## 7. Silverwing

**Size.**
- No size is ever given. She hatched in 36 AC, by legend from an egg laid in Alysanne's cradle, and so was of Vermithor's generation (F&B, "The Sons of the Dragon"). In 54 AC she carried the queen and a knight together (F&B). In 129 AC she was among the oldest and largest dragons (F&B, as the wiki groups them).
- **Against the others.** In Jaehaerys's reign only Balerion and Vhagar were larger than Vermithor (TWOIAF), so she was probably a little smaller than her mate **(judgement)**. About the Cannibal's size **(judgement)**.
- **Against Drogon.** Some 3 to 5½ times **(judgement)**.

**Shape.** Not described. For the model **(choice)**: a lighter build than Vermithor's.

**Colours.** Silvery (F&B, "Birth, Death, and Betrayal"; "The Short, Sad Reign of Aegon II"). Her fire is not described; the wiki guesses blue, from the Gullet's mix of colours **(unverified)**.

**Character.** Gentle and friendly to strangers; of the four dragons tried at the Sowing she is the only one not said to have killed anyone (F&B, "The Red Dragon and the Gold"). She and Vermithor often lay coiled together (F&B, "Rhaenyra Overthrown").

**Riders and fate.** Queen Alysanne, by 48 AC until her death in 100 AC; then no rider for 29 years, in a cave of the Dragonmont; Ulf the White, from the Sowing in 129 AC. Ulf betrayed Rhaenyra at Tumbleton and was poisoned in 130 AC. Silverwing went wild and made her lair on a small island in the Red Lake, where she was still living in Aegon III's regency; she was dead by 153 AC, when the last dragon died.

**Its moment.**
- **The Wall, 58 AC.** Alysanne flew to Castle Black. Silverwing hissed and snapped at every cold gust off the ice, and three times refused to fly north of the Wall, turning south each time, though she had never refused the queen before (F&B, "Triumphs and Tragedies"). On the same journey she came to White Harbor, where tens of thousands turned out, and to Winterfell, where Lord Alaric would not have her inside the walls, and on the way north to Queenscrown (same; ASOS ch. 40, Bran III). All of these are on the tile. For the tile: three runs north from Castle Black at the Wall, each breaking off short and turning back south.
- **The Hightower, 54 AC.** She perched on the top of the Hightower and fanned its beacon with her wings, and Oldtown gasped (F&B, "Birth, Death, and Betrayal"). On the tile: the Hightower and its beacon, which the tile already lets a dragon's breath reach. Here the wings fan it instead.

**The series.** HOTD makes her a deep silver, slender, with a long, horse-like head like that of its Syrax (TV Tropes).

## 8. Caraxes, the Blood Wyrm

**Size.**
- **At each age.** By 72 AC he was a young dragon of riding size, and the dragonkeepers thought him the fiercest of the young ones in the Dragonpit. In 75 AC Meleys outflew him; in 92 AC he easily outran the Velaryon fleet (F&B, "Policy, Progeny, and Pain"). At his death in 130 AC he was at least 58.
- **Against the others.** During the Dance he was about half the size of Vhagar (F&B, "The Blacks and the Greens"), and lighter and quicker than she was (F&B, "Rhaenyra Triumphant", as Gelo e Fogo gives it).
- **Against Drogon.** Some 2½ to 5 times **(judgement)**.

**Shape.** The books make him red, huge and lean, formidable in battle, and old and cunning by the end (F&B, "The Blacks and the Greens"). His name, the Blood Wyrm, was given him by the dragonkeepers for his fierceness. **The books never say his neck was long**: that is the series' design, and much fan art's. Lean, together with wyrm, an old word for a serpent-like dragon, makes a long and snaky build a fair reading of the books **(judgement)**. His shriek once shattered every window of a tower at Maidenpool (F&B, "Rhaenyra Triumphant", as Gelo e Fogo gives it). For the model **(choice)**: the longest and thinnest neck and body of the eleven, on wings no larger than Meleys's.

**Colours.** Red. His eyes, horns and fire are not described.

**Riders and fate.** Prince Aemon, 72 to 92 AC; Prince Daemon, by 105 AC to 130 AC. In the fall into the Gods Eye, Vhagar tore open his belly and tore off a wing; he dragged himself ashore and died before the walls of Harrenhal. Daemon's body was never found.

**Its moment.**
- **The Kingspyre, 129 AC.** Daemon took Harrenhal by landing Caraxes on top of its tallest tower, the Kingspyre, and the castle yielded without a fight (F&B, "A Son for a Son"). On the tile: Harrenhal's Kingspyre, 32 high. A perch to show: wings half open, head up, shrieking.
- **The duel above the Gods Eye, 130 AC.** Daemon waited at Harrenhal thirteen days. On the fourteenth Vhagar came, and at sunset the two rose; their cries carried a dozen miles. Caraxes fell on Vhagar from above and locked his jaws on her neck, and they came down together into the lake, throwing up water as high as the Kingspyre (TPATQ). On the tile: Harrenhal and the Gods Eye. For the tile: the stoop out of the sunset, the two locked and spiralling down, and the great plume of water; no fire near the Isle of Faces.

**The series.** HOTD gives him an extra-long neck, a very narrow middle, and fins on his hind legs that act as a second pair of wings, with a high, shrill cry (TV Tropes); its effects supervisor likened him to a red Chinese dragon (Television Academy, 2023). The fins and the pinched waist are the series' alone.

## 9. Dreamfyre

**Size.**
- **At each age.** She hatched in or shortly before 32 AC, when the nine-year-old Rhaena bonded with her, and by 35 AC she could carry Rhaena at twelve (F&B, "The Sons of the Dragon"). In 43 AC she was younger and smaller than Quicksilver (same). From 73 AC until her death in 130 AC, at about 98, she lived in the Dragonpit, the last years chained; the books say such dragons grow less.
- **Against the others.** Vermithor grew bigger than she did, though she was older (F&B, as the wiki reads it). She is always called slender. In 129 AC she was among the oldest and largest (F&B, as the wiki groups them). About half Vhagar's size, and slighter than the others of her age **(judgement)**.
- **Against Drogon.** Some 2½ to 5 times **(judgement)**.

**Shape.** Slender (F&B, "The Sons of the Dragon"), with silver crests (F&B, "Birth, Death, and Betrayal"). Her head, horns, neck and tail are not described. For the model **(choice)**: the slimmest body of the eleven, with a crest of silver spines.

**Colours.** Mostly pale blue, with silver markings; silver crests; pale blue wings (F&B, "The Sons of the Dragon"; "Birth, Death, and Betrayal"). Her fire is not described.

**Riders and fate.** Princess Rhaena, daughter of Aenys I, from 32 AC until Rhaena's death at Harrenhal in 73 AC; Queen Helaena, from about her eleventh year until 130 AC. When Helaena died, Dreamfyre rose in the Dragonpit with a roar and snapped two of her chains. In the Storming of the Dragonpit in 130 AC she was the only one of the four dragons there to break free. A bolt took one of her eyes; half-blind, she flew into the great dome, which cracked, and half of it fell on her (F&B, "Rhaenyra Overthrown").

**Its moment.**
- **The Dragonpit, 130 AC.** She is why the tile's Dragonpit lies broken. On the tile: the Dragonpit on Rhaenys's Hill. For the tile: a pale blue dragon rising inside the ruined ring, circling under the broken ribs of the dome, and away; nothing of the mob or the fall.
- **Elsewhere on the tile.** In 54 AC she came down on King's Landing out of the clouds like a storm, as Septon Barth wrote; in 56 AC Rhaena scattered her daughter's ashes on the wind from her back; at Casterly Rock she was given a whole stable; and while searching for Aerea she was seen from Oldtown and Highgarden to the Arbor (F&B, "Birth, Death, and Betrayal"; "Triumphs and Tragedies"; "A Surfeit of Rulers").

**The series.** HOTD drew her first a smoky near-black with dark purple wings, and later dark violet with pink markings, antler-like horns and spines down her neck (TV Tropes). The books' pale blue and silver are nearly the opposite.

## 10. Meleys, the Red Queen

**Size.**
- **At each age.** She had never been ridden when Alyssa claimed her in 75 AC, and was among the swiftest dragons in Westeros, easily outflying Caraxes and Vhagar (F&B, "Policy, Progeny, and Pain"). By 129 AC she had grown lazy, though fearsome when roused (F&B, "The Blacks and the Greens"), and old and cunning (TWOIAF, "Aegon II"). She was at least 54 when she died.
- **Against the others.** She might have stood a chance against the older Vhagar alone, but not against Vhagar and Sunfyre together (F&B, "The Red Dragon and the Gold"). Gelo e Fogo reads TPATQ as making her one of the largest and oldest dragons of the Dance (**unverified**). About Caraxes's size, half Vhagar's **(judgement)**.
- **Against Drogon.** Some 2½ to 5 times **(judgement)**.

**Shape.** The books make her swift and nothing more. For the model **(choice)**: compact and swept back, built for speed.

**Colours.** Scarlet scales, which gave her her name; pink membranes in her wings; and a crest, horns and claws as bright as copper (F&B, "The Red Dragon and the Gold"). Her claws are copper, not black. Her fire is not described. To tell her from Caraxes **(choice)**: scarlet with pink and copper against his plain blood red, and compact against his long and lean.

**Riders and fate.** Princess Alyssa, 75 to 84 AC; Princess Rhaenys, the Queen Who Never Was, 87 to 129 AC. At Rook's Rest in 129 AC she closed her jaws on Sunfyre's neck as Vhagar fell on them both; all three crashed, and she died with Rhaenys. Her head was drawn through King's Landing on a cart.

**Its moment.**
- **Rook's Rest, 129 AC.** She came over the sea from Dragonstone, scarlet in the sun; the scorpions could not hurt her; then the fight of three dragons a thousand feet up (F&B, "The Red Dragon and the Gold"). Rook's Rest is not on the tile, and it is a battle.
- **On the tile instead: the race, 75 AC.** When Alyssa flew with her brothers Aemon and Baelon, Meleys easily beat Caraxes and Vhagar (F&B). For the tile: three dragons racing round King's Landing, the red one pulling ahead. Also on the tile: she was to fly over the Velaryon fleet as it closed the Gullet, the sea between Blackwater Bay and Dragonstone (F&B, "The Blacks and the Greens"), and in 89 AC Rhaenys flew her to Highgarden (F&B).

**The series.** HOTD gives her a hawk-like head with small spikes and no fin on her tail (TV Tropes). The scene the series is known for, Meleys bursting up through the floor of the Dragonpit at Aegon II's crowning, is the series' alone: in the books Rhaenys sat on Rhaenyra's council on Dragonstone when the war began.

## 11. Sheepstealer

**Size.**
- **At each age.** He hatched when the Old King was young: in or after 35 AC, or after 48 AC; either way younger than Vermithor. He went unclaimed and lived wild on Dragonstone (F&B, "The Red Dragon and the Gold"): by the wiki's reckoning for more than seventy years, so that he was well over fifty when Nettles won him in 129 AC.
- **Against the others.** Grand Maester Munkun wrote of a skinny brown girl on a skinny brown dragon, but Munkun never saw them (F&B, "Rhaenyra Triumphant"). The Cannibal drove him off at the Sowing. Large but lean, smaller than the Cannibal, and perhaps as big as Caraxes **(judgement)**.
- **Against Drogon.** Some 2 to 4½ times **(judgement)**.

**Shape.** Thin, if Munkun is right. Nothing else. For the model **(choice)**: the rangiest and roughest of the eleven, thin in the neck, with ragged edges to his wings, as a dragon no keeper ever groomed.

**Colours.** An ugly mud brown (F&B, "The Red Dragon and the Gold"). His fire is not described.

**Character.** Not aggressive unless provoked, but ill-tempered; fond of mutton. He ate the odd sheepdog but never harmed a shepherd (same).

**Riders and fate.** Nettles, his only rider. At the Sowing he killed more would-be riders than the other three dragons together. He fought at the Gullet, laired in the Dragonpit after King's Landing fell, and hunted Aemond with Daemon from Maidenpool. When Rhaenyra named Nettles a traitor they left, last seen over the Bay of Crabs. He was later reported at Crackclaw Point and in the Mountains of the Moon; in 134 AC royal soldiers met him and a ragged Nettles in a cave there, sixteen men died, and the two flew deeper into the mountains, never recorded again (F&B, "The Lysene Spring and the End of Regency"). The Vale clans' tales of a fire witch may be of them (TWOIAF, "The Vale").

**Its moment.**
- **Dragonstone, 129 AC.** Nettles won him by leaving a freshly killed sheep out for him every morning until he let her climb on (F&B, "The Red Dragon and the Gold"). On the tile: Dragonstone, and the crownlands' flock and shepherd. For the tile: a brown dragon swooping on a field, lifting a sheep, and leaving the shepherd be.
- **The Mountains of the Moon, after 130 AC.** Last seen flying into them (F&B). On the tile: the Vale's mountains about the Giant's Lance. A brown dragon going over the peaks, and not coming back.
- The Battle of the Gullet, at the mouth of Blackwater Bay, is on the tile, but it is a battle.

**The series.** HOTD makes him gaunt, with a crocodile-like head, snaggle teeth, an underbite and crooked wingtips (TV Tropes). The books give only the brown and the thinness.

## 12. Terrax

**Is there such a dragon?** Yes, in one paragraph of TWOIAF ("Beyond the Free Cities: Sothoryos"). He is not in F&B, TPATQ, The Rogue Prince or A Song of Ice and Fire, and the wiki lists no other book for him, TRotD included. He is not a Targaryen dragon and not of the Targaryen era.

**Size, shape and colours.** None is given.

**Rider and fate.** Jaenara Belaerys, a dragonrider of Valyria before the Doom. She flew Terrax farther south into Sothoryos than anyone had gone before, and came back after three years having found only jungle, desert and mountains; she said Sothoryos was as large as Essos, a land without end. His end is not known.

**Its moment.** The three-year flight over Sothoryos. Not on the tile, which is Westeros alone.

**The series.** Not in it.

**What the chart's Terrax most likely is.** This dragon: the name is spelled the same, and fan artists have painted him (one painting is on the wiki). His place on the chart, between the Cannibal and Silverwing, and his look are the artist's own invention, since the books give neither. The similar-sounding Tyraxes, Joffrey Velaryon's dragon in the Dance, does not fit that place: in 129 AC he was young, a little smaller than Arrax and Vermax, and not yet big enough for battle (as the wiki gives it). His colours are not in the books either; an artist reports the author describing him as pale violet with dark purple horns and purple fire (**unverified**). The wiki suggests the name nods to Terrax the Tamer, a Marvel villain from the comics the author loved (**unverified**).

**For the tile.** Nothing in the books to build from, and no place on the tile. If he is built anyway, he is a free design and should be named as one.

## 14. Scale

**Drogon, the one measured dragon.** His wings span twenty feet in ADWD. With the tile's own span of 1.35 times the length, that is about 4.5 m from nose to tail tip: about the length of a real car. On the tile he is 14 long with a span of 19, beside a car 7 long, so against the car he is already about twice the Drogon of ADWD. That is kept here.

**How big the great dragons were against him, by the books' hints (judgement).** Arrax, about a dozen years old, was a fifth of Vhagar's size (F&B, "A Son for a Son"). Young dragons of that age were about the size of a warhorse (Moondancer) and could carry one rider, as Drogon barely can in ADWD; taking Arrax as one to two Drogons makes Vhagar five to ten. The throats agree: a mounted man down Vhagar's gullet, an aurochs or a mammoth down Balerion's (AGOT ch. 13), against a dragon head in ADWD a little larger than a horse's. That puts Vhagar somewhere around 25 to 45 m long and Balerion around 30 to 60 m. "Size" in the books is loose; here it is read as length, since that is what the eye compares.

**Squeezed to fit.** At the tile's Drogon of 14, the books' ratios would make Balerion 90 to 180 long, on a board 208 by 226. So the scale below squeezes the jump from Drogon to the great dragons, and keeps the books' proportions between the great dragons themselves, since those are what make each one known: Balerion the biggest by a clear margin, Vhagar close behind, Caraxes half of Vhagar, and the others in a band about half of Vhagar. One number to turn: Vhagar's length. Everything else follows from the column "Against Vhagar".

Spans are 1.35 times the length, as for the tile's three, except where a dragon's shape is meant to differ: Balerion's broader wings, and Caraxes's longer neck, which lengthens him without widening his wings.

What this means on the board:
- Balerion at 77 across is over a third of the board's width, and his shadow would cover King's Landing and its bay at once, as the books' towns lay in his shadow. Vhagar at 59 is twice the width of the Red Keep's ring.
- The earlier note held a dragon to a span of 20 so that none was wider than a castle. The great dragons break that on purpose. They need height, Balerion well above the Hightower's 48, and wide turns, and are best seen one or two at a time.
- The books make the old giants slow (Balerion in his last years; Vhagar against Meleys and Arrax) and Meleys and Caraxes quick. Fewer, slower wingbeats for the biggest would follow.
