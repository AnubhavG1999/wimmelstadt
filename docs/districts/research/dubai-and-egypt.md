# Dubai and Egypt district: research notes

Checked 18–19 Nov 2026. † = from memory, not re-verified (the shared web-search budget ran out part-way). About 1,800 words: the asked-for lists would not fit in 1,600.

## DUBAI (with Abu Dhabi)

**1. Character.** A flat tan coast with one needle-thin silver tower far taller than all else, a white sail on an islet and a palm-shaped island. One very wide road runs between walls of glass towers, an elevated metro beside it. Old Dubai is the opposite: low sand-coloured houses with wind towers round a creek of wooden boats; inland, red dunes then bare mountains.

**2. Ground.** Sheikh Zayed Road (E11) parallels the coast to the Abu Dhabi border, 7–8 lanes each way, Metro Red Line alongside. Along it from the south-west (order†): Marina and Bluewaters, the Palm, Burj Al Arab, Downtown (inland side), Museum of the Future, the Frame, then the Creek (Al Fahidi on its south-west bank, Deira's souks opposite). Abu Dhabi is further south-west: Yas Island first, the Grand Mosque at the city's mainland entrance†. Jebel Hafeet is inland at Al Ain on the Oman border; Jebel Jais far north in Ras Al Khaimah.

**3. Landmarks.**
1. **Burj Khalifa, Fountain, Mall.** 828 m† stepped silver-blue spire, three-lobed plan; lake with a line of jets at its foot; the mall a low wide box; a boulevard loops round.
2. **Burj Al Arab.** 321 m white sail, helipad disc near the top, on an islet 280 m offshore; one private curving bridge.
3. **Palm Jumeirah.** Trunk, fronds, crescent; Atlantis (salmon-pink twin blocks joined over an arch†) at the apex; monorail up the trunk, road tunnel to the crescent†.
4. **Old Dubai.** Creek with open wooden abras; Al Fahidi's coral-stone houses with wind towers and a square fort, Dubai's oldest building; Deira's roofed gold and spice souks†; walking lanes, roads along the banks.
5. **Museum of the Future.** Upright silver ring with a hole, calligraphy-shaped windows, on a green mound by the twin Emirates Towers†, heading the tower canyon; road and metro alongside.
6. **Dubai Frame.** 150 × 95 m gold† picture frame in Zabeel Park; old Dubai to its north, the tower canyon south.
7. **Marina.** Cayan Tower (306 m, titanium-coloured, twisting 90°); Ain Dubai (250 m wheel, 48 cabins) on Bluewaters Island, reached by bridge†.
8. **Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque.** White marble, 82 domes, four minarets, courtyard, reflecting pools; roads pass around only.
9. **Yas Marina Circuit and Ferrari World.** 5.281 km, 16 turns, floodlit, round a yacht marina; the W Abu Dhabi hotel (two blocks and a bridge under a curved gridshell) straddles the track; the pit exit tunnels under the track. Ferrari World's vast red three-pointed roof† is alongside.

**Does Ain Dubai run?** It shut in March 2022, reopened 26 Dec 2024 and paused in spring 2026 as a security precaution. Its official site now lists hours and takes bookings; I found no dated reopening notice.

**4. Roads and car culture.**
- Jebel Hafeet road: 11.7 km, 60 turns, three lanes (two up, one down); car park and hotel on top.
- Jebel Jais: 36 km of hairpins up the UAE's highest mountain; 2.83 km zip-line.
- Yas Marina hosts Formula 1's finale (3–6 Dec 2026).
- Dubai Autodrome, Motor City: 5.39 km, 17 turns, FIA Grade 1; 24H Dubai each January.
- Dubai Police supercars: white with dark green, since 2013; Bugatti Veyron, Aston Martin One-77, Lamborghinis; tourist-area patrols only.
- Emirates National Auto Museum: pyramid-shaped, about 200 cars, off E11, 45 minutes from Abu Dhabi.
- Dune convoys of white Land Cruisers and Patrols; camel racing with robot jockeys†.

**5. Businesses.** Emirates (red Arabic-calligraphy wordmark†); ADNOC petrol stations (blue, falcon emblem†); Ravi Restaurant, Satwa, since 1978 (plain canteen, pavement tables†); Arabian Tea House, Al Fahidi (courtyard of an old pearl merchant's house); Al Mallah, Satwa (green neon, shawarma†); Gold Souk jewellers (windows solid with gold†). Parody: Abra Kadabra water taxi (عبرة كادابرا); Burj Al Karak tea kiosk (برج الكرك); Mahattat Al Jamal, "Camel Station" petrol (محطة الجمل).

**6. Street life.** Taxis: sand-cream body, roof colour by operator (red, yellow, green, orange, blue, a few purple); pink roof means a woman driver. Driverless metro on a viaduct, gold shell-shaped stations†. Delivery motorbikes, air-conditioned bus shelters, toll gantries, date palms in the medians†. Signs in Arabic and English.

**7. Scenes.** Fountain show with a police Bugatti parked for selfies; abras criss-crossing while a dhow is loaded by hand; a 4x4 convoy cresting a red dune above a camel train; supercars queuing for a hotel valet; race cars passing under the Yas hotel, yachts watching.

**8. Watch out.**
- Burj Al Arab: closed since April 2026 for an 18-month restoration (to about October 2027). Drone debris started a small façade fire on 28 Feb 2026; a Palm hotel and the airports were also hit. Model everything intact; leave the conflict out.
- Al Fahidi Fort (Dubai Museum): closed since 2021; in Sept 2026 its reopening was "weeks or months" away.
- Dubai Fountain reopened 1 Oct 2025 after a rebuild.
- Trademarks: Ferrari name and horse, Emirates, Atlantis, W, the police crest, car badges: shapes, not logos.
- Mosque: complete, white, calm; nothing parked in its courtyard, no signs or gags on or beside it. No Qur'anic text as decoration, no rulers' portraits, no caricatured national dress; leave the gulf unnamed (name disputed).

## EGYPT

**1. Character.** Three sand-coloured pyramids stepping down in size along a diagonal, on a desert shelf that stops dead against a dense brown city. A green river ribbon with white triangular sails and a lattice tower on an island; upriver, column-forest temples and a cliff with four seated giants. On the east edge, a ruler-straight canal carrying ships far too big for it.

**2. Ground.** Down the Nile, south to north: Abu Simbel on Lake Nasser's west shore, 230 km south-west of Aswan; Luxor, with Karnak and Luxor Temple on the east bank and tombs in the west-bank hills†; Cairo, with Gezira Island mid-river; the delta, with Alexandria on its north-west coast†. The pyramids stand on the desert edge about 9 km west of the Nile and 13 km south-west of central Cairo; the Grand Egyptian Museum is 2 km north-west of them. The canal runs 193 km from Port Said (north) past Ismailia to Suez (south). Farmland is a narrow strip, then sand; Cairo is tight irregular mid-rise blocks under flyovers†.

**3. Landmarks.**
1. **Giza pyramids.** Khufu (largest, north-east); Khafre to its south-west (slightly smaller but looks taller on higher ground; smooth casing left at the tip†); Menkaure (smallest, further south-west); queens' pyramids beside Khufu and Menkaure. No road through: private cars and tour buses barred since April 2025; electric shuttles from a new gate on the Fayoum road, to the south.
2. **Great Sphinx.** 73 m long, 20 m high, low on the plateau's east side, facing east to the city, at the foot of Khafre's causeway.
3. **Grand Egyptian Museum.** Low sand-coloured wedge, translucent stone front with triangle patterns†, hanging obelisk; side walls aligned on Khufu and Menkaure. Opened 1 Nov 2025, public from 4 Nov.
4. **Cairo Tower and feluccas.** 187 m concrete tube in open lotus latticework, round crown, on Gezira Island, which bridges cross†; feluccas below, open boats with one tall white lateen sail†.
5. **Karnak and Luxor Temple.** Karnak, 2.5 km north of Luxor: twin-tower sandstone pylon, hall of 134 giant columns, obelisks, sacred lake. The walkable 2.7 km Avenue of Sphinxes (reopened Nov 2021) joins it to Luxor Temple.
6. **Abu Simbel.** Four 20 m seated kings cut in an ochre cliff, a smaller temple beside; lifted 65 m in the 1960s into an artificial hill on the lake shore.
7. **Canal crossings.** Suez Canal Bridge at El Qantara (cable-stayed, 70 m clearance, road over); El Ferdan railway swing bridge (the world's longest swing span); road tunnels north of Suez, at Ismailia and south of Port Said; pontoon bridges that swing against the banks.
8. **Bibliotheca Alexandrina (2002).** Tilted disc 160 m across sloping to the sea, grey granite wall carved with 120 scripts, on the seafront road†.
9. **Lost lighthouse.** Three tiers (square, octagon, cylinder), over 100 m, ruined by the 1303 earthquake; Qaitbay's fort, a squat cream castle†, was built on the spot in 1480 from its stone.

**Ever Given.** Aground 23–29 March 2021. 400 m long, "EVERGREEN" in huge capitals on a dark-green† hull. Northbound in the single-lane stretch about 10 km from Suez, she lay diagonally, bow in the east bank, stern against the west (most reports; one says the reverse). One small excavator (yellow†) dug at the bow while tugs and dredgers worked; over 400 ships queued.

**4. Roads and car culture.**
- Pharaons Rally (1982–2015): desert rally-raid that started and finished at the foot of the pyramids; defunct.
- Automobile and Touring Club of Egypt classic parade (April 2026: about 60 cars of the 1930s–40s through historic Cairo); Cairo Classic Meet.
- 6th October Bridge, the elevated highway across Cairo†; Tahya Misr bridge, record-wide cable-stayed†; Cairo–Alexandria Desert Road†; Alexandria's Corniche†.
- Everyday classics: locally built Fiat 128s, Ladas, Peugeot 504 estates, Beetles†.

**5. Businesses.** Abou Tarek (koshary; three or four floors on Champollion Street, the founder's cart displayed in front); El Fishawy, Khan el-Khalili (mirrors, carved wood, brass tables in the alley†); Marriott Mena House, 1886 (palm garden below the pyramids; historic wing shut for renovation); Café Riche, 1908, Talaat Harb Street; El Abd patisserie (queues†); EgyptAir (Horus head on a navy tail†). Parody: Koshary Abu Turbo (كشري أبو تيربو); Felucca Express river taxi (فلوكة إكسبريس); Banzeenet El Haram, "Pyramid Petrol" (بنزينة الهرم).

**6. Street life.** Cairo taxis: newer ones white with a black band, older ones black and white; Alexandria's yellow and black. White microbus vans, door open†. Tuk-tuks banned from Cairo's main streets since Nov 2025: side lanes only. East Nile monorail running since May 2026; Alexandria's blue-and-cream Raml tram stopped on 1 April 2026 for a rebuild (back 2027–28). Giza's camels and horses wait in set zones. Bread cyclists with a tray on the head, rooftop satellite dishes†. Arabic signs; Eastern Arabic numerals on plates†.

**7. Scenes.** The stuck ship with one tiny digger at the bow, tugs at the stern, ships queuing in the lake; Giza shuttle bus, camel line-up, tourists "pinching" a pyramid tip; feluccas tacking past the tower above a bridge jammed with taxis; Luxor dawn balloons over the west bank†; a classic-car parade round a downtown square.

**8. Watch out.**
- Changed: Khufu's boat left its museum beside the Great Pyramid for the new museum (2021); the Giza entrance moved to the Fayoum road; Cairo's trams are gone; Groppi (Talaat Harb Square) is under restoration, reopening unconfirmed.
- Trademarks: "EVERGREEN" and the ship's name (invent one), EgyptAir's Horus, Marriott.
- Pyramids and temples are tombs and sacred sites: no climbers, no mummy or alien jokes. Mosques and Coptic churches: calm skyline, no signs or gags attached. The digger driver disliked the memes: make him the hero.
- Leave out soldiers, checkpoints, war memorials, protest squares, leaders' portraits and flags. Both canal banks are really Egyptian (Sinai lies east): draw no border on it.
