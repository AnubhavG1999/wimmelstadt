**Merseyside / Liverpool district brief**

Searches confirmed the stadium and Goodison facts. Landmark, shop and livery details come from general knowledge and are marked "verify" where less certain. No web page contained instructions.

**1. Character**
From above, Liverpool is dark red-brick and slate terraces in tight grids, broken by a dock wall of black and sandstone warehouses along the river. The skyline moment is the Pier Head: three pale Edwardian stone buildings, the tallest topped with two bronze-green Liver Birds, beside a modern white Museum of Liverpool. Two football grounds sit either side of one big green park: one a sea of red seats, the other a brick bowl. A third, new brick-and-steel bowl sits on the water at Bramley-Moore Dock. Palette: red brick, sandstone cream, slate grey, river grey-brown.

**2. Ground**
- Mostly flat, rising gently to a ridge inland (Everton and Anfield sit on it). The Mersey frontage runs along the west edge.
- North docks (Bramley-Moore, Wellington, Sandon, Huskisson): a chain of enclosed water basins behind a long dock wall, with Regent Road (A565) as the waterfront artery.
- Royal Albert Dock: a square of five-storey red-brick warehouses with cast-iron columns around a water basin, south of Pier Head.
- Stanley Park: a large green oval with a lake and the Gladstone Conservatory, between the two grounds.
- Streets: dense terraced grids around both grounds; narrow cobbled lanes (Mathew Street) and Georgian squares in the centre.

**3. Landmarks**
1. **Royal Liver Building**: pale stone, about 90 m, two clock towers each with a bronze Liver Bird; faces the river on Pier Head. The Strand runs in front; no road through it.
2. **Cunard Building and Port of Liverpool Building** (with the Liver Building, the Three Graces): Cunard is a flat-topped Italian-palazzo block; Port of Liverpool has a dome and corner turrets. They sit side by side with the Liver Building. Roads pass in front, none through.
3. **Anfield (Liverpool FC)**: oval red-seat bowl, four stands. The Kop is behind the goal facing Walton Breck Road; Main and Centenary Stands on the touchlines; Anfield Road stand rebuilt (reopened 2024), capacity about 61,000. Pale cantilever roofs, red and white trim. Anfield Road, Walton Breck Road and Oakfield Road ring it; none through. It is on the north-east edge of Stanley Park.
4. **Goodison Park (Everton)**: compact old ground, brick and steel facades, double-decker Main Stand, the church of St Luke wedged into one corner, royal blue seats and trim. West edge of Stanley Park. Goodison Road and Bullens Road hug it; none through.
5. **Hill Dickinson Stadium (Everton, Bramley-Moore Dock)**: brick-clad bowl with a pale roof, set in an old dock with the dock wall kept. Capacity 52,769; opened August 2025. Listed hydraulic tower and pump house stand beside it. On the Mersey north of the centre; Regent Road runs along the landward side. About 2.5 to 3 km from Goodison.
6. **Royal Albert Dock**: red-brick warehouse square (five storeys, arched ground floor) around a basin, with Tate Liverpool, the Beatles Story and the Maritime Museum. South of Pier Head, on the river. No road through, only pedestrian arches.
7. **Museum of Liverpool**: modern white block with large glazed ends on the Pier Head waterfront next to the Graces (opened 2011).
8. **Liverpool Cathedral and Metropolitan Cathedral**: Anglican is a huge red sandstone tower on the ridge; the Metropolitan is a round, tent-like cone with a lantern crown, white and grey. At opposite ends of Hope Street. No road through either.
9. **Mersey Ferries terminal** (Pier Head): floating landing stage with a modern glass terminal. Verify current routes.
10. **Stanley Park**: green oval and lake between the grounds, with the glass-and-iron Gladstone Conservatory. Roads run around; footpaths cross.
11. **Cavern Club (Mathew Street)**: brick vaults below street, neon sign at street level. Inland from Pier Head.
12. **Chinatown Arch (Nelson Street)**: red, green and gold pagoda-roofed gateway; the road passes under it.

**Relations.** Goodison is on Stanley Park's west edge, Anfield on the north-east edge, roughly half a mile to a mile apart, a short walk across the park. Bramley-Moore Dock is separate, about 2.5 to 3 km south-west of Goodison on the river, north of Pier Head.

**Goodison status.** Everton's men left in 2025. Goodison is Everton Women's permanent home from 2025/26, with 2026/27 fixtures listed (for example v Aston Villa, 25 October 2026). It is not being demolished. Tours restarted July 2026 per one guide. Everton in the Community and the Everton multi-academy trust are reported there; the community HQ move was only "expected" in 2025 reporting. The ground is council-owned and leased by Everton. Capacity figures conflict between sources, so treat them as unverified. I found no 2026 source on any redevelopment or housing plan.

**5. Street life**
- Buses: Arriva (teal/blue) and Stagecoach (blue/orange). Verify, as regional bus franchising may change liveries.
- Black cabs.
- Merseyrail Class 777 trains: grey with yellow ends.
- Ferries: Royal Iris of the Mersey and the Dazzle Ferry (Peter Blake geometric camouflage). Royal Iris colours: verify.
- Furniture: red phone boxes, Victorian lamp posts, dock bollards, cobbles.
- People: tourists on Beatles tours, buskers, dockside joggers.
- Matchday: red scarves and shirts at the Anfield (east) end of the park, blue at the Goodison (west) end.

**6. Small scenes**
1. Matchday crowd streaming across Stanley Park, red one way, blue the other.
2. Ferry boarding at the landing stage.
3. Cruise liner or tall ship at Pier Head.
4. Beatles busker on Mathew Street.
5. Dock cranes and a cargo ship on the north docks.
6. Wedding party at Albert Dock.

**7. Watch out**
- Everton's men left Goodison for Hill Dickinson Stadium (opened August 2025; name is a law-firm sponsor and may change). Show Goodison as a quieter Women's and community ground.
- Club crests and names are trademarks; use colours and a stylised parody.
- Hillsborough (1989): memorials at Anfield (near the Shankly Gates) and elsewhere should be left out or handled with great respect.
- Also sensitive: the 2025 Liverpool parade incident and the Diogo Jota tributes at Anfield (2025).
- I did not verify every pub or shop's current status.
