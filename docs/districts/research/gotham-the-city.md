# Gotham City, the place, for the tile

This note is for a tile of Gotham City as DC's comics draw it: DC's main continuity, the city's official map (drawn in 1998 for No Man's Land), and DC's reference works. It covers the city as a place. Batman, his vehicles and his rogues are in a second note being written beside this one. Here a character appears only where a place belongs to one, as Ace Chemicals is the Joker's and the Iceberg Lounge the Penguin's. Read on 2026-11-20.

**What the collector asked for, relayed on 2026-11-20:** Arkham Asylum and Wayne Manor on the tile as well, and a tile with the feel Gotham has. Arkham and the Manor are in the layout (section 5). The feel has a section of its own (section 4).

**What the tile takes nothing from:**

- the films: Burton's, Schumacher's, Nolan's, Reeves's and Joker;
- the television series: 1966, Gotham and The Penguin;
- the animated series;
- the Arkham games.

Where a place is known mainly from one of these, this note says so and leaves it out of the recommendation. One complication: in 1992 the comics took on the 1989 film's look on purpose (section 3), so a comics Gotham cannot be wholly free of it. The tile works from what the comics say of the look, not from the film's sets.

**What North America already has.** The Chrysler Building, the Empire State Building, the Flatiron Building, the Golden Gate Bridge and Liberty in her pond (`app/src/tiles/north-america/NOTES.md`). Gotham uses none of them. Its harbour statue is the comics' blindfolded Lady Justice (item 42), not a second Liberty.

## How sure each statement is

- **[w Page; issue]**: from that page of the DC Database (dc.fandom.com), with the comic the page cites. **Unless a second source is named, the statement rests on that single fan wiki page.** "Uncited" means the wiki gives no issue for it.
- **[wp Page]**: from that English Wikipedia article.
- **[b]**: from Eliot R. Brown's own account of drawing the official map, on his website.
- **[dc]**: from DC's own website.
- **[c Name]**: from an article about the comics by an outside writer, read in full. One writer's reading.
- **[s]**: from a web search's summary of pages I did not open. Weak.
- **[mem]**: my own memory of the comics, not confirmed today. Check it before building.
- **[real]**: a fact about the real world, from memory.
- **[tile]**: this note's proposal. It is not in the comics.
- **[film]**, **[tv]**, **[game]**: from a film, a television series or a game. Not to be used.

**Caveat on sources, read first.**

- The DC Database refuses the plain fetcher, as Fandom did for the earlier notes. Its public interface (`api.php`) answered in the desktop app's browser pane, and about sixty of its pages were read through it as wikitext with their footnotes.
- Its article pages and image files sit behind a bot check, so **the map itself could not be looked at**. The sketch of the map in section 1 is assembled from what the wiki's pages say the map shows, and from its maker's account. It is not a copy of the map.
- Wikipedia's Gotham City article files Crime Alley, the Iceberg Lounge, Ace Chemicals, the Monarch Theatre and Old Gotham under the Arkham games. All of them were in the comics first, as the DC Database's first appearances show. Wikipedia was used here for the location, the Destroyer storyline, the Batcave, the Bat-Signal and Wayne Manor.
- No comic was opened. Most of what is said of looks rests on one fan wiki, and the comics themselves draw the city differently from artist to artist (section 3).

## 1. The geography

### Where Gotham is

- **New Jersey.** Wikipedia gives this as the comics' tradition [wp Gotham City]. It cites:
  - Amazing World of DC Comics #14 (1977);
  - World's Finest Comics #259 (1979);
  - Detective Comics #503 (1983);
  - Batman: The Cult #4 (1988);
  - Mayfair's Atlas of the DC Universe (1990);
  - Batman: Shadow of the Bat Annual #1 (1993).
- **Gotham County.** The DC Database puts the city in Gotham County, New Jersey [w Gotham City]. The Essential Batman Encyclopedia calls it Kane County: a county on the eastern seaboard, with rolling countryside round the city [w Gotham County].
- **Metropolis across the bay.** Gotham and Metropolis are sometimes twin cities on either side of Delaware Bay, with Metropolis in Delaware (World's Finest #259; the Atlas of 1990; Secret Files & Origins Guide to the DC Universe 2000) [wp Gotham City].
- **At about Atlantic City.** The Atlas's map of 1990 puts the city there [c iFanboy].

### The official map

- **Who drew it.** Eliot R. Brown, for No Man's Land, at the call of Denny O'Neil, then group editor of the Batman books, in late 1998. Chuck Dixon gave him the list of place names. The draft is dated July 1998 and the final 2 September 1998 [b].
- **Where it appeared.** It was published with No Man's Land in 1999 and reprinted several times. In Batman: Gotham City Secret Files & Origins #1 (April 2000) its places are numbered and keyed in a list [b]. It has defined the city's geography ever since [s Smithsonian; ComicsAlliance].
- **Its model.** Manhattan with Brooklyn folded in, the east side drawn as Manhattan's own mirrored to face west [b].
- **What it holds** [b]:
  - the city as essentially one island, with bridges and tunnels to the mainland that the government blows in the story;
  - bridges like the Manhattan, Brooklyn and Triborough Bridges, with Chicago-like bridges in the middle of town;
  - highways that ring and cross the island;
  - docks, a business district, fine houses, meat-packing and garment districts;
  - Chinatown, the Upper East Side, a Diamond District and a large park in the middle;
  - a stadium in the north, and an airport named after Archie Goodwin;
  - a few old steam tunnels under the river that the public does not know of.
- **Later revisions** [w]:
  - the Narrows were added as an island after Batman Begins [w Gotham Narrows];
  - Zero Year's map made Amusement Mile residential [w Amusement Mile];
  - the Prime Earth map put Arkham back on an island of its own [w Gotham Narrows].

### Water

- **The Gotham River** runs between the whole city and the mainland: Sommerset Township in the west and Bristol Township in the north, on the No Man's Land map [w Gotham River; first in Batman #86].
  - It was mined while the city was cut off [w].
  - It freezes in hard winters: one creature fell through its ice, and the police once pulled a man frozen in a block of it [w].
- **The Sprang River**, a tributary, crosses the city between Uptown and Midtown. Burnley is north of it and Coventry south [w Burnley; Coventry]. Gotham Harbor is at its eastern end [w Gotham Harbor]. Mercey Island has the Gotham River on its west and the Sprang on its east [w Mercey Island].
- **The Finger River** runs between Midtown and Downtown [w Coventry; Gotham Narrows].
- **Gotham Harbor**, also called Miller Harbor, is the city's cargo harbour [w Gotham Harbor; first in Batman #20].
- **Gotham Bay** holds Blackgate Isle [w Blackgate Penitentiary] and the island of the Statue of Justice, which overlooks the docklands [w Statue of Justice]. The Earth-One statue faced the Atlantic [w]. That the bay lies south and east of the city is my reading of these pages [w + mem].
- **Two points of land** [w]:
  - Tricorner, a roughly triangular island holding the city's southernmost point, joined to the rest by three bridges [w Tricorner; first in Robin vol. 2 #28];
  - Cape Carmine, the city's easternmost point [w Gotham City East End].

### The islands, north to south, and the mainland

- **Mercey Island, called Arkham Island.** It lies off the coast north-west of the city, between the Gotham River and the Sprang [w Mercey Island; first in Batman: Shadow of the Bat #38].
- **The northern island, Uptown:**
  - Otisburg, the northernmost district [w Otisburg];
  - the Hill, near the docks [w];
  - Burnley, Newtown, the Bowery and Robbinsville [w];
  - Park Row and the East End [w Park Row; Gotham City East End];
  - Gotham Heights [w];
  - the zoo, in Uptown by Batman: The Ultimate Guide (2022) [w Gotham Zoo];
  - Amusement Mile, on a promontory at the north-east corner [w Amusement Mile].
- **The middle island, Midtown:** Coventry, Robinson Park, and the Upper East and Upper West Sides [w Coventry; Robinson Park].
- **The southern island, Downtown:**
  - Old Gotham, the first village, in the south [w Gotham City];
  - the Financial District, where the first Pinkney building stood [w Old Gotham];
  - the Diamond District [b; w Gotham Narrows];
  - Chinatown on the west side [w Burnside Bridge];
  - the City Hall District at the east end [w Gotham City Hall].
- **The mainland:**
  - **Bristol**, directly north, where the rich live. Wayne Manor stands in its Crest Hill neighbourhood, about fifteen miles outside the city [w Bristol Township].
  - **Sommerset**, north-west and west [w Sommerset Township]: suburbs, the first Arkham Asylum, Dayton National Forest, Sommerset Woods, Slaughter Swamp, and the airport at Little Stockton.
  - **Burnside**, west across the river from Chinatown. It is a district only since the New 52 (Batgirl vol. 4 #35, 2014); before that it was part of Sommerset [w Burnside].

## 2. Districts and landmarks

Each entry gives where it is, its look as the comics draw it, and where it first appeared, as the DC Database gives it. Where the comics give no look, the entry says so, and the tile's own proposal comes in section 5.

### Old Gotham and Downtown

- **Old Gotham.** The architectural scheme of Cyrus Pinkney and Judge Solomon Wayne, which began in the 1840s with the first "Gotham Style" building, in what is now the middle of the Financial District [w Old Gotham; first in Legends of the Dark Knight #27, 1992]. Section 3 gives what the style is. Its streets include Finger Avenue, Logerquist Avenue and Strip Avenue [w Old Gotham].
- **GCPD Headquarters** [w GCPD Headquarters; first in Detective Comics #27]:
  - built in 1899, where the streets meet at Five Points on the lower East Side, as part of Pinkney's Old Gotham;
  - the old building was later left empty when the police moved to a newer one, and the earthquake of 1998 destroyed it.
  - Look: Gothic of Pinkney's kind and nothing more specific [w]. The current building's look was not found.
- **The Bat-Signal** [wp Bat-Signal]:
  - a searchlight with a bat emblem fixed over its lens, throwing a bat onto the clouds of the night sky;
  - on the roof of police headquarters;
  - first in Detective Comics #60 (February 1942).
  - Its comics history:
    - taken down when Batman and the police fell out in Gotham Central (2002 on);
    - an improvised one smashed by Gordon in No Man's Land (1999);
    - a new one put up on the GCPD roof in Batman Eternal #52 (2015).
  - Once made into a jack-o'-lantern with an orange bulb and painted eyes, in Batman: Haunted Knight (1996).
  - The searchlight in the 1966 series and the films is [tv] and [film].
- **Wayne Tower** [w Wayne Tower; first in Legends of the Dark Knight #27, 1992]:
  - the head office of Wayne Enterprises, first called the Corolla Building, which Bruce Wayne bought after the Destroyer's bombings;
  - before the earthquake it was the tallest building in Old Gotham, over 1,000 feet [uncited];
  - **twelve gargoyles stand round its top, one guarding each way into the city, and a hidden thirteenth was added later for those who come by air** [uncited]. The thirteenth catches Bruce Wayne after he is kicked from the top floor in Batman vol. 2 #2 (2011) [mem, for the issue];
  - an observation deck under a great glass dome at the top;
  - smaller linked buildings for escape routes;
  - linked to the railway lines round it [uncited, and close to the monorail hub of Batman Begins, which is film].
- **The Wayne Foundation building** [w Wayne Foundation]:
  - Bruce Wayne lived in its penthouse from the late 1960s to the early 1980s [wp Wayne Manor];
  - a great ornamental tree in the middle of the building hid passages down to the garage;
  - the garage was a second Batcave, with a private lift up to the penthouse.
- **Gotham Clock Tower** [w Gotham Clock Tower; first in Showcase '94 #12]:
  - in Old Gotham, built and owned by Wayne Enterprises, and strengthened against earthquakes;
  - Oracle's home and headquarters, and so a place of hers.
  - Look: a large clock tower, no more given.
- **Gotham City Hall** [w Gotham City Hall; first in Batman #30]:
  - in the City Hall District at the east end of Downtown, on Brown's map;
  - moved to the Upper West Side after No Man's Land, and back again in the New 52.
  - Look: not found.
- **Gotham Cathedral** [w Gotham Cathedral]:
  - In the comics: built in 1790 in Gothic by an undying man, Mister Whisper, as a trap for souls. This is the story Batman: Gothic (Legends of the Dark Knight #6 to #10, 1990) [w; the issues mem].
  - The wiki gives its first appearance as the 1989 film, whose climax is set there [film].
  - So it is in the comics, but it is known mainly from the film. See section 8.
- **The Diamond District.** Brown added it to the map [b]. It is Downtown, south of the Finger River [w Gotham Narrows]. The wiki has no page for it, and its look was not found.
- **Chinatown** [w Gotham City Chinatown; first in Detective Comics #39]: on Downtown's west side, at the Brown Bridge. Its tea house and triads belong to stories, not to its look.
- **The Gotham Gazette** [w Gotham Gazette; first in Batman #4, 1941]: the city's paper, syndicated across the country. Its building's look was not found.
- **Penguin Umbrellas Inc.**, the Penguin's first company, a front for smuggling [dc; Batman #70, 1952]. No place is given.

### Midtown

- **Robinson Park** [w Robinson Park; first in Batman #405, Year One, 1987]:
  - the largest park in the city, about 300 acres, in Midtown, dedicated in 1784;
  - beside it the Knightstone Sports Complex;
  - in it [mostly uncited, and much like Central Park]:
    - Finger Castle beside Mike Lake, from which the whole park can be seen;
    - the Robinson Park Reservoir, the city's largest skating rink in winter;
    - an Alice in Wonderland statue near the entrance gates;
    - a soldier on horseback near the South Gate on Elliot Street;
    - the Forum of the Twelve Caesars at the north end;
    - a monument to the civilian heroes of No Man's Land;
    - a carnival now and then.
  - Poison Ivy held the park in No Man's Land [mem].
- **Coventry** [w Coventry; first in Batman: No Man's Land #1]: a middle-class district north and west of the park, with docks on its west side.
- **The stadium.** Brown put a large one in the north [b]. The wiki names a Knightsdome in Otisburg [w Otisburg].

### Uptown and the East End

- **The East End** [w Gotham City East End; first in Batman #404, Year One, 1987]: the notorious part of the northern island, round Crime Alley.
- **Park Row, called Crime Alley** [w Park Row; first in Detective Comics #457, 1976]:
  - once an elegant East End street; the stretch round the old cinema is the one called Crime Alley;
  - the Thomas Wayne Memorial Clinic is there, Leslie Thompkins's [w Park Row; Wayne Foundation];
  - on his return to the city, Nightwing mended a broken street lamp there [w];
  - it is where the Waynes were killed. See section 8.
- **The Monarch Theatre** [w Monarch Theater]:
  - the cinema the Waynes had left that night, after seeing The Mark of Zorro (1940) [w]. The film is named in The Dark Knight Returns (1986) [mem];
  - built of heart pine in 1928 [w, uncited];
  - **it had no name in the comics until the 1989 film named it** [w]. The comics have used the name since.
- **The Bowery** [w Bowery; first in Secret Society of Super-Villains #11, 1977]: on the Uptown island and part of the East End, full of taverns that criminals use. The Sprang Bridge is in its part of town.
- **Otisburg** [w Otisburg; first in Batman: No Man's Land #1, 1999]: a residential district on the northernmost island, at the Trigate Bridge. Its name is a joke from the 1978 Superman film [w]; the district itself is the comics'.
- **Amusement Mile** [w Amusement Mile; first in Batman #569, 1999]:
  - a promontory at the city's north-east corner, named for its fairground;
  - from 1910 the Solomon brothers built nickelodeons and cinemas there, then Carlo Geraldi built an amusement park, and other attractions followed;
  - the whole fell into ruin with the city's industry, and its buildings were sold to the city's villains as hideouts;
  - the wiki likens its history to Coney Island's [w];
  - since the New 52, Haly's Circus has stood there, Nightwing's circus [w].

### Off the island: Arkham, Wayne Manor and the Batcave

- **Arkham Asylum** [w Arkham Asylum; first in Batman #258, 1974]:
  - **Name and founding.** The Elizabeth Arkham Asylum for the Criminally Insane, founded in 1921 by Amadeus Arkham on land north of the city that he had from his mother. In an early story it was Arkham Hospital, in a New England town called Arkham.
  - **First building.** In Sommerset, on the mainland north-west of the city [w Sommerset Township]. Victorian inside, until Jeremiah Arkham replaced its rooms with corridors laid out as a labyrinth. Bane blew it up just before Knightfall (1993).
  - **Second building.** Eric Mercey, a cement magnate, built a great Gothic mansion among the wooded hills of Mercey Island, and the asylum moved into it [w Mercey Island; first in Batman: Shadow of the Bat #38, 1995].
  - **Third building.** After the second was wrecked, a mix of classical and Gothic, with its main block laid out as a Panopticon.
  - **Prime Earth.** On Arkham Island from the start, its buildings full of hidden rooms and passages put in by the builder, Ambroos Lydecker. It later gave way to Arkham Tower.
  - **Arkham Asylum: A Serious House on Serious Earth** (1989, Grant Morrison and Dave McKean) based the house's plan on Glastonbury Abbey and Chartres Cathedral [s Wikipedia].
  - **The gates.** Its gates as the comics draw them were not found. The wrought-iron arch with the name in it that people remember may be the animated series' or the games' [mem]. The tile's gate is its own (section 5).
- **Wayne Manor** [w Wayne Manor; first in Detective Comics #27, 1939]:

  - **Style.** Gothic Revival, as the city is Gothic [wp].
  - **The "W".** In Batman: The Return of Bruce Wayne its plan forms a W and its gardens the shape of a bat [wp, marked "citation needed"]. A toy's delight if true. Check before building.
  - **After the earthquake** (Cataclysm, 1998) it was rebuilt as a fortress, Gothic with battlements, with solar panels and a heliport [wp].
  - **Gates.** A gate round the grounds, and a larger front gate [wp].
  - **Under it.** The hill is partly hollowed for aircraft, and an underground river is wide enough for the Batboat [wp].
  - **On Prime Earth** the city once seized it and made it Arkham Manor [w].
- **The Batcave** [wp Batcave; w Batcave]:
  - **First.** Secret underground hangars in Batman #12 (1942), and the name in the comics from Detective Comics #83 (1944) [wp]. The DC Database counts it from Batman #12 [w].
  - **Its ways in:**
    - Earth-Two: a tunnel to an old barn where the cars were kept;
    - later: a stair or a lift from behind a grandfather clock. The clock began in the 1943 serial and has been in the comics since [wp];
    - Earth-One: **a hidden door in the side of a hill, opened from the car** [w];
    - an underground stream that runs out to a river [w].
  - **Inside** [wp]:
    - a large colony of bats;
    - the trophies: **the giant Lincoln penny** (World's Finest Comics #30, 1947), **the mechanical Tyrannosaurus** ("Dinosaur Island", Batman #35, 1946), and a giant Joker playing card (seen in Forever Evil #4, 2014; where it came from is not given).   - **After the earthquake** it gained eight new levels and a computer on an "island" platform [wp].

## 3. The look of the whole city

### What the comics' makers said

- Denny O'Neil saw Batman's Gotham as Manhattan below Fourteenth Street at "eleven minutes past midnight on the coldest night in November" [wp Gotham City; his afterword to the Knightfall novel, 1994].
- Metropolis as New York by day and Gotham as New York by night is credited to Frank Miller and to John Byrne [wp; Wikipedia marks the source as weak].
- Neal Adams held that Chicago was the model: a city of back alleys, where Batman fights [wp].

### Pinkney's "Gotham Style": the comics' own rules for the look

- **What it is.** Judge Solomon Wayne and the architect Cyrus Pinkney laid the foundations of the modern city from 1840 (Batman: Gates of Gotham #2, 2011). Wayne meant Pinkney's buildings as a bulwark against the wild and its evil (Legends of the Dark Knight #27, 1992) [w Gotham City; Old Gotham].
- **Its rules.** Pinkney meant the city as one living whole that would itself fight evil (Batman Secret Files and Origins #1) [w Gotham City]:
  - **gargoyles**, to frighten people onto the right path;
  - **rounded edges**, to confuse evil beings;
  - **thick walls**, to keep virtue in;
  - **many raised walkways**, with some buildings joined so that they could not stand alone.
- **How it spread.** The first such building drew others round it, and became the heart of the Financial District. Wayne and Pinkney raised about a dozen more. The style was copied in Gotham and elsewhere, though the architectural journals all condemned it. Pinkney died young, on the eve of his fortieth birthday [w Old Gotham].
- **The Destroyer, 1992.** In the "Destroyer" story (Batman #474, Legends of the Dark Knight #27 and Detective Comics #641, written by Alan Grant), a man obsessed with Pinkney blows up modern buildings to bare the Pinkney buildings hidden inside them [wp Gotham City]. The 1989 film's designer, Anton Furst, drew for Legends of the Dark Knight #27 [wp]. **This is how the comics took on the film's look**, so the comics' Gotham since 1992 shares it by design.

### Gargoyles

They are the Pinkney rule the tile can show everywhere: on cornices, at corners and over doors. Wayne Tower's crown of twelve is the best single example, one gargoyle facing each way into the city [w Wayne Tower].

### Art deco

- Writers on the comics describe their Gotham as a mix of art deco, neo-Gothic and later styles, with spires and gargoyles [s Comicvine; Architizer].
- One comic, Batman: Death by Design (2012, by Chip Kidd and Dave Taylor), draws an art deco station, Wayne Central [s Architect magazine].
- The strongest art deco Gotham, though, is the 1989 film's and the animated series' [film; tv].
- So **the tile's art deco is its own choice** [tile], kept to the setback towers and stepped crowns of the 1920s and 1930s that any American city of the time has. No set of the films is copied.

### The giant props

In the Golden and Silver Ages, the city's rooftops carried huge advertising props: a typewriter, a penny and the like, where villains staged their crimes. By the 1970s they were gone. Arkham Asylum: Living Hell #3 (2003) explains it as a joke: a chain of props knocked over like dominoes, and a ban on them called the "Sprang Act", after the artist Dick Sprang [w Gotham City, trivia]. The giant penny in the Batcave is one of them (World's Finest #30) [wp Batcave]. For a toy diorama they are a gift: a few giant props on the roofs are Gotham's own, and they are cheerful [tile].

### How to make it read as Gotham at a glance, in a toy

1. **The Bat-Signal on a cloud** over the police headquarters' roof. More than anything else, this says Gotham.
2. **Gargoyles on every cornice**, and a crown of twelve on the tallest tower.
3. **Dark stone and lit windows**: a city of soot-black Gothic blocks whose windows glow amber.
4. **Water and bridges**: an island city of rivers, bascule bridges and a harbour, with a prison on one island and an asylum on another.
5. **A mansion on a wooded hill**, with a cave door lit beneath it.
6. **A chemical plant's green glow** on the waterfront, and a dead fairground's lights on a pier.
7. **Steam from the street grates, and rain.**

Section 4 turns these into light, weather, colours and small scenes.

## 4. The feel of Gotham

The collector asked for a tile with the feel Gotham has. This section says what that feel is in the comics, and how a tile of this kit can have it.

### What the feel is, in the comics

- **Night.** O'Neil's coldest night in November, and Miller's and Byrne's New York by night (section 3).
- **Weather.**
  - rain;
  - fog;
  - snow, with the Gotham River freezing in hard winters [w Gotham River];
  - storms: Zero Year sets its siege of the city in a hurricane [w Gotham City].
  - Rain on rooftops is most of what people remember of the comics' city [mem].
- **Height.** Rooftops, water towers and fire escapes, with gargoyles to perch on [mem]. The city is seen from above, which is how the board is seen.
- **Old stone and old iron.** Pinkney's gargoyles, round corners, thick walls and walkways (section 3).
- **Steam.** Brown's old steam tunnels under the river [b] give the steam that rises from the streets [tile].
- **Contrast.**
  - Bristol's mansions on their hill [w] against the East End's tenements [w];
  - Old Gotham's Gothic against the waterfront's tanks and cranes;
  - a fairground that was once bright and is now shabby [w Amusement Mile];
  - the light of one signal on the clouds over all of it.

### Light and the hour

- **What the kit allows.** **The kit gives a tile no hour and no weather of its own.**
  - `TileDef` in `app/src/engine/tile.ts` has no field for either.
  - `engine/look/weather.ts` keeps one weather for the whole city.
  - A tile learns how dark it is only through `k.each(fn(dt, time, night))`.
  - Signs brighten with `0.8 + 1.8 × night`, and glows with `0.45 + 3.0 × night` (`engine/build.ts`). By day, Gotham's neon and lit windows are as dim as anyone's.
  - The address takes `?hour=` and `?weather=` for the whole board only.
- So "a tile with its own night" is work, in the tile or in the engine. What it takes is under "Keeping its mood by day" at the end of this section.
- **Best hour.** Gotham is at its best at dusk and by night: `?hour=21` to `23`. It should be the board's best tile at night, as North America's ring of lights is at night now [tile].

### The weather

- **Rain.** Rain is the comics' Gotham. On the city's own weather (`auto`), rain and storm come round for everyone. Rain by night is where wet streets and signs show best (`building-a-tile.md`). Check Gotham at `?weather=rain` by night first, and make it look best there [tile].
- **Fog.** Banks of low fog on the river and the bay. A tile can lay them as pale, see-through flat sheets a unit or two above the water, drifting slowly in `k.each`. Check them for flicker against the water [tile].
- **Snow.**
  - The Robinson Park Reservoir as a skating rink [w Robinson Park];
  - ice along the Gotham River's banks [w Gotham River];
  - gargoyles with caps of snow [tile].

### The sky

- **The Bat-Signal** [wp]: a beam from the GCPD roof up to a low cloud, with the bat's oval on the cloud's underside [tile]. A cloud has to be built, since the tile has no sky of its own:
  - a few dark cloud slabs, high over the north-west quarter of the board. From the opening view a thing in the air is seen about 0.9 of its height further north-west, so a cloud 100 up over the north-west corner is seen beyond the board, against the room, and hides nothing of the tile;
  - the beam a see-through cone, faint by day and bright by night through `night`.
  - Seen from the north-west (`az` 222) the cloud is in front of the tile; check it there. It may need to fade when the view turns, or to sit lower and smaller.
  - It also hangs over whichever tile lands to the north-west on the board. Ask the collector (section 9).
- **Searchlights** [tile]:
  - the GCPD helicopter's beam on the streets (`air.ts` helicopter, with a cone of light that sweeps);
  - two searchlights crossing in front of the Iceberg Lounge on a party night, as at a premiere.
  - Each beam is a mesh of its own and costs. Keep to three.
- **Bats** [tile, after the cave's colony, wp Batcave]: at dusk a cloud of small bats pours out of a crevice in Crest Hill and wheels over the river.

### Sound

The engine has no sound. No audio code was found in `app/src`. The screen only shows what Spotify plays. Rain, a foghorn, the Clock Tower's bell on the hour and a siren now and then would be Gotham's, but they would be engine work for another day [tile].

### Keeping its mood when the rest of the board is in daylight

**What the tile can do by itself:**

1. **The dark palette** above, so that Gotham reads as dusk even at noon. This costs nothing, and does most of the work.
2. **Windows and neon that stay lit by day.**
   - `Build` has `look` "for a tile that writes a material of its own" (`tile.ts`). A material of the tile's own can keep a floor under its glow by day, so that its windows and signs read as lit in daylight.
   - Ask whoever keeps the engine whether a tile may do so, and note it under "For the engine".
3. **Its own weather objects:**
   - the cloud and the Bat-Signal;
   - fog over the water;
   - steam;
   - searchlights;
   - if wanted, rain of its own: streaks inside its own rectangle, in `k.group`, moved in `k.each`.
4. **More of it at night.** The `night` value in `k.each` lets the tile turn its own effects up as night falls: denser steam and fog, a brighter beam.

**What needs the engine** (for "For the engine"):

1. **A mood of the tile's own.** `TileDef` could say `{ hour?: number; weather?: WeatherName }`, and the engine apply it inside the tile's rectangle. This is the real answer and the most work, since the sun, the sky and the fog are one for the board now.
2. **Something smaller.** A darkening of the light inside one tile's rectangle, and a `night` value of its own for that tile's signs, glows and windows. Gotham would then sit in a permanent dusk while its neighbours have noon.
3. **A canopy that only casts shadow** [tile; untested]: a mesh that is not drawn, but casts a shadow over the whole tile, so that Gotham stands in shade while its neighbours are in sun. Whether the shadow map allows it was not checked. `building-a-tile.md` says the shadow map is drawn once.

**The honest alternative.** Gotham follows the city's hour like every other tile, and is the board's best tile at night. When Gotham is the point, open the board with `?hour=22&weather=rain`.

## 5. One tile

### What fits

The whole city does not fit, and should not try to. The tile is the comics' map squeezed onto one board, as Blackwater Bay squeezed its bay: **bearings kept, distances not** [tile].

The comics' own geography already suits the opening view:

- the mainland with Wayne Manor is **north**;
- Mercey Island with Arkham is **north-west**;
- Gotham Bay, with the prison and the statue, is **south and east**.

So the hill and the asylum stand at the back of the view, and the bay and its islands at the front. Old Gotham's tall towers stand in the **west**, where the comics put Downtown's first village in the south and west [w], and where what they hide falls off the board.

What it keeps:

- the Gotham River along the north, with the asylum's island in its wide western reach;
- the Sprang River, cut diagonally across the city to the harbour;
- Old Gotham and Robinson Park on the west bank;
- Uptown, the East End, the fairground and the chemical works on the east bank;
- the harbour and the bay in the south-east.

What it leaves for another tile:

- Chinatown, Burnside, Tricorner, the Finger River and the Narrows;
- the airport, Sommerset's woods and swamp, and the stadium.

**The view it opens with.** From the bay at the front, the eye runs up the Sprang River, under its bridges, to Arkham on its island at the far end. Wayne Tower stands at the left, Crest Hill and the Manor across the back, and the fairground's wheel at the right. The Sprang runs north-west, almost exactly along the line of the view, so nothing built stands in front of Arkham: the river is the view's corridor.

### Roads: a pattern of its own

What no other tile has [tile]:

- **A junction over water**: the Trigate's ring, with three arms to three lands.
- **Bascule bridges** carrying a city's avenues over a river through its middle, as Brown's map has them [b].
- **A grand circuit of the river**: one road over the Kane Bridge, along under Crest Hill past the cave door, and back over the Trigate.
- **Pinkney's walkways** over Old Gotham's streets, for people only.

Traffic keeps to the right, as in the United States [real]. The routes, after the doc's rule for traffic that keeps right (mirror Japan's: loops round blocks turning one way only, outer lanes as one loop):

1. **Old Gotham's grid** on the west bank: one loop round each block, in the lane by the kerb, and the outer lanes as one longer loop.
2. **Uptown's grid** on the east bank, the same way.
3. **The waterfront run.** **The road worth watching**, at the front of the view. It runs along Old Gotham's bay shore past the Diamond District, the Iceberg Lounge and the pier, up the Sprang's west bank, over the southern bascule bridge, along the harbour's north shore past Ace Chemicals and the docks, and back over the northern bascule bridge. It shares lanes with both grids where it joins them. Nothing stands south of it but water, so nothing hides it.
4. **The circuit of the river** (a route that goes round and comes back). From Uptown's outer lane over the Kane Bridge, west along the riverside road under Crest Hill past the cave door, up a spur to the Manor's forecourt ring and back down, on west to the Trigate's north-east arm, round the hub's ring with a spur out to Arkham's gate ring, and back over the south-east arm to Otisburg. The hub is a roundabout over water: `lanes.ts`, as Europe's roundabouts.
5. **The fairground road.** Out along the promontory to a turning circle before the wheel.

## 7. Where it goes on the map

- The comics put Gotham in New Jersey [wp], at about Atlantic City on the Atlas's map of 1990 [c iFanboy].
- So: **`place: { lon: -74.42, lat: 39.36 }`**, Atlantic City [real].
- It is real land, so it needs no `land` and no `words`. The pin's label is the tile's name.
- The alternative, for those who read the twin cities across Delaware Bay as the rule, is Cape May: `{ lon: -74.91, lat: 38.94 }` [real].
- North America's pin is at `{ lon: -96, lat: 39 }` and Urbana-Champaign's at `{ lon: -88.2, lat: 40.1 }`, so Gotham's stands well clear of both.

## 8. Problems: what a cheerful toy should leave out or soften

- **The Waynes' murder.**
  - Park Row and the cinema exist because two parents were shot in front of their son [w Park Row].
  - Nothing of that is shown: no pearls, no gun, no figures in the alley, and the cinema has no name and no film of 1940 on its canopy.
  - The clinic stands for the street's better side.
  - Or Park Row is left out entirely (section 9).
- **The Joker's origin at Ace Chemicals.** A man falling into a vat; and The Killing Joke as a whole, a story of cruelty.
  - Ace is shown as a works with green-lit vats and nobody on its catwalks.
  - Its "toxic" status (Prime Earth) is not lettered.
- **Arkham Asylum and mental illness.**
  - Its official name and its stories cast the mentally ill as monsters: the founder's family murdered, the builder going mad with an axe [w Arkham Asylum].
  - The tile shows a Gothic house on a wooded island and its gate, and no people at all: no straitjackets, no faces at barred windows, no "criminally insane" on a board.
  - Its board says ARKHAM, or ARKHAM ASYLUM if the collector wants the full name.
- **Blackgate.**
  - A prison, condemned once by Amnesty International in the comics [w].
  - It is shown as an island fort with a searchlight, and no prisoners.
- **Gotham Cathedral.**
  - A place of worship, drawn with care and never the setting for anything.
  - Its comics story (a plague and a trap for souls) and its film story (a fall from its tower) are both left out.
  - It may be left out entirely, since it is known mainly from the film (section 9).
- **The disasters.** None of them is shown, and no ruins are:
  - the earthquake and No Man's Land;
  - the Destroyer's bombs;
  - the Statue of Justice's head blown off;
  - Zero Year's flood;
  - the Joker's gas.
- **Guns.** None, anywhere on the tile. The police carry none in their figures.
- **The poor as scenery.**
  - The East End is described with muggings and street-walkers [w], and Park Row once as skid row [w].
  - The tile shows working streets: washing, a coffee cart, children's chalk on a pavement.
  - No one homeless as decoration, no sex work, no drugs.
- **The Underground Highway** sheltered the homeless and Killer Croc [w]. Only its boarded portal is shown.
- **Trademarks.**
  - The bat emblem, Batman's name, Wayne Enterprises' logo and the like are DC's.
  - As ADR 0009 asks of real names, signs are plain lettering, never a logo.
  - The Bat-Signal shows a plain bat silhouette in an oval, not the exact emblem of any era [tile].
- **Copying the films.** The pieces in section 2's table of film places stay out, above all:
  - the Batcave's waterfall;
  - Lady Gotham;
  - the Narrows;
  - Axis Chemicals;
  - the monorail;
  - the cathedral's bell-tower climax.
- **Real New York.** No real New York landmark is copied: North America has them. Gotham's look comes from Pinkney's rules, not from the Chrysler Building.

## 9. Questions for the collector

1. **Night.** Now: Gotham follows the city's hour like every tile, with a dark palette, and is at its best at night. The other way: a mood of its own while the board is in daylight, which needs work in the engine (section 4).
2. **The cloud for the Bat-Signal.** Now: a small dark cloud over the north-west corner, which overhangs the tile behind it on the board. The other way: the beam alone, into the sky, with no cloud and no oval.
3. **Crest Hill along the north edge.** Hill and Manor reach 28 and hide a strip of the tile behind. The other way: a lower hill, with the Manor at 20.
4. **Park Row.** Now: the clinic and an unnamed cinema, and no hint of the murder. The other way: leave it out.
5. **Arkham's name on its board.** Now: ARKHAM. The other way: ARKHAM ASYLUM, or no board.
6. **The cathedral.** In the comics since 1990, but known mainly from the 1989 film. Now: optional, drawn plain. The other way: leave it out.
7. **The Iceberg.** Now: both the Lounge on the waterfront (1995) and the floating Casino (2012). The other way: one of them.
8. **The giant props** of the Golden Age. Now: a typewriter, a playing card and an umbrella on three roofs. The other way: none, for a graver city.
9. **The screen.** The Iceberg Lounge's bill, the Gazette's late edition, or the rain forecast.
