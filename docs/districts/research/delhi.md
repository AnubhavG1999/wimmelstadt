DELHI CATALOGUE (for Wimmelstadt). Confidence note: web search was weak (mostly 2022 news and travel blogs). Items marked [verified] were confirmed by a search; the rest is from background knowledge and should be spot-checked before it matters.

1. CHARACTER
Old Delhi (Shahjahanabad) is a dense, low, tightly packed mass of flat-roofed brick and plaster buildings with red-sandstone and white-marble monuments rising out of it, a spaghetti of lanes, and overhead cable tangles. New Delhi (Lutyens' Delhi) is the opposite: wide diagonal avenues, round-abouts, huge green lawns, and pale cream and red-sandstone colonnaded buildings with low domes and chhatris. From above, the contrast is brown/grey crowded fabric versus green geometry with a red-and-cream accent. Colour key: Red Fort red; Jama Masjid red with white-striped domes; India Gate sandstone; Humayun's Tomb red with white dome; Taj white. Autos, yellow-green, are the main street colour.

2. GROUND
- Terrain: flat alluvial plain. The only relief is the low Raisina Hill (Rashtrapati Bhavan) and the Ridge, a wooded rocky strip to the north and west.
- Yamuna: runs north to south along the EAST edge of the city. In the Mughal era it flowed at the Red Fort's east wall; it has since moved about 1 to 2 km east. Today it is a broad, shallow, brown-grey channel with sandbanks, a floodplain, bridges (Iron Bridge by Salimgarh, Signature Bridge to the north) and ghats. It is polluted, so water in the toy should be murky, not blue.
- Old Delhi: Chandni Chowk is a straight avenue running west from the Red Fort's Lahori Gate to Fatehpuri Masjid, with a maze of narrow lanes (galis) and bazaars branching off. The Jama Masjid sits on a raised platform south of it. Walled city with surviving gates (Delhi Gate, Kashmere Gate).
- New Delhi: a hex-and-diagonal grid. The main axis runs east-west from Rashtrapati Bhavan (west, on Raisina Hill) through Vijay Chowk and down Kartavya Path (lawns, canals, fountains, lamp-posts on both sides) to India Gate (about 2.5 km), then on toward Purana Qila (east), then the river. Janpath runs north-south across it. Connaught Place (about 1.5 km north of the axis) is two concentric white colonnaded rings with 7 radial roads and a green park in the middle.
- Parks: Lodhi Garden (tombs among lawns), Sunder Nursery by Humayun's Tomb, the Ridge, India Gate lawns.
- Agra and the Taj [for the toy Yamuna]: the Taj stands on the right (west or south) bank of the Yamuna at a river bend, with the river directly behind (north of) its terrace. Mehtab Bagh is the garden on the opposite bank. Agra is roughly 230 km from Delhi by road, and the Yamuna flows past both the Red Fort and the Taj. Taj layout: white marble tomb on a plinth, flanked by two red-sandstone buildings (mosque and jawab), with a four-part charbagh garden, a long reflecting pool and a red Great Gate at the south end.

3. LANDMARKS (most iconic first)
1. Red Fort (Lal Qila): long red-sandstone walls about 2 km around, an irregular octagon, with crenellations, chhatri-topped octagonal towers and the large Lahori Gate with two small domes. Faces east to the old river course and west to Chandni Chowk. The Lahori Gate opens onto Chandni Chowk; Netaji Subhas Marg runs along the west and south walls.
2. India Gate: a 42 m tall triumphal arch of sandstone, a squat rectangle with a broad central arch and a flat top cornice with a shallow dome-bowl. Stands at the east end of Kartavya Path, in a circular roundabout. Road passes around it; an empty canopy to the east.
3. Rashtrapati Bhavan: very wide, low, cream-and-red sandstone block, large copper-green central dome on a drum, wings with chhatris and columns. On Raisina Hill, at the west end of Kartavya Path, with Vijay Chowk in front. Road in front, not through it.
4. Jama Masjid: red sandstone and white marble, three white-striped onion domes and two tall minarets (about 40 m) with white stripes, on a high platform with big stairs. Just southwest of the Red Fort, south of Chandni Chowk. Road around its base, not through it.
5. Humayun's Tomb: red sandstone with white marble inlay and a white double dome on a wide square plinth, in a square charbagh. In the southeast, near the Yamuna side.
6. Taj Mahal (Agra): white marble tomb, central bulb dome with four small chhatri domes, four slender minarets at the plinth corners, red-sandstone mosque on one side and mirror building on the other, reflecting pool. Closes the axis at the river. No road through it; the long forecourt is pedestrian.
7. Qutb Minar: a tapering red-sandstone fluted tower about 73 m, with five stories and balconies. In far south Delhi, no river link. (Optional, may be too far.)
8. Lotus Temple: white marble, 27 petal-shaped shells in three rings, with a ponds and a lawn. In the south. Optional.
9. Akshardham: pink sandstone and marble temple on a raised platform, many small domes and spires, on the east bank of the Yamuna. A road passes beside it.
10. Connaught Place: two concentric rings of white colonnades with cream-white facades and big ad boards, with a round park in the middle, with Janpath and Parliament Street radiating out. Roads run through all of it.
11. Parliament House (Sansad Bhavan): a circular colonnaded building, at the north end of the axis on Sansad Marg. The newer triangular Parliament building stands beside it, sandstone-coloured with a roof of triangular modules.
12. Safdarjung Tomb or Purana Qila: optional extras. Purana Qila is a rough red-stone fort about 1 km east of India Gate.

4. BUSINESSES AND SHOPS
- Karim's (Old Delhi, near Jama Masjid): Mughlai restaurant; plain frontage with a big sign in Hindi/English/Urdu and red-and-white lettering.
- Paranthe Wali Gali: lane of paratha shops with small stalls, bright signs.
- Haldiram's (Chandni Chowk): sweets and snacks; red-and-orange sign.
- Ghantewala (Chandni Chowk): old sweet shop, small frontage, hand-painted sign.
- Jalebiwala (Dariba Kalan): jalebi stall at a street corner, steaming vats.
- Chaina Ram Sindhi Confectioner (Fatehpuri): old sweets shop.
- Moti Mahal (Daryaganj): restaurant, butter chicken fame.
- Khan Chacha (Khan Market): kebab stall.
- Wenger's (Connaught Place): bakery, traditional cream frontage with pastries in window.
- Kwality (Connaught Place): restaurant, old-style frontage with signage.
- United Coffee House (Connaught Place): ornate interior, with a grand frontage.
- Gaylord (Connaught Place): classic restaurant.
- Indian Coffee House (Connaught Place): round, café-style.
- Janpath and Palika Bazaar markets: stalls with colourful canopies.
- Central Cottage Industries Emporium (Janpath): crafts store, plain big sign.
- Chandni Chowk's spice market (Khari Baoli): sacks of spices in front of shops.

5. STREET LIFE
- Vehicles: auto-rickshaws (green body, yellow roof, CNG; a new rule restricts new three-wheelers to electric from 1 Jan 2027 [verified, so existing CNG autos stay for now]); cycle-rickshaws (colourful canopies) in Old Delhi; e-rickshaws (small battery tuk-tuks, various colours); DTC buses (green, orange and lime-green low-floor buses, with new electric ones in lime-green and orange); Metro trains (silver with a coloured stripe per line: yellow, blue, red, violet, etc.); black-and-yellow taxis and white app-cabs; scooters; handcarts; cows and dogs occasionally.
- Street furniture: street lamps, sandstone-coloured bollards, cream-and-red lampposts along Kartavya Path, flower beds, tangled overhead cables, banyan trees, jaali railings, hand-painted signs, kiosks.
- Scripts: Devanagari (Hindi), Latin (English), Urdu (Nastaliq) around Jama Masjid, and some Gurmukhi (Punjabi) for gurdwaras.
- People: shopkeepers, rickshaw pullers, school children in uniform, tourists, soldiers on parade, chai-wallahs, street vendors, pigeon feeders.

6. SMALL SCENES
1. Republic Day parade along Kartavya Path, with a tableau float and marching bands (26 Jan).
2. Independence Day flag-hoisting at the Red Fort (15 Aug), kites in the sky.
3. Diwali lights and sweets stalls, with firecrackers kept small.
4. Holi colours (March): coloured powder, no hostile elements.
5. Chandni Chowk food crawl: a jalebi vat, paratha stall and chai vendor in a lane.
6. Iftar or Eid market near Jama Masjid in Ramadan: lanterns, dates, food stalls (respectfully, no depiction of prayer in detail).
7. Kite-flying on rooftops around Independence Day.
8. Wedding procession (baraat) with a horse, a band and lights.

7. WATCH OUT
- Renames [verified]: Rajpath is now Kartavya Path (since Sept 2022). The Central Vista area is now called the Kartavya Bhavan area. The new PMO complex is Seva Teerth, and Kartavya Bhavan 1 and 2 were inaugurated on 13 Feb 2026; other Common Central Secretariat buildings are due by about June 2027. Governors' Raj Bhavans are being renamed Lok Bhavan.
- Red Fort: a car blast near it on 10 Nov 2025 killed 11 to 13 people (reports differ). The monument and Lal Quila Metro reopened within about a week. [verified] Keep the toy version calm and avoid any security or incident theme. The Red Fort now opens daily, including Mondays (Feb 2026). [verified]
- Yamuna: it is heavily polluted and sacred to many Hindus; do not mock it. Keep it simple and neutral, perhaps with a small ghat.
- Religious sensitivities: Jama Masjid and the temples are active places of worship. Do not put cars, jokes, parodies or tourists-as-gags on or inside them. Avoid depicting prayer. Avoid depicting cows as jokes. No alcohol, beef or pork items on signs near temples and mosques. Handle flags, the national emblem and the Taj with care (the Taj is a tomb). Nothing political or communal.
- The toy Taj is in Agra, about 230 km away, so moving it to this district is a deliberate liberty. Worth a small sign saying "Agra" in the toy world.
