# Wimmelstadt district research: The Alps; Monaco and the Riviera

Checked 18-19 November 2026. Search and page-fetch limits were hit mid-task, so some items rest on one source or a search-result summary (sources marked * were not opened in full). **(u)** = from memory, not checked this session. No files were created. Body is roughly 1,700 words.

## A. THE ALPS

**1. Character.** A white saw-toothed spine with one unmistakable hooked pyramid, the Matterhorn. Grey roads climb both flanks in stacked hairpins to saddles, each with a lone hotel or kiosk strip. Red trains cross stone viaducts; yellow post buses serve valleys of dark-timber chalets.

**2. Ground.** Bands by height: lake and valley floor (chalet villages, railway, river), conifers, bare rock carrying the hairpins, the saddle between two peaks, then glacier and summit. Roads cross saddles, tunnels run beneath the same saddles, cable cars jump from valley to summit rock. Passes shut about November to May. Real layout: Stelvio's 48-hairpin ramp faces north-east into German-speaking South Tyrol, its other ramp drops to Italian Bormio; the Matterhorn stands on the Swiss-Italian crest above dead-end Zermatt; St. Moritz lies on a high eastern lake, reached over the Landwasser Viaduct; Grossglockner is the far Austrian end; Mont Blanc and its tunnel are nearest Monaco.

**3. Landmarks.**
1. **Matterhorn** (4,478 m): lone four-faced pyramid, crooked tip, dark rock, snow streaks. No road; cable cars reach the neighbouring Klein Matterhorn.
2. **Stelvio Pass** (2,757 m): 48 stone-numbered hairpins on the north-east (Prato-Trafoi) ramp, 34 towards Bormio (counts vary). Summit street: blocky hotels, cafés, kiosks, a bank branch, chapel, Coppi monument, obelisk, cable cars to the summer-ski glacier. Road runs through.
3. **Hotel Belvédère, Furka** (1882; 2,272 m): tall narrow block in the crook of a hairpin on the Valais side; pale walls, red shutters, painted name (u). Rhône Glacier tongue and meltwater lake behind, now far back.
4. **Landwasser Viaduct**: six slim dark-limestone arches, 65 m high, 136 m long, curving straight into a tunnel in a sheer cliff near Filisur. Rail over, river under.
5. **St. Moritz**: village stacked on the steep north shore of its lake (a white disc in winter), leaning church tower as its emblem, grand hotels above.
6. **Grossglockner road**: toll gates at Ferleiten and Heiligenblut; lookout tower on the Edelweißspitze spur; road passes through the Hochtor tunnel (2,504 m); dead-end spur to the Kaiser-Franz-Josefs-Höhe terrace facing the Pasterze glacier.
7. **Zermatt**: chalet village below the Matterhorn, car-free since 1931. Road ends 5 km short at Täsch's parking terminal; shuttle train every 20 minutes; rack railway to the Gornergrat hotel and observatory.
8. **Gotthard** (2,107 m): hospice, museum, lake and five wind turbines on the saddle; granite-cobbled Tremola hairpins on the south side; tunnel portals far below. Road over and under.

**4. Roads and car culture.**
- **Stelvio** (SS38): numbered stones count down from 48 to the summit; the Umbrail road to Switzerland joins near the top (2,503 m). Toll-free as of October 2026; a toll is being discussed.
- **Furka**: the Goldfinger (1964) road. Its owners report (July 2026) supercar posing and drifting at the Belvédère bend. Steam rack trains on the old line; car trains through the base tunnel.
- **Grossglockner**: 47.8 km toll road, Bruck to Heiligenblut, 36 hairpins; opened 3 August 1935 with a hillclimb next day; manufacturers' brake-test road; car exhibition at Kaiser-Franz-Josefs-Höhe. 2026 car ticket €46.50.
- **St. Moritz**: **The I.C.E. St. Moritz** (International Concours of Elegance) on the frozen lake still runs: 30-31 January 2026 (50 cars; judging Friday, laps on the ice Saturday), next 29-30 January 2027, ice permitting.
- **Tunnels**: Gotthard road tunnel (1980) gets a second tube in 2030. Mont Blanc had only night closures in 2026; 3.5-month closures return in 2027 and 2028.

**5. Businesses.** Badrutt's Palace, St. Moritz (stone tower, green roof) (u); Hanselmann café-confectioner, St. Moritz (painted façade) (u); Hotel Monte Rosa, Zermatt (white, green shutters) (u); Tibet Hütte, Stelvio ("Tibetan-style" hut above the pass); Belvédère buvette and souvenir kiosk (June-October); PostAuto (yellow coaches); Glacier Express (red panoramic cars (u)). Parodies: "Gasthof zur letzten Kehre" (Last Hairpin Inn), "Albergo Tornante 49", "Chesa Marmotta" (Romansh: Marmot House).

**6. Street life.** Yellow post buses (a summer line crosses Umbrail and Stelvio); red narrow-gauge trains; Zermatt's small, narrow electric taxis and buses (about 500 vehicles, 20 km/h limit); bikers, cyclists, classic-car convoys. Furniture: numbered hairpin stones, toll booths; snow poles, avalanche galleries, yellow hiking signposts (u). Scripts: German, Italian, French, Romansh; South Tyrol is bilingual (Stilfser Joch / Passo dello Stelvio).

**7. Small scenes.**
- Supercars queueing for the photo at the shuttered Belvédère, police watching for drifters.
- Classic racers lapping the frozen lake past a marquee.
- Red train half inside the cliff tunnel, photographers below.
- Stelvio summit: bikers at a kiosk, cyclists at the pass sign, a July skier heading for the cable car.
- Täsch: sports cars left in the terminal while owners board the shuttle.

**8. Watch out.**
- Belvédère: closed since 2015; still owned by the Carlen family, who insist it is "no lost place". The Valais government ordered the Rhône Glacier ice grotto closed (reported 5 September 2026; appeal possible).
- Stelvio: the summit bank's owner, Banca Popolare di Sondrio, was merged into BPER on 20 April 2026 (new signage unverified). Summer skiing stopped early in August 2026 for heat.
- Sensitivities: Blatten was buried by a glacier collapse (28 May 2025); about 40 died in the Crans-Montana bar fire (1 January 2026); eleven in the 2001 Gotthard tunnel fire. No buried-village, bar-fire or tunnel-fire scenes.
- Rights: Glacier Express, PostAuto and hotel names and logos are trademarks; Swiss cross and arms are protected (u). Mountains are free to depict.

## B. MONACO AND THE RIVIERA

**1. Character.** A steep amphitheatre of cream towers packed round a rectangular yacht harbour, mountains directly behind. A flat-topped rock with palace and old town closes one side; the Casino crowns the other. A ribbon of Armco, red-white kerbs and grandstands loops the port and climbs the hill.

**2. Ground.** A south-east-facing slope falling into the sea. Port Hercule lies between the Rock (south-west) and Monte-Carlo (north-east); flat La Condamine behind it carries the start straight. Mareterra, 6 hectares reclaimed from the sea and opened 4 December 2024, sits east of the Casino headland, between the port's outer jetty and the Grimaldi Forum. Above: the Basse Corniche at sea level through Monaco, the Moyenne mid-cliff through Èze, the Grande Corniche about 500 m up over the Col d'Èze and La Turbie. The Col de Turini (about 1,600 m), a forested three-road junction inland, joins the district to the Alps.

**3. Landmarks.**
1. **Casino de Monte-Carlo**: cream Belle Époque palace, twin turrets, green copper roofs (u), Opéra attached, garden square in front, Hôtel de Paris alongside. Circuit crosses the square.
2. **The Rock and Prince's Palace**: cliff-sided plateau about 60 m above the sea; pale palace with clock tower (crenellations and cannon-lined square (u)); guard changes daily at 11:55. Road tunnels pass under.
3. **Port Hercule**: rectangular basin of white yachts, open-air pool on the inner quay. Circuit follows the quays.
4. **Fairmont Monte Carlo**: long, low, curved pale slab on the shore, rooftop pool (u). The hairpin loops round its forecourt; the circuit tunnel runs beneath.
5. **Oceanographic Museum** (1910): white-stone neo-Baroque block about 100 m long, rising 85 m sheer from the sea on the Rock's outer cliff.
6. **Mareterra**: low green peninsula, ship-like 17-storey Le Renzo block, 16-berth marina, pine park, promenade. Pedestrian; just outside Portier.
7. **Sainte-Dévote chapel**: small chapel with tiled forecourt behind Turn 1.
8. **La Rascasse**: low bar inside its corner at the port's south-west end.
9. **Trophy of the Alps (Augustus), La Turbie**: Roman monument on the ridge; the Grande Corniche passes beside it.

**4. Roads and car culture.** **Circuit de Monaco** (3.337 km, 19 turns, 78 laps), lap order: start on Boulevard Albert Ier, pits on the harbour side → **Sainte-Dévote** (right, at the chapel) → Beau Rivage climb → Massenet (long left past the Opera) → **Casino Square** (right, past Casino and Hôtel de Paris) → downhill past the Tip Top bar → **Mirabeau** Haute (right) → **the hairpin** (left, slowest corner, wrapping the **Fairmont Monte Carlo**; name unchanged in 2026; formerly Station, Loews, Grand Hotel) → Mirabeau Bas → **Portier** (right, at the sea; Mareterra beyond) → **tunnel** (curving right under the Fairmont) → **Nouvelle Chicane** (left-right onto the quay) → **Tabac** (left; a tobacconist's kiosk) → **Swimming Pool** (Louis Chiron left-right, then right-left round the Stade Nautique pool) → **La Rascasse** (tight right round the bar) → **Anthony Noghès** (right; the race's founder) → straight.
- 2026: Historic Grand Prix 24-26 April, E-Prix 16-17 May, Formula 1 4-7 June; build-up from 23 February, dismantling to 17 July.
- **Rallye Monte-Carlo** (January): 2026 added a 2.69 km super special on the harbour section (first Monaco street stage since 2008) and ended over the **Turini** (La Bollène-Vésubie to Moulinet), podium at Port Hercule; same finale planned for 24 January 2027.
- Prince's car collection: 54 route de la Piscine, by the pool, since July 2022.

**5. Businesses.** Casino de Monte-Carlo; Hôtel de Paris (white, domed entrance) (u); Café de Paris (terrace on the square) (u); Hôtel Hermitage (new suites and bar, 2026); Fairmont Monte Carlo; La Rascasse (chequered floor, engine-part ceiling); Tip Top bar (as of 2023); Stars of Monaco (the former Stars'N'Bars, on the quay). Parodies: "Casino de Mont-Wimmel", "Hôtel de l'Épingle" (Hairpin Hotel), "Bar Le Rouget" (another fish).

**6. Street life.** White taxis (white is required); CAM buses, red and white on the electric midibuses; the Bateau-Bus, an electric solar catamaran crossing the port (suspended during the Grand Prix); palace guards in white summer uniform (u). Furniture: Armco, catch fences and grandstands February to July, red-white kerbs, palms. French signs; Monégasque on some old-town street names (u); red-and-white flags.

**7. Small scenes.**
- Racing cars nose to tail at full lock round the hairpin.
- Valets and a phone-waving crowd outside the Casino.
- A crane lifting a stranded car over the harbour barrier, yacht party watching.
- A rally car doing donuts on a wet January quay.
- Snowbanks, fans and a lamp-pod rally car on the Turini at night.

**8. Watch out.**
- Recent: Mareterra redrew the shore east of Portier. La Rascasse reopened 9 April 2026 after a four-month refit. Since 2026 the Grand Prix runs on the first weekend of June, not late May; it is contracted to 2035 and gets its first sprint on 4-6 June 2027. No layout change in 2026; 2025 widening proposals were not adopted.
- Renamings: Fairmont not renamed (renovated 2025). The Columbus has been "Columbus Hotel Monte-Carlo, Curio Collection by Hilton" since December 2023. Stars'N'Bars closed around the end of 2022; its premises became Conscientiae, then Marius, now Stars of Monaco. Tabac kiosk: sources disagree whether it survives.
- Rights: SBM holds and enforces "Casino de Monte-Carlo" marks; treat Grand Prix names, team liveries and the princely arms as protected (u). Parody names, no logos.
- Sensitivities: Princess Grace died after a 1982 crash on the road down from La Turbie (u): no corniche crash scene. Avoid the Turini night stage's nickname "Night of the Long Knives"; say "Nuit du Turini".
