### All three
- Two legs and two wings, with the wings as the forelimbs. The author says so plainly on his blog: his dragons have two legs, the wings are the forelegs, and four-legged dragons exist only in heraldry ("Here There Be Dragons", Not a Blog, 11 July 2024, read today). The books themselves do not spell it out in what I could read.
- They hatched together from three eggs (black-and-scarlet, green-and-bronze, cream-and-gold) and were named for Daenerys's husband and brothers. AGOT ch. 72; ACOK ch. 12.
- Build: as hatchlings their wings already span about three times their body, and most of a dragon is neck, tail and wing (ACOK ch. 12). They grow lean and long, not stout.

### What only the series shows (do not use)
- Viserion as a blue-eyed dragon of ice breathing blue fire, and breaking the Wall at Eastwatch. Not in the books.
- Rhaegal's death at sea and Drogon carrying a body away. Not in the books.
- The series' dragon heads, horn shapes, wing shapes and its colour schemes, and Daenerys's look as an actor plays her.
- Flight in formation is not in the books either: in ADWD the three are never seen flying together as a team. It is the collector's wish, and a toy's licence.

## 2. Three dragons for the tile

The collector's brief for the dragons, which replaces an earlier wish for cute ones:

> "I dont want the dragons to look cute, I want them to look cool! no over proprotioned iw ant them to give fighter jet energy"

So: three dragons after the books' three, in the books' own colours and flame colours, built in the books' lean proportions, flying like a display team of fast jets. They fly in formation most of the time and split up now and then, as an event. They breathe fire now and then, and the fire harms nothing and goes only where this section allows.

Settled before building:
- **Mostly together, split now and then.** About five sixths of the time the three are in formation; a split comes about once in three circuits (the timeline below).
- **No rider.** The dragons fly without anyone on them. This is the design's choice and is open to the collector: a rider would be a portrait of a character, and the series' look of her is the one people know.
- **The Wall and the Hightower stand as toys at the heights section 3 proposes**: the Wall 42 and the Hightower 48, under the project's standing rule that heights are roughly to scale and looking good comes first. The flight's heights below are set against them.

### The build: a fast jet that is a dragon

**Two legs, and wings as the forelimbs.** The author has said this outright: his dragons have two legs and two wings, the wings are the forelegs, and four-legged dragons exist only in heraldry ("Here There Be Dragons", Not a Blog, 11 July 2024; read today). The legs work as an undercarriage: folded back flat under the belly and the root of the tail in flight, swung down a second and a half before landing, folded up again after take-off.

**Proportions, as the books suggest.** The books give one ratio: as hatchlings their wings span about three times their body, and most of a dragon is neck, tail and wing (ACOK ch. 12). Reading "body" as the trunk with the head, and with a long neck and tail making the whole dragon about 2.2 times that, the span comes to about **1.35 times the length from nose to tail tip**. That is the ratio used here, and it matches the books' picture of a dragon that is mostly wing. Fractions of the length nose to tail tip (L):

| Part | Share of L | Shape |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Head | 0.09 | A long narrow wedge, like a jet's nose: flat on top, a sharp jaw line, small eyes set far back under a heavy brow ridge. Narrower than it is deep. |
| Horns and frill | horns 0.05 | Two horns swept straight back from the brow, in line with the head, and a frill of three short spikes on each side behind the jaw, swept back too. Nothing sticks up or out. |
| Neck | 0.25, in 5 joints | Held straight out ahead in fast flight, so head, neck and body make one line; an S-curve only when slow, perched or turning its head. |
| Body | 0.20, in 3 joints | Slim and deep-chested: deeper (0.07 L) than wide (0.05 L) at the chest where the wings join, tapering to narrow hips. The keel under the chest is the jet's belly. |
| Tail | 0.46, in 10 joints | Long, thinning to a whip, ending in a flat **spade-shaped fin** set upright, 0.04 L long, that works as the rudder: it swings into each turn. |
| Spinal plates | | A row of low, swept-back triangles from the crown to the tail fin, tallest over the shoulders (0.025 L), lying close to the line like a dorsal spine. |
| Wings | span 1.35 L | Long arm and longer finger bones, the membrane drawn taut between them: see "The wing". |
| Legs | | Thigh, shin and a foot of three claws, all folded flat when flying. |

What makes it read as a jet rather than an animal: the straight line of head, neck and body in fast flight; everything (horns, frill, plates, legs, wing tips) swept back along that line; a tail fin that moves like a rudder; and wings that change shape with speed.

**Sizes in tile units.** The books make Drogon's span about 6 m (ADWD ch. 52), which at the city's 3.2 m to a unit is 2 units: a speck. The dragons are toys like everything else that has to be read. The size was chosen from the whole-tile view: at a zoom of about 270, a thing needs about a fifteenth of the picture's height (18 units) to read as a shape from that distance, and Drogon's span is set there. The other way, a dragon must not be wider than a castle's whole footprint: the Red Keep's ring is about 30 across and the toy keeps 20 to 26 high, so a span up to 20 is the most. Drogon is clearly the largest in the books; the other two are close, and Rhaegal is made slightly the larger, as a choice the books do not settle.

### The wing, and how it sweeps

A wing is three moving pieces, built from boxes and flat membranes:
1. the **arm**, a tapered box from the shoulder to the wrist (0.22 L), turning at the shoulder in two ways: up and down (the beat) and back and forth (the sweep);
2. the **hand**, four finger bones from the wrist (thin boxes, the longest 0.45 L, fanning back), turning at the wrist as one piece, back towards the body (the fold);
3. the **membrane**, flat triangles between the shoulder, the wrist, each finger tip, and the body at the hips, rebuilt from those points every frame (about ten triangles a wing). It stays taut: its trailing edge runs straight from finger tip to finger tip, with only a slight scallop. Draw it two-sided (`face3`, or `tri` in `parts.ts`).

Its shape follows speed, like a swing-wing jet's. With speed **v** in units a second, the sweep is set from v and eased over half a second:

| State | When | Arm swept back | Hand folded back | Span (share of full) | Wing held |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Spread** | slow flight (v under 22), the climb, the orbit while waiting | 5° | 0° | 100% | level, tips a little up |
| **Cruise** | formation flight (v about 30) | 20° | 15° | 85% | level, a slight upward V |
| **Fast** | the low pass, the run-in (v about 45) | 40° | 45° | 60% | flat: a delta with the body down its middle |
| **Dive** | diving out of the burst (v over 50) | 55° | 70° | 40% | tucked close, almost a dart |
| **Flare** | the last two seconds before landing | 0°, arms brought slightly forward | 0° | 100% | cupped, tips well up; body pitched up 35°, tail down, legs down |

Beats are **few and strong**, because a jet does not flap:
- a beat is a fast **downstroke** (0.3 s) with the wing spread, then a slower **upstroke** (0.45 s) with the hand half-folded, then open again;
- **climbing** and **taking off**: three to four beats in a row, then a glide;
- **cruise**: a single beat every five to eight seconds, otherwise a long glide;
- **fast pass and dive**: no beats at all, the wings held swept;
- **flare**: two hard back-beats, the wings pushing forward, to stop.
The three dragons beat at their own times, never together, except on the pull-up into the vertical, where all three beat at once: that is the moment they show the most wing.

### Flying like a display team

From how formation display teams fly (the Red Arrows are the best known; see the sources), in plain words:
- **The arrowhead (the vic)**: the leader at the point and the other two behind on either side. The home formation. Drogon leads, Rhaegal left, Viserion right, each 7 units back and 7 out from the leader's tail: close enough that the wingtips nearly overlap, as a display team flies.
- **Echelon**: all three in a diagonal line to one side of the leader, each the same step back and out. Used for a pass along a coast, so that the view sees all three side by side.
- **Line astern**: one behind another, nose to tail, 4 units between. Used along the Wall, where one after another reads best.
- **The low fast pass**: down low, fast, wings swept, straight and level, past the spectators.
- **The break**: from a vic or an echelon, the three peel away one after another, a second apart, each rolling into a hard turn away from the line, so that they fan out.
- **The vertical burst**: all three pull up together into a straight climb, and at the top they burst apart, each to its own direction, like a firework. The salute of fire straight up goes there.
- **The rejoin**: the leader flies steady and slow; the others come up from behind, faster, cut inside the leader's turn and slide into their places.
- **Rolls**: now and then, a full roll about the line of flight; in line astern, one after another (a ripple).

Every turn is **banked**: the dragon rolls into the turn so that its wings tilt towards the inside, as a jet's do. Rule: **bank = atan(v × r / 13)**, where v is the speed in units a second and r the rate of turn in radians a second, never more than **78°**. With 13 in place of gravity, a turn of radius 25 at speed 30 banks about 70°, and a gentle turn hardly at all. The body pitches up into a climb and down into a dive by the slope of its path, the neck and tail following a joint behind. The tail fin swings into the turn by a quarter of the bank.

Speeds, against a toy car, which drives at about 10 to 15 units a second: **cruise 30** (twice a fast car), **fast pass 45, dive up to 55**, **slow and orbit 20**, **perched 0**. Heights, against the toy Wall at 42 and the Hightower at 48: cruise at **55** over land, so that no tower comes within 8 of a dragon; the low pass at **18 to 22 over water**; along the Wall's face at **32**, below its top and 6 south of its face; the vertical climb up to **110**. Keep 8 units between any dragon and anything built, and check the whole route against the plan before building.

### Fire, as a jet's

A breath has two parts:
1. **The glow.** For 0.6 s before the flame, the throat and the base of the neck glow from within, growing brighter, in the dragon's flame colour: a dim box inside the neck joints whose colour is turned up. It warns the eye where to look.
2. **The lance.** A long narrow cone straight out of the jaws: about 0.2 wide at the mouth and 1.4 at its end, **13 units long for Drogon, 11 for Rhaegal and Viserion**, made of thin flat flakes that stream back along it and fade. **Short bursts of 0.4 s** in a pass; **one long lance of 2 s** in the vertical climb and in a solo's single breath. Each in its own colours:
   - **Drogon**: a black core with a bright red edge and red flakes, so that it reads against a dark sky (ASOS ch. 42);
   - **Rhaegal**: orange and yellow, with streaks of green running through it (ADWD ch. 68);
   - **Viserion**: pale gold, with red and orange flakes (ADWD ch. 68).
At night a lance casts a light (`k.lights`) only where the ground beneath it is empty or water.

Where a breath may go, and nowhere else:
- **straight up into the sky**: the vertical climb;
- **over the open sea**, at least 15 units from any ship or shore, aimed level or up;
- **into the ruined Dragonpit**, down onto its empty sand inside the broken dome;
- **onto a beacon**: the Hightower's great fire, which burns green a moment after, and the iron beacon on the Wall's top at Castle Black;
- **into the crater of the Dragonmont** on Dragonstone, which is fire already.

Never over Winterfell's godswood or the Isle of Faces, never over a sept, never over a town or the tourney ground, never towards anything that moves, and never low over land.

### What else gives the feeling of speed

In order of how much each is worth:
1. **The bank and the sweep.** A dragon that rolls hard into a turn and changes its wing shape with its speed is most of the jet feeling, and it is already in the build above.
2. **Shadows racing over the ground.** Each dragon casts a flat dark shape the outline of its wings, laid 0.06 above whatever is beneath (the ground, a lawn, water at -0.9), stretched a little along the line of flight and paler the higher the dragon is. Three of them slide over fields and castles fast and silent, often before the dragons themselves are noticed. Cheap: three flat meshes moved each frame. Over a building it lies on the ground beneath, hidden by the building: that is acceptable.
3. **The vertical and the burst.** The moment everyone waits for: give it the long fire and the three-way burst, and keep it over the bay at the front of the opening view.
4. **Wingtip trails.** A thin pale ribbon from each wingtip, from its last 0.6 s of positions (a strip of about twelve points), shown only when the bank is over 45° or the speed over 40, and fading at once. Over the North in cold air they can be whiter and last a little longer. Cheap: six short strips.
5. **Perched like a parked jet.** On the Dragonmont ledge or the Dragonpit's rim: wings folded flat along the body, legs down, neck up and still, tail laid out behind, the head turning now and then. Something to find when the sky is empty.
6. **The leader's head.** Drogon's head turns into a turn a quarter of a second before his body does, and glances at a wingman now and then in a long straight. Small, and it makes him the leader.

### The things for one tile

Heights are the books' where given. The toy height follows the project's rule: 3.2 m to a unit for a thing known by its height, a small landmark up to twice that, and mountains as toys. Numbers in **bold** in the "Toy" column are a suggestion.

**Beyond the Wall and on it**

1. **The Wall.** A wall of ice across the whole north of the board. 700 ft (213 m) high, about 300 miles long (AGOT ch. 19 and ch. 26, Jon II and IV; ch. 48). Grey or bluish white by weather and hour; a ragged top lined with cranes and catapults (AGOT ch. 19). Straight from Eastwatch to Castle Black and winding west of it (AGOT ch. 30). Model: one long sloping slab, its face in vertical facets, bluer near the top and dirtier at the foot, with a walk along the top. At scale it is 67 units; as a toy, **40 to 44**, the tallest thing on the board after the Hightower and taken as a landform, as mountains are. Why: it is the first thing anyone thinks of, and the board's north edge.
2. **Castle Black.** No curtain wall at all: scattered stone towers and big timber halls against the Wall's south face, joined underground in winter (AGOT ch. 19). The King's Tower, round, about 100 ft (30 m), battlemented, overlooking the gate and the foot of the stair; the Lord Commander's Tower; Hardin's Tower, leaning, its battlements broken; the Tower of the Lance, the tallest, thin and crumbling, a third as high as the Wall; the Shieldhall of black stone; the common hall, a long timber hall; the armoury; the rookery over the maester's timber house (AGOT ch. 19, ch. 21; ADWD Jon chapters). Model: 5 towers, two of them leaning or broken, 3 timber halls with steep roofs. **Toy towers 10 to 16**.
3. **The switchback stair and the winch cage.** A wooden stair of many flights zigzags up the Wall's face on great frozen beams; beside it an iron cage on a chain is wound up and down by a winch at the top, carrying stores and up to ten men (AGOT ch. 19; ASOS Jon chapters). Model: bars for the stair, a box of bars for the cage, a crane arm at the top. The cage goes up and down every minute or so. Why: the best moving thing on the Wall.

9. **Winterfell, the walls.** Two great granite walls with a wide moat between, the inner about 100 ft (30 m) and the outer about 80 ft (24 m); towers on the outer, many more set in the inner (ACOK ch. 50, Theon). A small sept built for Catelyn stands in the yard (with care).

## 4. The layout

### What lies where in the books

Westeros is long from north to south and narrow, widest in the south. From the books' maps, measured by eye with the Wall's 300 miles as the ruler, so rough **(unverified)**:

- The Wall runs east to west across the whole north, with the sea at its east end (Eastwatch) and mountains at its west (the Shadow Tower). Castle Black is near its middle.
- The North is huge, about as large as the rest put together (a figure widely attributed to the author, **unverified**). Winterfell lies near its middle, a long way south of the Wall; White Harbor on the east coast south-east of it; Bear Island off the west coast.
- The Neck, a narrow land of bog between two seas, with Moat Cailin at its north end.
- South of the Neck, the riverlands: the Twins on the Green Fork, the Crossroads Inn south of them, Riverrun to the west, Harrenhal on the Gods Eye south-east of that. The Vale and the Eyrie lie east, behind mountains. The Iron Islands lie off the west coast, and Casterly Rock and Lannisport on the west coast south of them.
- King's Landing on the east coast, on Blackwater Bay, with Dragonstone in the sea east of it. The kingsroad runs from the Wall through Winterfell, Moat Cailin, the Neck, the Crossroads, to King's Landing, and on south-east to Storm's End (wiki route, with AGOT ch. 13, ch. 28; ACOK ch. 9).
- South-west of King's Landing, the Reach: Highgarden on the Mander, and Oldtown at the south-west with the Hightower.
- Storm's End on the south-east coast; Dorne across the far south, Sunspear at its south-east.

As fractions of the length from the Wall (0) to the south coast (1), roughly: Winterfell 0.2, Moat Cailin 0.4, the Twins 0.48, the Crossroads 0.53, Riverrun and Harrenhal 0.55, the Eyrie 0.5, Casterly Rock 0.6, King's Landing 0.65, Highgarden 0.8, Storm's End 0.75, Oldtown 0.92, Sunspear 0.97.

## 5. Roads and what moves

### Which side

Westeros has no rule of the road in the books. Keep **left**: riders in a world of swords pass sword hand to sword hand, which is the old story behind keeping left in Britain, and the books' manners are English. Japan and Hong Kong keep left too, so the rule is not new; Europe has both. The wink: at each gate a small painted board shows an arrow and a shield, and the inn's signpost leans left.

### The road worth driving: the kingsroad

The spine of the tile. From the tunnel mouth under the Wall at Castle Black, south past Mole's Town, through Winter Town past Winterfell's east gate, across the North in long straights and easy bends, onto the causeway through the Neck between Moat Cailin's towers, over the Trident, through the Crossroads, along the shore of Blackwater Bay and in through a gate of King's Landing, out again and south-east to Storm's End. The wiki says it enters King's Landing by the Dragon Gate and leaves by the River Gate **(unverified against the books)**. One route there and back, with a turning loop at each end (the engine's `thereAndBack` in `lines.ts`): at the Wall inside the tunnel's mouth, at Storm's End round the foot of the drum tower. It is never wide in the books (paved near King's Landing, two ruts in the south, a little-travelled track beyond Winterfell: ACOK ch. 9), so it is two lanes all the way, and the way it looks changes as it goes: snow and timber, then bog on stilts, then fields, then the bay.

Its road pattern for the board, which no other tile has: **one long spine with branches, running through gates**. Every branch leaves the spine at a crossroads (the inn's), and every stretch of note passes through something: a tunnel in ice, a castle's gate, a bridge with a tower on it, a city's gate.

The other roads the books name, as branches:
- **The river road**: west from the Crossroads to Riverrun and on to Lannisport and Casterly Rock.
- **The goldroad**: from King's Landing west to Lannisport. Join it to the river road near the Rock so that the two make one loop through the west.
- **The roseroad**: from King's Landing south-west to Highgarden and on to Oldtown, ending at the Hightower's bridge to Battle Isle.
- **The high road**: east from the Crossroads into the mountains, through the Bloody Gate, to the Gates of the Moon at the mountain's foot. It climbs gently and stops there: the Eyrie is reached by mule path, not road, as in the books. (A climb of hairpins is Japan's, and is not repeated.)
- **The causeway through the Neck**: the kingsroad itself on low stilts over the bog (`roadDeck`), between Moat Cailin's towers.
- **The Twins' bridge**: a short branch west off the kingsroad crossing the Green Fork over the Freys' bridge, under the Water Tower and through both castles' gates, and back. A road that passes through a tower on a bridge is the tile's best moment for a hero.

Through-gates for a road: the tunnel in the Wall (a tunnel's mouth), Winterfell's King's Gate (the road passes the gate, not through the castle), the two castles of the Twins and the Water Tower, the Bloody Gate, two gates of King's Landing, and the Threefold Gate of Sunspear if a road reaches Dorne (one straight road through three walls is exactly what the books describe).

Rules from the kit: routes are closed loops and never cross on the level, so the river road and the goldroad loop is one route, the roseroad a there-and-back, the high road a there-and-back with a turning loop at the Gates of the Moon, and where a branch meets the kingsroad the two share a lane and merge.

### What moves besides the traffic

- **The dragons** (section 2).
- **Ravens**: one leaves the rookery at Winterfell every couple of minutes and flies to Castle Black and back; one from the Citadel's tower at Oldtown flies north to King's Landing. A small black shape with a few wing-beats and glides, on a line of its own (`train` on a path in the air, as the jumbo jet is).
- **The winch cage** on the Wall, up and down on its chain.
- **A drawbridge** at the Twins or at Winterfell's gate, which rises and falls when nothing is on it. Traffic does not wait for bridges in the engine yet, so it must lift only at the castle's own gate on a lane no route uses, or between vehicles on a road with few. Note it for the engine.
- **Ships** on lines of their own, as Hong Kong's ferries: two or three ironborn longships on the western sea, oars out; Lannister cogs with crimson sails between Lannisport and the Rock's sea gates; a royal galley and trading cogs in Blackwater Bay; a black ship of the Watch at Eastwatch's quay; a swan-shaped ship from the Summer Isles, white and tall, sailing round from Oldtown (AFFC, Samwell chapters, **look unverified**).
- **The mummers' wagon** on the kingsroad (item 50), on a line of its own beside the road, or as a `standingCar` that changes place.
- **Rope bridges** at Pyke, swaying.
- **The water wheel** at Riverrun's Wheel Tower, turning.
- **Steam** off Winterfell's pools, and smoke from the Dragonmont.

### Winks at the toy cars

- **The tourney ground as a car meet**: the lists are a drag strip with a low barrier down the middle, the stands full, and the pavilions each with a car in front of it in the house's colours (a grey car at the wolf's pavilion, a crimson one at the lion's). A hero's place on show in front of the royal stand, facing south-east.
- **A rally stage on the kingsroad**: north of the Neck, a stretch with banners at the bends, spectators on a snowbank, a start arch at Winter Town and a finish at Moat Cailin. A hero's road to be seen on.
- **The Crossroads Inn as a roadside stop**: its yard a car park with a hero's place on show, the stables beside it.
- **Castle Black's yard** at the foot of the stair, where a hero waits for the cage: a fourth place on show.
- **The Twins' bridge as a toll**: a barrier that lifts at the Water Tower (the Freys famously charge for the crossing).

## 6. Names, heraldry and care

### Names and signs
Signs are in the Common Tongue, which is English, in plain lettering. The places keep the books' names. Inns and shops follow the tile rule: the sign says what is sold (Inn, Smith, Armourer, Bakery, Stew), not a name, except the Inn at the Crossroads, which is a place's name. No logo or lettering from the series.

Draw each as a flat banner in the project's own hand: one figure, two or three colours, no fine detail. The direwolf, lion, stag and dragon need a simple outline each; the others are shapes.

Not used: the flayed man of House Bolton, and any arms whose figure is cruel.

### What to keep out
- **Cruelty and death**: no executions, no heads on spikes (the Red Keep's gate and Maegor's spiked moat are drawn without them), no Moon Door scene at the Eyrie, no gallows, no wedding at the Twins, nothing at the broken tower but birds, nothing of Harrenhal's or Moat Cailin's stories, no burning of the gods' statues at Dragonstone, no wildfire. Dragon fire never touches anyone or anything built (section 2).
- **Battles**: the Trident and the Blackwater are rivers, not battlefields. No chain across the bay, no sunk ships.
- **Only-series things**: the series' Iron Throne, its Wall lift and Castle Black, its Winterfell and its Dragonstone (and the real places it was filmed), its dragons' heads and wings, its blue fire, the destroyed Sept, the actors' likenesses, the title sequence's clockwork map, any of its lettering. The Iron Throne is left out altogether: it is indoors, and the series' version is the one people know.
- **Daenerys and riders**: the dragons fly without riders, a choice open to the collector (section 2). A person on a dragon would be a portrait of a character, and the series' look of her is the one people know.

### Septs and godswoods
They are the places of worship of that world, and the tile treats them as it treats a temple or a church elsewhere.
- **A sept** has seven sides and seven statues or seven windows, one for each face of the Seven. Show it whole, clean, lit warm from within at night, with a few calm figures on its steps. Never a joke, never a fire near it, never a dragon's route low over it.
- **A godswood** is old forest left untouched, with a white weirwood at its heart whose carved face is long and solemn. Draw the face as a few dark cuts: not frightening, not comic. No light show, no dragon fire, a pool still before it, perhaps one figure kneeling. The Isle of Faces and the weirwood ring beyond the Wall are the same.
- **The crypts** are a tomb: only the door, a lantern and quiet.
