UIUC research note: the campus of the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, with Campustown and a slice of Urbana

All reading was done on 2026-11-19. Facts are marked where it matters:
- [src] read in a source listed at the end;
- [OSM] taken from OpenStreetMap data (building outlines, storey counts, positions, check dates), which is community-edited and usually but not always right;
- [unverified] background knowledge or inference that no source confirmed. Treat these as questions, not facts.

Positions are given in metres from a reference point near the Armory (40.1060 N, 88.2315 W), as x east and z south, the same way round as the board. They were worked out from OpenStreetMap coordinates and are good to about 10 to 20 m.


## 1. The place in a paragraph

The University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign is a very large land-grant university spread flat across the prairie between two small cities, Champaign to the west and Urbana to the east, with Wright Street as the line between them [src: addresses on either side of Wright Street are Champaign and Urbana respectively]. It is known for engineering and computer science, for agriculture (it keeps the oldest experimental cornfield in the United States in the middle of campus), for a long green Main Quad of red-brick and limestone buildings closed at one end by a domed auditorium and at the other by a Georgian student union with a cupola, and for Big Ten football and basketball in a colonnaded 1923 stadium and a 1963 concrete "flying saucer" arena. Campustown, the strip of bars, cheap food, bookstores and 20-storey student towers along Green Street, runs west from the Quad into Champaign. Somebody who studied there looks first for the Quad seen from the Union steps, the Alma Mater statue at Green and Wright, the Altgeld bell tower, the Morrow Plots with corn growing in them beside the library, Green Street at night, the residence halls they lived in, and the white MTD buses that tie it all together. Everything is flat: there is no relief to speak of, only the shallow Boneyard Creek running through the engineering campus.

### The three must-haves

1. **Illini Union** (MUST). The student union and hotel at the north end of the Main Quad, 1401 West Green Street, Urbana [src].
   - Where: its long axis runs north-south between Green Street (north) and the head of the Quad (south). Harker Hall stands just east of it [src]. Across Green Street to the north is Engineering Hall, about 60 m away [src, OSM]. The Alma Mater is about 140 m to the west-north-west at Green and Wright [OSM].
   - Two buildings in one: the original block of 1939-41 is the northern part, on Green Street; a matching block was added to the south in 1963 (sources also give 1960 to 1962 for the money and 1964 for completion), so the Union now reaches south to the head of the Quad [src]. In plan it was designed as a capital "I" in honour of the university [src].
   - Style: Georgian Revival (also called American Georgian or Colonial Revival), modelled on colonial Williamsburg [src].
   - Two cupolas, both clock towers [src]. The northern cupola, over Green Street, is open: arches about 30 feet (9 m) tall, holding the 1878 class clock and the chapel bell saved from Old Main (University Hall), which stood on this site until 1938 [src]. The southern cupola, over the Quad, is enclosed with glass windows that match the bay windows below it [src]. Each carries an 11-foot (3.4 m) bronze weathervane with an 8-foot arrow shaped like an arrowhead [src].
   - The Quad front: a southern veranda (a porch) closes the north end of the Quad, with the glazed cupola above it [src]. This is the face to model: it looks straight down the Quad, 490 m south to Foellinger Auditorium, which faces it back [src, OSM].
   - Materials and colours: [unverified] red brick with white trim, a dark slate-grey roof and white-painted cupolas, in the manner of its Georgian neighbours; no source confirmed the colours. Storey count: [unverified] about four storeys plus a basement; the hotel is on the third and fourth floors of the south part [src], so at least four.
   - Height: no source gives one. The open cupola is 9 m of arches plus a 3.4 m vane on top of the roof.
   - Inside (for small scenes): a hotel, a bowling alley and arcade, quick food, student offices [src].
   - Why: it is the north anchor of the Quad, the place everybody meets, and the view from its south steps down the Quad is the campus postcard.
   - To model from boxes: a long brick body with a hipped roof, a porch with white columns on the south end [columns unverified], a square base with an octagonal or round lantern of glass on top (south), and a second, open arched lantern on the north end. The two cupolas at either end are the outline that identifies it.

3. **The Six Pack** (MUST). The group of six residence halls in Champaign, south-west of the Armory, now the core of Stanley O. Ikenberry Commons [src].
   - Which halls: Forbes, Garner and Hopkins on Gregory Drive (opened fall 1958, on the former parade ground) and Scott, Snyder and Weston on Peabody Drive (opened 1961) [src]. They were built for men, with two dining halls [src].
   - What they looked like: six alike halls, connected, each rectangular round a courtyard of its own [src]. Each is four storeys [OSM; floor plans for floors 1 to 4 on the housing site, src]. Their footprints are about 65 m east-west by 45 m north-south [OSM]. Brick colour and roof: [unverified; ask].
   - What has gone:
     - The two dining halls, Gregory Drive and Peabody Drive, were demolished in June and July 2010 [src].
     - Garner Hall was demolished at the end of the 2011-12 school year to make room for the extension of Nugent Hall [src].
     - Forbes Hall was demolished to make room for a new hall near Gregory Drive and First Street; most of its rubble was recycled [src]. Wikipedia gives 2013 for the demolition [src]; a news report gives the replacement's opening as August 2016 [src]. The replacement is Wassaja Hall, 1202 S. First Street, dedicated 8 September 2016, in the north part of the Commons [src].
   - What stands now: Hopkins, Scott, Snyder and Weston survive, air-conditioned, with lobbies being renovated [src]. Around them:
     - Nugent Hall (opened 2010, extended 2012), four storeys, the largest footprint (about 128 by 75 m) [src, OSM], on Garner's side of the site.
     - Wassaja Hall (2016), on Forbes' site, about 500 beds, with a public artwork of weathered-steel baskets that are lit from inside at night and grow larger towards the entrance [src].
     - Bousfield Hall (opened for fall 2013 per a 2011 plan, src), six storeys, L-shaped round its own courtyard, with a glassy corner on its public side, at First Street and Peabody Drive, the south-west corner of the Commons, on what had been an empty field [src].
     - The Student Dining and Residential Programs Building (SDRP), home of Ikenberry Dining Center, the one dining hall for the whole group, which seats over 1,200 [src]. It runs along Gregory Drive between Arbor and Euclid streets [src], on the north edge of the group [OSM].
   - Where the courtyard is: each surviving hall has its own courtyard [src]; the 2005 plan was for a new shared lawn, a "quad", in the middle of the Six Pack, where the dining halls had stood [src]. [unverified] that the lawn was built as planned.
   - Layout [OSM, x east / z south in metres]: Hopkins (-507, 385), Weston (-348, 334), Scott (-415, 442), Snyder (-334, 420), Nugent (-490, 273), Wassaja (-549, 280), Bousfield (-571, 438), Ikenberry Dining (-325, 240). So the group fills the land between First Street (west) and Fourth Street (east), Gregory Drive (north) and Peabody Drive (south) [src], about 250 by 200 m. Barton and Lundgren halls (on Fourth Street, north) and Taft and Van Doren (Fourth Street, south, five storeys) are part of the Commons but not of the Six Pack [src, OSM].
   - Why: a must-have, and one with a story: four of six original halls in a ring of new ones. Build the four survivors alike, as low four-storey courtyard blocks, and the new halls visibly different (taller Bousfield with a glass corner, Wassaja with lit baskets).

### The Main Quad

4. **The Main Quad lawn.** A long open lawn running north-south from the Illini Union to Foellinger Auditorium, about 490 m between the two buildings and about 100 m between the building fronts on either side [OSM]. Paths cross it diagonally, sidewalks line both sides, yew hedges hide bike racks, and a wide walk west of the Quad is framed by urns [src]. Trees: honey locusts in groups, oaks and maples [src]; the elms that once lined it are gone [src]. Fifteen buildings stand on or close to it [src]. It is in Urbana. Why: the centre of everything; on the board it should be the open axis the opening view looks up.

5. **Alma Mater.** Bronze group by Lorado Taft, dedicated 1929, about 13 feet (4 m) tall and 10,000 pounds, on a granite base, at the corner of Green and Wright streets, just north of Altgeld Hall (about 64 m) [src, OSM]. A robed woman stands in front of a classical throne with her arms held out in welcome; a male figure, Labor, in a blacksmith's apron stands on her right, and a female figure, Learning, in a classical gown, on her left; they join hands over the throne [src]. A long flower bed runs from the statue towards the corner, called Alma Mater Plaza [src]. Which way she faces: the base has a Green Street side and an Altgeld side [src], so she faces either north to Green Street or south to Altgeld; [unverified] north. It stood behind Foellinger until 1962 [src] and was restored 2012-14 [src]. Why: the most photographed thing on campus; students climb onto the throne for photographs [src]. Small landmark: may be drawn at twice scale.

6. **Altgeld Hall and the Altgeld Chimes.** Richardsonian Romanesque, built 1896-97, designed by Nathan Clifford Ricker, at the north-west corner of the Quad at Wright and Green, address 1409 West Green Street [src]. Its tower is 132 feet (40 m), modelled on the Allegheny County Courthouse in Pittsburgh [src]. Rough stone walls, round arches, a round turret at the front corner (the "1896 front turret") [src]; stone colour [unverified: pinkish-grey]. The tower holds fifteen bells (about 7.5 tons in all) which have rung since 1920 [src]; the daily concert is 12:50 to 13:00 on weekdays in term [src]. Status in November 2026: a renovation. The exterior, roof and tower work is done; the bells were taken down, stored, put back with their patina and reconnected in November 2025; whether they are played again is up to the School of Music and the chimesmaster; interior work began on 1 July 2026 and runs to winter 2028 [src]. There may still be scaffolding at the tower and a construction compound, and a covered walkway with concrete barriers on the west side of Wright Street [src]. Why: the bell tower is the tallest old thing on the Quad and its sound is the campus clock.

7. **Foellinger Auditorium.** The domed auditorium closing the south end of the Quad, facing the Union [src]. Beaux-Arts, by Clarence Blackall, 1907 [src]. Essentially a circle 120 feet (37 m) across with a large entrance block (vestibule) on the north side [src]: two-storey limestone Ionic columns in front of three arched red-brick doorways, flanked by brick masses with brick pilasters [src]. A copper dome (since the 1983-85 renovation) with a 4.5-foot pineapple finial and 396 lights set in it [src]. Built on purpose low enough that the dome shows [src]. Height: not found. Why: the other end of the Quad axis; the lit dome is a night landmark.

8. **English Building.** West side of the Quad, north of Lincoln Hall, 608 S. Wright [src, OSM]. Red brick Georgian Revival, 1905, by McKim, Mead & White, with massive chimneys and dormers; a two-storey colonnade added in 1913 faces the Quad, with towers beside it [src].

9. **Lincoln Hall.** West side, north-west of Foellinger, 702 S. Wright [src, OSM]. A large building round a big auditorium, with terra cotta reliefs telling a story and a strongly oversized cornice [src]; gabled roof [OSM]. Its Wright Street gateway was a gift of the class of 1913 [src].

10. **The Eternal Flame.** A pillar between Lincoln Hall and the English Building on the west side of the Quad, a class gift (the Daily Illini says the class of 1912) [src]. Its exact form could not be confirmed [unverified]. A small marker for the west walk.

11. **Henry Administration Building.** West side, north of the English Building, south of Altgeld, 506 S. Wright [src, OSM]. Stone base, red brick upper storeys, two-storey carved pilasters on the Quad side, repeated bays; three storeys, flat roof [src, OSM].

12. **The east side: Davenport Hall, Noyes Laboratory, Natural History Building, Harker Hall.** From south to north along Mathews Avenue [src, OSM]:
    - Davenport Hall: one of the largest footprints on the Quad (about 60 by 120 m), a central portico with two end pavilions [src, OSM].
    - Noyes Laboratory (1902): Richardsonian Romanesque, a low arch in rough stone flanked by grouped arches [src].
    - Natural History Building (1892): four storeys of brick [OSM], evacuated for structural faults in 2010, closed 2014-17 and reopened in August 2017 after a renovation of about 70 to 79 million dollars [src].
    - Harker Hall: Second Empire, once with a mansard roof, now hipped after a lightning strike; just east of the Union [src].
    - The Literatures, Cultures and Linguistics Building, a plain brick block, stands south of Davenport [src].

13. **Gregory Hall and Smith Memorial Hall.** The south corners of the Quad: Gregory Hall to the west of Foellinger, Smith Memorial Hall (music) to the east, between Foellinger and the Foreign Languages Building [src]. Gregory Hall is brick [OSM]. Smith Hall's looks were not checked [unverified].

14. **Illini Hall site.** The old Illini Hall (once the YMCA and the Union's first home) was demolished in spring 2023; a new building is going up, authorised in March 2025 and due to be finished in late fall 2027, name not yet chosen [src]. It is on Wright Street opposite Altgeld; the exact corner was not confirmed [unverified: Wright and John]. On the board: a building site with a crane, not a finished building.

### South of the Quad

15. **Main Library.** Between Armory Avenue and Gregory Drive, south of Gregory Hall, 1408 W. Gregory Drive [src]. Georgian Revival by Charles A. Platt, 1924-29: red brick, limestone arches at a three-bay main entrance on the east front, high arched windows, a green slate roof with eleven dormers and four tall chimneys, two limestone sculptures by the east door; a bookstack wing on the west [src]. Footprint about 120 by 90 m [OSM].

16. **Undergraduate Library, becoming the Archives and Special Collections Building.** Built 1969 entirely underground, two levels round a sunken open courtyard, east of the Main Library and joined to it by a tunnel, west of the Morrow Plots [src]. Closed 13 May 2022 [src]. Now being rebuilt: in August 2026 it was about 53 per cent done, with new brick plaza walls, a west entrance ramp and a west pavilion with a roof, an east addition and loading dock; finished building expected July 2027, grand opening spring 2028 [src]. On the board: a paved plaza with a sunken courtyard and low walls, one low pavilion, and site fencing. The popular story that it was put underground so as not to shade the Morrow Plots is disputed: an open plaza was an equally important reason [src].

17. **The Morrow Plots.** The oldest experimental agricultural field in the United States, from 1876, a National Historic Landmark since 1968 [src]. Bounded by Gregory Drive (south), the Observatory (north), the Woese Institute for Genomic Biology (east) and the Undergraduate Library (west) [src]. About 60 by 80 m in all [OSM]; sources disagree on the plot sizes (three plots of a fifth of an acre, or of half an acre, total 0.8 acre) [src]. The northern plot has been in corn every year since 1876; the middle one is corn and soybeans; the southern one a rotation with alfalfa [src]. Fencing: not described [unverified: a low fence]. Why: corn growing in the middle of a university, beside a library. In summer it is tall green corn, in autumn brown stalks, in winter bare earth or stubble.

18. **The Astronomical Observatory.** North of the Morrow Plots, south of Smith Hall [src]. A small building with a dome [unverified: brick, 1896, one telescope dome]. A good small landmark between the Quad and the plots.

19. **McFarland Memorial Bell Tower.** On the South Quad, 185 feet (56 m), built 2008-09, designed by Fred Guyton; a carillon of 48 or 49 bells (sources disagree), played by computer [src, OSM]. Position about (366, 352) [OSM]. Given in memory of a woman who died of cancer [src]: see Care. A plain modern brick and stone tower [unverified]; architecture students and one blogger have called it ugly [src]. Why: the tallest thing on the south campus and on the Quad's axis.

20. **South Quad: ACES Library and Mumford Hall.** South of Gregory Drive across from the Main Library [src]. The ACES Library (Funk Library), an octagonal building that dominates the north end of the South Quad [src], 35 m high [OSM]. Mumford Hall faces David Kinley Hall across the South Quad [src].

21. **Stock Pavilion.** The southern end of the main campus, 1402 W. Pennsylvania Avenue [src, OSM], a one-storey livestock arena [OSM]. Looks: [unverified] brick, 1913, with a long roof. Why: animals in the middle of the city; a small scene with cattle or horses.

### Athletics, to the south

40. **Gies Memorial Stadium.** Football stadium, 1402 S. First Street, Champaign, opened 1923, dedicated 1924 [src]. Renamed from Memorial Stadium to Gies Memorial Stadium on 9 September 2025 [src]. U-shaped: two-deck stands along the east and west sides, a single-deck horseshoe at the south end, and a 5,000-seat structure at the north end; 60,670 seats [src]. Its signature is 200 stone columns along the east and west sides, 183 of them carved with the names of university alumni who died in the First World War (182 men and one woman); the dedication was extended to the Second World War dead in 2002 [src]. A press box with suites on the west side [src]. A 12-foot statue of Red Grange (2009) and the Grange Rock at the north end [src]. A video board, 36 by 96 feet from 2013, over the south horseshoe, and a new 17,315 square-foot display in 2026 [src]. Field: Zuppke Field [src]. Footprint about 200 by 210 m [OSM]. Height: not found.

41. **Grange Grove and game day.** A free tailgating area directly west of the stadium, open from 07:00 on game days [src]. About two hours before kick-off the team walks through it among fans, cheerleaders and the Marching Illini (the "Illini Walk"); the drumline plays in front of the Red Grange statue [src]. Tents, grills, people in orange. Why: the best place for a crowd scene and for parked cars.

42. **State Farm Center.** The basketball arena, opened 1963 as Assembly Hall, 1800 S. First Street, by Max Abramovitz (Harrison & Abramovitz) [src]. A folded-plate concrete dome about 400 feet (122 m) across, poured as 24 wedge sections, rising about 125 to 128 feet (38 to 39 m) above the floor at its centre; the roof is held by 614 miles of steel wire wound round its base [src]. Widely called the "flying saucer" [src]. Renovated 2014-15 [src]. Footprint about 160 by 140 m [OSM]. Why: the saucer is the second great outline of the south campus.

43. **Activities and Recreation Center (ARC).** 201 E. Peabody Drive, a large gym about 200 by 110 m, between the Six Pack and the stadium [OSM]. Looks: [unverified].

### The slice of Urbana

46. **Busey-Evans residence halls.** Busey Hall (cornerstone 1916, the university's first residence hall) and Evans Hall (1924-26), on Nevada Street in Urbana [src, OSM], just south-west of Basil Thai. Older brick halls [unverified].

47. **Illinois Street Residence Halls: Wardall and Townsend.** On Illinois Street in Urbana, 1964; Wardall Hall is 12 storeys, Townsend 5 [src, OSM]. Height of Wardall: not found [unverified: about 35 m].

48. **Florida Avenue Residence Halls: Oglesby and Trelease.** College Court, Urbana, completed 1966; two 12-storey towers with flat roofs, each about 45 by 20 m [src, OSM]. Height: not found [unverified: about 35 m]. The tall towers in the south-east of the campus.

49. **Hallene Gateway.** A sandstone doorway carrying the state motto, saved from Old Main (University Hall, 1871, which stood where the Union is) when it was demolished in 1938, stored at Allerton Park, restored and set up in 1998 on a small plaza at Lincoln Avenue and Illinois Street [src]. A favourite backdrop for graduation photographs [src]. Why: it ties the east edge of the tile to the Union, where the building it came from stood.

### The railway and downtown Champaign (west edge)

50. **Illinois Terminal and the railway.** 45 E. University Avenue, Champaign, opened 1999: a three-storey transport centre owned by MTD, with offices and a banquet hall [src, OSM]. Amtrak trains stop there: the City of New Orleans (Chicago to New Orleans) and the Illini and Saluki (Chicago to Carbondale) [src]. The track and platform belong to Canadian National, successor to the Illinois Central [src]. The line runs north-south through downtown Champaign about 0.8 km west of Wright Street [OSM]. The old Illinois Central depot of 1925 is across University Avenue [src]; OSM has a restaurant at 100 N. Chestnut in a building called "The Station" [OSM]. Whether the tracks are on an embankment or at street level here was not confirmed [unverified]. Why: the place everybody arrives and leaves at the start and end of term.

51. **Papa Del's.** Pizza, at 1201 S. Neil Street, Champaign, at the south-west edge of the tile; OSM checked 2025-04-02 [OSM]. Open status in 2026 not confirmed. [unverified] it was long on Green Street before moving.

What cannot be kept is the size of things: at that scale a street of two lanes with its sidewalks (16 units) is 160 m wide, so most streets must go and the buildings must be drawn larger than their share. ### What stands at the back

The view comes from the south-south-east, so a thing H high hides about 1.3 H of ground to its north-north-west. In this place that works out well:
- The tall towers (Campustown, Burnham 310) are in the north-west quarter of the board, which is exactly where tall things belong. Keep them there.
- The Quad is a long open north-south axis, so the opening view looks straight up it to the Union's south front with its glass cupola. Keep Foellinger low, as it was built to be, so that it does not hide the lawn; at 3.2 m/unit even a 30 m dome (unverified) hides only about 12 units.
- The McFarland tower (17.5 units) stands south of the Quad on the axis and hides about 23 units to its north-north-west: the south-west corner of the Morrow Plots and part of the Main Library. Nudge it a few units east of the axis, or accept that.
- The FAR towers (about 11 units) are near the south-east corner, in front. They hide about 14 units north-north-west of themselves, which is open ground round Illini Grove. That is acceptable; drop them a storey or two if they hide Busey-Evans.
- State Farm Center (12 units) sits at the south edge in front of the stadium, about 30 units south of it, so it does not hide the stadium. The stadium in turn stands about 30 units south of the Six Pack and should not hide it unless it is taller than about 20 units.
- Basil Thai is in the east, near the front, on the east face of its building: the opening view sees its shopfront. Keep anything south-south-east of it below about 6 units (Busey-Evans should be kept low).
- The must-haves are all seen from the opening view: the Union at the head of the open Quad, Basil Thai on the east side facing the camera, the Six Pack in the south-west with only the low ARC between it and the camera.


## 4. Roads and what moves

- **The road worth watching:** Green Street, from First Street through Campustown, across Wright Street and along the north edge of the Quad past the Union to Lincoln Avenue. Two lanes with bike lanes and a 25 mph limit between Wright and Fourth [src]. Towers on both sides in Campustown, low buildings and the Union in Urbana. Keep the south side of Green Street low where it should be seen (rule: anything on the south side taller than about its distance from the middle line hides the road).
- **Heaviest traffic:** [unverified] University Avenue, Neil Street and Lincoln Avenue (city arteries), and First Street and Kirby Avenue on game days. Green Street and Wright Street are slow and full of buses, bikes and people [src for Wright: buses only between Daniel and John].
- **Buses (Champaign-Urbana Mass Transit District, MTD):** about 118 buses in service, all built by New Flyer [src]; 40-foot buses and 19 articulated 60-foot buses [src]; twelve run on hydrogen fuel cells (ten 40-foot, two 60-foot), the rest are diesel-electric hybrids [src]. Since 2020 new buses are painted white with small accents at the top and bottom corners, in blue and red [src]; older ones have a swooping band [src]. Campus routes have colour names, for example 12 Teal, 13 Silver, 22 Illini, 21 Raven, Yellow, Green and Gold [src]. In the kit: a "bus" type painted white. The articulated bus is a reason for a vehicle of the tile's own shape.
- **The railway:** Canadian National's line, north-south through Champaign, carrying Amtrak's City of New Orleans and the Illini and Saluki [src] and [unverified] long freight trains. On the board: one track along the west edge with Illinois Terminal on it, a passenger train (silver Amtrak cars [unverified]) that runs there and back.
- **Bicycles:** everywhere; bike lanes on Green Street [src] and racks behind yew hedges on the Quad [src].
- **Other things that move:** students on foot crossing the Quad, the Marching Illini on game day, a hotel shuttle, delivery vans on Green Street, and squirrels.
- **Traffic keeps right.**

## 7. Care

- **Gies Memorial Stadium's columns** carry the names of 183 alumni who died in the First World War, and the stadium is dedicated to the Second World War dead too [src]. Draw the columns plainly and never use them as a backdrop for a joke; no names on them.
- **The McFarland Memorial Bell Tower** was given in memory of a woman who died of ovarian cancer [src]. Draw it as a bell tower, nothing more.
- **The Eternal Flame** on the Quad is a memorial-like class gift [src]; keep it quiet.
- **Wassaja Hall** is named for Wassaja (Carlos Montezuma), in 1884 the first Native American graduate of the university [src]. Its basket artwork is about his journey home [src]. Treat it with respect.
- **Chief Illiniwek**: a mascot played by students in Native American dress, retired in 2007 and still argued about [unverified for the date; the controversy is well known]. Leave out entirely: no figure, no headdress, no dance.
- **Protests**: the Quad and the Alma Mater are where demonstrations happen. Draw no protest, no flags of causes, no slogans.
- **Unofficial St. Patrick's Day**, a heavy-drinking student event in Campustown [src: a Wikipedia article exists], is better left out; bars can be bars without it.
- **Altgeld and Illini Hall** are building sites in 2026 [src]: drawing them as finished is a liberty to note.
- Places of worship near campus (churches and religious houses round Green Street and Wright Street [unverified]) should be drawn with care if used at all.


## 8. Weather and seasons

- **Winter:** January averages a high of about 33 F (1 C) and a low of about 18 F (-8 C), with about 23 inches (58 cm) of snow a year in normal winters, most of it between December and March [src]. Snow on the Quad lawn with the diagonal paths cleared, snow on the Union's roof and cupolas, the Alma Mater with snow on her shoulders (a well-known photograph is of the Union through falling snow over the Quad [src]), the Morrow Plots bare and white, the saucer of State Farm Center white.
- **Autumn:** football season. Oaks and maples on the Quad turn red, orange and yellow [src: oaks and maples]; brown corn stalks in the Morrow Plots; orange crowds walking south to the stadium on Saturdays.
- **Summer evening:** July averages a high of about 85 F (29 C) and a low of 65 F (18 C) [src]. Tall green corn in the Morrow Plots, students on the Quad lawn, the lit copper dome of Foellinger, Green Street's signs on, the amphitheatre at Krannert. Thunderstorms are common [unverified].
- **Spring:** Quad green again, magnolias and redbuds [unverified], graduation photographs at the Alma Mater and the Hallene Gateway [src].
