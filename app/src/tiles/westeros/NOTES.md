# Westeros: notes

This file records what a later reader cannot see in the code.

**Where the tile stands, on 19 November 2026:** every part of the land is built, the roads carry their travellers, three dragons fly, three ships sail and a kraken haunts the channel by the Hightower. The checks under "What was checked" have been run. What is left is the collector's tuning, under "Open, for the collector", and "For the next tile" is written as far as the work has got.

## Sources and rights

The books only: A Song of Ice and Fire, Fire & Blood and The World of Ice and Fire, as the two research notes read them, [westeros.md](../../../../docs/districts/research/westeros.md) and [westeros-dragons-and-more.md](../../../../docs/districts/research/westeros-dragons-and-more.md). The second note says what it could verify and what it could not.

Nothing comes from the television series: not its dragons, its castles, its throne, its lettering or its map. Place names from the books go on plain boards. A house's arms are drawn from their plain description, and on a banner or a cloak there are only its colours. No character is portrayed, and the Iron Throne is left out.

## How the tile is put together

`plan.ts` is the tile as numbers and draws nothing: the coasts, every lot with its height, the Wall, the roads' middles with their gates, bridges and turning rings, the rivers, the islands, the places for a hero, where Drogon lands, and the line the dragons fly. `land.ts` lays the ground and the water from it, and `roads.ts` the roads, the lanes and the fires by the wayside.

What stands on the ground is built region by region, each in a file of its own: `wall`, `holds`, `winterfell`, `neck`, `vale`, `riverlands`, `kings-landing`, `stormlands`, `reach`, `west` and `dragonstone`. A region hands over a model for each of its lots (`models.ts` says how), and `city.ts` builds a lot from its model, or as a plain box with its name on a board if it has none. So the tile was whole at every stage, and a region's file can be rebuilt without touching another. `look.ts` holds what the castles share: stone by where it was quarried, roofs, the loops and lancets that glow at night, ramparts, towers, banners, braziers, the weirwood, the name board. `country.ts` plants and builds the open country between the castles, last of all, and asks `space.ts` what ground is open and how tall a thing may be there.

`dragon.ts`, `dragons.ts`, `flight.ts`, `show.ts`, `fire.ts` and `trails.ts` are the dragons. `ships.ts` and `kraken.ts` are what moves on the water. `wayfarers.ts` are the travellers, and `herald.ts` the picture on the board that shows what is playing.

The regions and the open country were built by Opus builders, one to a file, each from the same brief and a task of its own. Each came back with a report, was judged in pictures from the opening side and from behind, by day and by night, and was sent back with exact numbers where it fell short. The plan, the ground, the roads, the dragons, the ships and the kraken are the main session's.

`westeros` in the page's console, on the development server, has the tools the tile was looked at with: `hold` stands a dragon still, `at`, `rate`, `split` and `goto` set the show's clock, `off` takes the dragons away, and `look`, `close`, `sheet` and `fourViews` take pictures with `wimmel.shot`. `?bare` leaves the ground empty, with one of each kind of traveller in a row. On every load the console says how many triangles stand still, part by part, how near the dragons come to what, and what the opening view sees of the roads.

## The ground

A continent between two seas. The sea runs down both edges and widens to the south, and the south-east corner is open water. The Wall is the north edge, and the Frostfangs stand over its west end. North of the Neck the ground is snow. The Neck is bog. South of it the ground is green, and Dorne is sand.

- Rivers, each a shape of its own that stops a hair short of the water it runs into: the Trident from Riverrun to the bay, the Green Fork into it from the Neck, the Mander along Highgarden's north wall, and the Blackwater's last stretch into the river mouth under King's Landing. The Trident's mouth is a small funnel cut into the corner of the bay, so that the river ends square on its far side.
- Still water: the Gods Eye, Winterfell's moat, Queenscrown's lake, Riverrun's rivers, and the pools of the Neck. The last four are "wet lots": the ground is left out there, and whoever builds the place floors it.
- Islands, laid as level ground in the sea: Bear Island, Dragonstone, Battle Isle, and the foot of Casterly Rock.
- High ground is each region's own, built as faceted hills, and `groundAt` in `regions.ts` is the highest of them at any place. The dragons' shadows fall on it, and a dragon's tail lies on it where it lands.

## Roads

Traffic keeps left: the collector's choice.

The pattern, which no other tile has: **a tree with three loops in it, on which every lane turns left at every meeting of roads.** The kingsroad is the trunk, from beyond the Wall to the fork before Storm's End, and every other road leaves it or a branch of it. The roads cut the land into four pieces: three inside the loops, and everything outside them. Each route is the one lane that runs all the way round one piece, keeping it on its left. So at a meeting of roads nobody goes straight on across anybody: each lane turns left round its own corner, as on a roundabout where everyone takes the first exit. No lane crosses another and none joins one, so nobody ever waits, and the engine's check finds no two roads that cross on the level.

- `kingsroad`, the long way round the outside, about 935 units: from the ring beyond the Wall south through the tunnel in the ice, through Castle Black, past Winterfell, through Moat Cailin's Gatehouse Tower, left at the crossroads up the high road through the Bloody Gate to the Gates of the Moon and back, over the Trident by the inn, through the Dragon Gate and along King's Landing's quays and river front, over the Blackwater through the River Gate, up to Storm's End and round its drum, through Sunspear's three gates and back, down the roseroad, over the bridge to Battle Isle and round the Hightower, up the ocean road and the west coast between the Gods Eye and Casterly Rock, over the Twins' bridge under the Water Tower, and north again to the Wall.
- `riverlands`, about 160: north up the kingsroad from the crossroads, west over the Twins' bridge, and back east along the river road.
- `westerlands`, about 365: west along the river road, south between the lake and the Rock, east along the goldroad through the Lion Gate and along the river front, and north along the quays.
- `reach`, about 265: west along the goldroad, down the ocean road, back along the roseroad to the fork, and north over the Blackwater bridge.

A branch ends in a ring to turn in, which traffic goes round with the clock: round a weirwood beyond the Wall, in the yard of the Gates of the Moon, round Storm's End's drum, in Sunspear's inner court, and round the Hightower.

**The fork before Storm's End** is where four roads meet: the kingsroad from the bridge, the road up to the castle, the road to Sunspear and the roseroad. It lies 28 from the middle of the castle on the line of the climb, and the ramp begins at 22: a lane that comes down has left the ramp before it turns, and lanes turn on a radius of 2 there, where everywhere else it is 3.6. It lay on the ramp's foot at first, and a traveller clipped the ramp's wall: see "What was checked".

A lane is 4 wide and a road 8, narrower than the city tiles' roads, because nothing wider than a wain uses them. The roads have no markings. Every road is packed earth where it meets another: two colours in one plane would flicker. Between their ends the goldroad is yellow stone, the roseroad rose-coloured setts and the ocean road shell sand, and the kingsroad north of the Neck is frozen mud.

Storm's End stands on a headland 3.2 high, so the road climbs to its gate, and the ring round its drum is the one stretch of road that is not on the ground. Its wall is low towards the sea, so that the opening view sees a traveller come round the drum.

**Fires by the wayside** stand where another tile has street lamps: an iron basket of burning wood on a post, to one side of the road and the other in turn, wherever a road runs through open country. There are 27. In a castle's yard and at its gates the torches and braziers are its builder's.

## Who travels the roads

Nobody drives a plain car: the collector's choice, "sparse or no cars, maybe we can substitute with something else that feels on brand". So a hero is the only car on the tile, and round it go seven kinds of traveller, 17 in all: a rider at the gallop, a knight under a banner, two brothers of the Night's Watch with a torch, a cart, a haywain behind two oxen, a covered wain, and a wheelhouse behind four white horses. A rider's cloak and a knight's banner are in the plain colours of a house, and there are no arms on them. They are toy figures of lead and wood, and stand in one pose, as toy horses do: they do not move their legs. Torch and lanterns burn at night, and none throws a beam on the road.

The engine draws each kind as one batch, and they keep their distance and their lanes as plain traffic does. The engine gained this for the tile: see "For the engine".

## What is playing

Every tile has one board that shows what is playing: the cover, the title and the artist. The engine does all of it, through a sign of kind `screen`. It is the one modern thing on this tile.

**The herald's board at the tourney ground**: a painted board on a timber frame under a shingled roof, west of the royal stand, facing the opening view, with a herald and his trumpet beside it. A tourney has a herald, and the meet has its bill. It is the sign `herald` in `signs.ts`, and the riverlands' file hangs it. It was planned east of the stand, and moved when King's Landing's wall turned out to stand there.

When nothing plays, the board shows its own picture (`herald.ts`), and that is what most people see. It is a bill a herald would paint, bold enough to read from across the tile: **"WINTER is COMING"**, the Starks' words, beside a grey direwolf's head set in a cart wheel that stands in a snowdrift, with "fit your winter wheels" beneath, and "The wheelwright, Winter Town" as the one who paid for it. It is an advertisement, as the boards of the built tiles are, and the wink at toy cars is the wheels. The wolf is this tile's own drawing.

One board is enough. A second, a singer's board in the yard of the inn, was thought of and left out.

## The tile's place in the world

The table stands on a desk whose top is an old map of the world, and each tile has a pin in it. Westeros is in no atlas, so it brings its own land (`place` in `index.ts`): its coast as the books describe the continent, in this tile's own drawing, 27 degrees from north to south, in the empty South Pacific at 139 west and 30 south. The words lettered beside it: "Hic sunt dracones". The map's own sea serpent swims clear of its southern tip, to the south-east, and the last of the words run under its tail.

On the map the continent is drawn long and narrow, as in the books. On the board it is squeezed: see "Liberties".

## The dragons

Three, after the books' three and in the books' colours: Drogon black and scarlet, 14 long with a span of 19, whose fire is black shot with red. Rhaegal green and bronze with wings of jade, 12 and 16, whose fire is orange and yellow veined with green. Viserion cream and gold, 11.5 and 15.5, whose fire is pale gold flecked with red. Section 2 of the second research note is their design: lean and long, two legs with the wings as forelimbs, everything swept back, wings that change shape with speed, a hard bank into every turn, few strong wingbeats and long glides, fire as a narrow lance after a glow in the throat, and shadows that race over the ground. They carry no rider: the collector's choice. They are this tile's own drawing, and nothing of them is taken from the television series.

**How one is made** (`dragon.ts`). A dragon is one mesh of rigid parts: a head with its horns and frill, a jaw, five joints of neck, three of body and ten of tail, and for each wing an upper arm, a forearm, a hand of three claws and four fingers, with a leg of three bones under each hip. On every frame each part is turned and moved to where the pose puts it, and the skin of the wings is drawn afresh between the shoulder, the elbow, the wrist, the finger tips and the flank. The tail follows where the body pointed a moment ago. The material is the city's cel shading with both sides of every face drawn, and light of its own in the eyes and the throat.

**How one flies** (`dragons.ts`, `flight.ts`, `show.ts`). The show is places as a function of time: the leader's line through the plan's waypoints, and a slot beside it for each of the other two that changes with the formation called. From where a dragon was and will be come its speed and how hard it turns, and from those its heading, its bank (never more than 78 degrees), how far its wings sweep back (spread below 22 units a second, a dart above 55), the turn of its head into a bend before its body follows, and the ribbon off each wing tip in a hard turn. It beats its wings when it has to work, as a great bird does: without a pause while it climbs, two or three beats and then a glide of two or three seconds while it cruises, one shallow flick now and then when it is fast, and not at all when it comes down, dives or rolls. Over a display circuit each beats for about a third of the time, in ten or twelve bursts, each in its own time, the black one slowest. All three beat together on command at the pull-up, and one beats on command to brake before it lands and to leave. The body rides each beat, lifted through the downstroke and let down through the upstroke, with its neck giving the other way so that the head stays steadier than the chest. The tail sways in a slow wave of its own. In a glide the wings are never quite still, and in a turn the inner wing is drawn in and the outer one reaches. The collector asked for this: with a beat every five to eight seconds they had looked too rigid. A wing that beats draws no ribbon from its tip.

**On the ground** a dragon with two legs stands as a bat does: on its feet and on the claws at the wrists of its wings, set down ahead of its chest and wide of it, elbows out, chest held up, fingers folded back along its flanks, neck run forward and down so that its head hangs low. The collector asked for this in place of an upright sitting pose, which looked like a bird's. Feet and wing claws are set on the ground by reach: each limb bends to where it must stand and hangs towards the ground while it is still out of reach, so the same joints carry the flare, the landing and the leaving. The tail is laid out behind it on whatever ground is there: where the ground falls away, as on a rim, it hangs down over the edge and lies on the slope below.

**A display circuit**, 38 seconds:

1. The run-in: east along the south shore in a vic, down to the sea in the south-east corner, and a banked turn to the north.
2. The low fast pass: north along the east edge over open water, at 45 a second and 22 high, in echelon. Each gives one short burst ahead, a third of a second apart.
3. The vertical: a pull-up off the end of the pass, all three beating together, straight up to 110 over the mouth of the bay, slowing until they almost hang. One long lance of flame each, straight up.
4. Off the top to the west-north-west, down to 55 over the bay and the north end of the city, and north up the kingsroad over the Twins and the Neck.
5. Once round Winterfell at 57 high, the other way to the clock.
6. North-east to the Wall's east end, and west along the Wall in line astern at 37 high, below its top, with a roll one after another.
7. Left at the Shadow Tower and south down the west coast at 50, in echelon, past Pyke and Casterly Rock.
8. Once and a quarter round the Hightower at 40, in trail, and east along the south shore to the run-in.

The research note gives one circuit as about 100 seconds. That does not agree with its own speeds: at 30 a second a dragon crosses the whole tile in under 8 seconds. The speeds are kept, since they are the feeling of a jet, and the circuit is as long as it comes out.

**A split**, about one circuit in four and never two in a row, 60 seconds: at the top of the vertical they burst three ways and are apart for 24 seconds. Drogon dives to the sea in the south-east corner with one long breath over open water, comes back north past Storm's End and up the west side of Dragonstone, turns hard to the right north of the Dragonmont, and comes down over its back onto the high rim of its crater. There he stands braced for nine seconds, the crater glowing before him, facing the view the tile opens with, his tail hanging over the rim behind him. Then he drops off over the crater with three strong beats, goes round the south of the island and climbs to the others. Rhaegal goes west across the riverlands, south over Pyke and past the Rock, very low down the sea past the Hightower on its seaward side with one short breath, and once round Highgarden. Viserion climbs in widening circles over Winterfell, runs the Wall alone, and comes back down the east side over the Vale. They come together again over the city.

Where Drogon lands is in the plan, as `DRAGONMONT`: the place, the way he faces, the level table under him, and the crater. The mountain is built round those numbers. At first he landed on a level shelf in the corner of the mountain's lot, and a shelf that large in a corner left the mountain no room to slope: it stood as a block. See "For the next tile".

Where a breath may go: straight up; over open sea; into the Dragonpit's empty sand; onto the Hightower's beacon and the beacon on the Wall at Castle Black; and into the Dragonmont's crater. Nowhere else, and never over a godswood, a sept, a town or the tourney ground. Fire harms nothing. Only the first two are used so far.

**What they cost**: a little under a millisecond a frame on the build machine for all three with their fire, shadows and ribbons, and 14 draw calls. They cannot cast real shadows, because the engine draws its shadow map once: each has a flat shadow of its own, draped on the ground. The three shadows share one name, because the check for surfaces that fight takes two meshes of one name and one colour for one, and where two shadows cross they only darken.

## What else moves

**Ships** (`ships.ts`). Three under sail go round one long way through the south-east sea, a third of the way apart: down the mouth of Blackwater Bay past Dragonstone, round the corner of the stormlands, about in the sea off Dorne, and back up the east side. A merchant cog of the crownlands with a red band on her sail, a trader out of Braavos with a purple hull and purple sails, and a galley of Lys with a striped hull. Each is a mesh of its own. They keep clear of the quays, of Dragonstone and of Storm's End's rocks by 4 or more. The ships at the quays are their builders' and lie still.

**The winch cage** (in `wall.ts`) makes a slow round: a while at the foot of the Wall, up the ice with a brother in it, a while at the walk, and down. The cage is one mesh and its chain another, one bar that is drawn as long as the cage hangs low.

**The kraken** (`kraken.ts`). The collector asked for it: the channel between the Hightower and Lannisport read as empty, and he wanted a kraken there, half seen, that goes up and down the channel and shows itself now and then. Most of the time it is a dark shape just under the water that glides along the channel, its arms trailing behind it. At one of three places it stops and comes up: a head with an eye to each side, and ten arms that rise out of the sea one after another, curl and wave, two of them longer than the rest and clubbed. It is up for about eleven seconds in every thirty. Then it goes down, sinks deep where it is hardly seen, turns, and comes back at the next place. While it is up its shape under the water is gone: the collector saw that it had stayed, pointing the way the beast had been going. The beast is one mesh whose parts are placed by hand while it is up, as the dragons' are. Its shape under the water is a flat one, drawn dark and thin a hair over the sea, because the engine's water is not seen through. It keeps to the north-east of the strip of water that the Hightower hides from the opening view.

## Liberties taken with the books

With the map:

- The whole continent is squeezed onto one board. North to south is squeezed hardest, and the North most of all: Castle Black stands at Winterfell's north wall, and Moat Cailin at its south-east corner.
- **Harrenhal stands on the Gods Eye's east shore**, not its north. What stands north-north-west of a tall thing is hidden by it, and there it would have hidden the river road.
- **The Gods Eye is small**, so that the road down the west side passes between its shore and the foot of Casterly Rock.
- **The kingsroad swings east to the bay and runs along King's Landing's quays**, outside the wall, where the books take it through the city. Inside, the three hills would have hidden it.
- **The crossroads lies north-east of King's Landing**, at the foot of the Vale, and the Trident is crossed just south of it, as in the books.
- **Dorne is the middle of the south edge, and the south-east corner is open sea.** It is the water the dragons' low pass and their fire need, at the front of the opening view.
- **Four roads meet before Storm's End**: the roseroad leaves the kingsroad there, and the road to Sunspear with it.
- **Oldtown is a harbour row, the Citadel and the Starry Sept on the south shore, and the Hightower on its isle**: the city between them is left out, because the ocean road needs the ground.
- **Every lane turns left at every meeting of roads**, as under "Roads".
- Bridges stand where the books have a ford or a ferry: over the Trident by the inn, and over the Blackwater.
- Rivers are short and narrow, and only the Trident, the Green Fork, the Mander and the Blackwater's mouth are there.

With the places:

- **Winterfell's ward is raised to 6**, as a castle on a mound, so that the godswood, the pools and the glass gardens are seen over the walls.
- **Storm's End stands on a headland and its wall is low towards the sea**, where the books give it a wall a hundred feet high on every side. A tall wall there would hide the whole bailey and the fork north-west of it. The buildings of its bailey are fronts set into the wall's inner face, because the lane round the drum leaves no ground between the drum and the wall.
- **The Dragonmont is a steep cone**, 30 high on a foot 16 across: the island is no wider. Its summit is a level table for Drogon and a crater before it.
- **The Twins' keeps stand behind their gatehouses**, to the north, each 8 wide and 5 deep: the ground between the Green Fork, the road and Moat Cailin allows no more. They have no hall.
- **The Red Keep has no White Sword Tower and no barbican**: there is no room. Its heart tree is drawn as an oak among ordinary trees. Where the three gates that stand shut are is this tile's choice.
- **King's Landing's fish market is beside the Sept's plaza and on the wharf**, not inside the River Gate, where the Red Keep's hill is.
- **Harrenhal's gate is between two of its towers in the south wall**, the only room there. Its bear pit is built up from the ground, which cannot be dug. Its towers and walls have a few arrow slits and no other windows.
- **Riverrun's court is small**, and its wheel stands still, as every wheel on the tile does.
- **Sunspear's Sandship is on the sand by the court**, and its name stands on the middle of its three gates.
- **The tourney ground is a meet, and the inn's yard a place to pull in.** These are the collector's own interest, put where the opening view shows it. The rally stage on the kingsroad in the North is another.
- **The open country is thinner than was meant**: about 375 trees of the 900 hoped for. No tree is taller than 9, none taller than 5 where it would stand before a castle, and none so tall that a road or a name board is lost behind it.
- A kraken in the Sunset Sea is this tile's own: the books speak of krakens as the ironborn's arms and as sailors' tales.

## Names on boards

There are 41 name boards and the herald's board. Every name is a place in the books, lettered plain, as the research notes give it:

The Wall, Castle Black, The Shadow Tower, Eastwatch, Queenscrown, Mole's Town, Winterfell, Winter Town, The Smoking Log (Winter Town's inn and alehouse), White Harbor, Bear Island, Deepwood Motte, Moat Cailin, The Twins, the Inn at the Crossroads, the Bloody Gate, the Gates of the Moon, The Eyrie, the Giant's Lance, Riverrun, Harrenhal, Tourney ground, Casterly Rock, Lannisport, Pyke, King's Landing, the Red Keep, the Great Sept of Baelor, the Dragonpit, the Dragon Gate, the Lion Gate, the River Gate, Dragonstone, Dragonmont, Storm's End, Sunspear, Highgarden, Oldtown, The Hightower, the Citadel, and the Starry Sept.

How they were checked: every name but four is in the research notes, most with the book it is in. The second note gives the names of King's Landing's gates as from memory, and neither note has the Smoking Log. On 19 November 2026 a web search of A Wiki of Ice and Fire confirmed the Dragon Gate, the Lion Gate and the River Gate, and the roads they open on as the tile has them: the kingsroad north, the goldroad west, and the kingsroad and the roseroad south. It confirmed the Smoking Log as Winter Town's inn and alehouse. The three gates that stand shut have no board, and where they stand is this tile's choice. The colours of some northern houses, which the second note marks unverified, are only on the shields at the rally stage, as plain colours.

The boards stand where the opening view reads them, and the open country plants nothing tall before one.

## What was checked

- **Roads.** `checkRoads` runs on every load of the development server: no road crosses another on the level, and none runs through anything built but through its gate. It goes by the plan's boxes.
- **Lanes against what is really built.** Rays along every lane, at three places across it: upwards for anything lower than 5.8 over the road, and ahead at two heights for anything standing in it. This found what the boxes could not: a traveller coming down from Storm's End clipped the ramp's low wall as it turned for Sunspear, and rode a hand above the ground there. The fork was moved for it. What is left is the sides of Sunspear's three arches, which are 5.2 over the road at a lane's outer edge and 6.4 at the crown: no traveller is that tall.
- **Traffic.** Twenty minutes in steps of a twentieth of a second: the nearest any two travellers come is 3.8, where the two lanes of one loop run together at the crossroads; nothing stands still; none follows another closer than 10; and no lane is shared.
- **Surfaces in one plane.** `wimmel.fights('westeros')` comes back empty, and stays empty over 160 seconds of the show with the dragons, the ships and the kraken moving.
- **The dragons' circuit.** `clearance.ts` flies both kinds of circuit on every load, in tenths of a second, with each dragon's wing swept by its speed and banked by its turn, against every lot of the plan. Nothing comes nearer than 8. The nearest are Casterly Rock and the Wall's top (8.6), Storm's End's drum (8.7) and the Dragonmont (9.2). The landing is left out, since a dragon is meant to be near what it lands on.
- **Places for a hero.** Each of the seven is clear of anything built, by rays through a box the size of a car, and seen from the opening view at its middle and its four corners. One low corner of a car at the tourney ground is behind the Lion Gate's tower.
- **What the opening view sees of the roads**, by the plan's boxes, on every load: about a quarter of the long route is behind something, most of it north of King's Landing behind the city, beyond the Wall, and behind gates. The count takes the Dragonmont as a box 30 high, which it is not.
- **Sides and weather.** From 42, 132, 222 and 312, by day and by night; rain at dusk and by night; snow by day.
- **`npm run typecheck` and `npm run build`** pass. `main` had not moved since it was last merged, at 15d4458.

## What it costs

On the build machine, each tile alone on the board in a window 866 by 526, the pane not in front, so the numbers are rough: Westeros takes about 5 ms a frame and North America about 3. A little under 1 ms of that is the dragons.

| | Triangles that stand still | People | Trees | Moving |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Westeros | 102,500 | 474 | 759 | 17 travellers, 3 dragons, 3 ships, a kraken, a cage |
| North America | 42,400 | 259 | 57 | 99 vehicles |

That is more than twice a built tile's triangles, where the page that says how to build a tile asks for about twice. They are spread thinly: King's Landing 11,400, the riverlands 11,300, the Reach 11,000, Winterfell 9,400, the Wall 8,500, the west 7,900, the open country 7,900, the stormlands 7,200, the holds 6,800, the Neck 6,750, the Vale 6,200, Dragonstone 5,300, the roads 1,600 and the land 1,100. No one lot is dear, so a trim would be a tenth off every part. It was not done: a frame is mostly the heroes' own triangles, and the saving could not be measured. It is the first thing to do if the whole board runs slowly.

## For the engine

Asked for and given while this tile was built:

- **Traffic of a tile's own shape.** A tile can hand the engine kinds of traffic beside its routes. Without it this tile would have had cars or nothing.

Still wanted:

- **A window as narrow as it is asked to be.** A window is four tenths of its cell wide whatever its kind, so a wall with few windows gets wide ones: Harrenhal's first slits were panes 2 wide. A castle needs a slit. Here it is a dark quad a hair proud of the wall.
- **A turned box whose faces know their length.** `obox` gives its faces no wall length, so a window spec on one draws a single stretched window. `rampart` in `look.ts` builds its wall as a box that is turned and moved instead.
- **Water that is seen through, or water of a darker kind.** The Neck's pools want to be black and are pale blue, and the kraken's shape under the water is a flat mesh laid over the sea.
- **Trees for snow and for dark woods.** `k.tree` gives one green: the wolfswood's pines are bright in the snow.
- **Something that keeps a thing in the air in focus.** The soft focus blurs by depth, so a dragon high over the table is blurred when the view is close, and so are the towers under it. `?focus=off` and the B key turn it off for the whole view.
- **Shadows for what moves.** The shadow map is drawn once, so anything a tile moves casts none. The dragons carry flat shadows of their own. A second, small shadow map for moving things would let them shade the towers they pass.
- **A check of lanes against what is built.** `checkRoads` goes by boxes that the tile hands it. The rays that found the fault at Storm's End are a few lines, and belong beside it.
- **A count of triangles part by part.** `city.ts` keeps one for this tile and says it in the console. `wimmel.info()` could.
- **Strata on steep cut faces.** `drawField` draws teeth where a field is cut off steeply at the board's edge; the regions here draw their own cut faces.
- **Where the map's words go.** The words beside a land that a tile brings run under the map's own sea serpent here.

Built in this folder and worth moving to the engine: the rays along lanes (they were run from the console and are not in the code), `space.ts`'s way of asking what ground is open and how tall a thing may be before a name board, the registry of name boards in `look.ts`, the ship in `ships.ts`, and from the regions' files `vault` and `doorway` (King's Landing), `palm` and `q4` (the stormlands), `face4` and `boat` (the riverlands), and `taper` and the clipped drawing of a field (Dragonstone).

## For the next tile

- **Write the plan as numbers before anything is drawn, and stand every lot as a box.** The whole tile could be looked at, and its roads checked, before one castle was built. But the plan's faults only showed when the castles stood: a gate's lot a unit off its road, a river under a turning ring, two lots sharing ground, a fork on the foot of a ramp. Draw each lot's box, each ring and each river's width on one picture from above before building, and look at where they touch.
- **Give a mountain room to slope.** A level place on a height has to stand near the middle of its ground. In a corner it leaves two sheer faces, and the thing reads as a block whatever is drawn on it.
- **Builders, one to a file, from one brief.** Twelve were built side by side in about forty minutes each. What made it work: a plan they could read as numbers, a shared file of looks, a rule that a builder touches only its own file, and a brief that says what is judged. What cost time: every save reloads every tab on the development server, so run a second server with hot reload turned off for judging, and tell the builders about it; and the browser runs out of tabs, so close the builders' tabs when they come back.
- **Judge in sheets.** One picture made of six or nine, each cut to what matters, from the opening side and from behind, by day and by night, is read in one look. A builder sent back with exact numbers takes three to five minutes.
- **Say how tall a window is to be, and look at black stone by day.** The engine's windows are pale panes by day. On pale stone they are windows; on black stone they are an office block.
- **A blanket rule costs more than a measured one.** Keeping every name board clear by a zone in front of it took 76 trees and a farm. A limit on height by how far before the board a thing stands took none.
- **Check the lanes against what is built, not only against boxes.** And check the flat-surface fights while things move: two of this tile's moving shadows crossed only now and then.
- **Count triangles from the first day**, part by part, in the console. This tile was at 100,000 before anybody added them up.
- **When the pane is not in front, frames do not run by themselves** and times are rough. `wimmel.frame` runs them, and `wimmel.advance` lets time pass.
